# In Limbo — Adventure Design Document
*Working title. Proper names established below.*

---

## Overview

**System:** D&D 5th Edition (2014 ruleset / SRD 5.1)
**Level Range:** Tier 1–3 (levels 1–15)
**Structure:** 9 chapters across 3 acts
**Tone:** Philosophical and mysterious — questions of freedom vs. security, truth vs. comfortable lies, order vs. chaos. Planescape tradition.
**Player Characters:** Native inhabitants of the Pax

### The Central Premise
The Pax is a 1,000-year-old stable domain floating in the plane of Limbo — a multicultural society built and maintained by its mysterious rulers, the Autarchs, and their monastic order, the Foundation. The Pax is approaching its millennium anniversary. It is also slowly dying.

Pieces of the island are breaking off into the chaos. Storms and earthquakes — unknown for a century — have begun appearing at the borders. Objects and writings in frontier regions occasionally change or vanish when unobserved. The Foundation's peacekeepers are fighting more and more, and losing ground.

The dark secret at the heart of the Pax: those who join the Foundation's inner order are brainwashed into servitude. The most powerful chaos-shapers (Anarchs) are connected to arcane machines buried beneath the Sovereign that slowly drain their life force to maintain the realm's stability. Only the Autarchs know the full truth. The Foundation's rank and file — Peacekeepers and Scholars — have been conditioned to accept that Shapers live in "sacred seclusion" and never questioned it.

The player characters are ordinary citizens drawn into events that will determine the realm's fate.

## The World: The Pax

### Physical Description
The Pax is a large island (~500–1,000 sq miles, roughly Luxembourg-sized) of solid matter floating in the roiling chaos of Limbo. It is one of the larger stable domains in the plane, maintained for a full millennium through a combination of the Autarchs' will, the Foundation's Shapers, and the arcane machines beneath the Sovereign.

**The Sovereign** — a tall, spindly mountain on the northern edge of the city of Meridian. Seat of the Autarchs and the Foundation's primary monastery. The River Constant issues from a great waterfall on its face.

**The Artificial Sun** — a large arcane construct several miles above the Sovereign's pinnacle, created by the Autarchs at the Pax's founding. Governs a 12-hour day/night cycle. Its light is the same every day; the Pax has no seasons in the traditional sense.

**The River Constant** (formally: *The River Constant*; commonly: *the Constant*) — born from the Sovereign's waterfall, flows through Meridian, splits into eastern and western branches, and eventually falls off both sides of the island into the chaos of Limbo below. Primary source of irrigation and transportation for the entire realm.

### The Fraying — Signs of Instability
The Pax's stability is failing. These phenomena currently increase in severity as one moves from the capital toward the edges of the island. The capital remains entirely unaffected.

- **The Fraying:** Chunks of the island's edges calve off and slowly dissolve into the chaos. This has accelerated in recent years.
- **Structural instability:** Earthquakes and violent storms — previously unknown — now occur with increasing frequency in border regions.
- **The Unobserved Effect:** Objects, writings, artworks, and possessions in frontier regions occasionally change or disappear when not regularly viewed or used. Reality only holds its shape where attention is directed.

### Regions of the Pax
- **Meridian** — the capital city (see below)
- **The Interior** — settled farmland, towns, and villages between Meridian and the frontier; largely stable
- **The Frontier** — outlying settlements near the edges of the island; increasingly subject to chaos instability and Slaadi raids
- **The Edge** — the active fraying boundary; some regions have already dissolved back into Limbo

## Meridian — The Capital City

Built against the north face of the Sovereign. The River Constant bisects the city, splitting it into an East Bank and a West Bank before its branches fall off the island's edges. The river is crossed by numerous bridges and is a major artery of daily commerce.

**Character:** Architecturally eclectic — styles from across the planes intermingle with experimental local forms pioneered by the city's own builders. Neighborhoods are grouped by proximity and history rather than defined purpose. The city is organized chaos that somehow works.

**Governance:** Meridian is governed by a large council with representatives from dozens of guilds, neighborhood associations, religious orders, trade companies, and civic clubs. Every citizen belongs to several such organizations and is represented through them. The Autarchs permit the council to manage daily affairs within the bounds of a deliberately vague founding charter. A Witness construct is permanently stationed in the council hall; a Created (Witness model) called the Charter Minder occasionally interjects to clarify points of order. Citizens widely believe the Autarchs observe all proceedings through it.

**Notable Locations (to be detailed in DM Guide):**
- The Sovereign and its approaches (restricted)
- The Council Hall
- The Foundation's main monastery
- The Freelancer's Guild hall (Caske Amanthri's base of operations)
- The Constant waterfront (commercial docks and river traffic)
- Major temple district (Devoti shrines and Path preaching corners)
- The East Bank (wealthier, more established neighborhoods)
- The West Bank (more mixed, market-heavy, where the Freelancer's Guild operates)

## The Autarchs — Rulers of the Pax

The Autarchs are the founders and rulers of the Pax. Their nature is deliberately ambiguous and is never revealed in the adventure.

**Appearance:** Tall, spindly humanoid forms with elongated heads, limbs, and digits. When seen in public — which is rare — they are always fully robed in elaborate, voluminous garments with identical reflective metal masks bearing expressionless, stylized humanoid features. Each Autarch wears a specific color. Their masks show viewers their own faces.

**Forms of Address:** "Your Autarchs" (direct address) · "Our Autarchs" (citizen speaking to others) · "The Autarchs" (informal)

**Number:** Unknown. Exactly 11 robe colors have been observed; citizens believe there are 11 Autarchs. No new color has appeared in over 100 years. Citizens believe new Autarchs are elevated from the Foundation's ranks when a new color appears. The true number has never changed and will not be revealed.

**Public Appearances:** The Autarchs appear only at:
- Semi-annual Foundation initiation ceremonies
- Civic holidays: Founding Day, High Harvest Week
- Occasionally at funerals or weddings of citizens who have rendered special service to the Pax

At least one Autarch always appears; never more than six at once. Numbers fluctuate seemingly at random.

**Behavior:** Never eat or drink. Move with economy. Never speak directly. Each Autarch is attended by Foundation members, a Throne, and a Speaker construct that serves as their individual voice. When the Speaker speaks during formal ceremonies, the Autarch performs ritual gestures — their elongated forms making these movements profoundly alien.

**The Right of Petition:** Any citizen may petition the Autarchs directly by traveling to the Sovereign. The Autarchs strongly discourage this. If the petition is deemed unworthy, the Speakers announce "FRIVOLOUS!" in chorus. The petitioner is immediately fined or imprisoned with no right of appeal. The right is technically legal and practically terrifying.

**Preferred Methods:** Misinformation, obfuscation, patience. Violence against citizens is a last resort. As the PCs grow more notable, direct elimination becomes less viable; the Autarchs shift to discrediting and isolating them instead.

## The Created — Construct Servants of the Autarchs

Sentient constructs built through an arcane process, purpose-built for specific functions, made exclusively in service of the Autarchs. All have glowing arcane eyes. Universally alien in appearance (function over form). Little is known of their lifecycle or culture. They are self-aware but appear to have little independent personality — with exceptions.

### Known Models

| Model | Function | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Speaker** | Autarch's personal voice | Squat cylindrical body, wheeled, rotating faceplate (pleased/neutral/upset), flat uninflected voice; never interacts with anyone except as Autarch mouthpiece |
| **Docent** | General servitor, messenger, errands | Wagon-like body, four stubby legs, four spindly arms, smiling head; most likely to interact with citizens; mumbles and hums; carves geometric designs into wooden frame; silent in Autarch presence |
| **Throne** | Living seating apparatus | Articulated wood-and-metal frame; magical levitation; lowers for Autarch to kneel in, lifts 4.5 feet off ground; small head with nose-like sensor; head rotates backwards when occupied |
| **Guardian** | Bodyguard and Sovereign security | Black stone; four horse-like legs (deceptively fast); four arms (two with manipulators, two for slamming); no neck or mouth; chest vocalizations; dour and duty-obsessed |
| **Witness** | Surveillance | Wall/ceiling-mounted; flat and architectural; cluster of slowly rotating arcane eyes; citizens no longer consciously notice them |
| **Courier** | Long-distance messenger | Slender, many-jointed insect legs; sealed document compartment; travels alone; never stops for conversation; appearance in a frontier settlement is always significant |
| **Ledger** | Archives and records | Mobile filing cabinet body; quill-tipped arms; tracks population data, property records, Anarch census figures; *contains records with anomalies that don't match the official story* |
| **Mender** | Medical and repair | Long precise fingers; pale; the only Created that routinely touches other beings |
| **Tender** | Sun and infrastructure maintenance | Floating, luminescent; interfaces with arcane mechanisms; rarely seen close-up; increasingly urgent behavior is an environmental clue that things are failing |

**Named Created:** Telegram (Docent; the PCs' recurring point of contact with the Autarchs' apparatus)

## The Foundation — The Monastic Order

The beloved protectors and peacekeepers of the Pax. The Foundation is deeply trusted by citizens; the Autarchs are privately disliked and dismissed, but the Foundation is revered.

**Recruitment:** Members are theoretically volunteers, but significant social pressure falls on those with inborn Anarch ability. Children must be donated to the order by age 10; this act is considered highly virtuous. After 8 years of training, initiates may formally join or leave with minimal stigma. Those who join take a new name and permanently sever contact with their families.

**Life in the Order:** Once initiated, members never leave their monasteries except on official duties. Public uniform: plain white robe, red sash, face-obscuring cowl. Demeanor: benign, calm, rarely interactive with outsiders.

**The Dark Truth:** All initiated members are conditioned to suppress questioning and enforce loyalty. Peacekeepers and Scholars are told Shapers live in deep meditation and sacred seclusion. Only the Autarchs know the Shapers are connected to the machines.

### The Three Sub-Orders

**Peacekeepers** — The majority. Stationed throughout the Pax as law enforcement and chaos-creature combatants. Light-touch law enforcement; broad interpretive authority; prefer mediation and soft power. Their combat role has been growing with the crisis.

**Scholars** — Reside and work in the monasteries. Study chaos, magic, and matters of interest to the order. Concentrated in Meridian's main monastery.

**Shapers (Anarchs)** — The most secretive sub-order. Work to maintain the Pax's existence within Limbo. Never seen in public. The most powerful are connected to the arcane machines. None of this is known outside the Autarchs.

## Factions of the Pax

### The Devoti
The Pax's primary philosophical and religious tradition. The majority of citizens count themselves among the Devoti.

Encompasses the personal worship of deities of any neutral, good, or chaotic alignment — or no deity at all. The primary focus is chaos through the lens of personal understanding, individual freedom, and the sovereignty of all thinking beings. No central hierarchy; independent temples and shrines.

**Holidays:** All Gods Day (parade of deity effigies through the streets) · Day of Contemplation (private fasting and reflection followed by communal feast)

**Role in the Adventure:** The Devoti's core belief in personal sovereignty is an implicit indictment of the Autarchs' system. Individual temples will be forced to choose sides in Acts II and III.

### The Path
A small, public fringe sect dismissed by most citizens as well-meaning cranks. Followers are called Adherents.

The Path split from the Devoti's founding tradition shortly after the Pax was established. Core doctrine: adherence to order is the spiritual power that keeps the Pax separate from the chaos of Limbo. They preach that the Autarchs are usurpers stealing the people's spiritual power, using the Foundation and the Devoti as unwitting pawns. They do community good works (feeding the poor, tending the sick), which is why they're tolerated. Their theological framework is allegorically correct but misidentifies the mechanism. They become increasingly credible as the crisis deepens.

**Leader:** Brother Candor — an extremely austere older human in a plain black robe; balding white hair; spectacles at the end of his nose. Sonorous voice. Masterful debater of obvious intelligence. His gravitas is all that keeps him from being laughed off entirely. He is right about everything.

### The Freelancer's Guild
An organization of agents available for hire across a broad range of skills and services — some of them quasi-legal. Run by Caske Amanthri. No philosophical agenda; purely pragmatic. Their network spans Meridian and reaches into the frontier. Operationally important for the PCs in the middle and later acts.

### Planescape Factions Present in the Pax

| Faction | Presence | Relationship to Adventure |
|---|---|---|
| **Xaositects (Chaosmen)** | Largest faction presence; many live just outside the Pax's borders | Distrust the Autarchs' order; potential late-game allies |
| **Free League (Indeps)** | Significant presence in the merchant quarter | Value freedom; horrified by the truth about the Foundation |
| **Doomguard (Sinkers)** | Small but vocal | Believe dissolution is natural; complicate the Dragon's narrative |
| **Transcendent Order (Ciphers)** | Small monastery separate from the Foundation | Philosophy overlaps with Githzerai; potential inter-faction bridge |
| **Revolutionary League (Anarchists)** | Some embedded in radical Path circles | Overlap in goals; differ radically in methods |

## External Factions

### The Githzerai
A nearby monastery of Githzerai aware of the Pax and its history. They maintain deliberate distance from the realm — the Pax's ordered nature unsettles them philosophically. They will interact with the PCs if their goals align or if there's something in it for them. **Critical:** if the Githzerai learn the truth about the enslaved Anarchs, they will declare the realm must fall and become an implacable enemy. This is one of the most consequential player choices in the campaign.

**Key NPC:** Zerith — Githzerai monk; agent at large for the nearby monastery; businesslike and dispassionate; more tolerant of outsiders than most of her kind.

### The Slaadi
Long aware of the Pax; long incapable of organized action against it. As the realm's stability has weakened, the outlying regions have become easier pickings and Slaadi raids have intensified. A powerful Slaad lord — Vraakon the Ascendant — has become fixated on destroying the Pax as a demonstration of supremacy.

**Vraakon the Ascendant** — Fixated on destroying the Pax. Lacks true organization but can coerce lesser Slaadi. Secretly cultivated by Thantarnax, whom he essentially worships. He is unaware of how thoroughly she uses him. He is the primary antagonist of Act II; his defeat in Chapter 6 is a genuine victory — and a resource loss Thantarnax has already accounted for.

## The Antagonists

### The Autarchs (Potential Antagonist)
Not simply villains — they are desperate preservers who have committed terrible acts in the name of survival. Their preferred methods are misinformation and obfuscation. Violence against citizens is a last resort. As PCs grow more notable and their discoveries harder to suppress, the Autarchs shift from neutralizing to isolating to watching from a distance. They can be negotiated with, reformed, or overthrown.

### Vraakon the Ascendant (Mid-Campaign Antagonist)
Slaad lord. Primary antagonist of Act II. Defeated in Chapter 6. A battering ram Thantarnax has aimed at the Pax's defenses.

### Thantarnax the Unraveler (Final Antagonist)
Ancient female chaos dragon. Patient, philosophical, committed to chaos as an end in itself rather than out of malice. Has been planning the Pax's destruction for 1,000 years since being defeated by the Autarchs in her youth. Uses dominated proxies to weaken the Pax's defenses over decades. The final confrontation of Chapter 9.

**Key history:** ~1,000 years ago, a young Thantarnax challenged the newly-founded Pax and was decisively defeated by the Autarchs. She has grown to full ancient power since, with a single purpose. She may possess knowledge of the Pax's founding that no one else holds.

**Vraakon relationship:** She cultivated his ambitions. He worships her. She uses him as a disposable asset.

### The Githzerai (Conditional Antagonist)
Become hostile only if they learn the truth about the enslaved Anarchs. Whether and when the PCs share this information is one of the campaign's critical choice points.

### Brother Auspex — The Breaking Moment
At the Millennium Celebration (Chapter 4), Auspex's conditioning cracks. He cries out the name of a fellow Foundation member who "became a Shaper" decades ago and was never heard from again. The name he cries is the Foundation name — the identity taken at initiation. A PC with the right background might recognize the old name beneath it.

Because Auspex is elderly, famous, and beloved, the Autarchs' Speakers can credibly frame the incident as a breakdown caused by age and overwork. Most citizens believe this. The PCs are in the crowd.

After the incident, Auspex is quietly removed from his public duties and effectively placed under house monitoring. Reaching him in private becomes a significant late-Act II objective.

## Adventure Structure

### Act I: A Thousand Years (Levels 1–4)
*Establish the world, introduce the cracks, plant early seeds of suspicion*

**Chapter 1: A City of Wonders (Levels 1–2)**
- PCs are introduced as native inhabitants of Meridian during anniversary preparations
- All major factions introduced: the Foundation (respected), the Devoti (ubiquitous), The Path (harmless cranks), the Freelancer's Guild (useful), the council (chaotic and functional)
- Telegram arrives to deliver a message — the PCs' first encounter with the Created
- Inciting incident: a desperate Courier arrives from a frontier settlement with news of catastrophic attack
- PCs are motivated to investigate (through Caske, through the council, through personal connection)
- Introduction of Brother Auspex (the respected elder) and initial Peacekeeper interactions

**Chapter 2: The Fraying Edge (Levels 2–4)**
- Frontier exploration; Morning Wren becomes the guide
- Multiple frontier settlements in various states of chaos-threatened crisis
- Environmental encounters: chaotic terrain, matter-shifting hazards, random elemental pockets, The Unobserved Effect in action
- First major Slaadi encounters (Red and Blue Slaadi raiders)
- First subtle hints that something is wrong with Foundation members stationed on the frontier (behavioral oddities, strange silences, a rescued member who seems "empty")
- Discovery that Slaadi attacks show unusual coordination — someone or something may be organizing them
- Return to Meridian with disturbing news that the Autarchs minimize

---

### Act II: The Shape of Things (Levels 5–9)
*Escalation, partial revelations, political maneuvering*

**Chapter 3: Beyond the Border (Levels 4–6)**
- PCs venture outside the Pax for the first time into raw Limbo
- Navigation mechanics: will-based movement, matter transformation hazards, gravity-free zones
- First contact with Zerith and the Githzerai — cool, disciplined, suspicious
- First confirmed encounter with Thantarnax's dominated proxies (creatures behaving against their nature in suicidal attacks)
- Meaningful encounter with The Path outside the realm — their preaching begins to sound less like nonsense
- Discovery of a Xaositect enclave just beyond the Pax's borders — a potential information source and ally

**Chapter 4: The Millennium Celebration (Levels 6–7)**
- The 1,000-year anniversary: a grand multi-day celebration in Meridian
- Major social hub: all factions present, political maneuvering, rumors
- The Autarchs appear publicly (through Speakers, Thrones, the full ceremony)
- **The Breaking:** Brother Auspex's conditioning cracks; he cries out during the ceremony; Speakers immediately frame it as a breakdown; Autarchs offer the PCs a reward to let it go — first direct attempt to neutralize them
- Planescape faction delegations attend; the Xaositects and Doomguard are conspicuously present
- End of chapter: the PCs must choose whether to dig deeper

**Chapter 5: Shadows Beneath (Levels 7–9)**
- Investigation arc: PCs pursue the truth about the Foundation
- Autarchs deploy escalating misinformation, false witnesses, bureaucratic obstruction
- Vraakon launches first major coordinated Slaadi assault on the frontier — crisis diverts attention and resources
- PCs discover hidden passages and chambers beneath Meridian
- Partial discovery of the arcane machines — evidence of something deeply wrong, full picture not yet revealed
- Autarchs attempt to have PCs discredited or quietly removed (non-lethal at this stage)
- End of act: PCs hold evidence; must decide what to do with it

---

### Act III: The Truth and Its Consequences (Levels 10–15)
*Full revelation, faction alignment locks in, final confrontation*

**Chapter 6: The War Without (Levels 9–11)**
- Vraakon launches full-scale assault — a major battle for the Pax
- Zerith and the Githzerai offer conditional assistance (information exchange)
- PCs must hold the line while managing political fallout from Chapter 5
- Climactic confrontation with Vraakon — major boss battle
- Aftermath: the Pax is damaged but standing; the Autarchs are weakened and more desperate
- Thantarnax stirs — a dramatic sighting or her largest proxy attack yet signals that the real threat is coming

**Chapter 7: The Heart of the Machine (Levels 11–13)**
- Full revelation: the PCs access the arcane machines beneath the Sovereign
- The enslaved Anarchs are found — some are people the PCs know or recognize
- Moral crisis: destroying the machines may cause the realm to collapse
- The Autarchs are confronted directly — desperate, not evil for its own sake, but unwilling to stop
- Key choice point: reform, expose, or dismantle
- If Zerith and the Githzerai have been allies and now learn the truth → they declare the realm must fall; a new front opens
- The Path's long-dismissed preaching proves allegorically correct; their following surges; the Devoti fractures by temple; the council fractures by interest

**Chapter 8: Alliances (Levels 13–14)**
- The PCs must build the coalition to face Thantarnax
- Multiple faction negotiations — each party wants something
- Reform track: Autarchs transition toward voluntary Anarchs; machines begin shutdown
- Overthrow track: PCs establish new governance in the middle of a crisis
- Thantarnax begins direct assault — dominated creature waves of increasing scale
- Preparation for final confrontation; possible excursion to locate Thantarnax's lair in the deep chaos

**Chapter 9: Reckoning (Levels 14–15)**
- Final confrontation with Thantarnax the Unraveler
- Set piece varies by player choices: defend the Pax, lure Thantarnax to prepared ground, or storm her lair
- Custom Chaos Dragon stat block (CR 20–22, built on 5e Ancient Dragon framework)
- Multiple endings based on cumulative faction standings and key choices

## Possible Endings

All four outcomes should be achievable based on cumulative player decisions:

| Ending | Conditions | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| **Overthrow the Autarchs** | Autarchs remained hostile; truth exposed publicly | Dragon defeated; Autarchs deposed; new governing council established; realm enters uncertain but free new era |
| **Reform the Autarchs** | PCs negotiated; Autarchs gradually cooperated | Dragon defeated; machines shut down; Autarchs work with voluntary Anarchs; 1,001st year begins with fragile hope |
| **The Realm Falls — Bittersweet** | Machines destroyed too early; no alternative found | Dragon defeated but the realm dissolves; PCs lead a great exodus into Limbo; a new wandering community survives |
| **Realm Survives, Changed** | Dragon defeated; Githzerai alliance secured | Smaller realm maintained by willing parties; Githzerai establish a presence; a genuinely new era begins |

## Key Mechanics to Design

1. **Limbo Navigation Rules** — Will-based movement, Intelligence/Wisdom saves to maintain direction, matter transformation encounter table
2. **Anarch Abilities** — Optional mechanic for PC Anarchs: simplified chaos-shaping usable 1–3 times per day (non-class-specific)
3. **Chaos Dragon Stat Block** — Custom CR 20–22 built on 5e Ancient Dragon framework; dual breath weapons (confusion cone + variable energy line); spell-like abilities (chaos hammer, word of chaos, protection from law); immunity to compulsion
4. **Faction Relationship Tracker** — Four levels per faction: Hostile / Neutral / Friendly / Allied; determines ending availability
5. **Multiple Endings Framework** — Decision tree in Chapter 9 keyed to faction standings and three key choice points
