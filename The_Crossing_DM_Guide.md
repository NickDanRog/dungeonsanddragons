# THE CROSSING
## Dungeon Master's Guide

*A D&D 5th Edition adventure for characters of levels 1–20*

---

# INTRODUCTION

## About This Adventure

**The Crossing** is set across three distinct parts of the world, connected by the sea and divided by everything else. The Perfect Isles — the Archipelago — sit in the middle of the Middle Sea: a prosperous island nation built from nothing two centuries ago by political exiles who carried their magic with them when they fled. It is a society assembled by deliberate choice, and that choice is visible in everything: its cooperative institutions, its plural culture, its living relationship with the belief-spirits it cultivates and tends. A few days' sail to the east lie the Sacred Lands — the ancient eastern continent, home to the oldest primordial spirits in the world, whose health is bound to the land itself. Together, the Archipelago and the Sacred Lands exist in proximity and partial contact. Neither has much in common with what lies across the Middle Sea to the west: the Unity, a vast industrial nation that outlawed magic centuries ago and does not understand what that decision has cost — or who else it has cost besides themselves.

The beings most mortals call gods are not deities. They are belief-spirits — vast, potent, and real, but created and sustained by mortal faith. They change as belief changes. The true primordial forces of this world are the Old Gods: ancient, fixed, bound to the land, and suffering. The Old Gods of the Sacred Lands are largely intact — their connection to the land and to the peoples who live within it remains strong, if strained by the Unity's colonial encroachment. In the west, it is a different story. The Unity's centuries of accelerating development — its mines, its roads, its cities, its industries — consumed and reshaped the land faster than it could recover, driving the western Old Gods one by one into a torpor from which they may never wake. The land in the west is dying because the spirits who maintained it are gone. The Old Gods who remain are aware of this, and they are deliberating whether mortals can be permitted to continue existing. Several have already been captured or co-opted by the Unity's shadow institutions. The rest are watching. The party's task — which they will not fully understand until Act IV — is to appear before those Old Gods and argue that mortals deserve to live.

This adventure has no single villain. Its primary antagonist is an institution: the Unity, which has caused catastrophic damage through two centuries of expansion without understanding why the consequences kept compounding. Within that institution operates the Cabal — a shadow faction whose leaders know exactly what they are doing and consider themselves justified. Among the Old Gods themselves, several are genuinely opposed to mortal survival and will not be persuaded easily, if at all. Player agency is the structural foundation of this adventure. Everything the party does across five acts becomes evidence they will present at the Conclave: who they freed, what they chose when there was no good choice, what kind of relationship they built with the spirits and peoples of both worlds. Three distinct Conclave outcomes are possible across five vote results, each shaped by a different accumulation of player decisions. None of them is guaranteed.

---

## How to Use This Guide

This book is organized as follows:

- **The Realm** covers everything you need to understand and portray the Archipelago, the Sacred Lands, and the Unity — their peoples, their institutions, their histories, and their secrets. Read this section thoroughly before running the adventure.
- **Running the Adventure** covers all mechanical and narrative systems: milestone leveling, spirit relationships, the Crossing mechanics, and the Three Votes framework for Act V.
- **Acts I–V** present the adventure act by act. Each chapter begins with a summary and DM guidance, followed by scene-by-scene descriptions with read-aloud text, DM notes, encounter information, and skill check DCs.
- **Appendices** contain all custom stat blocks, spirit domain descriptions, random encounter tables, and research clue reference material.

**Read-aloud text** is presented in blockquote format:

> *This text is meant to be read aloud or paraphrased to your players. It describes what characters see, hear, and experience — not what they conclude.*

**DM notes** appear in italics and address pacing, likely player divergences, and conditional outcomes. Where the text says *"if the party does X,"* treat that as conditional logic, not prescription.

## Adventure Synopsis

**Act I: The Four Keys (Levels 1–4)**
The party is selected by the Oracle — the Archipelago's most sacred institution, a belief-spirit incarnated in a mortal host whose pronouncement names specific individuals as the new Circle — and drawn into something they do not yet fully understand. The act establishes the Archipelago, introduces the belief-spirits and their relationship to mortal faith, and takes the party east into the Sacred Lands, where they learn two rituals no one in the Isles has performed in living memory and receive. The have their first encoutner with one of the Old Gods and receive the first frank account of what they are deliberating and why. Act I ends at sea, the party returning home to the Archipelago with both rituals, the name of the first spirit, and a warning about the state of the world that none of them were prepared for.

**Act II: The Middle Passage (Levels 4–9)**
The party crosses the Middle Sea westward to Daliketh — the Unity's eastern port city, and the first place most of them have ever set foot in a nation that considers what they are to be criminal. They operate undercover inside a sophisticated society that has no framework for understanding magic or spirits, and must build relationships, cultivate contacts, and navigate the Unity's surveillance apparatus while completing the mission that brought them here. Act II ends with the party having made their first real mark on the Unity and earned the network they will need to go deeper.

**Act III: Into the West (Levels 9–12)**
From Daliketh, the party travels west through the Unity's interior — through Balnor, the capital, and eventually to Jindark on the northern coast, where the Cabal has imprisoned and Old God —  Huma, the Storm Whale — within the city's harbor, and has been drawing on his power for years. Freeing him is the act's central objective, and it is not a clean job: the structure that contains him is built unto the city's harbor, and everyone who lives and works in Jindark's lower districts lives in the shadow of what is about to happen. The act ends with Huma free and the consequences already in motion.

**Interlude: The Return**
Between Acts III and IV, the party makes the crossing back across the Middle Sea to the Archipelago. This is a transitional period — a chance to rest, report, reconnect with the factions and contacts they left behind, and reckon with what they have done. The Isles are not unchanged by their absence.

**Act IV: Of Gods and Men (Levels 12–15)**
The party returns to the Sacred Lands to find the situation transformed by Huma's rescue. The must make relationships with the eastern factions including a regenade Elven king bent on restoring his people's pride, and an Old God who wants to prevent the destruction of his lands. The act culminates in the confrontation between the Unity's forces and this Old God: a reckoning the party has been building toward since Act I, whose outcome cannot be fully controlled and will determine the shape of everything that follows. The act closes with the knowledge that a Conclave among the Old Gods has been called for the first time in millennia. The party has been invited to attend as representatives of the mortal races where the continued existence of their kind will be discussed.

**Act V: Twilight of the Old Gods (Levels 15–20)**
In the aftermath of the confrontation, the party has six months and a task they do not yet know how to complete: reach the Godshome. The investigation takes them through a network of sources — spirits, witches, Old Gods, and ancient lore — before the answer becomes clear: the Archipelago has a god of its own, and she must be found and woken before the Crossing to the Godshome can be made. Finding and naming Cyrencia, the New God of the Isles, is what opens the path — and her revelation upon waking, that she created the Circle, reframes everything that came before. The Crossing to the Godshome passes through five Ordeals set by opposed Old Gods. The Conclave follows: petition, Great Debate, three votes. Whatever the Old Gods decide, the Cabal has prepared one final move. The ending is built from the accumulation of everything that came before.

## Themes and Tone

**The Crossing** is an adventure about belief — what it creates, what it sustains, and what its absence destroys. The belief-spirits of the Archipelago are not metaphors; they are real beings. The Old Gods of the western continent are gone because the Unity's centuries of expansion consumed and reshaped the land faster than it could recover — not through malice, but through the compounding momentum of development without understanding, until the spirits that maintained the world were driven into a silence they may never break. The adventure asks whether that damage can be repaired, and at what cost, and whether the people who caused it deserve the chance to try. The answer is not given in advance. Aim for weight throughout: the Archipelago is genuinely good, and players must come to love it before they understand how much depends on it. The Unity is not a caricature — it has real institutions, real people, and real believers. The Cabal's members are true believers, not cynics. The opposed Old Gods have legitimate grievances. Even those among the Old Gods who insist on consensus above all are not obstructing — they reflect something true about how a world that has to last must work. Every major choice in this adventure should feel like it matters, because it does.

## Adjusting for Your Party

**The Crossing** is designed for a party of four to five characters. For other party sizes:

- **Three characters:** Reduce enemy hit points by 25% and lower encounter DCs by 1–2. The Ordeals in Act V should be simplified to two tests each rather than three, and the research window should provide clues more liberally.
- **Six characters:** Add hit points to named antagonist encounters throughout. The Final Showdown in Act V should include additional Cabal operatives as persistent combatants alongside the Cabal's leadership. The Ordeals may include additional waves or complications at the DM's discretion.

**If the party rushes Act I:** The road to the Sacred Lands contains encounter hooks that can be shortened without breaking the story, but the first Crossing should not be rushed — it is the party's introduction to the spirit world and needs space to register. If the party moves quickly through the Old Man's shrine, allow time for his warning to land before they depart.

**If the party invests deeply in spirit relationships:** This is the intended experience. Spirit relationships have mechanical consequences in Act V, but they are also simply good drama. Give spirits room to be characters, not objectives.

**If the party treats Crossings as combat challenges:** Not all spirit encounters are solvable by force, and the Ordeals in Act V specifically are not. Brief this expectation early and reinforce it through the Crossings in Acts I–IV, where the limits of force in the spirit world should become apparent before the stakes are at their highest.

# THE REALM

---

# THE ARCHIPELAGO

The Perfect Isles — known collectively as the Archipelago — are a group of islands in the middle of the Middle Sea, situated between the western and eastern continents without belonging to either. They were not discovered but created: approximately two hundred years ago, a coalition of magical practitioners fleeing persecution by the founders of the Unity used their combined power to raise a chain of islands from the sea floor, concealing them from the outside world through layers of obfuscating magic that have been maintained and reinforced ever since. The original exiles were united not by culture or tradition — they came from dozens of backgrounds and carried dozens of separate teachings — but by what they had escaped. That origin is still visible in the Archipelago's most fundamental social fact: not a nation but a collection of communities, each preserving its own identity, gathered in proximity by shared necessity and bound by the understanding that dissolution would mean death.

The Archipelago has grown considerably since its founding. The original chain of islands has been expanded by magical means several times over, and new communities have arrived across the generations as the Wayfinders brought more exiles out of the Unity. Today the Archipelago is home to more than thirty distinct factions — some numbering in the hundreds, others in the thousands — each with its own dress, practices, and place in the life of the Isles. The factions are unified by shared history and shared danger, and divided on nearly everything else. No single faction holds authority over the others, and the Archipelago has no unified government in any conventional sense. What it has instead is the Council, the Wayfinders, the Seawatch, and the memory of the crossing that made all of it possible.

The Archipelago's dominant anxiety is discovery. A few days' sail to the east lie the Sacred Lands; the Unity lies much further west, across the full breadth of the Middle Sea — a crossing of approximately two months. Of the two continents, the Unity poses by far the greater existential threat, and the Isles' institutional posture reflects this: caution above action, preservation above change, the Barrier above all. This has been a sound strategy for two centuries. It has also calcified, in the present generation, into something that is itself becoming dangerous — a fear of change so deep that it resists even the changes the Archipelago must make to survive.

## The Barrier

The Barrier is not a wall. It is an accumulation of obfuscating magic built up over two centuries, maintained by the Wayfinders and reinforced by successive generations of Isles practitioners who understood what discovery would mean. Ships that enter the Barrier's range through ordinary navigation experience disrupted compasses, thickening fog, persistent and inexplicable course errors, and — if they persist — the growing certainty that there is nothing here worth finding. The effect is not violent; it is discouraging. Vessels that have entered the Barrier's influence have turned back without quite knowing why.

The Unity has heard rumors of the Perfect Isles' existence. A handful of its ministries — those dealing with the supernatural and with rebellious citizens — believe the rumors may have substance, and view the Archipelago, if it exists, as a useful pressure valve: it draws away potential troublemakers without requiring state action. This is, broadly, a correct assessment, and the Isles' Council prefers it remain so. Returning from outside the Barrier requires a **sending stone** given out sparingly by the Council; ordinary vessels cannot navigate the return crossing without Wayfinder guidance.

## Key Locations

### Kartos's Isle

The largest of the Archipelago's islands — called the Big Island informally by virtually everyone, though the formal name honors one of the founding factions' leaders — Kartos's Isle is the Archipelago's administrative and spiritual center. The Council meets here, the Oracle's primary residence is here, and the Wayfinders' headquarters is here. The island is large enough that its many significant settlements feel genuinely distinct - these include Dremma in the north, Sanctuary inland, and Heida in the northwest, separated by two days of overland travel at the island's full extent. The interior is hilly, forested, and traversed by well-maintained tracks. The Council site sits at the island's geographic center, a half-day's walk from any of the main settlements.

### Dremma

Dremma sits in a bay on the northern coast of Kartos's Isle and serves as the Archipelago's primary port — the single point through which all travel to and from the outside world passes. It is a working town rather than a scenic one, its character shaped by the constant movement of vessels, cargo, and people through the Barrier. Every ship that has ever left the Isles for the Sacred Lands or the Unity departed from Dremma's harbor; every exile the Wayfinders brought home arrived at its docks. The town has accumulated around this function the full infrastructure of a busy transit hub: inns, warehouses, chandleries, and the dozens of small trades that cluster wherever ships stop. The **Waning Moon**, an inn near the harbor, serves as lodging for the party both before departure and on their return.

### Sanctuary

A quiet village approximately a day's travel inland from Dremma along a track that climbs through wooded hills before descending into the valley where the village sits. Sanctuary is organized around the practical needs of the Oracle and the sect that serves her: the community grows rice in the surrounding valley, maintains the Oracle's residence and staff, and provides the infrastructure of pilgrimage without any of its pretension. The **Oracle's Pagoda** stands on a hilltop above the village — the Oracle's primary residence when she is on the Big Island and the most sacred site in the Archipelago. Carved into the bedrock beneath the pagoda is a well-lit ritual space, prepared for exactly the kind of work the Oracle oversees.

### Heida

Located on the northwest of Kartos's Isle, Heida is remote enough that reaching it requires a full day's journey from Dremma along coastal tracks. The village exists, functionally, to support the **Wayfinders' Lodge** — the headquarters from which the Archipelago's operations into the Unity are planned and staged. Most of Heida's residents have some connection to the Wayfinders or their support network; the settlement grew up around the Lodge's needs rather than independently of them. The **White Willow House**, a teahouse that caters to travelers and Lodge visitors, is the primary social gathering point for outsiders in the village and the place where most non-Wayfinder business in Heida gets done.

### The Council Site

At the geographic center of Kartos's Isle, two days' overland travel from Dremma, the Council site is a modest compound built around a large circular meeting hall and the adjacent buildings that support it. The architecture is deliberately unassuming — the Archipelago's founders were people who had lived under the weight of imposing institutional buildings, and their successors have been careful to build the Council's home in the spirit of a gathering place rather than a seat of power. Representatives travel here from across the Isles for full sessions; the site maintains a small permanent staff and can accommodate delegates for extended stays.

### Other Islands

The Archipelago encompasses a number of other islands of varying size and significance. **Santama**, one of the southern islands, is home to **Candela**, a village built primarily around the **Nexus** — a substantial library maintained by the Pentad and the Archipelago's primary repository for scholarly work outside the spirit world. Other islands host individual factions or small communities and are generally accessible only to those with specific business there.

## Power Structure

### The Council

The Council is the Archipelago's closest approach to a unified government, though what it governs is narrow by design. Any recognized faction may send a representative; the Council's authority extends to decisions that affect the Archipelago as a whole — the Barrier, external relations, major resource allocations, and responses to existential threats — and stops there. Faction sovereignty within each community's domain is total. The Council operates by deliberation and consensus, a process that is slow by intention: the founders understood that a governing body drawing from thirty-odd communities with incompatible traditions could not function by majority rule without fracturing, and built accordingly. The Council is therefore slow to act, prone to extended deadlock on difficult questions, and largely content with both facts. That content has served the Archipelago well in quiet times and may not  in what is coming.

### The Oracle

The Oracle is not a person. She is a belief-spirit — the spirit of Belief itself, incarnated in a mortal host — and the Archipelago's most sacred institution. The spirit's connection to its host is complete and lifelong from the moment of the bond; the host speaks with the Oracle's knowledge and the Oracle learns through the host's experience. The current host is **Maya**, a girl of twelve at the adventure's beginning, well-trained in her role despite her age and precise in both her speech and her bearing. The Keepers of the Veil serve the Oracle in all practical matters and maintain records of her pronouncements going back to the Archipelago's founding. Those pronouncements are not interpreted by the Council: they are enacted. This is the one domain in which the Council's usual deliberative process gives way to something that moves with remarkable speed.

### The Council and Its Factions

The relationship between the Council and the Archipelago's factions is one of deliberate ambiguity maintained by long practice. The Council is the forum; it is not the government. Factions that disagree with a Council decision may express that disagreement through their representative and, if the disagreement is strong enough, simply decline to participate in the relevant action. This has produced, over two centuries, a culture of negotiated interdependence: factions cooperate because cooperation has historically been the condition of survival, not because any authority compels them. The **Concordat** mediates disputes between factions when they escalate; the **Wayfinders** and the **Seawatch** are the two institutions that function as something approaching unified civic bodies, the former managing the Isles' relationship with the outside world and the latter guarding the Barrier's integrity.

## Factions

The Archipelago's thirty-three recognized factions represent the full range of traditions, philosophies, and practices that the original exiles carried with them from the Unity. No two are alike, and many are in active disagreement with each other on a variety of fundamental questions. What they all share in common is the memory of the crossing and the understanding that they cannot survive without each others.

---

### Spiritual

**The Godcallers** are a monastic sect whose entire practice is organized around remembering what the Unity tried to make the world forget. They maintain detailed records of the deities worshiped across the western continent before the One True Way's purge — their names, their stories, their rites — and can often be heard chanting those names as they go about ordinary life. Their robes are black and tattered, sewn with white ribbons bearing the names of forgotten gods and fragments of scripture; the ribbons accumulate over a practitioner's lifetime, and an elder Godcaller's robes can carry thousands of names. They are the Archipelago's primary institutional memory of the cost of the Unity's suppression.

**The Keepers of the Veil** are a small order of female nuns who serve the Oracle and maintain detailed records of her utterances and teachings going back to the Archipelago's founding. They dress in white with head coverings and veils and follow a vow of public silence. They are universally respected and politically untouchable. What is less widely known is that the Keepers also maintain a covert scrying network monitoring developments in both the Unity and the Sacred Lands; their intelligence work operates entirely behind the vow of silence, which makes them easy to underestimate. When they choose to share what they have observed, their interpretations can be cryptic, but they are rarely wrong.

**The Twilight Order** commune with the spirits of the Archipelago's dead and maintain detailed genealogical records going back to the founding. They offer, to those who know how to ask, consultation with departed ancestors — a service that makes most people uneasy so that the Order is careful not to advertise widely. Their records of lineage and their capacity to reach the recently deceased make them quietly indispensable to families with questions the living cannot answer.

**The Blue Star Adepts** combine druidic practice with significant arcane study, making them among the most technically literate factions on the Isles. They are familiar with technology and use it more readily than most, though carefully — comparisons to the Unity are not a compliment in the Archipelago, and the Blue Stars are aware of this. They serve the community as healers and are particularly knowledgeable in astronomy, which grounds both their practical and spiritual work.

**The Greensingers** are a small, insular faction deeply attuned to the minor nature spirits of the islands. Their practice of summoning these spirits — what they call spirit singing — is incidental to daily life in their community rather than a formal ritual; they maintain a genuine, informal relationship with the Isles' spiritual ecology that is unlike anything in the broader Archipelago. They know more about the native spirits of the Perfect Isles than any other faction, and their knowledge becomes unexpectedly relevant as the party begins to understand the relationship between mortal belief and the spirit world.

**The Wild Hunt** is a small circle of druids who live as close to nature as possible, count many members with lycanthropic blood among their ranks, and place the druidic tradition of shapechanging above all other practices. On the first new moon of each year they conduct a ritual hunt across Kartos's Isle, and anyone found outdoors during the hunt is captured and held for ritual ransom until their release can be negotiated. Most people on the Isles simply stay indoors that night and consider the matter settled.

**The Order of the Five Mountains** is a highly spiritual monastic order whose practices seek enlightenment through the careful understanding of the physical world and the spirits that live within it. Where the Even Hand turns inward, the Five Mountains turns outward; theirs is a philosophical relationship with the spirit world rather than a ritual one. They are among the most broadly engaged of the monastic factions and among the most curious about the Crossings the party will perform.

**The Path of Virtue** is a highly structured order organized around strict adherence to seven cardinal virtues: an offshoot of the broader Pathist philosophy, formalized and given martial expression. They are among the most morally earnest factions on the Isles and among those most likely to hold strong opinions about the ethical dimensions of any course of action — and most likely to express those opinions unprompted.

---

### Arcane

**The Truebloods** are a faction of sorcerers from multiple sorcerous bloodlines, united by devotion to the preservation and practice of magic itself above all other concerns. They care about magic; they care considerably less about the broader Isles community, a position that makes them politically difficult and their knowledge of innate magical traditions unmatched. Most factions treat the Truebloods with the weary tolerance reserved for institutions that are indispensable and unpleasant in roughly equal measure.

**The Discordians** admit only those with Wild Magic in their heritage and are as unwelcome for their habitual mischief as they are celebrated for their spectacular and utterly unpredictable magical performances. The relationship between the Discordians and the Truebloods — who will admit any sorcerous bloodline — is one of mutual exasperation maintained across generations with something approaching affection.

**The Order of the Seven Stars** is an esoteric group that makes pacts with beings they believe represent a higher spiritual reality. These beings are in fact uncaring extraplanar entities. The Order is sincere, well-intentioned, and largely unaware of this. Their esoteric knowledge makes them powerful arcane practitioners however, though they are often reticent to share what secrets they have gleaned from their otherworldly patrons. 

**The Avengers** are a semi-monastic order that makes pacts with lower planar entities in preparation for what they see as an inevitable war with the Unity. They believe that a final confrontation with their ancient enemy is a matter of when, not if, and they are committed to being ready. They are among the most militant factions on the Isles and among the most resistant to any approach to the Unity that does not culminate in its destruction.

**The Guardians of the Hidden Vale** are practitioners who undergo a ritual sanctification at seven sites in a hidden valley in the Sacred Lands, forming bonds with the fey spirits of the region. They are the Archipelago's best source of knowledge about the Sacred Lands and maintain the most active ongoing presence there of any Isles faction.

**The Eightfold Path** is the largest faction of wizardly practitioners on the Isles, a semi-monastic group that teaches the eight schools of magic as a form of esoteric knowledge: each school a discipline, each discipline a path toward understanding. The Path is somewhat insular and rarely engages with non-members on equal terms, but the depth of their arcane knowledge exceeds that of any other faction. Their senior members are rearely comfortable letting others forget this.

**The Arcane Practitioners Association** functions more as a guild than as a traditional faction — a collection of independent wizards gathered for mutual support, bound by the goal of improving the reputation of arcane practice through public works. They are the most approachable of the wizardly factions and the most likely to maintain strong relationships with non-practitioners.

---

### Philosophical

**The Pathists** are less a faction than a broadly shared philosophical orientation; their teachings, centered on the spiritual journey specific to each person, are broad enough to encompass members of many other factions simultaneously. They are the largest single group on the Isles, and their values permeate Isles' culture without translating into a coherent political bloc. The Path of Virtue is an offshoot, formalized into martial practice. Most people on the Isles identify with Pathist principles at some level, whether or not they identify as Pathists.

**The Wayfinders** are devoted to the protection and preservation of the Perfect Isles and take it upon themselves — at considerable personal risk — to seek out and rescue magical practitioners and others who wish to escape the Unity, bringing them to the Isles. They are among the most broadly respected factions in the community; their work is understood to be both necessary and dangerous, and their members are chosen accordingly. The Wayfinders also manage the Barrier and control access through it, giving them a structural role in the Archipelago's security that no other faction shares.

---

### Martial

**The Lodge of Perfection** is a formal academy of martial practice organized around the principle that the use of weapons can be elevated to an art form equivalent to any other. The Lodge offers tutelage to anyone willing to commit to their rigorous training, regardless of faction affiliation, and is one of the few institutions on the Isles that openly advocates for military preparation against the Unity.

**The Wild Bunch** is a roving fellowship of bravos, duelists, and martial adventurers united by a shared appetite for challenge and spectacle. They travel the Isles seeking feats of strength and daring, settle disputes through formalized duels designed to be impressive rather than lethal, and have no doctrine, fixed base, or meaningful leadership. While they barely qualify as a faction, they are difficult to ignore.

**The Order of the Even Hand** follows a regimen of extreme physical discipline and meditation that eschews spiritual teaching entirely. The Even Hand has no doctrine about the spirit world and does not discuss it; their path to enlightenment is the inward perfection of the self through expanding its physical limits. They are respected for their discipline and for their willingness to act decisively when action is required.

---

### Civic and Cultural

**The Troubadours** are made up of singers, musicians, visual artists, and theatre companies — one of the only genuine sources of shared cultural life across the Archipelago's many communities. Many practice as solo entertainers; others have formed traveling companies. They are welcomed wherever they go. They are also one of the only Isles groups to regularly travel to the Sacred Lands, where their performances are received with considerable enthusiasm.

**The Pentad** is an organization of researchers and scholars structured around five disciplines: Chronicles, Literature, Poetry, Preservation, and Natural History. Their operations center on the **Nexus**, a substantial library in the village of Candela on Santama. They are the Archipelago's primary institutional memory for subjects outside the spirit world and produce most of its written scholarship. Their relationship with the Founders' Keep — the Isles' other major historical institution — is collegial rivalry maintained across generations.

**The Shadowed Disciples** are secretive to the point where many on the Isles doubt they exist at all. Those who claim to have encountered them describe an organization that offers clandestine services — conducted under cover of night — for those who can find them and meet their price. Whether they are a genuine faction or a useful fiction maintained by parties unknown is a matter of ongoing debate among those who take them seriously.

**The Greenwardens** have taken it upon themselves to protect the wild places of the Archipelago from encroachment by the Isles' growing settlements, ensuring that civilization does not overwhelm the natural landscape that conceals and sustains the community. They are combative by inclination and have made significant trouble for those they judge to be upsetting the natural balance. Most factions find them irritating; nearly all acknowledge that without them, the islands' natural character would have been lost generations ago.

**The Seawatch** patrols the stretch of Middle Sea along the western edge of the Barrier between the Archipelago and the Unity. Their methods rely on animals of sea and sky as advance scouts and on the Barrier's own magic as a turning force against approaching vessels. They are the Isles' first line of defense against discovery and are treated with corresponding seriousness by the Council.

**The Messengers** carry news and small parcels between the settlements of the Archipelago with fast boats and faster feet. Their neutrality is inviolable and their discretion absolute. They function as an unofficial postal service, and their couriers are welcomed and protected everywhere without exception. Without the Messengers, the fractious community of the Isles would lose one of its only reliable connections across its many islands.

**The Concordat** consists of diplomatic mediators whose sole purpose is maintaining the fragile peace between the Archipelago's factions. They serve as neutral arbiters, treaty-keepers, and negotiators when disputes escalate. They are universally respected in principle and universally resented in practice when they intervene. Without them, factional tensions would almost certainly have broken into open conflict generations ago.

**The Tidecallers** are the Archipelago's sailors, navigators, and sea-workers — the faction that maintains the fleet and knows the Middle Sea's currents, reefs, and weather patterns better than anyone else. Reliable travel between islands, and to the continents, would be considerably more dangerous without their expertise. They train their own and accept apprentices from any faction willing to commit to the sea life.

**The Honored Forge** produces items of exceptional quality across every craft discipline — metalwork, woodcraft, leatherwork, textiles, and more — many of them imbued with magical properties. A commission from the Forge is considered a mark of distinction on the Isles. They are among the wealthiest factions and use that wealth to support organizations and projects they consider worthy, a practice that gives them quiet influence considerably beyond their formal role.

**The Returned** are former Unity citizens who escaped to the Archipelago and organized around their shared experience. They hold the most current firsthand knowledge of Unity society, culture, and institutions of any group on the Isles, and are viewed with suspicion by many other factions, who question their loyalties. They are the most passionate advocates for eventually going back to liberate those still trapped within the Unity.

**The Provisioners** manage the Archipelago's food supply, coordinating fishing fleets, farming operations, and trade between the islands. Their work is unglamorous and essential. They have significant economic leverage and considerably less idealism than most factions; they care about feeding people rather than spiritual philosophy, and they are direct about it.

**The Founders' Keep** is devoted to the history and memory of the original exile. They maintain the Archipelago's oldest records, its founding documents, and the ancestral lineages of the families who made the first crossing. Their headquarters occupies an old keep and lighthouse complex built by the original exiles — one of the oldest standing structures in the Isles and among the most symbolically significant. They are deeply conservative, resistant to change, and view themselves as custodians of what the Archipelago fundamentally is and must remain. On this last point, they are not wrong; whether being right is sufficient has become increasingly uncertain.

**The Healers' Circle** is a combined mundane and magical healing organization that serves the entire Archipelago regardless of faction affiliation. Their complete neutrality and humanitarian mission make them the one faction that can move freely even between hostile groups. They accept no payment for emergency care; their operations are funded by voluntary contributions from the factions they serve.

# THE SACRED LANDS

The Sacred Lands is the larger of the two continents, stretching east and south of the Middle Sea to such an extent that the Unity's cartographers who first mapped the western coast had no sense of how much land lay beyond. Its geography encompasses dramatic ranges of climate and terrain: a temperate rainforest dominates the northwest, dense jungles fill portions of the interior, vast plains extend east from the forest edge, and the southern expanse is bounded by the **Ringing Mountains** — a high and ancient chain that encloses the **Great Weald**, a vast and still largely unmapped southern interior of forest and highland. The continent has no unified character; it is too large, too varied, and too sparsely settled for any such thing to have emerged. What it has is specificity: each region is itself, unmistakably and without apology.

The peoples of the Sacred Lands include all the races of the west — humans, elves, dwarves, halflings, gnomes, and others — alongside numerous peoples found rarely or not at all in the Unity: Tortles, Harengons, anthropomorphic-animal peoples of many kinds, fey creatures, lycanthropes, and wilder beings. These peoples are deeply fractious. Divided by race, creed, tradition, and geography, they are most often at peace with one another — genuine conflict between Sacred Lands peoples is rarer than their divisions might suggest — but unified action across the continent's communities has historically required extraordinary circumstances to produce. The fractiousness of the Sacred Lands' peoples is not merely political; it is cultural, rooted in the accumulated practice of communities that have never had reason to coordinate. They are most often at peace with one another, and genuine conflict between Sacred Lands peoples is rarer than outsiders sometimes assume. What they share is not culture or politics but a relationship to the world that the Unity has spent centuries dismantling in the west: the relationship between mortals and the spirit world in which both are understood to be real, present, and mutually obligated.

The spiritual ecology of the Sacred Lands is the healthiest in the world. The peoples of the continent worship the Old Gods directly and give regular offerings and gifts to the nature spirits of their particular places as a matter of ordinary life. The Old Gods are present here and largely intact. Nature spirits — the animating presences of particular streams, valleys, and farmsteads — are active and accessible throughout the continent; encounters with them are not remarkable and require no extraordinary ritual, only attention and respect. The relationship between mortal life and the spirit world in the Sacred Lands is what it was designed to be: mutual, ongoing, and sustained by daily practice rather than exceptional effort.

The relationship between all the peoples of the Sacred Lands and the Old Gods is direct, ongoing, and taken for granted in the way that only things deeply embedded in culture can be taken for granted. Offerings are given. Spirits are consulted. The natural world is understood to be animate and attentive in ways that the Unity has spent centuries training its citizens not to believe. The party, arriving from an Archipelago where the spirit world is known but largely conducted at a remove, will encounter in the Sacred Lands a version of the mortal-spirit relationship that is still functional — still daily, still ordinary, still reciprocal. It is the argument, made visible, for what the world could return to if the deliberation goes the right way.

The force that has come closest to bridging the continent's fractiousness is not a human leader but the presence and will of an Old God. **Carnuth, the Stag of the New Moon** — whose domain is the great temperate rainforest of the northwest known as the Wildwood — has taken on mortal-accessible forms and is actively organizing and leading the resistance to the Unity's colonial advance. That an Old God would do this is not, among the peoples of the Sacred Lands, surprising; that Carnuth specifically chose this commitment signals the severity of what is happening in the north. His survival is, for reasons that will take time to make fully clear, essential to everything that follows.

## Key Locations

### Keshigo

The western coast of the Sacred Lands receives travelers from the Middle Sea at Keshigo — less a proper town than the idea of one. It has no clear boundaries, no formal leadership, and no pretension to permanence; people have gathered in this general area because it is convenient, and what has resulted is something that functions without having been designed to. Trade and information move through Keshigo freely, if informally. Travelers arrive and depart in both directions; goods change hands without documentation; news and rumor circulate through a loose network of regulars who know where to find each other. The Guardians of the Hidden Vale maintain a quiet presence here through their Aasimar agent Pinion, who manages the sect's affairs in the region and can serve as an introduction to local knowledge and contacts. Keshigo is not a destination so much as a threshold — the place where the journey into the Sacred Lands properly begins.

### Meeting

A trading settlement in the old-growth forest interior, used by wild elf hunter-gatherer tribes and a handful of other peoples as a regular place of exchange. Goods change hands here — and news, and occasionally negotiation — both among the forest peoples themselves and with travelers pushing inland from Keshigo. Meeting is not a permanent settlement; it has no fixed leadership and no infrastructure beyond what the ongoing practice of trading has produced. It is reliably occupied during the trading seasons, quieter at other times. Caravans from Keshigo travel to Meeting regularly, providing the party with an alternative means of navigating the forest interior if they do not have a local guide.

### The Parna Village

A lightly settled village in the interior, predominantly human and halfling. Its economy runs on barter — money is of limited practical use here, and travelers who arrive with coin will find it buys less than expected. The village is modest and self-sufficient, neither welcoming nor unwelcoming of strangers, and draws on old relationships with the Witch whose cottage sits above the valley and the spirit world she tends. The Parna Village is the last settlement of any kind on the route to the Old Man's shrine, and travelers who have not provisioned here will find nothing between it and the mountain.

A small dwelling on a high hilltop above the Parna Valley, home to the Witch of the Silver Stream. The cottage is eclectic to the extreme — arcane paraphernalia, mismatched furniture, objects that suggest a wide range of interests and an unconventional collection of correspondents — but held in very good order: not chaotic, but purposeful. A working garden and yard occupy the grounds; the Witch's apprentices are often there when she has tasks for them. The cottage functions as the party's base of operations during their time in the Parna Valley, and the welcome here is genuine — within the Witch's terms. She will host the party as long as they need, honestly answer questions, and feed them well, but she will put them to work in the meantime.

### The Silver Stream Shrine

A three-hour hike from Hellie's cottage, up a narrow track to the highest hilltop in the region. The **Silver Stream** emerges here from a break in the rock face — a small waterfall that takes on the appearance of flowing silver in noon sunlight — and falls into a clear pool below. Behind the waterfall is a small shrine: maintained, its surfaces worn smooth by long use. This is where Hellie teaches the Summoning ritual and where the party performs their first contact with a nature spirit. The approach follows the track up through low-lying forest until the sound of water becomes audible; from that point, the shrine is close.

### Droma's Mountain Shrine

A three-day journey from Hellie's cottage, past the Parna Village and through the mountain foothills that rise on the other side of the valley. The Old Man of the Mountain's shrine occupies the summit of a particular peak at the edge of Droma's territory — no grand construction, but clearly maintained and intentionally chosen. It is here that the party performs the Summoning ritual for the first time without Hellie's guidance, calling upon an Old God who has been observing them throughout their time in his lands. The approach is difficult by the upper section, the terrain increasingly harsh as the foothills give way to the mountain proper. A powerful undead creature has taken up residence on the far side of the summit; the Old Man considers its presence an offense and will eventually require the party to address it.

### Tikatowa

A city on the western coast of the Sacred Lands, the Unity's eastern administrative and commercial hub, and the party's primary operating base during Act IV. Tikatowa began as a native port town and has never stopped being one; the Unity's presence over the past sixty years has been layered on top of something that predates it by generations, and the seams show throughout — in the architecture, in the social divisions of its districts, and in the names its residents use for each other. Full details appear in the Act IV chapters.

## The Wildwood

The great temperate rainforest of the northwestern Sacred Lands stretches from the northern coast inland for a distance that travelers who have not attempted to cross it tend to underestimate. It is an ancient forest — not merely old trees, but a landscape that carries its own history, its own internal geography, and its own governing presence. The Wildwood is Carnuth's domain. His power is strongest here. The resistance to Unity colonization is organized and fought from within these trees.

The forest does not announce itself by degrees. Travelers approaching from the western coast encounter the treeline and, within a few dozen feet, find themselves inside it — the canopy closing overhead, the sounds of the open ground replaced by the sounds of a very different world. The Wildwood is navigable by those who know it and disorienting to those who do not; Carnuth's attention to what moves through his domain means that nothing passes through unnoticed. The party enters the Wildwood in Act IV, in circumstances shaped by what they accomplished during their adventure in the Unity.

Among the peoples of the region, the wild elf king **Kalameris** has emerged as the most significant consolidating political force. He has gathered a coalition among the native peoples most affected by colonization and commands genuine authority among them — authority rooted in legitimate grievance, sustained action, and real political skill. His position on the Unity presence is not open to negotiation: he wants it eliminated. Not reduced, not accommodated, not managed — gone. He does not regard partial solutions favorably and has made this clear to anyone who has tested it. Kalameris is not a fanatic; he is a leader with clear eyes about what has been done to his people and reasonable conclusions about what must follow. The party will need to engage with this position honestly rather than treating it as an obstacle to work around.

## Unity Colonial Presence (DM Only)

The Unity has maintained a colonial presence in the western edge of the Sacred Lands for approximately sixty years, and what began as trade agreements and small settlements has grown into a significant administrative and military operation centered on Tikatowa. The colonial territory runs along the western coast from the northern shore south to the latitude of Tikatowa; the push inland has been more gradual, and its leading edge is where the most active conflict with Carnuth's resistance is currently taking place.

The violence is escalating. The colonial force is growing — additional troops and administrators rotate in from the Unity on a regular schedule — and the communities displaced by its advance have been moving south and west for years. By the time the party is in Keshigo during Act I's return journey, the refugee movement is visible and its cause is legible to anyone who asks. Tinsel, the gnomish woman the party may encounter there, was displaced from the northern Sacred Lands by colonial violence; her account is the party's first direct witness testimony of what the colonial advance looks like at the level of individual lives.

Tikatowa itself is a complex environment — not a uniform colonial city but a layered one. Its native population has its own name for themselves: *shigori*, from the local hobgoblin dialect, meaning roughly "us" or "people," used for anyone of the Sacred Lands regardless of race. The Unity and those fully aligned with it are called *megori* — "outsiders," or more precisely "not-people." Both terms are in common use and are not considered rude in most company, though Unity officials dislike them. The DM should understand Tikatowa as a place where the politics of colonization are visible in the everyday.

Carnuth's response to the colonial advance has been, for an Old God, unusually direct. He has adopted mortal-accessible forms specifically to make the resistance legible to the peoples he is leading — visible, communicable, capable of issuing instruction and demonstrating commitment. An Old God fighting a colonial advance at the level of tactics and morale is a being who has decided that the normal means by which Old Gods relate to the world are insufficient for the urgency of the moment. The DM should not treat Carnuth's presence in the resistance as incidental. It is the thing that makes the resistance coherent — and the thing the Unity's Cabal intends to eliminate.

# THE UNITY

The Unity occupies a medium-sized continent on the western edge of the known world, its climate temperate throughout — cool and wet in the northeast, warm and dry in the southwest, with few of the extremes that define the Sacred Lands. The continent is densely and methodically settled. Its roads, waterways, and now its rail lines connect every province to the center; its cities are maintained with a consistency that reflects not merely wealth but institutional will. For a traveler from the Archipelago or the Sacred Lands, arriving in the Unity is arriving in a place that has been tending to itself for centuries — and that knows it.

The Unity as it exists today is the product of approximately six hundred years of history moving in a single direction. A thousand years of war between the peoples of the western continent preceded the emergence of Sister Neema — a genuine prophet who received real divinatory gifts and founded the movement called the **One True Way** on two tenets: that the future is determined, and that mortal ingenuity rather than divine power would realize it. Neema's predictions never failed during her lifetime; the exhaustion of centuries of war made the promise of its end irresistible. After her death, the movement's leaders lost access to her gifts and continued by arranging for their prophecies to be fulfilled by any means necessary — a fraud sustained across generations. The conversion campaign eventually became the **Last War**: a military conquest that unified the western continent under the One True Way, launched an anti-magic and anti-divine pogrom, and declared the great work finished. The subsequent attempt to reorganize society produced generations of internal strife. Approximately three hundred years ago, senior True Believers who had been formed specifically to combat the supernatural uncovered evidence of the fraud; their leader, a half-orc named **Maakan**, spent a decade carefully spreading it. The civil war that followed — the **Great Schism** — lasted a generation. Maakan's faction, calling itself the Unity, preserved Neema's genuine teachings while rejecting the fraud. They installed a system of direct democracy with firm checks and balances and have advanced science and technology while actively suppressing the supernatural ever since.

The Unity has genuinely improved the lives of its people. Its social infrastructure is real; its civic warmth is not manufactured; its commitment to the material welfare of every citizen represents centuries of sustained effort. The standard of living across most of the continent is higher than anywhere else in the world, and the people who live in it largely know this and are grateful. But the land is now failing. Crops yield less each generation. Resources are harder to find where they were once plentiful. Water runs thin in places it never did before. The Unity has attributed this to resource exhaustion — a problem requiring external solutions — which is why its colonial project in the Sacred Lands is understood internally not as conquest but as necessity. What the Unity has not understood, and has no institutional framework to understand, is that its suppression of magic and divine worship is not merely a political position, it is a severance. The relationship between mortal belief and the spirit world is structural, not cultural, and what the Unity severed three centuries ago has been slowly killing the land since.

## Technology

The Unity is considerably more technologically advanced than any other region in the world — roughly analogous to the late Victorian era. Cartridge rifles and single-shot pistols are in common use; the constabulary is generally unarmed, but military and specialist units carry rifles. Gas lighting and heating are publicly available in all cities and most towns. Basic plumbing is standard in urban settings. Steam engines power both the steamship fleet and commercial locomotives — the latter a very recent invention, its first rail lines a source of enormous national pride. Dirigibles are the most beloved form of long-distance travel, and the Unity's aerodromes are among its finest civic architecture; the sight of airships at anchor over a city is as ordinary to Unity citizens as birds in the sky.

The Unity's technological achievement is genuine and should feel like one. It emerged from a civilization that channeled into material ingenuity the energy that other civilizations put into spiritual practice, and the results are remarkable. The DM should present the Unity's technology as genuinely impressive — the party's first sight of a cityscape lit by gas or an aerodrome full of aircraft should register as wonder alongside whatever else they feel.

## Culture and Society

The Unity's culture is the result of three centuries of a genuinely communal civilization managing, very successfully, to eliminate nearly all variation from the definition of what a good citizen is. Most of what this has produced is ordinary and benign. Some of what it has produced is not.

**Language and greeting.** The Unity's communal values are embedded in everyday speech. Standard greetings are *"Good day, citizen!"* and *"Greetings, brother/sister!"*; farewells are *"Unity endures!"* and *"Kind day to you, citizen!"*; common expressions of surprise or strong feeling are *"Unity preserve us!"* and *"By the Unity!"* These phrases are not performances — they feel genuine to the people who use them, because they are.

**Communal life.** Citizens go out of their way to help those in obvious distress. Neighbors visit each other unbidden; communal eating, sleeping, and daily activities are standard practice; there is little of the modesty or privacy instinct common in the Archipelago. Everyone is expected to contribute: useful roles are found for children, the elderly, the disabled. No one goes hungry, unclothed, or unhoused if it can be prevented — and it almost always can be. The public goods available to every citizen may not be of superior quality, but their universality is a real achievement.

**Work culture.** Shirking is not tolerated. The day is divided into strict working, resting, and social hours, with mandatory breaks during working hours and three days off per week — a weekend and a mid-week break. The rhythm is consistent across the continent; everyone is on the same schedule.

**Nosiness.** Citizens are openly curious and rarely hesitant to ask personal questions. Everyone is expected to have opinions on political matters at every level of governance. Expressing an uninformed political opinion draws attention. The recommended approach for new Wayfinders on their first missions: engage, stay neutral. *"I haven't made up my mind yet"* is always an acceptable response — Unity citizens hold it as evidence of civic thoughtfulness rather than evasion.

**Dress and economics.** Clothing is utilitarian in almost all contexts; ostentatious display of wealth is deeply frowned upon, as is excess in personal living. Citizens with money above their needs are expected to direct it toward public causes. Taxes are paid in labor, goods, or services at the citizen's choice; a citizen who works part of the year on a public enterprise continues to receive their private employer's salary, which counts toward the employer's own tax contribution. The line between public and private enterprise is frequently blurred.

**Arts.** Citizens love public art; music, comedic theatre, and political satire are the most popular forms. Poking fun at politicians and at the Unity itself is considered good form. But everyone instinctively knows where the line is, even if no one can precisely define it. A written law can be challenged in court. The instinctive line cannot be challenged, appealed, or even fully identified — it can only be violated.

**The absent future.** The Unity's founding ideology declared that the great work is finished — "universal peace and an end to history." That declaration did not die with the One True Way. It survived into the Unity's culture, quietly. Citizens have everything their ancestors fought for: they are cared for, housed, fed, entertained. What this does to hope is the hardest thing to explain to someone who has not lived inside it. Hope requires an open future — the genuine possibility that things could be different, better, changed. The Unity's culture forecloses this without forbidding it. No one tells citizens they cannot want something new. They simply stop.

**Education.** A publicly funded school system. As a holdover from True Way practice, children typically live at school during the work week, returning home on weekends and the mid-week break. The school system has long served as a vehicle for political and social indoctrination; three centuries in, this indoctrination is so thoroughly absorbed that citizens believe they are thinking freely — because they are, but within a framework given to them before they had any means to understand it.

**Law enforcement.** The constabulary is omnipresent, respected, and generally unarmed. Officers are friendly, helpful, and typically nosier than the average citizen. Those who seem suspicious are observed; if suspicion reaches a threshold, the matter is referred to specialists. Citizens who consistently refuse to live by Unity norms may be registered as **social deviants**, triggering a period of formal community realignment — monitored communal living, regular social evaluation, and close supervision, framed as support and care rather than punishment. Most citizens accept this framing, including those undergoing it.

Citizens with an inborn ability to practice magic are required to wear a **magic collar**: a specialized item that monitors magical use and immediately alerts authorities if the wearer casts a spell, transmitting their location at the time. Wearing one carries no social stigma; collar-wearers are regarded as model citizens. The party should understand that any magic use in the Unity is potentially traceable.

**Views on the outside world.** The Sacred Lands are understood as a land of opportunity — natural resources and a population in need of the Unity's enlightenment. The Archipelago is, officially, a myth; citizens who speak of it as real are dismissed. Unofficially, a few government ministries believe it might exist and view it as a useful pressure valve that removes potential troublemakers without requiring state action.

### The True Character of Unity Society (DM Only)

The Unity does not feel oppressive on the street. It feels pleasant, organized, and warm. The wrongness is the absence of things: genuine private interiority, the possibility of a different future, transcendence, children becoming themselves rather than citizens. The party may spend significant time in the Unity uncertain whether it is actually bad or simply different. The answer is that it is both — and the people who understand this best are the ones who cannot leave.

The school system is the sharpest edge. Children living at school during the work week has been normalized across three centuries into an unremarkable organizational fact. What it has produced is parents who relate to their children as weekend guests, children who feel more loyal to their school cohort than to their families, and a population shaped entirely by the state before it had any chance to be shaped by anything else. Most Unity citizens do not experience this as a loss. That is the point.

The communal warmth is real. Neighbors do help each other. People do care. But the same social infrastructure that produces genuine care produces total mutual accountability. Being different in any way that does not map to Unity norms is immediately visible and immediately uncomfortable. People learn, at a cellular level, to flatten their inner lives in public — not because guards are watching, but because everyone is.

The Unity does not look like despair — it looks like a civilization that achieved something. But Lon's domain is not suffering. It is the certainty that things cannot improve. Citizens who have been given every material thing they need and no permission to want something else — who have been told, at the level of cultural inheritance, that the great work is finished — may be feeding Lon without knowing it. If the party ever reaches Lon or his domain, this should be among the most devastating things they discover: not that the Unity's citizens are miserable, but that they have quietly stopped hoping.

## Key Locations

### Caskara Bay and Mahakam Island

A large sheltered bay on the Unity's eastern coast, accessible from the Middle Sea through a wide channel. The port city of Daliketh sits at the head of the bay; Mahakam Island, a large island in the channel, controls its mouth. This is where the party enters the Unity. 

### Daliketh

The primary port of Caskara Bay and the party's first encounter with a Unity city of any size. An industrialized city with a characteristic coal-and-gas smell that hits before the dock does; the skyline is a mix of smokestacks, gas-lit towers, and the full infrastructure of a late Victorian port economy, set against conspicuously clean streets. Notable districts include the port district, the city center, and **Northgate** — an outlying precinct where the **Garden House** is located, a boarding house and restaurant run by Wayfinder contact Neeta Whitecliff that serves as the network's Daliketh safe house. From Daliketh, the party travels to Balnor either by airship or, in the worst outcome, by sea north to Krasno Bay and then overland.

### Balnor

The capital of the Unity, deep in the continental interior. Balnor is everything the Unity is, written large. Great monuments and public artworks celebrate the people of the Unity and their historical struggle against the supernatural. The streets are ramrod straight, uniformly clean, and lined with tranquil public parks alongside the rowdy public squares in which Unity citizens enjoy each other's company with open civic pleasure. The army is considerably more visible here than anywhere else the party has been — military airships circle overhead, and army units in parade uniforms march the streets to the beat of raucous volunteer brass bands trailing in their wake. A grand plaza near the city's center is dominated by a hundred-foot stone figure of a laborer with one arm raised, breaking a chain; the chain's links, on close inspection, are carved with symbols of the old religions. The city's **great aerodrome** is its most beloved public institution: enormous, immaculate, and busy at all hours, its towers bearing dozens of dirigibles at any given time.

### Karale

A sizeable agricultural and manufacturing center on the route between Balnor and Jindark. Years of crop failures have driven chronic food shortages in one of the Unity's most important agricultural regions; material shortages compound the problem. The social safety net is holding — no one is without food or shelter — but much of the population has been left without purpose, and the unrest this breeds has not yet broken to the surface. The local government has seen constant council turnover. Social encounters in Karale should feel noticeably different from Balnor: the same Unity, in worse repair, performing normalcy over something that is starting to curdle. This is the Unity's internal contradictions at their most visible — a preview of where the whole continent is heading if nothing changes.

### Jindark

A small city in the northwestern province of **Jakor**, the party's final destination in the Unity. Built into hillsides descending from a high ridge to a harbor at sea level — roughly eight hundred feet of elevation — with most buildings constructed directly into the slopes, their lower levels on pillars or stilts. The harbor is enclosed by a massive artificial breakwater: a solid central mass with a shipping channel at either end, each overseen by a lighthouse. The breakwater top serves as a public promenade — wide enough for a market on one side and a walking path on the other, with views of harbor and open sea simultaneously. It is both Jindark's most distinctive civic feature and, unknown to its citizens, something else entirely.

Jindark's character is industrial and confident. Ores and minerals from the surrounding hills feed the city's forges; the train depot is strikingly busy for a city of this size. A large installation is visible under construction near the ridgeline — publicly described as an airship terminal. Social encounters here will feel distinct from Balnor and sharply differ from those in Karale: the Unity working exactly as advertised, citizens who believe in it and have material reason to. The oppressive undercurrent of the capital and the simmering unrest of Karale are both absent. This is the most dangerous version of the Unity to encounter: one that has given its people something to be genuinely proud of.

## Power Structure

### The Unity Council

The supreme governing body of the Unity, composed of representatives from the ten provinces. Every person on the western continent — regardless of race or gender — is a Unity citizen and participates in governance through a cascading system of councils: local councils of maximum one thousand citizens elect representatives to regional councils, then provincial councils, then the Unity Council itself. Terms of office are shortest at the local level — one year — and longest at the Unity Council level — ten years — with seats turning over on a rotating basis. Any member of any council may call for a plebiscite; if accepted by majority, the issue cascades down to the next level, potentially all the way to a local vote. The mechanism is complex and deeply beloved. Citizens are strongly attached to the principle that every voice counts, and they are not wrong: the principle is real, and it works, within the range of questions the Unity's political culture permits to be asked.

### The Ministries

The Unity's government operates through sixteen ministries that handle the practical work of governance. Most are unremarkable from the party's perspective. The most relevant to the campaign is the **Ministry of Justice**, which houses both the Advocates and — unknown to the broader ministry — the Cabal.

## Unity Institutions

### The Advocates

A special enforcement unit within the Ministry of Justice, distinct from the constabulary. The Advocates handle the suppression of rebellion, unauthorized magic practice, organized dissent, and anything that falls between the ordinary constabulary's authority and the army's. They have broader authority than the constabulary — they can compel cooperation and are not limited by local jurisdiction — and they are well-resourced, patient, and thorough. Ordinary Unity citizens have little direct contact with them and do not particularly fear them; what the Advocates do exists at the edges of ordinary civic life, after all. The Daliketh tower is a regional headquarters with cells below. The party's most significant encounter with the Advocates in Daliketh comes through the extraction mission; further contact may occur in Balnor depending on the party's behavior.

### The Cabal (DM Only)

The Cabal is not a ministry function. It is a shadow within the Ministry of Justice: a small, self-selected group of senior officials who concluded that the Unity's survival requires actions the Unity's laws and ethics do not permit, and who therefore take those actions without the Unity's knowledge or sanction. They have been operating in this mode for approximately thirty years.

Their philosophy is explicitly pragmatic over moral. They believe in the supremacy of the Unity above all else and understand clearly that what they do is, by the Unity's own standards, wrong. They do not consider this a problem. In their view, the Unity's idealism is a luxury afforded by the unpleasant work they perform in secret. The Unity can remain good precisely because they are willing to be something other than good on its behalf. They do not seek recognition. Discovery would destroy the thing they protect.

Their proof of concept — the capture of Huma the Storm Whale, using a device of their own construction — is the most significant covert achievement in Unity history. Huma's captive energy now powers the northern coastal region of the continent, producing its most productive land at a time when resources elsewhere are failing. The broader Unity government has no explanation for this anomaly. The Cabal intends to keep it that way. Their plan is to replicate the program: locating and securing the remaining Old Gods of the west, then extending the operation into the Sacred Lands. They do not frame this as conquest of the spirit world. They frame it as resource management — the Unity is dying, and this is how it survives.

The Cabal's regional leader in Jindark — one of the organization's original founders and a member of its inner circle — is **Circe Lovelace**: a short, serious, dark-skinned human woman with close-cropped hair. She presents as an Oath of Devotion Paladin whose oath is the Unity itself. She does not believe she has done anything that was not necessary. She cares genuinely for her subordinates. She has a standing escape protocol and will not be killed in the Citadel encounter — she reappears in Act IV. The Cabal's broader structure and membership beyond Lovelace are not fully mapped, even by most of its own members.

The party cannot simply expose the Cabal and expect the Unity's institutions to handle the rest. The Cabal has prepared for exposure. The argument against them is not primarily legal. It is philosophical: they must be shown that what they are doing is destroying the very thing they believe they are protecting.

## The Northern Coast (DM Only)

The province of Jakor — the northern coastal region where Jindark is located — is anomalously productive. Crops thrive. Resources flow. The standard of living in Jakor significantly outpaces the rest of the Unity, and citizens who notice this chalk it up to geography, traditional industry, or the character of its people. The truth is Huma.

The Cabal's containment system, built into the submerged foundations of the Jindark breakwater, channels Huma's captive energy through pressure differential turbines that convert it to electrical current — a phenomenon unknown outside the Cabal, and the mechanism behind Jakor's productivity. The current feeds the city's infrastructure; secondary effects sustain the surrounding region in ways the Cabal monitors but has never fully explained. The northern coast is the proof of concept for their program. The broader Unity government benefits from Jakor's output without understanding it and has no explanation for the anomaly.

When Huma is freed, the anomalous productivity ends. Whether the region declines gradually or destabilizes sharply depends on how his containment was breached and the degree of structural damage to the breakwater. This is not a footnote. It is a consequence the party will need to reckon with before they act, and the Cabal will use it as justification for everything they have done.

# PART IV: THE SPIRIT WORLD

Encarnia has a rich and complex spiritual ecology. Spirits are not gods in the conventional sense — they do not exist in a separate divine realm but are presences of varying age, power, and nature that exist alongside the physical world. Understanding what kind of spirit a given being is matters practically: their origins determine their behavior, their vulnerabilities, and the terms on which they can be reached.

## The Nature of Spirits

There are three distinct categories of spirit.

**Belief-spirits** are created and sustained by mortal belief. They are the spiritual embodiment of ideas, ideals, emotions, and concepts that mortals invest with meaning. They are real — genuinely powerful and capable of independent action — but they have no primordial connection to the world. They are passengers, not protagonists. Their strength waxes and wanes with the intensity and breadth of mortal belief, which means their health is inseparable from the health of the civilization that generates them.

**Old Gods** are primordial. They predate mortal civilization and are bound to the physical world itself. Their power does not depend on worship — they cannot be created or destroyed by mortal belief. Their health is tied to the land, sea, and sky they inhabit: when they suffer, the world suffers, and when the world is damaged, they are diminished. They have always been here. They will outlast everything the party encounters, if they are not destroyed first.

**Nature spirits** are a third category, distinct from both. They are the animating presences of specific physical places and features — a stream, a valley's farmsteads, a grove, a mountain pass. They are not created by mortal belief and predate organized worship, but they are far smaller in scope and power than the Old Gods. They rarely range far from their place, and when they take mortal forms, traces of their true nature persist. Nature spirits are most active in the Sacred Lands, where the spiritual ecology is healthiest. They exist in the Perfect Isles as well. In the Unity, most have been reduced to insubstantial remnants by centuries of development and suppression.

## How Spirits Work

**Names.** Most belief-spirits have names distinct from the ideal that empowers them — the spirit of Air Travel is named Ala, the spirit of Rebellion is named Revna, the spirit of War is named Maritas. A spirit's true name is not always the name mortals use for it.

**Forms and names change.** As mortal understanding of a concept evolves, so does the spirit embodying it. A spirit of justice defined by one civilization's laws may become barely recognizable to another civilization's prayers for the same ideal. These changes are genuine, reflecting genuine shifts in mortal understanding rather than disguise.

**Spirits can split.** When a belief expands to encompass distinct sub-beliefs, a new spirit can emerge from the originating spirit. The new spirit is a distinct being, but the originating spirit retains a paramount relationship to its offspring. The belief-spirit of Progress generated the belief-spirit of Air Travel in this way.

**Relationships.** Spirits have relationships with one another as complex as the connections between the beliefs themselves — siblings, rivals, lovers, enemies, dependents. These relationships are not metaphors. They are functional alliances, enmities, and entanglements with direct effects on how spirits behave and what they are willing to do.

**Indirect worship.** The Unity outlawed divine worship but did not eliminate mortal belief. The ideals the Unity is built on are held by its citizens with something approaching religious fervor. This has empowered a cluster of belief-spirits within the Unity's spiritual landscape, even without formal prayer.

## The Crossing

The Crossing is the bodily transit of living mortals into the spirit world, accomplished through the Communing ritual. Mortals who have performed the Crossing do not project their consciousness into the spirit realm — they are physically present in it, with all attendant risks. The passage into a spirit's domain varies by spirit and destination; each approach is distinct. The offerings required to complete the Crossing also vary; their nature is specific to each spirit contacted. Full mechanics for the Crossing, including ritual requirements and the specifics of each passage, are described in Running the Adventure.

## Belief-Spirits

### Hope
*Belief domain: Hope*

One of the more stable and coherent belief-spirits in the world, possibly because hope is a concept that has not changed dramatically in meaning across civilizations. She takes the form of a young girl seated beside a fountain. The fountain's fullness is a direct measure of the state of hope in the world — in the present period, it runs low. Her domain in the spirit world is an abandoned city square at twilight, the geometry of its buildings subtly wrong, the fountain reduced to a trickle. As hope strengthens in the physical world, the city fills and the twilight progresses toward dawn.

**Relationships:**
- **Lon (Despair):** Sibling, best friend, and rival. The two are inextricably linked — neither can truly exist without the other. Their relationship is one of deep intimacy and constant tension.
- **Revna (Rebellion):** A complex love-hate relationship. They are each other's most frustrating mirror: rebellion without hope is nihilism; hope without rebellion is passivity.

---

### Lon
*Belief domain: Despair*

Sibling and rival to Hope. Where Hope is sustained by the belief that things can improve, Lon is fed by the certainty that they cannot. As the world has worsened — the land dying in the west, colonial violence escalating in the east — Lon has grown considerably stronger. He is deeply saddened by this and secretly hopes for Hope to gain the upper hand again. 

**Relationships:**
- **Hope:** See above.

**Acceptable offering:** Unkown - the party won't approach Lon directly during the adventure, though if they decide to do so they will have to discover something appropriate  
---

### Revna
*Belief domain: Rebellion*

A younger spirit than Hope and Lon, born more distinct as the Unity's suppression became more complete — rebellion as a concept requires something to rebel against, and Revna grew more defined as the conditions for her existence became more concentrated. She is volatile, passionate, and not easy to work with. Her power is closely tied to the Unity's eastern colonies.

**Relationships:**
- **Hope:** Complex love-hate. They are each other's most frustrating mirror.
- **Maritas (War):** Her uncle — the brother of her father. A relationship she does not discuss unless pressed.

**Acceptable offering:** Blood — one party member must cut their palm and bleed willingly into Revna's stone bowl during a crossing. The act must be voluntary; a token gift or an unwilling offering will be returned.

---

### Maritas
*Belief domain: War*

Ancient beyond any other belief-spirit the party is likely to encounter — his origins predate the Unity by a vast span. He takes the form of a great bear of a man wearing fine chainmail, with a close-cropped dark beard, long hair, and a face permanently flushed — a complexion, not exertion. He is direct, occasionally blackly funny, and entirely without pity — for himself, or orhters.

**Current status (DM Only):** The Congress weakened Maritas by building a civilization nearly free of war, then tricked him into captivity in the theatre beneath their seat of power. He is currently held there. He remains fully formed and entirely himself — captivity has not diminished him, and he knows it.

**Relationships:**
- **Revna (Rebellion):** His niece — the daughter of his brother. He claims he cannot recall his brother's name. Perhaps he ate him. He finds this funny.
- **The Congress:** Deep and mutual enmity. The Congress considers him dangerous; he considers them cowards who built a world of enforced quiet and called it peace.
- **Novus (Progress):** An informal alignment — war and progress have always advanced together, and their interests frequently coincide.

**Acceptable offering:** A worthy fight — one party member must volunteer to face a challenger in Maritas's arena and conduct the bout with genuine effort. The outcome is irrelevant. An uninspired performance earns no passage.

---

### Ala
*Belief domain: Air Travel*

A belief-spirit of limited scope — not the broad abstract that empowers the great spirits, but the specific, focused faith that accrues wherever mortals have trusted their lives to the sky. He emerged from the belief-complex of Novus (a member of the Unity Congress), whom he considers his big brother. Ala takes the form of a male teenager in aeronaut gear of a distinctly antique cut — different in style from anything the modern Unity produces. He is cheerful, genuinely curious, and entirely without guile. He exists wherever air travel is practiced, most powerfully at Balnor, home of the largest aerodrome in the world. 

**Relationships:**
- **Novus (Progress):** His point of origin. Ala split from Novus as air travel became a distinct belief-category in its own right. Their relationship remains close.

---

### Harmony
*Belief domain: Cooperation*

A belief-spirit who exists wherever people of genuinely different beliefs, backgrounds, and ways of life choose to live and work together. She is not a spirit of agreement or conformity — her domain requires actual difference and the active choice to engage across it rather than withdraw or destroy. She is middle-aged in appearance, with long dark hair, and presents without particular distinction or ceremony. She is kind but can be aloof; her attention drifts toward the life around her, and she responds best to patient, respectful engagement rather than transactional urgency.

**Where she exists:**
- **The Perfect Isles:** The interplay between thirty-three distinct factions provides an unusually concentrated source of power. She is stronger here than almost anywhere else in the world.
- **The Sacred Lands:** The fragile but real coexistence between the Unity's colonial settlers and the native peoples of the east sustains her there.
- **The Unity:** She does not exist within the Unity. The Unity is not a collection of factions — there is nothing there for her.

**Domain (spirit world):** Harmony's courtyard: an open-air space five stories tall, lined with doorways marked with factional symbols, filled with a garden of statues, plants, and works of art, peopled with individuals and groups engaged in discussion, collaboration, and shared company.

**Relationships:**
- **Hope:** Aware of each other. Hope characterizes Harmony as very private, responsive only to a specific non-material gift.
- **Old Gods:** Finds them territorial as a rule, but respects their necessity. Carnuth's decision to go to war concerns her deeply.

**Acceptable offering:** A unified voice — in order to reach her in Act IV, all assembled faction leaders at the Council Site must authorize the party to speak on their collective behalf. This is both the offering and the key to opening the way.

### The Stranger
*Belief domain: Death*

The oldest belief-spirit in existence, and the most coherent. Death as a concept does not evolve or splinter — it has meant what it means since the first mortal died. The Stranger has been doing the same job since then, and he is more himself than any other belief-spirit will ever be. The other belief-spirits fear him. The Old Gods are wary of him in the particular way one is wary of something inevitable.

His job is shepherding mortal souls to what comes after. He describes himself as "just a humble shepherd." He means it, more or less. Each soul he collects gives him a brief surge of spiritual power that fades over days. He has been wondering whether a great many souls at once might give him something that lasts. He is willing to pursue this idea, and he has been working toward it through the Avengers.

**Form:** A man in a simple but immaculate black outfit and a broad-brimmed black hat. He speaks in a slow, unhurried drawl and is often funny — with the ease of someone who finds existence genuinely interesting. When someone prevents him from doing his job, all of that stops. He becomes very still, speaks in a flat, unblinking monotone, and does not raise his voice.

**Domain:** A dry, wooded crossroads near a stone well. He is never in a hurry.

**Relationships:**
- **Hope:** She is owed a favor by the Stranger. It becomes relevant if a party member dies and the surviving party members find a way to contact Hope before the Stranger collects their companion.
- **The Avengers:** His current contractors, though they do not know what he is. His offer to their leadership was simple: one service per year and a day in exchange for patron-level access to his power, no questions asked. His current request is the death of the Circle.

His full story role, the optional death encounter, and the proposition are in **The Stranger's Thread** in Running the Adventure.

## The Unity Spirit Committee

The Unity's outlawing of magic and divine worship did not eliminate the spiritual energy generated by its citizens' deeply held beliefs. The ideals the Unity is built on — law, the common good, public order, reason, knowledge, and unity itself — are held with something approaching religious fervor. This has empowered a cluster of belief-spirits tied to these ideals. None of them are directly worshiped as gods once were in the west. Most have only a fuzzy understanding of their own nature and place in reality. They are nonetheless genuine beings with genuine opinions and a surprising influence over the affairs of the Unity. They are sometimes referred to collectively as the Congress, though they do not govern one another and share no formal structure.

| Spirit | Domain | Form | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lexan** | Law | Frail old man leaning on a staff; powerful voice | The eldest member — evolved from pre-Unity western deities of Law; more self-aware and coherent than most; old antagonist of Novus |
| **Placida** | Common Good | Smiling, matronly woman; glowing blonde hair | The most self-aware member; exists among the Archipelago peoples as well, giving her unusual coherence; allied with Revna |
| **Concordia** | Public Order | Identical form to Placida; dark hair | Placida's near-twin; focused on the social fabric rather than formal rules; their relationship is one of quiet tension |

## The Old Gods

The Old Gods are primordial. They are not organized. They communicate freely — most often with those whose regions overlap — and are as often at odds with one another as not. Their relationships shift constantly.

Each Old God is represented in the physical world by a chosen Witch — a mortal practitioner with whom the Old God has formed a direct bond. Witch covens exist within each Old God's region; these covens tend the relationship between the Old God and the mortal communities of the area. A greater coven exists to which each regional coven sends a single representative; it meets rarely.

**The Conclave.** The Old Gods are engaged in a deliberation without recent precedent. Fourteen named Old Gods are weighing the fate of mortalkind in response to the rapid deterioration of the physical world — caused by the Unity's suppression of magic and the severance of the mortal-spirit relationship. The deliberation's verdict, in the language of the Old Gods, is not advisory. The following table reflects each Old God's current alignment:

| Old God | Domain | Conclave Stance | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Droma** | The Great Weald | Allied | Firmly convinced that mortals and Old Gods can coexist |
| **Carnuth** | The Wildwood | Allied | *If killed before the Conclave, his voice is lost* |
| **Huma** | The Roaring Shores | Allied | Willing to advocate for mortals; conditions attached |
| **Kakita** | The Long Coast | Allied | Narrowly — she will speak for the party solely because of Huma's rescue |
| **Genna** | The High Forest | Allied | Most aligned with mortals of any Old God; cannot understand why others don't cultivate similar relationships |
| **Green Man** | The Great Wilds | Neutral | Tolerates mortals; disinterested in their broader fate |
| **Skymare** | The Shining Plain | Neutral | Extremely aloof; her true position unknown even to other Old Gods |
| **Princessa** | The Bight | Neutral | Allied with Carnuth; *if Carnuth is killed, she crosses into opposition* |
| **Stone Sovereign** | The Dividing Range | Neutral | Speaks last; its final word follows the majority position |
| **Forvalaka** | The Dark Vale | Opposed | Hunts mortals; refuses all communication |
| **Feathered Lion** | The Expanse | Opposed | Wants the western population eliminated; predatory in approach |

### Droma — The Old Man of the Mountain
*Old God of the Great Weald*

**True name:** Droma. He communicates this to those who reach his shrine.

Within the Ringing Mountains, Droma is primarily a great ambulatory mountain — colossal, moving so slowly its motion cannot be detected without careful attention, capable of extraordinary destruction when it acts. Outside the mountains, he uses an avatar: a foul-mouthed old gnome in dirty clothes, an iron skull cap, and beautiful slippers made of precious gemstones. The gnome is where most mortals encounter him. His Witch is Hellie, who serves him directly. His domain encompasses the Parna Valley and the surrounding Sacred Lands, and he has been observing everything that happens in those lands since long before any mortal civilization took root there.

### Carnuth, the Stag of the New Moon
*Old God of the Wildwood*

Carnuth takes three forms tied to the moon's phase. During the new moon, at peak power, he is a gargantuan multi-legged stag of great magical and destructive force. During the full moon, at his ebb, he is a giant stag-headed battle sorcerer — physically reduced but magically formidable and capable of mortal-legible communication. By day he sleeps in forest meadows as a giant stag, harmless unless threatened. He follows natural thought patterns rather than mortal ones, focused on the Wildwood and the living world it represents. He is kind and generous when approached respectfully with an appropriate gift, and he has no capacity for tolerance toward those who despoil his lands.

**Relationships:**
- **Princessa (The Moonmoth):** Longtime rival and lover. Their alliance is one of the oldest between neighboring Old Gods.

### Huma — The Storm Whale
*Old God of the Roaring Shores*

**True name:** Huma.

A great antlered whale with amber eyes and electricity crackling along his fins and tail — he adjusts his size to context and is careful not to shock smaller creatures. His lands are the great northern fjords, forests, and stormy sea coast along the northern reaches of the western continent. The Unity could never develop that territory, and Huma survived longer there than other western Old Gods as a result. He was eventually captured in a weakened state.

**Current status (DM Only):** Huma is held captive by the Cabal's containment device, built into the submerged foundations of the Jindark breakwater. His energy is being channeled to power the northern coastal region of the Unity. His condition and the circumstances of his freeing are addressed in Act III chapter material.

**Note:** Huma is contacted via the Summoning ritual rather than the standard Communing ritual; he does not have a Communing passage.

### Kakita — The Thunderbird

**True name:** Kakita.

A titanic eagle with bright purple, red, and blue plumage and a screech like a thunderclap. ### Caspa — Three-Paw Wolf

### Baita — The Snow Queen
*Old God of the Pale*

**True name:** Baita.

A small, pale elven woman with electric blue eyes, wearing a gown of ice, borne about in an ice sleigh pulled by a team of polar bears. Completely dismissive of mortals, whom she considers beneath her notice — she gives attention only to those who bring significant gifts and flattery, which she finds amusing rather than convincing. ### Howler
*Old God of the Bleak Lands*

**True name:** Unknown.

Baita, Caspa, and the Feathered Lion describe him as a massive, deformed bear-like creature in a state of constant, agonized howling. Cyrencia did not encounter him directly. He shuns contact with all other Old Gods. His lands are depopulated — not of mortals alone, but of most animals and even plants. Cyrencia believes he may be a personification of nature's madness. ## Cyrencia
*The New God of the Isles*

Cyrencia occupies a category of her own. She is not a belief-spirit, not an Old God, and not a nature spirit in the conventional sense — she is all three at once. No existing category in mortal or spirit understanding fully describes what she is.

She was born when the founding practitioners of the Archipelago created the islands through magic. Their act was not merely arcane — it was an act of profound collective belief: belief that they could accomplish what had never been done, that their way of life could endure, that working together they could build something better. This belief did what the formation of any new land does: it called forth an Old God. But because this land was made through mortal belief rather than geological time, the Old God it produced was also, inextricably, a belief-spirit. She is old in power — as old as the Archipelago itself — and young in consciousness. She is the first being of her kind in the world.

Her name is Cyrencia. It was spoken once — at the moment of the Archipelago's creation — and never recorded. Her domain in the spirit world is the Archipelago as it existed before human arrival: the same islands, the same geography, stripped of all human construction, vibrating with two centuries of accumulated belief. She does not arrive as a form. She arrives as something the Archipelago does — a change in the weather, the behavior of light on the water, the sound the islands make. She then chooses a form, slowly, so she can be spoken with. She is enormous, and she is choosing to be smaller.

**Relationships:**
- **The Oracle:** The strongest belief-spirit connection available to Cyrencia before she was coherent. She used the Oracle as a conduit for years, producing visions the Oracle could not interpret. The Oracle is aware of this relationship after the fact.
- **The Old Man of the Mountain:** He sensed her presence and named her before the party arrived. Their relationship is that of elder and younger Old God — she has enormous respect for him, and he has recognized what she is.
- **Huma:** She is dimly aware of him across the sea. He feels muffled to her, as though something is blocking the connection between him and the lands he was once tied to.

### Beam
*Nature spirit of the Parna Valley farmsteads*

Beam's natural form is a scarecrow. When he takes a mortal form, the traces persist — straw clings to his clothing and he carries the slightly-out-of-step quality of a field left to its own devices.

### Kelpie
*Nature spirit of an unnamed island in the Middle Sea*

Kelpie takes the mortal form of a halfling-like mermaid and speaks in the third person. A grotto on the north shore contains a small shrine that serves as her place of power. She refers to regular visitors — now long absent — as "the other two legs of the sea": Tritons who once visited the island periodically and have since left the region without explanation.

*Belief-spirit of the Sahuagin tribe; the unnamed Middle Sea island*

A belief-spirit generated by a community of deformed Sahuagin that took up residence on Kelpie's island and profaned her shrine. He embodies whatever the tribe collectively believed in. A small-scale illustration of how even an isolated community generates a belief-spirit of its own. After the Sahuagin are destroyed, Alvolea rapidly declines in power; Kelpie reports having him firmly in hand.

## The Archipelago

### Maya (the Oracle)
*The Oracle; host of the belief-spirit of Belief; twelve years old*

Maya is twelve years old, slight, and carries herself with the measured composure of someone very carefully trained. She does not look like she is trying to appear older; she looks like she has been taught, very thoroughly, how to be present in a room that is larger than she is. Her role as the Oracle — host to the belief-spirit of Belief, the animating spirit of the Archipelago's spiritual tradition — requires her to channel pronouncements she does not always understand and communicate them to audiences that will sometimes not welcome what they hear. She does this with practiced precision and a steadiness that is not the steadiness of someone comfortable, but of someone who has learned how to hold still.

She is not without personality. Alone or in small groups her age shows: curiosity, occasional impatience, a dry sense of humor. She trusts the people who trained her and she trusts the process, even when the process produces things she did not expect. She is not yet quite sure whether she trusts herself.

**The DM should know:** Maya served as Cyrencia's conduit for years — receiving visions she could not interpret and conveying them faithfully. The full awareness of this relationship will come to her suddenly after the party wakes Cyrencia, and she will spend the rest of her life working through what this means. She may discuss it with the party if pressed.

### Vinna Maar

A half-elf woman of middle years, lean and composed, with the watchful stillness of someone accustomed to saying less than she knows. An elf of the Hidden Vale who travels posing as a simple traveler. ## The Sacred Lands

### Pinion
*Aasimar; Hidden Vale agent in Keshigo*

Pinion manages Hidden Vale affairs in the Keshigo region — which means, in practice, knowing what is happening in the western Sacred Lands, who is coming through, and who among them can be trusted. Their Aasimar nature is evident to those who know what they are looking at. They operate through a legitimate cover in Keshigo and are reachable through proper introductions; the party learns of them from Belladonna if their initial encounter with her goes well. Pinion can provide regional information and a direct introduction to Senna.

### Senna
*Half-elf ranger; Hellie's close friend and courier*

A half-elven ranger who carries letters from Hellie's correspondents across the region and uses the shared journey with the party to evaluate whether they represent a threat to Hellie or the Sacred Lands. She is self-possessed and observant in a way that reads as friendly but is professionally thorough. She greets Hellie very warmly if present. She does not travel beyond the Sacred Lands.

### Hellie (Witch of the Silver Stream)
*Human; Witch of the Silver Stream; Droma's Witch*

Hellie appears to be in her early forties, with slightly graying dark curly hair gathered in a tail thrown over her left shoulder. She comes out personally to greet visitors, sets them to work in the yard and helping with supper before she answers questions, and is generally kind in a businesslike way — a host who gives full attention and expects some in return. Her home is eclectic to an extreme but kept in very good order. Twin girls of approximately ten to twelve years, her apprentices, call her "auntie" and are always nearby; they have opposing heterochromia.

She serves Droma directly — he is the Old God whose domain encompasses the Parna Valley region and beyond — and was informed of the party's approach by local nature spirits approximately one day before their arrival. She will not volunteer this detail but will explain it plainly if asked. She can teach the Summoning ritual (the Witch's method for reaching spirits in their places of power) but not the Communing ritual. Witches do not enter the spirit realm unless invited; the Communing must be learned elsewhere, and she directs the party to the Old Man of the Mountain.

**The DM should know:** Hellie's foreknowledge, her connection to the local nature spirits, and her service to Droma are all genuine. She has been evaluating the party from the moment they arrived; her apparent openness is not incautious, because she is an experienced judge of people and sees no reason to be indirect once she has made her assessment.

---

### Tinsel
*Gnomish woman; Sacred Lands refugee*

A gnomish woman encountered on the party's return through Keshigo — displaced from the northern Sacred Lands by colonial violence. She is not broken by what happened to her; she is clear-eyed about it. She provides the party's first direct, personal account of the Unity's colonial presence and the active resistance being mounted under King Kalameris's leadership, and Carnuth's involvement. She speaks about specific people, specific places, and specific events she personally witnessed, which humanizes what is otherwise an abstract threat. She is useful as an information source and memorable as a person.

---

### Kalameris
*Wild elf; paramount leader of the eastern Sacred Lands peoples*

A wild elf who has consolidated power among the native peoples of the eastern Sacred Lands to a degree unprecedented in the region's history — not through diplomacy, but because the Unity's colonization made survival without coordination impossible. He is a legitimate ruler with genuine grievances and a clarity of purpose that does not leave much room for nuance: complete eradication of the Unity's colonial presence, by whatever means required. He is not interested in negotiated accommodation and does not pretend to be. His hostility to the Unity is not abstract ideology — it is the conclusion of someone who has watched what the Unity does and drawn the obvious inferences.

**The DM should know:** Kalameris is not a villain and is not wrong about the Unity. His obstacle role in the party's work comes from the narrowness of his position — he cannot be moved by arguments for moderate solutions, only by demonstration that alternatives he does not yet credit are possible. He has genuine popular support and dealing with him poorly has consequences beyond the immediate encounter.

### Takowa
*Male hobgoblin; Kalameris's chief lieutenant*

A hobgoblin of significant military competence who serves as Kalameris's primary gatekeeper — the person who screens visitors and determines which of them Kalameris should meet. He is not unreasonable if approached with honesty, and he respects demonstrated capability; a ritual duel to first blood is an accepted form of negotiation in this context. *Stat block: see Appendix.*

### Sima
*Tall, broad-shouldered half-elf; Carnuth's chief witch*

A half-elf man with a short grey beard and the physical presence of someone who has spent decades in physical country. He lives in a coastal village approximately fifty miles south of Tikatowa and serves as Carnuth's primary mortal representative — chief among the Old God's witches. He evaluates the party slowly and speaks carefully. He has tasks he will put to the party before he shares Carnuth's location, and at least one must be completed before he will trust them with it.

## The Unity

### Kaman
*Human; veteran Wayfinder; Mahakam Island network*

A human Wayfinder who has run the Mahakam Island smuggling operation for decades under legitimate shipyard cover. He handles every aspect of the party's transition to the mainland with organized calm, producing their documents the morning after their arrival. He does not waste words. He is unsentimental about operational losses — he has outlasted several cycles of compromised contacts — and prioritizes getting people home above everything else.

**The DM should know:** Kaman's network in Daliketh is genuinely at risk depending on how the party operates inside the Unity. He is aware of this going in, and he is not naive about what he is sending them into.

### Neeta Whitecliff
*Halfling; owner of the Garden House; Wayfinder contact in Daliketh*

Neeta runs a boarding house and restaurant in Daliketh's Northgate precinct. She has maintained her cover and her network for years through the combination of genuine hospitality and absolute discretion. She passes messages between the party and Pulara but does not know the full scope of the party's mission.

### Pulara
*Female Tiefling; Wayfinder contact in Daliketh; arbiter of party's Unity operations*

Pulara is the final authority on when the party can be trusted enough to operate inside the Unity. She is direct and demanding when she does contact them, and her standards are explicit — she will dress down poor performers without ceremony. She provides the party's airship tickets to Balnor and the sealed envelope containing their Balnor contact details.

### Malachai Ironshear
*Male dwarf; Wayfinder team leader; Daliketh*

A dwarf Wayfinder team leader staying at the Garden House while completing a separate recovery mission. He will characterize the party's conduct accurately to the Wayfinder network, in either direction.

### Renata
*Human; Wayfinder operative*

The uninjured member of Malachai's team and the guide for covert extraction work at the Advocates' Tower. She plans in advance and does not improvise unless she must; her value in any operation is preparation, not flexibility.

### Turno Ninefingers
*Halfling Rogue; Wayfinder specialist*

A halfling specialist who appears when needed and accomplishes what he is sent to accomplish without requiring explanation of how. He is quiet and professional; his presence ensures that things which need to happen quietly happen quietly.

### Inspector Esca
*Female human; Advocates inspector; Daliketh*

A professional Advocate inspector who interviews the party following a boarding house disturbance. She is not fishing for an excuse to arrest anyone; she is doing her job. *Stat block: see Appendix.*

### Kastel Swensalvadl
*Gnome; Daliketh underground smuggler*

A gnome who runs a local smuggling operation and will house the party at Kaman's request in exchange for manual labor. The arrangement is transactional, not collegial. He has no knowledge of the Archipelago, the Wayfinder network's scope, or the party's mission. He provides a bed, keeps his mouth shut, and expects his warehouse stacked by morning.

### Giacomo Hearthbane
*Male Halfling; deep-cover Wayfinder sleeper; Balnor*

A halfling who has lived in Balnor for over forty years as a senior manager at the Unity Bank — married, nine children, a recognized pillar of his community. He is all of these things. He is also a Wayfinder sleeper agent in semi-retirement, hosting the party because Pulara asked him to and because he trusts her judgment implicitly. He was not involved in the intelligence failure that shut down Balnor Wayfinder operations years ago and establishes this without defensiveness.

His terms are explicit: his wife Bedelia does not know about his clandestine work, his children arrive the second day after the party does, and both must be managed socially. If the party exposes him he will disavow them and turn them over to the authorities. He says this without malice, as a statement of fact. He means it.

**The DM should know:** Giacomo is not a coward — he has held this cover for forty years, which requires a different and more sustained kind of courage than field work. His willingness to act ends where his family's safety begins. The party should understand this before they test it.

### Gloof
*Imp; scribe; bound to the Scrivvner's Vial*

An imp bound to serve the holder of the Scrivvner's Vial as a scribe for one hundred years, of which ninety-nine remain when the party acquires him. If not convinced, he holds them for two days pending orders from above. He is not cruel; he is thorough. *Stat block: see Appendix.*

### Inspector Racewind Thrumblecastle
*Male Gnome; Advocates inspector; Balnor*

A gnome Advocates inspector with the bearing of someone who has heard every story. He interviews the party at their Balnor lodgings following the tiggering of the Arcane spying device. ### Vicona Praest
*Human woman; Unity Minister of Justice*

Circe Lovelace's nominal superior in the Ministry of Justice, though neither of them advertises the nature of that relationship. She arrives in the northern colonial zone for an official reception and carries intelligence that partially overlaps the Cabal's operational papers.

**The DM should know:** Praest is not a Cabal member. Whether this is willful or simply political is a question the party may eventually be positioned to press.

### Circe Lovelace (DM Only)
*Female Human; Cabal founder and regional leader; Oath of Devotion Paladin*

Circe Lovelace is a short, serious, dark-skinned human woman with close-cropped hair. She is an original founder of the Cabal and one of its inner circle members, classed as an Oath of Devotion Paladin — her devotion is to the Unity itself, elevated to the status of a sacred cause. She has done things the Unity would call monstrous. She knows this. She considers them necessary, and she has made peace with that many would find morally uncomfortable 

She is not a fanatic in the brittle sense. She is a true believer in the considered sense: she has thought through the implications of her position carefully and arrived at convictions rather than rationalizations. She cares deeply for her subordinates. She is calm in adversity because she planned for adversity. She has a standing escape protocol and will execute it regardless of how any encounter ends; she will not die in any single confrontation.

Her argument — that the Unity's survival requires actions the Unity's ethics do not permit, and that someone must therefore take those actions — is coherent and not obviously wrong. This is the source of her danger as an antagonist. She can make the party understand her, which is different from making them agree with her, and the distinction matters enormously.

**The DM should know:** Defeating Circe in any conventional sense does not end her or the Cabal. The argument against the Cabal is philosophical, not operational, and must be demonstrated rather than won by force. She will reappear in Act IV (and potentially Act V) and must eventually be reckoned with on those terms. *Stat block: see Appendix.*

### Finnean Barnatheses
*Gnome; original inventor of the airship; 228 years old; Balnor*

Ancient by gnomish standards, Finnean's office is crammed with a century of books, aeronautic drawings, and posters advertising flights that took place before most living people were born. He is 228 years old and carries it as a kind of deep settledness — not diminished, but finished, with the quality of someone who has already accomplished the thing he was put here to do and has been watching what the world made of it ever since. His initial response to the party's proposal is dismissal. He has been approached with interesting ideas for a century. Then he finds he cannot stop thinking about it.

His celebrity status within the Unity provides the party significant social cover during their time in Balnor. Once committed, he is total — he does not do things by half.

**The DM should know:** A mention of Ala — the belief-spirit of air travel, who considers Finnean a figure of personal significance and has referred to having "a lot of adventures ahead" with someone very like him — commits Finnean completely. He stays in the Isles after the crossing at Ala's suggestion, eventually establishing an airship-building collective in Heida and partnering with Beldwin Stonefinger if the party has cultivated that relationship. *Stat block: see Appendix.*

### Kore
*Half-elf druid; Wayfinder rescue; Mahakam Island*

A recent rescue awaiting passage to the Isles, housed in the Mahakam Island warehouse. Minor presence; represents what the Wayfinder networks exist to do. Has no knowledge of the party's mission. *Stat block: Druid (CR 2) — see Monster Manual.*

### Bedelia Hearthbane

Bedelia knows Giacomo periodically hosts old friends. She does not know about his clandestine work and has no reason to suspect it. She is warm, curious about guests in her home, and asks questions. # RUNNING THE ADVENTURE

Running the Adventure covers the mechanical and narrative infrastructure you need before running any chapter. Read this section before the chapter content. It describes how the adventure works at the structural level: the chapter and act divisions, milestone leveling, the ritual systems and their mechanics, the failure track in Act II, faction relationships, and how to manage the adventure's revelations and endings.

Everything in this section applies to the adventure as a whole. Scene-specific guidance belongs in the relevant chapter.

## Chapter and Act Structure

The Crossing is organized into five acts and twenty-three numbered chapters, with several optional interludes. Acts group chapters thematically and serve as the primary unit of dramatic pacing. Chapters are the working unit for preparation; each has a clear opening state and a clear conclusion.

*Optional interludes* are branching arcs that may or may not occur depending on party choices and NPC availability. They are marked with *(Optional)* in the structure below. See **Optional Content** for guidance on how to run them.

## Optional Content

Optional interludes are self-contained arcs that branch off the main chapter spine. They are triggered by prerequisites — usually a combination of party choices in earlier chapters and the availability of specific NPCs. Interludes do not replace chapters; a chapter ends when its stated conclusion is met, regardless of whether an adjacent interlude has been run.

**Running an interlude:** Introduce optional content naturally when prerequisites are met, through the appearance of the triggering NPC or situation. Do not announce that an arc is optional or steer the party toward or away from it; their choices determine whether they pursue it.

**Timing:** Interludes occur within their adjacent chapter's in-world timeframe. The Rescue Mission (Act II) triggers and resolves during the Daliketh period. A Fine Shindig (Act IV) is available during the Tikatowa downtime.

**Leveling:** The milestone table does not change based on whether interludes are run. Optional content provides tactical advantage, NPC relationships, and intelligence — not advancement.

## The Communing Ritual

The Communing ritual is the ritual the party uses to enter the spirit world and meet spirits in their domains. It was taught to the party by the Old Man of the Mountain. It differs from the Summoning ritual in a fundamental way: the Summoning brings a spirit to their place of power in the physical world; the Communing takes the party to the spirit in theirs.

**Performing the ritual:** Three things are required.

1. *The ritual words* — as taught by Droma. These do not vary between uses.
2. *A literal doorway* — any doorway serves as the threshold. A free-standing door works. A stone arch works. A drawn frame or a tent flap does not.
3. *An appropriate offering* — each spirit has specific expectations. An unacceptable offering means the passage does not open. See **The Offering System** below.

With all three present, the party performs the ritual. The doorway activates, and they step through.

**Location:** For most spirits, the ritual can be performed with any doorway anywhere. For spirits whose power is concentrated in a specific conceptual territory, the ritual must be performed in or near that territory. Contacting the Congress spirits requires proximity to Balnor; the ritual will not reach them from elsewhere.

**Duration:** The transition is instantaneous from the perspective of observers in the physical world. However long the party spends in the spirit world, no time passes for those who remain behind. The party is not aware of this until they are told.

**Group crossing:** All who step through the doorway together cross simultaneously. Anyone who waits behind remains in the physical world.

**The return:** When the conversation ends, the spirit opens a separate exit portal, or one appears automatically. The return passage is always distinct from the entrance. The party may not remain past the point when the spirit ends the meeting.

**Failure:** If the offering is wrong or absent, the threshold does not open. The party returns the way they came and may attempt the ritual again with a correct offering.

This space always culminates in a secondary threshold: a door, a gate, a surface of water, a line in the earth. Placing the appropriate offering at this inner threshold opens the way.

*Revna is not reached through a separate use of the Communing ritual. On the party's second visit to Hope's domain, Hope opens a portal within the spirit world and leads them through directly — a transit between domains, not a new crossing from the physical world.*

*Maritas's first contact is made through the Congress domain: the party crosses into the Congress's spirit world via the Communing ritual in Balnor, and Placida leads them from there to Maritas's cell. #### Hope *(First Visit)*

The doorway opens onto darkness. The party stands in a long series of passageways — not ancient or ruined but recently abandoned, in the way a house is abandoned when its occupants left in a hurry and expected to return: dust on a table that was set for a meal; a book left open and face-down on a windowsill; a child's toy in the middle of a floor; a coat hung at an angle that suggests the wearer was in motion. No non-magical light source provides useful illumination here; the darkness has a quality to it, as though it is holding something back.

A small, cool light moves at the edge of perception, somewhere ahead. The party must follow it.

The light stops before a plain stone altar set against a wall. Behind the altar is a door — solid, unremarkable, no keyhole, no handle, no visible means of opening it. It cannot be opened by mundane or magical force. On the altar, the party places their offering. If the offering is acceptable, the door opens.

*Beyond:* An open city square at twilight. The geometry of the surrounding buildings is subtly wrong — angles that almost resolve correctly without ever quite doing so. A central fountain produces only a trickle. A young girl sits beside it.

#### Hope *(Second Visit)*

The doorway opens into a house decorated for a wedding reception. The style is recognizably of the Archipelago — familiar decorations, paper flowers, the particular warmth of lanterns arranged for a celebration — but the space inside has no discernible relationship to any structure that might contain it. Room follows room: sitting rooms, corridors, a library with its long table covered in place settings, an upstairs landing that leads to a ballroom that leads back to the landing, an interior courtyard open to a sky that cannot be the outside sky. Everything is decorated. From somewhere that cannot be located, there is music.

A white garland lies among the other decorations — unremarkable, easy to overlook. Following it through the house leads, through many rooms, to a gifts table set in a space that feels more central than the geometry permits. The offering is left on the table.

A door at the far end of this room opens into Hope's domain.

*The acceptable offering for this visit is to be confirmed before Chapter 6 is written.*

#### Harmony

The doorway opens into a great hall in what appears to be a medieval castle — a vast stone space currently occupied by a crowd engaged in open, heated argument. Dozens of figures from distinct groups, recognizably different in dress, language, and posture, are contending across each other. The nature of the dispute cannot be determined; it shifts with every attempt to pin it down, factions realigning, positions moving. The scene has the texture of violence just barely restrained.

A high-DC Perception check — made only after investigation, not as a substitute for it — reveals liveried servants moving through the chaos with complete professional composure, carrying trays and adjusting chairs, following an entirely different set of priorities from everyone around them. Following the servants leads out of the hall and into a large, well-appointed kitchen where a significant catering operation is underway, calm and efficient.

When the party returns to the hall from the kitchen, the crowd has become a feast. The same figures are present; they are sharing food and drink. There is no visible memory of the dispute. The chamberlain — found in the kitchen during the party's investigation — leads them down a long corridor lined with statuary, all figures in poses of contention, assistance, and mutual compromise, and unlocks a set of beautifully carved double doors at its far end.

*Beyond:* An open-air courtyard, five stories tall, lined with doorways each marked with a different symbol — some recognizable as Isles factions, most not. The space is peopled with individuals and groups in conversation, collaboration, and shared company.

## The Summoning Ritual

The Summoning ritual was taught to the party by Hellie, the Witch of the Silver Stream. It is the method Witches of the Sacred Lands use to contact spirits — not by entering the spirit world, but by calling the spirit to their place of power in the physical one.

**How it works:** The party performs the ritual at the spirit's shrine or recognized place of power, presents an appropriate offering, and waits. The spirit manifests physically in their place, in whatever form they choose for mortal contact.

**What it can reach:** The Summoning works for nature spirits (Silver, Beam, Kelpie) and for Old Gods through their places of power. It cannot reach belief-spirits, who have no equivalent places of power in the physical world. It cannot reach a spirit who is distant from their place or cut off from it.

**Exception — Huma:** Huma is an Old God held captive in the breakwater foundations beneath Jindark. The Communing ritual cannot reach him; his captivity prevents him from manifesting in his domain. The Summoning ritual performed in the pressurized chamber where he is held does reach him — he is fragmentary and confused before the containment projectors are disabled, coherent and present after.

**The return:** When the spirit ends the contact, they withdraw. There is no return passage; the party never left the physical world.

## The Barrier and Return Travel

The Perfect Isles are concealed from the outside world by layers of obfuscating magic maintained by the Wayfinders over generations. Ships from the Archipelago can navigate out through the Barrier; ships from outside cannot navigate in.

**Returning to the Isles:** Travelers who have left cannot return without a **sending stone** issued by the Council. Activating the stone summons a Wayfinder-prepared vessel. The Council grants sending stones sparingly; the party receives one with their mission charter.

**The Pale Current:** The party's vessel for Act I — the Dremma-to-Keshigo run and the return voyage. Captained by Tavan Holt, a Wayfinder who has made this crossing many times; his crew knows the route well.

**The Dawnflower:** The party's vessel for Acts II and III, the Middle Sea crossing to the Unity and back. Introduced in Chapter 7. Captained by Warebock; Dalen and his animal partners — Funi the giant albatross and Kuni the giant dolphin — maintain near-constant watch around the ship.

**Distances:** The Archipelago is a few days' sail from the western Sacred Lands coast. The full crossing to the Unity takes approximately two months each way.

## Managing the Revelation of Secrets

The adventure's central revelation — that the spirits mortals call gods are belief-created mirrors of mortal thought, and that the primordial world has its own powers entirely separate from mortal faith — is not delivered at once. It layers across all five acts.

**Pacing guide:**

| Point | What becomes available |
|-------|----------------------|
| Chapter 4 (Droma) | Hints at the distinction without naming it — speaks of spirits who require faith and those who simply are; will not elaborate if asked directly |
| Chapter 6 (Hope) | Speaks about herself in terms that confirm, if attended to carefully, that she was created by belief and is changed by it; she does not frame this as a confession |
| Chapters 7–10 (training, Unity) | Tiamane's account of Unity ideology implicitly describes a civilization generating spiritual energy without understanding it; a perceptive party may begin to connect threads |
| Chapter 11 (Balnor / Communing) | Contact with the Congress makes the belief-as-mirror principle viscerally concrete — these spirits embody exactly what the Unity believes in, down to its contradictions |
| Chapter 14 (Huma) | Freeing an Old God illustrates the full contrast: Huma is not changed by what people believe about him; he was present before anyone believed anything |
| Act V (Conclave) | The full weight of the distinction — primordial beings deliberating the fate of mortalkind from a position entirely outside mortal faith; they cannot be prayed into mercy |

**Key NPCs and the revelation:**

*Tiamane* knows the Unity's history and begins to understand the spiritual implications during Acts II and III. By Act IV she has drawn her own conclusions. She does not share them unless directly asked; this is a personal reckoning, not a lecture.

*The Oracle* has spoken for Cyrencia without knowing what Cyrencia is. The revelation that she served as a conduit for an Old God — without having been informed — is a significant personal discovery. Allow space for it.

*Malachite* prioritizes the Wayfinder mission: get people out, keep the Isles safe. The spiritual mechanics are secondary to him. His evolving understanding shows not in discussion but in changed behavior — different urgency, shifted priorities.

**The Unity's indirect worship:** The Unity outlawed organized worship without eliminating belief. Deeply held civic faith functions identically to prayer at the spiritual level; the Congress spirits are empowered by it. When presenting this idea in play, avoid stating it explicitly. Let the party encounter the Congress, observe their coherence and power, and draw their own conclusions. If asked directly, the Congress spirits are themselves uncertain about their own nature — a truthful answer to a genuine question.

## The Stranger's Thread

The Stranger is a belief-spirit — the oldest in existence — whose job is collecting mortal souls at death and shepherding them to what comes after. He does not have strong feelings about individual mortals or their choices. He does have a plan.

**The Avengers' pact.** Some years before the adventure begins, as the Avengers made increasingly successful attempts to summon lower planar beings, the Stranger noticed them and made an approach. His offer was simple: patron-level access to his considerable power in exchange for one service per year and a day, delivered without questions asked. The Avengers' leadership agreed. The arrangement has been productive — they receive access to spiritual power they attribute to an unusually cooperative patron, and he has asked only once so far.

His current request is the death of the Circle.

Most of the Avengers' rank and file, including Chimal, believe their patron is a powerful being of the lower planes. Urdo Vhane is more perceptive. He has noticed that the patron does not behave like any fiend he has dealt with or studied, and his doubts are genuine — though he does not yet know what the patron actually is, and he has not shared his suspicions with anyone.

**Why the Stranger wants the Circle to fail.** The presence of a Circle introduces an unknown variable: physical-world agents who can call on belief-spirits opposed to him — Hope specifically — are something he wants neutralized before his larger plan matures. This is not personal. He does not dislike the party. He is a shepherd, and the world is his field.

**His plan.** A large simultaneous death event would give the Stranger a surge of spiritual power large enough for him to make his presence felt in the world in a fundamentally different way. He does not have a specific event in mind. He is patient. He is setting conditions. He is ready to roll the dice.

### The Optional Death Encounter

When a party member dies and the party does not have access to resurrection magic, that party member finds themselves at a crossroads: a dry, wooded clearing around a stone well. It is quiet. The Stranger is already there.

He greets them with the ease of someone who has done this many times. He is not unkind, and he is not in a hurry. He tells them it is time to show them to their final destination. He makes a quiet quip about it being "too bad the rest of your pals couldn't join us."

*This is a deliberate clue. The Stranger expects to collect all of them eventually — sooner rather than later, if things go his way. The dead party member may or may not recognize the significance of the comment at this moment. Do not telegraph it.*

The dead party member cannot leave the crossroads through any mundane means. The Stranger cannot be intimidated, conventionally bargained with, or defeated in combat here — he is at full power at the crossroads, and this encounter is not that kind. The only available intervention is through Hope.

**Intervening through Hope.** If the surviving party members contact Hope using the Communing ritual and ask her to intervene, she agrees immediately. The Stranger owes her a favor, and she will call it in on the party's behalf without hesitation. She teleports the surviving party members to the crossroads.

The Stranger regards them without surprise. He releases the dead party member without argument. He takes off his hat to Hope, replaces it, and tells the whole party — very quietly, in the flat monotone he reserves for moments when someone is preventing him from doing his job — that he will be seeing them again, real soon. Then he walks into the trees and is gone.

*The party should not be told in advance that Hope can intervene. This is a conclusion they need to reach themselves. If they contact Hope for any reason while a party member is at the crossroads, she will offer to help before the conversation goes further. If they contact her specifically to ask, she confirms she can act and asks whether they want her to. She does not make it complicated.*

---

### The Proposition

Some time after the optional death encounter — or, if that encounter did not occur, at a point after the Stranger's role in the adventure becomes known to the party — the Stranger visits the party member who died (or, if the encounter did not happen, a party member who has had reason to think directly about death) and makes them an offer. The visit occurs in a dream or at a quiet moment alone; he is considerate about the timing.

His proposition is simple. If the party member agrees to send him a few souls — under a personal arrangement, distinct from his deal with the Avengers — he will grant them a boon.

**The terms:**

1. When the party member kills a creature in combat, they may fulfill a use of the boon's terms by saying aloud: *"Stranger, this one's for you."* Souls sent to the Stranger in this way are bound to serve him for a year and a day; he transforms them into useful beings in his service — a horse, a dog, a raven, a footman — accordingly.
2. Only this party member may use the boon or fulfill its terms. It cannot be transferred.
3. The boon begins with five uses and is more powerful in scope than any other boon the party has received. *(Exact mechanics to be confirmed before the relevant chapter is written — should reflect the higher cost.)*
4. Uses can be renewed or expanded: each additional soul sent to the Stranger — each additional invocation of the phrase — adds one use to the boon's remaining total.

The Stranger does not press for an answer. He presents this as information, tells the party member he will take their first use of the phrase as acceptance of the terms, and leaves. He gives them time to think.

*Whether to accept is a genuine moral question. Souls sent to the Stranger serve him for a year and a day, after which their fate is unspecified. He implies, with characteristic understatement, that after their service is complete they receive "very comfortable accommodations." Whether this is true is his own business.*

---

### The Revelation Payoff

If the party learns the truth about the Avengers' patron and brings this information to Urdo Vhane in Act IV, his reaction will be controlled but unmistakable. He will not acknowledge being surprised. He will go quiet. He will ask how the party knows this, and he will listen carefully to the answer.

If the evidence is compelling, he thanks them, ends the meeting politely, and thinks for a very long time about what to do. What he does next — whether the Avengers faction begins to fracture, whether Vhane becomes a quiet ally, whether the revelation changes the Avengers' standing with the party — should be played out in Chapter 15 or developed as the DM sees fit based on the campaign's needs.

## Chapter 1: The Circle *(Levels 1–2)*

### Chapter Summary

The adventure begins before the party exists as a party. ### Scene 1: The Summons

> *The road to Sanctuary runs east through rice paddies and then into old-growth forest. An hour before the village, the canopy begins to thin and the trees step back from the path, and ahead the forest opens into a clearing that was not made by clearing — the trees simply stopped, as though asked to. At the center of the clearing, set low into the earth, is the Council site: a broad bowl of worked stone, its rim rising almost to ground level, open to the sky. Four bands of runic inscription circle the stone. Cylindrical pillars stand at the rim, etched from base to crown with the same script, and attached to their tops is a wide inverted bowl of woven wood and living vine that filters the light entering from above. Woven into this roof are charms and strips of inscribed cloth in such quantity that the whole structure moves slightly in any wind and makes, very quietly, what sounds like a room full of people at rest.*

> *Acolytes are waiting at the clearing's edge. They direct you down.*

The party members have been separately called to Sanctuary. They do not yet know why, or that they have been called as a group.

*The design of this scene belongs to the DM. Each PC's pretext should be tailored to their specific background — the source and the stated reason matter because they begin to reveal how deep the Oracle's information network runs. What follows is a menu of five example approaches; replace these with character-specific hooks before the first session.*

- **Wayfinder-adjacent:** A courier arrives with a brief, formally worded request from the Wayfinder liaison office. Presence requested at the Oracle's Pagoda at earliest convenience. No other reason is given, but the letter does contain an implication that a reason will be provided in person.
- **Hidden Vale contact:** A note delivered by hand, written in the Vale's internal cipher. A time, a location. For a Vale member or known contact, the authenticity is immediately clear; the unusual informality is not. At this meeting, the PC will receive their summons to tavel to visit the Oracle. 
- **Spirit-sensitive:** A local nature spirit, in passing, mentions that the Oracle has been asking about them. Or a dream with the pagoda in it, specific enough to linger. The Oracle's network includes non-human participants.
- **Scholarly or organizational:** An invitation to observe a demonstration of "recovered ceremonial knowledge" — vague enough to describe almost anything, specific enough to bring exactly the right person to the room.
- **Unaffiliated:** A member of the **Keepers of the Veil** appears without preamble — at the door, at a market stall, in an unguarded moment — and places a folded note in the PC's hand without speaking. The note bears no signature. It names a time, a place, and nothing else. By the time the PC thinks to look up, the messenger is gone.

*The pretext need not be fully truthful. PCs may be told only that their presence has been requested; the reason reveals itself in Scene 2. A PC who is skeptical of the summons has a valid instinct — address what would, specifically, cause that character to come anyway.*

The party meets each other at the clearing's edge, directed inward by the acolytes, or at the floor of the bowl itself. They have time for brief introductions. Wicker chairs line the rim above them, and most are already occupied by people looking down.

### Scene 2: The Circle's Meeting

*Most of the party will not have set foot in the Council site before. Attending any session of the Council is unusual for an ordinary citizen of the Archipelago; attending a private one — sealed to invited parties, conducted with the full weight of the institution — is something that does not ordinarily happen to people like them. The DM should establish this clearly before the session begins. The party stands at the center of the bowl. The people looking down from the rim are not performing gravity. They have it.*

A figure stands at the center of the bowl, waiting. She is perhaps twelve years old, in pale robes that have been carefully maintained. She has been watching the entrance since before the party arrived. **Maya**, the Oracle.

> *She is small for her age and entirely at ease standing at the center of something this large. Her eyes are older than the rest of her. When the last of you descends to the floor of the bowl, she waits for the quiet to settle.*

> *"The Circle has been named. That is why you are here."*

> *A pause.*

> *"I have waited three years to be able to say that." She does not look triumphant. She looks relieved. "I will explain what that means. And then I will tell you what you are being asked to do."*

**Attending the session:** This is a private Council session, convened at the Oracle's request. Those in the wicker chairs at the rim include:

- A **Blue Star Adepts** delegate — older, powerfully built; takes careful notes
- A **Greensingers** representative — small, quiet; visibly moved
- A **Lodge of Perfection** voice — the room's skeptic; will speak twice
- An **APA** observer — silent, clearly affiliated with Beldwin Stonefinger's organization
- **Vinna Maar** — Wayfinder representative; has been briefed; will speak after Maya

*The Avengers are not present and were not invited. The Hidden Vale does not send a representative; they evaluate the Circle another way.*

**What Maya says:** A Circle is not an Archipelago institution and is not subject to Council authority. It is a designation from the spirit world — a way for the spirit who resides within the Oracle to say, in a register the other spirits can hear: *these people act together, as one, to mediate between the spirit world and that of mortals.* When a Circle is named, spirits who are old and cautious and slow to trust know who they are speaking with. They know the Circle speaks with one voice rather than as separate individuals seeking separate things. Without it, approaching such spirits would be considerably more difficult.

The last Circle was named one hundred and twelve years ago. No one in the bowl has any personal memory of what it meant in practice. The Council has no authority to approve or block the naming — it sits entirely outside their institutional framework — and they have not objected. They are, however, watching closely. Most of them have not been in the room when something of spirtual significance like this has happened.

She presents the mission in outline: there is a spirit who seeks them, and a way to reach her. There is a teacher in the Sacred Lands, east of Keshigo on the western coast, who can provide the first key. More than this she cannot say here — the specifics belong to the road.

> *"There is a spirit who has been waiting for a long time to be heard. You are being sent to learn how to listen. That is not a small thing."*

**Vinna Maar descends into the bowl.** She is a half-elf woman in practical traveling clothes who moves through the Council site with the ease of someone who has been here before. The Wayfinders have made arrangements: a ship in Dremma, a crew, a sending stone. The party will be taken through the Barrier to Keshigo. They should plan for two to three months away, possibly longer. The mission's specifics should not be shared beyond the people in this bowl. She presents the party with a **sending stone** — rough dark-green stone, polished smooth, warm to the touch. They can activate it when ready to return to the Archipelago, and a Wayfinder-prepared ship will come within four days. She admonishes them: do not activate it early, and do not lose it.

**The council responds.** The Council members have questions. None raise a formal objection — there is nothing to object to; the party's naming is outside their authority — but they take it seriously. Give them individual voices: curiosity about the scope of what is being set in motion; careful questions about what obligations the Archipelago incurs if the Circle acts in the name of the Isles; quiet astonishment at being present when a Circle is named. *Most of the people on the rim of the bowl are witnessing something they will describe for the rest of their lives. The party should feel the weight of that.*

*If players ask about their individual qualifications:* Maya says she was informed. She does not say by whom. If pressed, she says she trusts the information she was given. This should feel like a partial answer, because it is.

**After the session.** The Council members depart from the rim, one by one. Maya remains at the center of the bowl and asks the party to stay. *This private exchange should be tailored to what the DM knows about the PCs.* She speaks to each of them briefly. She does not predict their future or make promises. She looks at each person as though she has already taken their spiritual measure and found them acceptable. She is very young, but she is serious, and she has no interest in making this more dramatic than it already is.

### Scene 3: The Road to Dremma

Two days northwest through the forest. The road is used and maintained, soft underfoot, cool in the shade of old canopy.

> *The road to Dremma runs northwest through the heart of Kartos's Isle on a path that has been walked long enough to compress the earth into something close to stone. The forest here is old — canopy closing overhead before noon, undergrowth thin for want of light, the road running between roots that have been growing since before the first settlers arrived. Birdsong carries through the middle distance. Other travelers pass in both directions: farmers with carts piled with produce bound for Dremma; a family who may be moving house, their belongings bundled onto the back of a patient mule; a pair of monks from the Path of Virtue in matching grey cloaks who nod and continue without stopping. It is a working road through a settled island, used daily by people with ordinary destinations.*

**Night 1 — Malachite and Sir Mellic:**

The party has made camp. An hour after dark, torchlight appears down the road — visible for some time before the figures arrive. They are unhurried.

**Malachite** is a tiefling, broad-shouldered, with the measured cadence of someone who has delivered this briefing many times. **Sir Mellic** is human, a head taller, in plate armor worn to a working shine. He is a Paladin of the Path of Virtue and almost never introduces himself. Malachite asks if they may sit and does most of the talking.

> *"Name's Malachite. I work with the Wayfinders. This is Sir Mellic — he doesn't usually introduce himself, so I've taken the liberty." Sir Mellic grunts. "We're going to walk with you to Dremma. I have a few things to cover before you ship out."*

Malachite's briefing is brief. Two rules. Do not identify yourselves as people from the Isles unless there is absolutely no alternative — it creates expectations and complications you do not need. Never bring anyone back with you: not a guide, not a friend, not someone in distress. That is absolute. If you meet someone you want to help reach the Isles, use the sending stone after you return and give the Wayfinders details; they have their own methods.

On extraction: if the mission goes wrong and the party needs to abort, they make their way back to Keshigo and use the sending stone. A ship comes within four days. That is the safety net. He says this matter-of-factly, without emphasis — not expecting them to need it, but wanting them to know it is there.

He is watching the party as much as briefing them — noting the questions they ask, the ones they don't, and how they speak to each other.

Sir Mellic speaks almost entirely in grunts and chuckles and will not engage directly unless addressed by name. *If the party treats him as a person rather than a piece of background, he opens up over the course of the evening.* He has spent years as a sea escort, ferrying rescued practitioners from the Unity to the Isles, and knows Unity culture through their accounts. He has never been inside himself. His opinions about the Unity are not uniformly negative, which surprises some people.

*If Malachite concludes the party is a genuine security risk* — sustained indiscretion, contempt for the Isles' secrecy, or hostility that feels real — *he notes it and informs his superiors in Dremma before departure. No immediate mechanical effect. Wayfinder NPC attitudes during Act II training may shift.*

**Day 2 morning — Belladonna:**

Malachite and Mellic are gone before the party wakes. No farewell. Shortly after the party is back on the road, they acquire a new traveling companion.

**Belladonna** is an elf woman with a small pack, good boots, and questions that do not stop. She says she is heading to Dremma to catch a ship south. She asks about the party's origins, their destination, the weather — each question flowing naturally from the last, suggesting genuine curiosity. She is a pleasant companion. She is also the **Hidden Vale**, and this is how the Vale evaluates people: not in formal settings, but in the small interactions of the road.

*She is not looking for impressive answers. She is watching how the party treats a stranger who is simply walking alongside them.* Patience and basic decency — answering honestly, asking questions back, sharing food without being asked — will eventually prompt her to reveal herself before they reach the city.

If she reveals herself, she gives the party the name and location of **Pinion**, the Vale's agent in Keshigo, and notes that Pinion can introduce them to a guide who knows the region and is willing to travel with reliable people.

> *"Her name's Pinion. She works out of Keshigo. Tell her I sent you and she'll do what she can. She can also introduce you to a woman who knows the inland routes well and is willing to travel with reliable people." A pause at a fork in the road. She turns off into the trees without looking back.*

*If she does not reveal herself, Pinion's name is unavailable through this channel. The party can still find Pinion in Keshigo through local inquiry, but without an introduction the initial contact will be more guarded.*

> *On the afternoon of the second day, the forest opens briefly where the land rises, and through a gap in the canopy you can see the water to the northwest — grey under overcast sky, the harbor just visible where it catches the light. Then the trees close again. When they open for good, Dremma is below you on the hillside above the bay.*

**Day 2, toward evening — Lady Vigo:**

About an hour before the road enters Dremma, a woman steps out of the tree line ahead with two men flanking her. She is human, well-dressed, and has the bearing of someone who is never lost.

> *She greets you as though she expected to find you at exactly this time, in exactly this place.*

> *"Please don't reach for anything. I'm not here to fight you — that would be wasteful at this stage. I only want to have a conversation. A short one."*

**Lady Vigo** is a senior operative of the Avengers. She knows the party is the Circle; she does not know what the Oracle charged them with. Her goal is to learn the mission. If she cannot, she will assess whether it represents a threat to the Avengers' interests — and whether it can be delayed or stopped.

She asks what the Oracle sent them to do.

*The decision tree:*

**If the party tells her the mission** — She thanks them and attacks immediately. She now knows what she came to learn and cannot afford to let them proceed.

**If the party cannot give a convincing account of shared purpose** — She attacks.

**If the party makes a coherent, specific case that their mission aligns with the Avengers' goals** (disruption of Unity institutions, reduction of Unity power, material damage to Unity interests) — She listens. A vague claim of shared hatred for the Unity does not satisfy her; she has heard that from people who proceeded to do nothing useful. She needs specifics: specific targets, specific methods, specific willingness to act. *This is not resolved with a Persuasion roll alone. It requires genuine engagement with her position and a case that holds together under her follow-up questions.* A convincing case causes her to step back, warn them that she will be watching, and let them pass.

**Combat:** Lady Vigo and her two attendants fight to subdue. She uses nonlethal force by default; her attendants follow her lead. If she is reduced below half her hit points — or if the combat turns decisively against her — she activates her ring.

> *One moment she is there. The next she is not. No flash, no sound. Just the space she occupied, and her two men looking at each other.*

*If she escapes:* Her agents reach the Pale Current in Dremma harbor that night. By morning, the hull has been quietly opened in one section — not enough to sink it, enough to require repairs. The captain is furious in the controlled way of someone who has been furious before and knows how useful it is. Repairs take two days; the departure of Chapter 2 is delayed. *Treat this as a grace period for additional Dremma interaction, not a crisis.*

*If she is captured:* Bring her to Dremma. She will not give useful information voluntarily. In the morning, two Avengers representatives appear at the inn and ask for her release — politely and repeatedly. They describe her conduct as that of a rogue element. They will not fight for her release. Releasing her prevents the Avengers from viewing the party as a direct threat — for now. *Lady Vigo reappears in later acts if she survives.*

**Encounter: Lady Vigo and Attendants.** See Appendix B for Lady Vigo's full stat block. Her attendants use the **Guard** stat block (*MM*, p. 347).

*This is a Hard encounter for four Level 1 characters.* Her nonlethal preference reduces effective lethality, and her escape mechanism limits the encounter's worst outcomes. She opens with *command* ("drop") or *charm person* to create early control before committing to melee. She will not pursue a party that withdraws.

*For a party of three:* position one attendant to cut off retreat, limiting the party's escape options without adding lethality to the front line.

### Scene 4: Dremma

The road drops out of the forest onto a low cliff above the harbor. The settlement spreads below: a working port without pretension, buildings functional and weathered, streets smelling of salt and woodsmoke. The **Waning Moon** occupies a corner one block from the waterfront, with dock lights visible through its north-facing windows.

> *The common room runs long and low, fire at one end, bar at the other. Tables are crowded for a weeknight. Someone is playing an instrument in the back corner, poorly but with enthusiasm. Woodsmoke and roasted fish. If you have been walking all day, it is an excellent smell.*

**Varley Rathmandantus** is already here. He finds the party before they finish their first drink — a gnome, elderly by gnomish standards, in academic robes that have seen years of use. He arrives at the table carrying a teapot and extra cups, somewhat unsteadily, which he sets down before asking whether he may sit. He pours for everyone, including himself.

He makes three separate attempts to arrive at the relevant topic before getting there. He wants details of the ritual — any ritual, the one they have found or expect to find. In exchange he can provide magic items immediately; he produces one from his coat pocket as a demonstration of good faith.

*If asked how he knows about the mission:* "One hears things." He will not elaborate. He genuinely considers this a sufficient answer.

*Varley is not a threat.* His interest in preservation is sincere and his items are real. Whether the Eightfold Path's interest in the ritual serves purposes beyond scholarship is a question this chapter does not answer. Play him as an annoyance with a genuine point, not a villain in waiting.

### Scene 5: Dremma — Departure

Morning. The Pale Current is two-thirds provisioned in the organized confusion of a departure day. Barrels are rolling. Captain Tavan Holt stands at the gangplank with the air of someone who has done this many times and still finds it imperfect.

**Beldwin Stonefinger** approaches before the party boards. Dwarf, broad, direct. He does not circle around his point.

> *"Name's Beldwin Stonefinger. Arcane Practitioners Association. I won't take much of your time." He holds out a card. "The Association wants what the Oracle has set in motion to be preserved regardless of what happens to you. We're not hedging — we just know how missions end, and we want the knowledge to survive even if you don't. When you come back, bring me what you learned. I'll make it worth your while. Each of you, individually." He holds eye contact until he is sure he has been heard. "Travel safely." He turns and walks.*

The party boards. The lines are cast off. The ship moves out into the harbor.

> *Dremma falls away behind you. The hills of the island are visible for a time above the rooftops, then just the harbor mouth,  then the island itself, then nothing but open water. Whatever the Oracle put in your hands — the charge, the sending stone, the weight of it when she said your name in the bowl — you are carrying it out to sea.*

## Chapter 2: First Voyages *(Levels 2–3)*

### Chapter Summary

The Pale Current leaves Dremma's harbor in the morning and the island falls behind. For four days the party crosses the open Middle Sea — through the Barrier's outer edge, through open water toward the oldest landmass any of them have seen. The Sacred Lands coast comes into view on the fourth day: forested, heavy, older than anything in the Archipelago.

Keshigo is a town, but not in any sense the party is likely to be used to. It is built around a single enormous trading house rather than a civic center, inhabited by people from a dozen different cultures with no shared authority over any of it, and scattered across its ground in a way that implies decades of accumulation rather than planning. The party's first task here is to locate the Witch of the Silver Stream: her name is known but her location is not, and the path to her runs through a network the party may or may not have access to, depending on what happened on the road to Dremma. They have a name — Pinion, the Hidden Vale's agent in the region — and either an introduction or a need to earn one. Either way, they will leave Keshigo on foot before the chapter ends.

**Characters introduced:** Captain Tavan Holt, Pinion, Senna.

---

### Scene 1: Through the Barrier

The Pale Current clears Dremma's harbor and picks up the morning wind off the northern coast. Within the first hour the island's hill country is only a silhouette above the waterline. Within two it is gone.

What follows is ordinary sailing — deck work, the rhythm of watch rotations, meals belowdecks — for approximately half a day before the fog begins.

> *It comes in gradually: first a haze on the horizon that you mistake for cloud, then a thickening at sea level that reduces visibility to the length of the ship, then a density of white that makes the air feel solid. The crew does not increase their pace. Captain Holt does not change his expression. He stands at the wheel and steers as though he can see perfectly.*

> *And then the fog tears.*

> *Not gradually. In sections, like cloth pulled apart at the seam — shredding into long horizontal strips, dissolving as they separate. What replaces it is not clear air. It is something the eye does not immediately know how to handle: layered sheets of light stretched across the water as far in either direction as you can see, translucent and slow-moving, draped like cloth hung out to dry on a line that runs all the way to the horizon. The colors shift as you watch — not cycling through a spectrum, but moving between states that are not quite colors, states without names. The compass needle spins freely. The sound of water changes. The light coming through the sheets does not come from the sun.*

> *The sailors continue to work.*

*Captain Holt does not explain what the party is seeing, and will not unless asked. His answer, delivered without looking up from the wheel: "That's the Barrier. We'll be through in a few minutes." He has made this crossing many times. He does not find it interesting.*

*The party has been living inside the Barrier their whole lives. They have never seen it from this angle.*

The uncanny light lasts approximately ten minutes, during which the ship moves as though through still water, the sails drawing air that shouldn't be there. Then it is behind them. The compass settles. The sea returns to its ordinary state.

> *Ahead: open water. Nothing in any direction. The Archipelago is gone from sight. The Middle Sea stretches out before you*

---

### Scene 2: The Crossing

Four days of open water.

Captain Holt runs a quiet ship. He answers questions directly and economically, volunteers little, and expects his crew to be professional — which they are. The party is not given formal duties on this leg but may take watches or assist as they like; Holt will accept a willing hand without ceremony and without particular gratitude.

*This passage has no required encounters. The DM may introduce brief atmospheric events — a sudden squall that passes in a few hours, a large dark shape passing below the ship at speed, a night sky unlike any the party has seen — but none of these should demand preparation. The purpose of this passage is transition: the party left the Isles as new members of the Circle; they are approaching the Sacred Lands as people in the process of becoming something they do not yet have words for. Give the table room for that.*

*For any character who has never left the Archipelago — which is most of them — this is worth a beat. What each of them carries into open water, what is visible in them when home is no longer visible behind them, is the DM's to observe and use. One question per player, at the right moment, costs nothing and gains a great deal.*

On the fourth morning, the shoreline appears.

> *Land. Low and forested, sitting on the horizon as though it has been there all along — which it has, for longer than anything you know how to measure. No cliffs, no dramatic approach. Just a dark line of trees above a dark line of beach, heavy and still in the morning haze, growing more detailed as you close the distance. It does not look like the Isles. The Isles were made. This was not.*

---

### Scene 3: Keshigo

The Pale Current makes landfall at Keshigo as the morning moves toward noon. The party disembarks on a beach of dark sand. The ship will take on water and supplies; Captain Holt's orders are to hold at anchor until the sending stone is activated or a standing departure date arrives.

They have not quite cleared the gangplank before people appear on the beach.

> *A wild elf woman with a basket of dried herbs and roots is already calling something in a language you don't know, gesturing at the ship, gesturing at the basket. Behind her a gnome in an apron has materialized from somewhere and is asking, in accented but comprehensible Common, whether you have "anything mechanical, clockwork, cogs, springs, anything at all." On his right, a hobgoblin in worn leather armor is holding up a side of cured meat with the patient expression of someone who knows exactly what it is worth and is prepared to wait all day. A fourth person — human, you think, though the dye in their clothes and the way they've wrapped their hair suggests neither the Isles nor anywhere near them — is speaking very rapidly in something that is not Common, making eye contact with each of you in turn as though one of you is bound to understand.*

> *They are not threatening. They are simply doing business, and the ship in the harbor is a potential transaction.*

Captain Holt fields the gnome's question without breaking stride — nothing to trade, this trip — and waves off the others in a way that suggests he has done this before.

> *Behind the beach: a settlement. Not a port, not a market town — something else. Buildings sit at irregular intervals back from the water, some permanent (timber and fitted stone, old enough that moss has gotten into the seams), some less so (thatch structures, an open-sided shelter, what appears to be a very large tent that has been in the same location long enough to have acquired a permanent fire ring). Between them, paths have been worn rather than built. The whole thing sits against the treeline as though the forest stepped back just enough to let it exist.*

Keshigo does not look like anywhere in the Archipelago. The Archipelago was assembled carefully, its communities built with intention. Keshigo has been accumulating. Whatever order it has found, it found without asking anyone to plan it.

*The settlement is inhabited by people from at least a dozen cultures, which becomes apparent in the first five minutes: different building traditions visible in adjacent structures, different scripts on door posts, different smells of cooking from different directions. The common thread, if there is one, is that most of the people here are doing something commercial or preparing to. Keshigo runs on trade — and on the specific kind of trust that forms between people who have to do business with each other in the absence of any authority that could enforce a contract.*

Before the party has gotten their bearings, a woman appears at their left shoulder.

**Rova** is a middle-aged hobgoblin with close-cropped grey hair and the easy bearing of someone who has lived in one place long enough to own it without owning anything. She introduces herself without preamble, asks where they've come from — and then, before the answer is fully out, asks why, where they're going, how long they've been at sea, whether they've been to Keshigo before, what they think of the beach, and whether anyone on the ship knows how to repair a specific kind of moss she's been searching for. She is not interrogating them. She is genuinely, specifically curious about all of it, and follows up on any answer that interests her.

*Rova is a fixture of Keshigo — has been for thirty years — and she knows everyone and everything that comes through. The DM may use her as an ambient information source: she can tell the party where to find anything in Keshigo, what the recent news is (rumors from the forest, who arrived last week, what the traders from the north are saying), and where the best food is. She asks nothing in return except the conversation, which she considers ample payment.*

When the party needs to move on, Captain Holt materializes beside them and provides the piece of information they actually need.

> *"Trading house," he says, nodding toward the settlement's center. "That's where you want to start. Large structure — you'll see the roof." He picks up a crate from a crewmember. "I'll be here for the if you need the ship. Let me know when you've found what you're looking for, and we can set ourselves back home. I assume you remember how to call for a ride?"*

**The Trading House**

The roof is visible from the beach: a broad, low structure with deep eaves, open on all four sides and held up by brick pillars rather than walls. Beneath it, in organized chaos, is a good portion of Keshigo's commercial life.

> *The trading house is full of people and stalls of every description — some permanent, constructed of timber and canvas and claimed by long use; others improvised from crates, folding tables, and what appear to be doors repurposed as display surfaces. The goods range from dried provisions to weapons to things that don't have obvious categories. The noise is considerable. Three separate negotiating parties are conducting their business at speaking volume simultaneously. Someone somewhere is cooking something with a great deal of smoke. Two children of a race you can't immediately identify are running a route through the stalls that appears to be a regular circuit rather than aimless — they know where they're going.*

**The Inn**

Built beneath and into the trading house's southern edge is Keshigo's inn, which is less an inn than a series of communal sleeping spaces accessed through a low door and managed by an elderly Haregon named **Pell**. Pell has the energy-conserving manner of someone who has answered the same questions ten thousand times and made peace with it. He produces a key for one of the sleeping spaces from a board of perhaps thirty keys, warns the party that they may need to share if traffic picks up, charges them a modest fee for the space and meals, and disappears back behind his counter.

*The sleeping spaces are simple — straw mattresses, wool blankets, hooks for hanging gear, a bolt on the door. They are clean, which is not nothing.*

### Scene 4: Seeking the Witch

**Keshigo Encounters**

The party will spend at least one night in Keshigo, probably two. The settlement's character — multi-cultural, anarchic, governed by nothing but long practice — should register over that time. Roll at least three of the following encounters during the party's stay, at the DM's discretion; additional entries may be used as filler between scenes or on the departure morning.

| d8 | Encounter | What It Shows |
|----|-----------|----------------|
| 1 | A wild elf hunter emerges from the treeline, drops a large carcass on the ground without ceremony, and immediately begins negotiating its price with three different buyers simultaneously — in three different languages — while a small and varied crowd of children (including at least one who is clearly not a child) watches with intense interest. | Informal multi-lingual economy; trade without mediation |
| 2 | A Lizardfolk herbalist sits behind an elaborate arrangement of roots, dried things, and small containers. She says nothing to anyone. The party's task: find the Witch of the Silver Stream, or find someone who can point them toward her. Local inquiry establishes quickly that a witch is known to live somewhere in the high country east of the trading post called Meeting — she is known to receive visitors, she is not hidden, and she is not something people in Keshigo discuss with strangers at length. The next step depends on what happened on the road to Dremma.

---

**Track A — With Pinion's introduction (Belladonna revealed herself in Ch 1):**

The party has a name and a location: Pinion, Hidden Vale, Keshigo. Finding them takes a few unhurried inquiries but no skill check — anyone who trades regularly through Keshigo knows where to find the person with the maps.

**Pinion's office** is a single room above the trading house's north edge: one table, one chair, no spare furniture, and every wall covered in hand-drawn regional maps. The maps are dense, annotated in at least three scripts, and clearly revised and re-revised over years.

**Pinion** is an Aasimar of indeterminate age whose celestial blood is not immediately obvious. There are no visible wings, no particular luminescence — what there is, instead, is a quality of bearing that reads as an oddity. A stillness when they are still that is slightly too complete. A precision of movement that leaves the impression of someone who never does anything by accident. They are lean, dark-complexioned, close-cropped; they carry a large bound journal that is always within arm's reach and that they consult the way other people consult their own memories. Belladonna's message arrived before the party did. Pinion greets them by name.

> *"I know why you're here. Sit if you want — there's only one chair, so work it out between you."*

With an introduction, Pinion is direct and specific. They cover:

- The character of the old-growth forest and what it means to cross it without a local guide
- The distance to the Witch's cottage: roughly a week overland, through old growth, with one significant settlement along the way
- **Meeting**: a trading post used by the forest peoples; the party will pass within a day's walk of it; it is a useful waypoint for provisioning and for asking questions
- An introduction to **Senna**, who carries letters for the Witch's correspondents and has guided travelers inland before

Before sending for Senna, Pinion asks the party a few direct questions — less to evaluate than to brief. They want Senna to know accurately who she is traveling with.

**Senna** arrives within the hour. She is a half-elven ranger in her middle years — self-possessed in a way that reads as friendly but is thorough underneath. She is carrying a letter pack for the inland route, which she was preparing to deliver anyway. She agrees to guide the party, names a departure time for the following morning, and asks a few practical questions about provisions and pace. She does not ask why they want to reach the Witch. She already knows, or close enough to it.

---

**Track B — Without Pinion's introduction:**

Local inquiry about the Witch produces imprecise results: she exists, she is known, she does not come down to Keshigo herself. Most people suggest asking the Hidden Vale's contact in the area. Finding that contact requires work.

**Check 1 — Investigation DC 12:** Asking the right people in the right order to identify Pinion specifically, rather than chasing general rumor. Success locates Pinion's office within a few hours. Failure produces a wrong lead that costs half a day.

Pinion's initial posture to an unintroduced party is professionally neutral and unrevealing. They listen, offer nothing, and wait.

**Check 2 — Insight DC 14:** Reading Pinion's careful manner to understand what they are actually waiting for — not credentials, but clarity of purpose. A party member who succeeds understands that the right approach is direct and honest, not impressive. This check grants advantage on Check 3 if successful.

**Check 3 — Persuasion DC 16 (DC 14 with advantage):** Making an honest, specific case for why the party needs to reach the Witch. Pinion is not easy to impress and does not respond to vagueness.

- *Success:* Full assistance — regional briefing and Senna introduction, as Track A.
- *Failure:* Partial assistance — Pinion provides regional orientation and directions to Meeting but declines to make the introduction. They are not yet satisfied. The party may proceed via the caravan alternative (see below).

*A party that reveals they are a Circle — that they were named and sent east — will find Pinion's posture change. A Circle has not been named in over a century, and Pinion, as Hidden Vale, will know what that means from the stories. They are not required to make this disclosure; it is simply true that Pinion has no interest in obstructing exactly this kind of work once they understand what it is.*

---

**The caravan alternative:**

Caravans from Keshigo travel the forest route to Meeting on a regular schedule during trading seasons. The party can hire on as travelers without a roll — coin is welcome here. From Meeting, the forest peoples can direct them to the Parna Valley region. This path reaches the Witch; it simply means arriving without a guide and without an introduction, which will be legible to Hellie in the first minutes of their arrival.

### Chapter 2 Conclusion

The morning of departure. Keshigo at the edge of the forest as the sun comes up.

> *She is waiting at the place where the track leaves the settlement — at the exact point where the trees close in on either side and the open ground behind you becomes, definitively, behind you. She has her pack, her bow, her letter bag. She gives each of you a look that registers you Then she turns and walks toward the wood.*

> *Within a few minutes, Keshigo is gone. Within twenty, the light changes — filtered now through a canopy so dense the sky is only visible in pieces. The forest is very quiet. The forest is also, you begin to realize, not empty.*

## Chapter 3: Into the Sacred Lands *(Levels 2–3)*

The overland journey from Keshigo to the Parna Valley takes approximately one week. The forest is old enough that travel through it feels like moving through something that has its own agenda — not hostile, but definitively occupied. This chapter ends when the party sits down to supper at Hellie's cottage. Level 3 milestone triggers on arrival.

**Chapter 3 Summary:** The party enters the old-growth forest and travels approximately one week overland to the Parna Valley. They may pass through Meeting mid-journey. On the final day the forest opens onto the valley and the cottage is visible on the hilltop. Hellie meets them at the gate — she was informed of their arrival a day earlier by local spirits. She will put the party to work around the cottage before supper. That evening, two neighbors join them at the table: an elderly gnome named Silver and a somewhat befuddled young man named Beam. Hellie answers questions, explains what she can, and directs them to the Old Man of the Mountain for what she cannot teach.

## Scene 1: The Forest Road

> *The forest closes behind you within the first mile. The canopy is high enough that the sky is still present — just in pieces, between the crowns of trees that are not individually large but are very old, old in the way that accumulates without announcement. The light through them is not dim; it is filtered, changed. Sound behaves differently here. There is more of it than you expect, and almost none of it is wind. *

> *The track is well-used. Other travelers have come this way. *

*If traveling with Senna: she sets a pace without consulting the party and does not slow it unless they ask. She is not unfriendly. She is occupied.*

*If traveling with the caravan: the column takes its time. There are more people on this track than you would have guessed from Keshigo's size, and all of them seem to know where they're going.*

## Scene 2: Meeting

The party reaches Meeting around the middle of the week.

> *The forest does not announce it. The track simply opens into a clearing — not a natural clearing, not exactly a constructed one either, but a space the forest has grown around rather than through. Old fire pits, spaced deliberately. Ground worn smooth in the traffic lines between them. A smell of cured hides and conifer resin. Several structures: a long covered shelter, open on both sides; a curing rack with something hanging from it; a low storage building with a closed door. Three wild elves are negotiating over the contents of a pack near the shelter. Nobody looks up. *
>

Meeting is not a town and not a camp. It is a trading point that has accumulated the minimal infrastructure necessary to function, and it maintains only that. Wild elf traders are the most consistent presence; others come and go with the seasons.

**Ravenstaff** — a wild elf elder who operates the storage building and functions as neutral broker for exchanges between the different peoples who use Meeting. He is old, precise, and without particular warmth or hostility. He trades at fair value; he does not inflate for travelers who don't know the local market because he is not a cheat and places a high value on the the reputation of this settlement.

If the party mentions where they are going — or if Senna names their destination — Ravenstaff has something to tell them. A pilgrim came through ten days ago moving quickly south along the Parna Valley track. Not trading, not stopping. From the north. He had the manner of someone who had seen something and needed to tell someone else about it. Ravenstaff did not get the full story; the man was in a hurry. People do not usually come out of the northern valleys with that kind of urgency.

*DM note: This is a quiet forward pointer — texture that becomes legible later, not a call to action. The party will meet Tinsel in Keshigo on their return journey. Neither this encounter nor Encounter 3 on the road should feel like setup; they are things that are happening in the world that the party happens to notice.*

**Trade at Meeting:** Standard provisions and camp supplies. Barter is strongly preferred; coin is accepted at reduced value. A limited selection of local medicinal goods is available — forest herbs, wound poultices, preparations for common ailments. While they last, a party member who uses one of these preparations each day gains advantage on Constitution saves against disease. Few weapons or armor are to be seen, and magical goods are vitually non existent.

## Scene 3: Arrival at Hellie's Cottage

**The approach:**

On the final day the forest begins to thin. The old-growth canopy gives way to younger trees, then to open ground, and then — around midday — to the edge of the Parna Valley.

> *The valley opens below the treeline without warning: wide, lightly settled, threaded through with a stream that catches light in flashes of silver and then loses itself in the woodlots. The farms here are not arranged in any pattern you recognize. They have grown where they grew, and the ground between them has been left to whatever wanted it. The sky is different from the coast — further away, somehow, or more present.  *
>
> *On the far side of the valley there is a hilltop higher than the others. There is a small structure at its top. The track descends. *

The village of Parna Valley is visible to the south, east of the forest edge. The track runs east of it and climbs the far hillside. The valley has the quality of somewhere farmed for so long that the land and the people farming it have come to an arrangement: it does not feel managed so much as mutually inhabited.

**Hellie at the gate:**

She was informed of the party's arrival approximately one day earlier by local nature spirits — she says this matter-of-factly if asked, as though it is simply how things work here (because it is). She comes out of the cottage before the party reaches the gate.

> *She is already outside when you reach the top of the path — standing at the gate of a low stone wall that runs around the cottage and its yard, watching you come up the hill. A human woman in her early forties wearing a plain work dress. Her dark curly hair, just begining to grey, is gathered in a tail thrown over one shoulder. She has been working outside and her hands are dirty. *
>
> *Two girls appear at the cottage door behind — identical, approximately ten or twelve years old, with mismatched eyes that are the inverse of each other's. They do not come forward. They watch you with open curiosity as you approach *

The two girls are Molly and Polly — Hellie's apprentices. Their opposing heterochromia is exact: what is blue in Molly's left eye is brown in Polly's right. If asked, they will state their names without particular warmth or coldness and return to watching.

*With Senna:* Hellie greets the party with the warmth of someone who has been expecting a specific group of people and is pleased they have arrived. She greets Senna separately and by name and wraps her in a warm hug. "I'm glad you're well." She does not make a ceremony of the reunion; she simply means it. She invites everyone inside.

*Without Senna:* Hellie is warm but slightly more careful in the first minutes. She looks each party member over before she speaks — not hostile, not suspicious, but with less information to work from than Senna's report would have given her. She settles quickly. By the time everyone is inside there is no functional difference from the other track. She will not withhold anything the party came for.

**Level 3 milestone.** The party has arrived.

**The cottage:**

> *Hellie's cottage is very much itself. Eclectic is understating it — arcane paraphernalia on every available surface, furniture that has clearly been acquired rather than matched, drying herbs hanging in bunches from every eave. But it is very tidy. Everything has a place; everything is in it. *

The guest room is small and genuinely comfortable — real beds with real real blankets, a window that looks down the valley. Hellie puts the party to work before supper. This is stated without apology and without checking whether they comply; she simply moves to the next task and expects them to follow. Wood splitting, hauling water from the well, any chores that will help get the house ready for supper. Hellie (and Senna, if present) goes into her workshop and closes the door, saying they can talk more at supper. 

Molly and Polly circulate throughout the afternoon. They answer any question asked of them directly and accurately, in as few words as possible. They are not talkative and they are not evasive. They call Hellie "auntie." If the party asks whether they are her nieces — after supper, when Hellie is present — she will explain that they are not, and that she simply likes the affectation.

## Scene 4: A Dinner Party 

Supper is served outside at a well-kept table when the light starts to go golden. Vegetable stew, fresh fish, homemade bread. The valley below the hilltop is quiet in the evening.

> *The table is set for more than just your party. Two of the chairs are already occupied when you come out — a very elderly gnome and a young man with a quantity of straw in his hair and clothes that can only be explained by an unfortunate afternoon, or perhaps a curse of some sort. Hellie introduces them as neighbors. The gnome's name is Silver. The young man's name is Beam. Silver nods. Beam smiles. *

**Silver** is very old in a way that reads as extreme even for a gnome. He is quiet with the specific quality of someone who has heard most of what will be said before and is paying attention to what is different. He eats very slowly. He asks each party member their name, individually, when the opportunity arises naturally. He asks where they are from. At some point in the evening, at a natural pause, he asks what they think of the valley.

**Beam** follows conversations a beat behind, as though translating from a language only he speaks. He smiles at the wrong moments — or rather, at things that are adjacent to what everyone else is smiling at. He seems to be genuinely enjoying himself. He occasionally reaches for something and misses it slightly, then corrects. The straw appears to be structural.

**Noticing something off:**

A party member who actively scrutinizes either guest may roll Insight.

- **Silver** — DC 16: he is too still between sentences. His reactions arrive slightly off-interval, as though he processes information by a different mechanism than anyone else at the table. His apparent age is remarkable even by gnomish standards and it does not read as infirmity — it reads as duration.
- **Beam** — DC 14: his relationship with the physical world is imprecise in a specific way. The misses and corrections are not clumsiness; he is abstractly elsewhere at the same time he is here. The straw does not appear to be something that accumulated on him today.

A successful check yields only the knowledge that *something here is not as presented.* Confirmation requires direct and honest engagement or a follow-up conversation.

**Hellie at supper:**

She answers questions and asks more in return. She wants to know what the party made of Keshigo, what happened in the forest, who they spoke to along the way. Her curiosity is genuine and specific — she has lived in this valley for a long time and the world outside it arrives in installments.

As the evening settles, she fills in what she can:

She cannot teach the Crossing ritual. It is old magic — older than anything Witches of the Sacred Lands practice. Witches do not enter the spirit realm unless they are invited. That is the relationship they maintain. She says this without apology; it is simply true.

What she can teach them is the ritual Witches use. It allows the practitioner to summon a nature spirit in its place of power — to call on it at the location where it is most present and ask it to appear. She offers to demonstrate it tomorrow at a nearby shrine. She will need a few things; she will explain in the morning.

Silver, at this point, mentions helpfully and apparently at random that the spirit of the stream is very fond of good bourbon.

Hellie does not comment on this. She continues.

She describes the other keys in general terms — the shape of the work ahead, the spirits the party will need to reach, the locations she knows of. This is the limit of what she can offer directly. The ritual they actually need cannot come from her.

What she can do is direct them to someone who can teach it. She calls him **the Old Man of the Mountain**. He can be found at a shrine on a mountaintop on the other side of the valley — three days' journey. She describes him as blunt. She does not add that he is the Old God she serves, nor that she has served him for most of her life, nor that she told him about the party a few days ago.

At sunset, the girls will light lanterns around the porch area, after which Hellie will shoo them off to bed. Beam and Silver make their exit around the same time. Hellie will stay awake into the evening if the party has more questions for her. The party is given the guest room, and the night passes uneventfully.
