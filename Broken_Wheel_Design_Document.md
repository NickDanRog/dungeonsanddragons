# BROKEN WHEEL
## Design Document

*Pathfinder 1st Edition Adventure Module — Design Blueprint*
*Compiled: April 2026*

---

## Adventure Overview

- **System:** Pathfinder First Edition
- **Level Range:** 1–20
- **Structure:** 3 acts, 7 Parts — each Part corresponds to the struggle for one of the continent's seven regions
- **Tone:** Historical and philosophical. The adventure examines what revolutions are made of and what they become. Every faction in this setting has tried to break a five-hundred-year cycle of rise, corruption, and collapse — and every faction has failed. The players will live inside that history as they fight to become the exception. The tone draws from historical revolutionary movements: the idealism of beginnings, the brutal pragmatics of sustained warfare, and the corrosive question of whether the cause survives its victory.
- **Player Character Type:** Natives of Zidonia, most likely drawn from the Grand Duchy of Severan or the broader Kingdom of Ambracia. The campaign begins with a popular uprising and ends with either the continent transformed or another turn of the wheel.

---

## Central Premise

Seventy-five years ago, a man named **King Xandar** united the fractious western backwater of the Highlands under a single banner and launched a campaign of conquest that, within a generation, had brought the entire continent under his rule. His original purpose was the same as every reformer before him: to break the cycle. Five centuries of religious wars, popular revolutions, and attempted unifications had left the continent in a state of chronic instability — and Xandar believed, not without reason, that only a unified continent under a just and capable ruler could end it.

He was right about the problem. He was wrong about the solution, and he did not live to discover his error. Halfway through the conquest, Xandar was killed. His daughter **Tegea**, trained in his image and committed to his cause, took command and finished what he started. But finishing a war of conquest requires different tools than beginning one, and Tegea — pragmatic, desperate, and increasingly isolated — made pacts with demonic entities whose price was her soul and the souls of her most trusted generals. The **High Kingdom** was built, the continent pacified, and Tegea and her **Five Immortals** have ruled it for thirty-odd years, sustained by dark power and growing slowly more distant from the ethical foundations Xandar laid.

The revolution the players will lead begins not from a grand plan but from a single act of imperial cruelty: the public execution of the royal family of Ambracia following their heir's failed popular revolt. The grief and rage that followed swept through the Grand Duchy of Severan and out across the kingdom, and the players are caught inside it.

What no one in the revolution yet understands is that Tegea was once what the players are trying to become. The High Kingdom's founding was itself a liberation movement. Its corruption was not inevitable — it was the accumulated product of choices made under pressure, each of which felt justified in the moment. The adventure's central question is not whether the High Kingdom can be defeated. It can be. The question is whether the players' revolution will survive its victory with its values intact — or become the next turn of the wheel.

**What players do not know at the start:**
- The High Kingdom's founding was a genuine liberation movement; Xandar was trying to break the same cycle the players are
- Queen Tegea was once ethical in the same way the players may begin; her corruption was gradual and felt justified at every step
- The demonic entities behind the Immortals' pacts have their own agenda that extends beyond supporting the High Kingdom — they have a stake in the cycle continuing
- The five-hundred-year cycle has a structural cause rooted in the continent's political geography that pure military victory will not address; every previous revolution has solved the wrong problem
- At least one of the Five Immortals carries private doubt about the course the High Kingdom has taken

## The World

*[Full setting content to be developed. Working notes below.]*

**World:** Zemia | **Continent:** Zidonia — divided into seven major regions.

### The Seven Regions

**1. The Grand Duchy of Severan**
A large island comprising roughly one third of the former Kingdom of Ambracia's territory. Considered a political and economic backwater by the High Kingdom, which maintains only limited but professional forces here. The revolution begins here. The island's relative isolation makes it defensible; its distance from the imperial center makes it an achievable first target.

**2. The Kingdom of Ambracia**
The players' home kingdom — the mainland territories beyond Severan. The execution of the Ambracian royal family was the spark that lit the revolution. The emotional weight of this theater is high; this is where the martyrs are buried and where the party's grievance is most legible to the local population. The High Kingdom fields a small but well-organized field army here.

**3. The Kingdom of Delft**
A large kingdom to the west of Ambracia. The population here does not share the Ambracian revolution's founding grievance — to them, the party arrives as foreign invaders with their own agenda. The players must win the population as well as the battlefield. The first Immortal governs this region.

**4. The Shining South**
A cold southern region consisting of powerful city-states and a large former church realm, now controlled by a reformed and corrupted imperial church organization. Navigating the Shining South requires politics as much as warfare — the city-states have divergent interests, and the church's reach is deep. The second Immortal heads the corrupted church.

**5. The Hundred Isles**
A long coastal region and collection of strategically significant islands to the north of Delft. Chief among them is a former elven kingdom, brutally suppressed by the High Kingdom. The elven question forces the revolution to confront what liberation means for peoples whose situation is worse than their own. The third Immortal rules here.

**6. The Confederation**
The largest of the seven regions. The Confederation had been a historical hotbed of factional conflict among its dozen states long before the High Kingdom arrived; imperial rule imposed order on top of existing rivalries rather than resolving them. The players must navigate competing agendas while the fourth Immortal commands a large field army against them and actively disrupts their efforts throughout.

**7. The Highlands**
The High Kingdom's heartland — once the continent's forgotten backwater, now transformed by decades of wealth extracted from the other regions into its economic and cultural center. The revolution's final theater. The fifth Immortal defends the Highlands, and Queen Tegea herself awaits at its end.

## The Antagonists

### Queen Tegea
*Ruler of the High Kingdom. Once her father's daughter. Now something else.*

Tegea fought Xandar's wars from adolescence and inherited his conquest mid-campaign at the moment of maximum difficulty. She is a skilled warrior and practiced magical user — Xandar trained her for leadership and she was, by his standards, exceptional. Her corruption was not sudden. It was a series of compromises, each of which felt necessary: pacts made in desperation, methods adopted for expediency, principles deferred until the crisis passed. The crisis never passed.

She is Lawful Evil in alignment, not because she abandoned her values, but because her values calcified into a rigid hierarchy in which order, High Kingdom survival, and the suppression of the cycle have displaced the goals those values were meant to serve. She still believes, in some part of herself, that she is doing what needs to be done. She is not entirely wrong about the problem. She is wrong about the solution, and she has been wrong long enough that the distinction may no longer matter.

Her arc is shaped by the party's Cause. In a Liberation or Covenant campaign, she is the final obstacle. In a Reformation campaign, she may be the final conversation. In a Dominion campaign, she is a mirror.

### The Five Immortals
*Tegea's most trusted generals and advisors, sustained by the same pacts as their queen.*

The Five Immortals were once exceptional individuals — commanders, scholars, administrators — who made the same bargain Tegea did and paid the same price. The centuries have taken each in different directions; they are not identical in personality or method. Each represents a different face of what the High Kingdom's pact has made: some have embraced what they became, others maintain a cold professionalism, at least one carries a private grief about it. They function as Tegea's reach across the continent, governing or commanding in the regions that matter most, and the party will face them in sequence across Parts III through VII.

The entities behind the pacts that sustain the Immortals and Tegea. Their agenda is not simply to support the High Kingdom — they have a stake in the cycle of five-hundred years continuing. They are not physically present until the party begins actively dismantling the pacts; at that point, their involvement becomes direct.

## Adventure Structure

### Act I — The Spark *(Parts I–II, Levels 1–5)*
*In which the players find themselves inside a revolution that has not yet decided what it is, establish a foothold in their home territory, and are forced for the first time to formally choose what they are fighting for.*

### Act II — The Expansion *(Parts III–V, Levels 6–14)*
*In which the revolution crosses beyond its home territory into lands that do not share its history or its grievances, confronts the first of the empire's true power in the form of the Immortals, and discovers that winning wars is easier than winning people.*

### Act III — The Reckoning *(Parts VI–VII, Levels 16–20)*
*In which the party faces the full weight of imperial power, confronts what the revolution has become across years of fighting, and makes the final choices that determine whether the wheel breaks or turns again.*

---

### Part I: The Grand Duchy of Severan *(Levels 1–3)*
The revolution's birthplace. News of the Ambracian executions has reached the island, and the popular uprising is already beginning. The players must establish the initial resistance, consolidate control of Severan, and transform a grief-driven uprising into something capable of taking the fight to the mainland. Imperial forces here are limited but professional; the primary challenge is as much organizational and social as military.

*The Cause is chosen. Mass combat framework introduced. Custom economy rules introduced.*

#### Part I Objectives *(5 main + 1 bonus; completing all 5 advances party to level 3)*

**Objective dependency:** Objective 1 → reveals Objectives 2 and 3 simultaneously. Objective 3 → reveals Objective 5. Objective 4 has independent reveal conditions (three paths). Bonus objective triggers if the Duke is freed via Objective 4.

---

**Objective 1 — Take Command of the Rebel Forces**
**Type:** Military | **Visible:** No (hidden; party is unaware of the objective system until this objective is completed)
**Revealed:** The objective system itself is unknown to the party until this objective completes. Completing it triggers simultaneous revelation of the system, this objective's completion, and all objectives that unlock from it.
**Complete:** The party formally assumes command of the Freedom Brigade from Lieutenant Breznice and organizes the survivors under their leadership.

---

**Objective 2 — Rescue the Messenger**
**Type:** Intelligence | **Visible:** No (hidden until Objective 1 complete)
**Revealed:** Revealed when Objective 1 is completed.
**Complete:** The party travels to Sava and attempts to rescue the Messenger from High Kingdom captivity. *Attempt-based:* the objective completes on the attempt regardless of outcome — the Messenger will have vanished from her holding cell before the party arrives, and this does not constitute failure.

---

**Objective 3 — Defeat the Imperial Forces on Severan Isle**
**Type:** Military | **Visible:** No (hidden until Objective 1 complete)
**Revealed:** Revealed when Objective 1 is completed.
**Complete:** General Telaris is killed or captured; Governor Selunis is killed, captured, or recruited to the rebellion; all High Kingdom forces in the region are destroyed, surrendered, or withdrawn.

---

**Objective 4 — Release the Captives from the Emerald Coffin**
**Type:** Diplomatic (path through combat) | **Visible:** No (hidden; three independent reveal paths)
**Revealed:** Any one of the following:
1. Governor Selunis is recruited by the rebellion, OR Governor Selunis reveals the Emerald Coffin under interrogation (high DC Intimidation, Bluff, or Diplomacy check required).
2. Two successful Gather Intelligence strategic actions targeting Vilniva: the first surfaces rumors; the second reveals the full conspiracy.
3. The party discovers the secret entrance to the undercastle during exploration of Hancastle, the ducal palace in Vilniva.
**Complete:** The party enters the undercastle, defeats or drives off Sigluun (the Emerald Coffin's daemon guardian), and frees the Grand Duke, Lord Emil, or both. Note: the path to completion runs through a combat encounter with Sigluun, though the objective's character is Diplomatic — the core act is the rescue.

---

**Objective 5 — Declare a Cause for the Rebellion**
**Type:** Cause | **Visible:** No (hidden until Objective 3 complete)
**Revealed:** Revealed when Objective 3 is completed.
**Complete:** The party formally declares a Cause for the rebellion. This objective is always the terminal objective for a region — its completion signals readiness to advance to the next Part.

---

**Bonus Objective — Release the Duke from Demonic Corruption**
*(Hidden — not identified as a bonus objective when revealed)*
**Type:** Diplomatic | **Visible:** No (hidden; conditional on Objective 4 completion with the Duke freed)
**Revealed:** Any one of the following (Objective 4 must be complete and the Duke must have been freed):
1. The party asks the Duke about his captivity; a player succeeds on a high DC Sense Motive check to detect his denial.
2. Both the Duke and Lord Emil are freed — Lord Emil raises the subject independently, after which the Duke's denial can be detected with a Sense Motive check.
In either case, a follow-up high DC Knowledge (Religion) check identifies the nature of the demonic corruption and its consequences. Lord Emil, if present, succeeds automatically.
**Complete:** The Duke's demonic corruption is removed via one of two paths:
- Lord Emil is present and casts the necessary dispelling spells.
- The party travels with the Duke to the Shrine at Rhunu (rock islet off the southwestern coast of Severan), where Father Tomais and the monks can perform the ritual.

### Part II: The Kingdom of Ambracia *(Levels 4–5)*
The revolution expands to the mainland territories of its home kingdom. A small but organized imperial field army must be defeated and the kingdom's population, many of whom supported the heir's failed revolt, must be consolidated behind the new revolutionary leadership. The site of the executions will be confronted directly, and the party will face early questions about what they are building on the martyr's memory.

*First major mass combat engagement. The revolution's character begins to take shape publicly.*

### Part III: The Kingdom of Delft *(Levels 6–8)*
The first foreign theater. Delft's population has no particular loyalty to the High Kingdom, but neither does it have reason to welcome an Ambracian-led revolution. The party must argue for its cause as much as fight for it, winning over local factions while defeating the High Kingdom's more substantial military presence. The first Immortal appears here — the party's first direct encounter with what Tegea's pact has made.

*Faction persuasion mechanics. First Immortal encounter.*

### Part IV: The Shining South *(Levels 9–11)*
Political complexity dominates. The Shining South's city-states each have their own interests, and the corrupted church — now headed by the second Immortal — has deep institutional reach. No single military victory resolves this theater; the party must navigate the region's factions while dismantling the church's imperial grip from within or without.

*Political faction mechanics. Religious institution mechanics. Second Immortal encounter.*

### Part V: The Hundred Isles *(Levels 12–14)*
A maritime theater with a specific moral weight: the former elven kingdom, brutally suppressed, whose people have every reason to distrust outsiders promising liberation. The party must decide what the revolution's promises mean to a population that has already been failed by every power that held this region. The third Immortal rules here.

*Naval elements. The revolution's values are tested against a population with legitimate grievances against all comers. Third Immortal encounter.*

### Part VI: The Confederation *(Levels 15–18)*
The largest and most complex theater. A dozen states with divergent agendas and little common history of voluntary cooperation; imperial rule suppressed their conflicts without resolving them. The party must govern here, not just fight — building consensus among factions that distrust each other as much as they distrust the revolution. The fourth Immortal commands a large army and will actively disrupt, interdict, and undermine throughout the Part rather than waiting for a single confrontation.

*Maximum political and military complexity. The revolution must govern as well as fight. Fourth Immortal encounter.*

### Part VII: The Highlands *(Levels 19–20)*
The High Kingdom's heartland. The revolution arrives at the place it began — the former backwater that became the empire. The wealth and infrastructure of six conquered regions is concentrated here. The fifth Immortal stands as the final guardian, along with any Immortals who survived previous encounters. Queen Tegea waits at the end. The shape of the final confrontation depends entirely on the party's Cause and the accumulated weight of every choice made before it.

*Final confrontation. All Causes converge. The wheel's fate is decided.*

## The Cause

**The Cause** is the players' declared revolutionary goal, chosen formally during Part I and recorded at the start of the campaign. It is not a constraint — players may make choices that diverge from their stated Cause at any point, and the GM should allow this freely. Cause switching is possible and narratively significant. The Cause is a compass, not a cage: it tells the party what they set out to be, and the gap between that declaration and their actual choices is the campaign's most important dramatic material.

Four Causes are available:

| Cause | Core Drive | The Wheel's Fate |
|---|---|---|
| **Liberation** | Free all seven regions; restore self-governance to each people | Broken — if the regions can hold |
| **Covenant** | Build a new governing structure that prevents the cycle from repeating | Broken by design — or not built, and the wheel winds up again |
| **Reformation** | Purge the demonic influence; reform the High Kingdom into what Xandar intended | Repaired — if Tegea or a successor can be trusted with it |
| **Dominion** | Rule the continent | Turned — the party becomes the next empire |

The GM should reference the party's Cause when presenting choices throughout the campaign, particularly at the conclusion of each Part. Moments where Cause-aligned and Cause-divergent options are both available are the campaign's best opportunities for meaningful player reflection.

## Possible Endings

### Ending 1: The Free Continent *(Liberation)*
*The revolution succeeds. The High Kingdom is dismantled. Each region governs itself.*

The High Kingdom is destroyed, Tegea is dead or exiled, all five Immortals are defeated, and each liberated region has been set on a path to self-governance. Whether this holds depends on the work done across Parts III–VI — the faction relationships built, the populations won, the institutions established or neglected. A party that did the political work alongside the military will leave behind something durable. A party that won every battle and ignored the politics will leave behind a continent that may fragment back into the old conflicts within a generation. The wheel may stop here, or it may be winding up again for a different revolution.

### Ending 2: The New Order *(Covenant)*
*The revolution builds something no one has built before.*

The party has not merely defeated the High Kingdom — they have established a new constitutional order across the liberated continent: a confederation, a league, a republic, or something else that creates structural resistance to the cycle's return. This requires winning over the Confederation's factions, building founding documents and institutions, and surviving the political cost of consensus-building rather than simple conquest. It is the hardest ending to achieve and the most explicitly optimistic. The wheel stops because it has been replaced.

### Ending 3: The Purged Kingdom *(Reformation)*
*The demonic pacts are broken. The High Kingdom is transformed.*

The party has traced the corruption to its source — the demonic patrons whose pacts sustain the Immortals and Tegea — and severed it. What this requires, beyond defeating the Immortals and confronting the patrons directly, is a decision about what remains. Whether Tegea can be reached, whether the High Kingdom's administrative structures are worth preserving, whether Reformation can be accomplished without becoming complicit in what is being reformed — these are the questions the Reformation track forces onto the table in Part VII. This ending demands that the party engage with the empire as a salvageable institution rather than an enemy to destroy, and it offers no clean victory. The wheel is repaired. Whether it runs straight depends entirely on who holds it.

### Ending 4: The New Empire *(Dominion)*
*The revolution wins. The revolution becomes the empire.*

The High Kingdom is defeated. The party rules. The continent is theirs. Everything the High Kingdom built — the administrative apparatus, the military power, the wealth extracted from six regions to build the seventh — is now the party's instrument. The adventure ends not with a final battle but with a final question: standing at the height of power, having become what they fought, what do they do next? The party has access to every tool that corrupted Tegea, and they have watched what those tools made of her. This ending does not judge the party. It presents them with the exact situation Xandar faced, gives them full knowledge of what Xandar's daughter did with it, and leaves them to write the next chapter. The wheel turns.

## Key Mechanics to Design

1. **The Cause System:** Framework for tracking the party's revolutionary goal across all seven Parts. Includes GM guidance for presenting Cause-aligned choice moments, handling Cause switches, managing mixed-Cause parties, and determining which ending the party has earned at the campaign's close.

2. **Mass Combat (PF1e SRD):** The existing mass combat rules from d20pfsrd.com are the foundation for all large-scale military engagements. Each Part includes at least one mass combat engagement. Army composition, stat blocks, and scenario specifics to be developed per Part in the GM Guide.

3. **Revolutionary Economy (Custom):** *[Rules to be provided by user.]* Tracks the revolution's economic capacity to sustain a military campaign across the continent. Integrates with mass combat to determine what forces the party can field. Tied to the Regional Loyalty system below.

4. **Regional Loyalty:** Each region has a loyalty track reflecting how committed its population is to the revolution's cause. High loyalty improves army recruitment and economic output; low loyalty creates complications and opens regions to imperial reconquest. Actions during standard adventure content — political choices, treatment of populations, how the party handles difficult moral questions — all affect loyalty.

5. **Immortal Escalation:** The Five Immortals are aware of the revolution from the moment it becomes visible and respond to it. Surviving Immortals share intelligence and may coordinate. The fourth Immortal (The Confederation) actively disrupts throughout Part VI rather than waiting for a set-piece confrontation. The GM should track surviving Immortals and adjust late-campaign encounters accordingly.

6. **Cause Divergence:** A GM-facing tracking tool for noting when the party's choices diverge significantly from their stated Cause. The accumulated weight of these divergences shapes the ending — a party that declared Liberation but governed like an empire will find the ending reflects what they did, not what they said.

7. **Objective Leveling:** Advancement tied to objective completion within each region. Each region contains a set number of objectives; some begin visible, others are hidden. One bonus objective per region exists (hidden; not marked as bonus when revealed). To advance a level, the party must complete objectives equal to the sum of their current level and the next level (e.g., level 1→2 requires 3 objectives, level 2→3 requires 5, and so on). The campaign contains just enough objectives to reach level 20 before the final confrontation with Tegea. Objectives can be missed, bypassed, or failed; bonus objectives offset losses. It is possible, though not intended, to arrive at the final confrontation below level 20. Regions 1–3 are completed in sequence; Regions 4 and 5 may be tackled in either order; Regions 6 and 7 are sequential.

## Institutions and Factions

### The High Kingdom
*Public face:* The legitimate government of the continent; a force for order and stability after five centuries of chaos. Many citizens, particularly in the Highlands and Ambracia's more integrated communities, believe this sincerely.
*Hidden truth:* Sustained by demonic pacts. The senior leadership is functionally immortal and has been drifting, for decades, toward the interests of its patrons rather than its subjects.

### The Revolutionary Forces
The party's army and political organization. Begins as a popular uprising in Severan and Ambracia, grows as liberated regions join. Its name, structure, and founding principles are for the party to determine during Part I. Its character across the campaign reflects the accumulated weight of their choices.

### The Church of [THE SHINING SOUTH]
*Public face:* The established religious institution of the southern regions, reorganized under imperial authority.
*Hidden truth:* The second Immortal heads it. The "reorganization" was a purge. A remnant of the original church survives in hiding; the party may find it in Part IV.

### The Elven Remnant (The Hundred Isles)
The survivors of the suppressed elven kingdom. Scattered, diminished, and deeply skeptical of outside promises. Their situation is the defining moral fact of Part V. What the party does for or to them — and whether they mean it — will define the revolution's character in that theater and color the ending.

- The economic constraints of sustaining a years-long military campaign
