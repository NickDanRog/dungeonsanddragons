# The 33rd Rune — Design Document

---

## Adventure Overview

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **System** | D&D 5e (2014) |
| **Level Range** | 1–12 |
| **Structure** | Fully linear — 12 chapters across 4 acts |
| **Tone** | Heroic quest with political and investigative texture; escalates toward cosmic stakes |
| **PC Type** | Natives of Talumh; members of a found family built around a missing scholar mentor |
| **Setting** | Four human communities scattered across the continent of Talumh |

---

## Central Premise

Two hundred years after the Great War, humanity has found a fragile peace under the Settlement — the Lord and Lady's decree scattering the twelve human communities to the far corners of Talumh. That peace is eroding.

A human religious movement called the Path of Man has been growing for generations. On its surface it preaches human dignity, self-determination, and freedom from dependence on the Tuthá and their divine patrons. Its founder, a philosopher called **Satvan the First**, is a brilliant and genuinely well-intentioned man who believes, not unreasonably, that humanity deserves to determine its own destiny. He is not a monster. He is a man whose vision has been slowly warped by corrupted forces whose true nature he does not understand.

In the cosmology of Void Magic — the rune-based magic that has been the province of human Magicians since before the Great War — practitioners speak reverently of **the Adi**: the ancient, unknowable beings who first revealed the runes to Men and granted them the power to shape the Void. The Adi are, in truth, the Shadow Lords — corrupted spirits who escaped their banishment and have been cultivating the Path of Man as a tool for generations. Satvan does not know this. No human does.

Contemporary magical theory holds that exactly thirty-two runic structures constitute the whole of what can be known. But in every culture that practices Void Magic, there exists a persistent legend: a **33rd Rune**, mythical and forbidden, that when combined with the other rune-forms will give its wielder unlimited power. Satvan believes this legend completely, and has spent decades searching for it. The 33rd Rune, in truth, is real — and it is exactly as powerful as the legends claim, though not in the way anyone expects. It is not a weapon or a force of destruction. It is a vastly empowered Wish spell: a single, world-altering miracle, the most powerful act of will that can be expressed through Void Magic.

Satvan intends to find the 33rd Rune and use it to wish away the Lord and Lady's covenant with the Tuthá — to demand, from the universe itself, that humanity be free of divine subjugation. What he does not know is that the Adi — the Shadow Lords — have been carefully shaping his understanding of the Wish for decades. The wish he makes will be channeled entirely through their power. At the moment of casting, whatever he intends, the Shadow Lords will ensure the wish is interpreted as they need it to be: a miracle that breaks the ancient Void seal, the sacrifice the spirits gave their lives to create, and opens Talumh to the Shadow Lords at last.

Satvan is not trying to end the world. He is trying to free his people. The Shadow Lords have simply arranged things so that those two intentions, at the critical moment, are indistinguishable.

The PCs are members of the found family of **Priya the Wanderer**, a wandering scholar and rune researcher who had come dangerously close to uncovering the 33rd Rune's true nature and location — and understanding what the Adi truly are. When Priya disappears without explanation, the PCs follow the trail of clues she left behind — a trail that will carry them across four human communities, deep into the heart of the Path of Man's expanding empire, and finally to the ruins where the fate of Talumh itself hangs in the balance.

## The World

Talumh is a large island continent surrounded by an infinite, impenetrable ocean mist. At its heart is Trí-na-nóg, the ancient floating city drawn into a secluded mountain valley by the Lord and Lady — the two great guardian spirits who maintain the covenant with the Tuthá, the fey races of the world. Talumh is wild, magical, and beautiful, with a geography that tends toward the extreme: its mountains are high, its canyons deep, its plains vast and its jungles impenetrable.

Two hundred years after the Great War, the twelve scattered human communities are the primary civilization of Men on this continent. They are geographically remote from each other, and the Settlement — the Lord and Lady's decree — still holds, though its grip loosens year by year as contact between communities slowly resumes. The Tuthá watch, and wait.

The world is alive with magic. Four types of magical practice exist: **Sorcery** (the natural magic of the Tuthá and those connected to the land), **Elemental Magic** (wild nature power, practiced by Greensingers and Summoners), **Void Magic** (the dangerous rune-based magic revealed to Men by the Adi — the mysterious ancient beings who are, unknown to any mortal, the Shadow Lords in disguise — practiced by Magicians), and **Divine Magic** (granted by great spirits to their chosen practitioners). Void Magic is the most volatile — every casting risks catastrophic failure and leaves dramatic visual evidence in the air.

### The Six Geographic Regions of Talumh
- **The Expanse** (west): vast plains and rolling hills; two large human communities; the Yashim Mountains home to the Dwarfs
- **Wilderland** (central): mountains and valleys; Goodlund (Halfling homeland); Sacred Valley; the heart of the continent
- **The Pale** (north): cold, remote; Darkwood; Twilight Vale (Feytouched settlement); Shining Plain
- **The Long Coast** (south coast): Grand Strand; delta country; the Asura River and its many-channeled mouth
- **The Sward** (east): rough eastern wilderness; the Dune Sea; Crystal Forest; hostile terrain
- **The Knife** (southeast peninsula): tropical; Chula Jungle; coastal trade routes

## The Four Communities

### Fountain (Act I — Home)
A small city sitting at the base of the great escarpment that separates the Expanse from Wilderland. Named for the spectacular waterfall that crashes down the cliff face and feeds the Sona River, which flows west through the Expanse. Fountain is a place of color — multicolored tile roofs, religious banners, and the constant sound of rushing water. It has a deep religious tradition rooted in the Path of Light, the human folk religion that worships a singular Creator.

Two client towns — **Grove** (a trading village in the lowlands below the falls) and **Portal** (a river port where small seagoing vessels make the long journey south) — serve as commercial satellites to the city above. These towns facilitate almost all of Fountain's trade with the outside world.

Fountain is beautiful, traditional, and deeply unequal. A wealthy aristocracy controls the city's religious institutions and the trade that flows through Grove and Portal, while a large underclass of laborers and dock workers lives in the shadow of the falls. This tension is rarely spoken of openly. The Path of Man has not yet gained a foothold here, but the city's wealth gap is fertile ground for its message.

**Key feature:** The Sona River is the primary travel route south from Fountain, making the city the gateway between the wild Wilderland interior and the coast.

### Window (Act II)
The largest human settlement on the continent, Window was built on a cluster of small islands in the Asura River delta where it meets the sea. Each island is a close, tightly-knit community connected to its neighbors by arching stone bridges. The buildings are constructed from dark river stones, stacked and mortared in a distinctive style that gives the city a sober, austere beauty. Window is often shrouded in mist and river fog, giving it a dreamlike quality that belies its hard-nosed commercial character.

Window is the closest thing to a capital that humanity has in these scattered centuries. Its port serves as a hub for seaborne trade between human communities up and down the coast, and the city's Portmasters' Guild wields enormous economic power. The Path of Man moved carefully into Window over two generations — first as a philosophical society, then as a political faction, and finally as the dominant religious authority. The conversion was quiet, methodical, and almost complete.

Window under Path of Man control is orderly, industrious, and deeply suspicious of outsiders. The Path's hierarchy controls the major guilds, the city watch, and the civic councils. Dissent exists but is suppressed. The PCs will find allies here, but they will have to work to find them.

**Key feature:** Window is the most important trade hub in the human world. Whoever controls Window controls the flow of goods — and information — between communities.

### Shade (Act III)
Shade is a canyon city, its buildings carved directly into the stone walls of a great gorge cut through the Dune Sea's western edge by the small Lakhsa River. The city takes its name from the vast canvas shades strung overhead between the canyon walls, blocking the brutal desert sun and giving the streets a cool, amber-tinted light. It is one of the stranger and more beautiful human settlements in Talumh.

Shade is governed by an Elder Council of seven, elected by the clans that inhabit the canyon's major residential tiers. The council is fiercely independent and deeply suspicious of outside influence — a useful trait when the Path of Man began sending missionaries, but less useful now that two of the council's seven seats have quietly come over to the Path's position. The remaining five are split between open opposition, neutrality, and nervous silence.

Shade survives by trade — a long overland caravan route connects it to Window, moving through the hardlands of the Sward. That route also makes Shade dependent on Window's goodwill, a fact the Path of Man has exploited with great skill. The city's citizens are broadly non-religious and pragmatic. They will follow whoever keeps the water flowing and the caravans moving.

Beyond the canyon walls lies the Dune Sea: hostile, beautiful, and ancient. Nomadic fey tribes and desert humanoids make the wasteland their own, and Shade has been at low-level war with them for generations. This desert also hides something old — something that drew Priya the Wanderer to this region and brought her closer to the 33rd Rune's location than anyone had been in centuries.

**Key feature:** The Elder Council is the adventure's most explicit political battleground. PCs can actively work to turn votes, and the council's final alignment shapes the climax.

### Wheel (Act IV)
Wheel sits at the edge of the Chula Jungle on the Knife, looking out at the Singing Sea. It is named for its port — a great circular harbor, impossibly regular, cut from the living rock by a legendary magician-inventor named **Vira the Maker** centuries ago. Ships enter through a narrow channel, and inside the wheel the waters are perfectly calm regardless of the sea's mood.

Wheel is many things: a free port, a smuggler's haven, a pirate waystation, and the most chaotic permanently inhabited city in the human world. Its "government" consists of a rotating Portmaster selected by the major trading houses for one-year terms, with essentially no ability to enforce anything beyond the port's commercial rules. The city's unofficial motto is that anyone can do anything as long as they can afford to.

The Path of Man arrived here not with coercion but with money and stability. In a city that has always struggled with violence and disorder, the Path's offer of community, purpose, and protection proved genuinely attractive to a large portion of the population. A significant minority has converted. An equally significant minority is preparing to fight. The tension between them crackles under the surface of every transaction.

Wheel matters because the 33rd Rune must be cast from a specific location: an ancient, pre-Settlement Void Magic site of immense power, submerged beneath the Singing Sea. The ritual requires a place where the Void is close — a location where the rune's world-altering energies can be channeled without the caster being destroyed in the attempt. Vira the Maker discovered this site centuries ago during the construction of the harbor, built the wheel-harbor as a permanent access point above it, and buried the knowledge in records that only his heir now possesses. The Path of Man has been in Wheel for years, quietly working toward access to those records. The PCs arrive to find they are almost too late.

**Key feature:** Wheel's wheel-shaped harbor is the adventure's climactic arena, and the city's piratical chaos is both obstacle and potential ally.

## The Antagonists

### Satvan the First
**Role:** Founder and spiritual leader of the Path of Man; the adventure's primary antagonist
**Alignment:** Lawful Neutral (sliding toward Lawful Evil by Act IV)
**Epithet origin:** "The First" — self-applied; a signal that he sees himself as the first to articulate what humanity truly deserves

Satvan the First began as what he appears: a sincere, brilliant philosopher who articulated a genuine grievance. From his perspective, humanity was drawn through the Void to this world by the grace of the Creator, fought a catastrophic war that their divine benefactors the Adi set in motion, and was then scattered across a continent as punishment by the Lord and the Lady — beings of tremendous power who imposed their will on Men without consent. His argument — that humanity must build its destiny on its own terms, free of divine manipulation — is not wrong.

In Satvan's theology, the Adi are the rightful patrons of humanity: ancient, unknowable beings who recognized Men's potential and gifted them the runes. He venerates them as the source of everything the Path of Man draws on. He does not know that the Adi are the Shadow Lords. He does not know that the divine power his followers wield is a leash, or that the Adi have been cultivating his movement for generations as a tool for their own return to Talumh. He believes the Virtues — the divine force the Path invokes — are an expression of humanity's own spiritual potential made manifest. He is wrong. And as his obsession with the 33rd Rune deepens, he has begun performing small acts of moral compromise that he dismisses as necessary costs. He turns away from reports of his followers' excesses. He chooses not to investigate the fate of scholars who asked the wrong questions.

He is not yet a monster. By the end of the adventure, he may become one.

**Arc:** Satvan begins the campaign as a rumor and a philosophy — the PCs encounter his words and his followers long before they encounter him. When they finally meet him (likely in Act III), he is the most reasonable person in the room. He has answers. He has arguments. He has a version of history that is genuinely compelling. The PCs' task is not just to stop the casting of the 33rd Rune — it is to decide what, if anything, they owe someone like Satvan, and whether the truth about the Adi is a weapon or a mercy.

### The Shadow Lords (known to Men as "the Adi")
**The force behind the Path of Man.** The Shadow Lords are corrupted spirits who escaped their banishment through the Dream of the One and have been manipulating events in Talumh from beyond the Void ever since. They cannot enter the world directly — the seal the spirits sacrificed themselves to create prevents it. The 33rd Rune, as a supremely empowered Wish spell channeled through their power, is their key: if Satvan casts it and makes even the most well-intentioned wish for human liberation, the Shadow Lords will ensure that wish is executed as a miracle that unmakes the Void seal.

**To humanity, they are "the Adi"** — the First Ones, the primordial beings who recognized the potential of Men and gifted them the runes. The Path of Man venerates them as the spiritual source behind the Virtues. The Void Magic tradition traces its lineage to their revelation. No human has ever questioned this account, because the Shadow Lords have been careful to ensure there is no counter-narrative. Priya the Wanderer had come close to questioning it. This is part of why she was silenced.

The Shadow Lords do not have a single spokesperson in this adventure. Their influence is present in the form of what the Path of Man does not know about itself, in the disturbing things that happen when Path of Man rituals go wrong, and in the growing sense among the PCs that something is cosmologically wrong. A specific Shadow Lord — **Penumbra**, the most cunning of their number who first enabled the escape from banishment — is the ultimate architect of events, though direct confrontation with a Shadow Lord avatar may or may not occur at the DM's discretion.

## Key NPCs

### Priya the Wanderer
**Role:** The PCs' mentor; the inciting absence at the heart of the adventure
**Status at adventure start:** Missing — circumstances unknown

Priya the Wanderer is a scholar, collector, and field researcher of renown among the small world of rune historians and void-magic theorists. For thirty years she has traveled Talumh building a network of researchers, contacts, and expedition partners. She asks hard questions, keeps meticulous records, and adopts lost people the way other scholars adopt lost manuscripts. The PCs are her found family — people she pulled out of difficult circumstances and gave a purpose, a home base in Fountain, and a reason to be curious about the world.

Priya went silent three weeks before the adventure begins. Her study in Fountain shows evidence of a hasty departure. Her notes are not there — but some of her coded correspondence is, and it is enough for the PCs to begin.

What the PCs will gradually discover: Priya had identified the probable location of the 33rd Rune and realized what it actually does — not what Satvan believes it does, but what its casting would truly unleash. She encoded this discovery and dispersed it across multiple hidden caches in the four communities, knowing that if she were taken, the Path of Man would search her home first. The adventure is, in part, about reassembling what Priya knew.

**Fate:** Whether Priya is alive, imprisoned, dead, or something stranger is a design decision to be made during DM Guide development. Her fate should hinge on PC actions and the path through Act III.

### Tuthá Emissaries (established)
Three Tuthá encounters are woven into the adventure's structure, representing the courts' cautious but genuine investment in the PCs' success:

**The Lady's Agent (Act I, Chapter 3):** A mysterious Tuthá emissary appears as the PCs prepare to leave Fountain. She offers a warning, a fragment of useful knowledge, and an acknowledgment that the quest has been noticed. She will not answer direct questions about the Lady's intentions. She is the first signal that the PCs' journey has significance beyond what they know.

**The Lord's Intervention (Act III, Chapter 9):** At the moment of near-certain death near the close of Act III, the Lord (or a powerful agent acting in his name) intervenes decisively and without explanation. The ambiguity of whether it was the Lord himself or his champion is intentional and should be maintained in play. The message is clear: the PCs matter.

**Vira's Heir (Act IV):** A powerful but intensely private Feytouched sorceress, the heir to Vira the Maker's legacy and the custodian of his notes and records. She has lived in Wheel for decades in deliberate obscurity, wanting nothing to do with the city's chaos, the Path of Man, or anyone who comes asking questions. She can be reluctantly convinced to help the PCs — but it will take patience, trust-building, and probably proof of what the Adi truly are. Her magic is significant, and her knowledge of Vira's wheel-harbor and its secrets is essential to the climax.

### Additional NPCs (to be developed in DM Guide)
- A Path of Man inner-circle figure in Window who is having doubts
- An Elder council member in Shade who serves as the PCs' primary local ally
- The Portmaster of Wheel (term incumbent during the adventure)
- A Greensinger or Summoner who has been tracking the Path of Man's Dune Sea activities
- A Shadow Lord-touched Path of Man enforcer who serves as a recurring antagonist across Acts II–IV

## Adventure Structure

### Act I — Fountain (Chapters 1–3, Levels 1–4)
The PCs begin in Fountain, the city they know best. Priya has been missing for three weeks. Her study has been searched. A contact is dead.

**Chapter 1 (Levels 1–2):** The PCs investigate Priya's disappearance in Fountain. They uncover the presence of covert Path of Man agents in the city — unusual, given Fountain's strong Path of Light tradition. They find a coded message left by Priya pointing south.

**Chapter 2 (Levels 2–3):** Deeper investigation reveals Priya had been researching the 33rd Rune and had found something significant. The Path of Man's agents in Fountain attempt to shut down the investigation. The PCs discover Priya left for Window and that she knew she was being watched.

**Chapter 3 (Levels 3–4):** The PCs follow the Sona River south through Grove and Portal, gathering allies and additional fragments of Priya's research. On the road, Path of Man agents make a serious attempt to end the investigation permanently. As the PCs prepare to depart for Window, they receive a cryptic visit from a **mysterious agent of the Lady** — a Tuthá emissary who offers a warning, a fragment of useful knowledge, and a reminder that the PCs' quest is not unobserved. The agent does not explain her full purpose, and will not be drawn on questions about the Lady's intentions. She will not be the last emissary the PCs encounter.

### Act II — Window (Chapters 4–6, Levels 4–7)
Window is Path of Man territory. The city is orderly, the streets watched, and the PCs are outsiders in an unfriendly place.

**Chapter 4 (Levels 4–5):** The PCs arrive in Window and immediately feel the city's oppressive orderliness. They must find underground contacts — former associates of Priya's who have gone quiet. The Path of Man's Influence is high here; the PCs' actions can begin to erode it.

**Chapter 5 (Levels 5–6):** The PCs uncover the Path of Man's hierarchy in Window and how deeply the city has been reorganized around the sect's structure. A key figure — a high-ranking Path functionary with growing doubts — can be turned. This contact provides a lead on the 33rd Rune's location (somewhere in the Sward, accessible from Shade) and on Priya's last known whereabouts.

**Chapter 6 (Levels 6–7):** The Path of Man moves to capture the PCs before they can leave. A tense extraction through Window's island bridges and fogbound delta leads to a ship bound for the southern coast. The act ends with the PCs having seriously damaged the Path's influence in Window — or having been forced to flee with less than they needed.

### Act III — Shade (Chapters 7–9, Levels 7–10)
Shade is the pivot point of the adventure. The Path of Man has not yet won here — but it is close.

**Chapter 7 (Levels 7–8):** The PCs arrive via the long caravan route from the coast. The Elder Council is split. Two seats favor alignment with the Path of Man; two are opposed; three are watching. PCs must navigate local politics, earn council trust, and understand what Shade needs to stay free. They also encounter the first direct evidence of the Dune Sea's ancient ruins.

**Chapter 8 (Levels 8–9):** Priya's trail leads into the Dune Sea — an ancient site she was surveying before she vanished. The PCs venture into the desert to find Priya's cache, discover the true nature of the 33rd Rune, and possibly encounter Priya herself. The Path of Man sends a significant force into the desert to stop them.

**Chapter 9 (Levels 9–10):** The PCs return to Shade with what they found and make their case to the Elder Council. This is the climax of the influence tracker mechanic — the council vote tips one way or another based on everything the PCs have done in Act III. The PCs also encounter Satvan the First for the first time, either in Shade itself or at the edge of the Dune Sea. He is reasonable. He is compelling. He is wrong. Near the close of the act, when the Path of Man's forces close in and the situation turns desperate — a point of near-certain death — **an agent of the Lord intervenes**. Whether this is a powerful Tuthá champion acting in the Lord's name, or the Lord himself walking briefly in mortal guise, is left to the DM's discretion; either reading should remain ambiguous in play. The intervention is decisive but brief. The Lord does not explain himself. The PCs are alive because they need to be, and they leave Shade knowing that powers older than the Settlement have been watching them.

### Act IV — Wheel (Chapters 10–12, Levels 10–12)
The Path of Man has found what they need to complete the ritual. Wheel is where the final pieces converge.

**Chapter 10 (Levels 10–11):** The PCs arrive in Wheel to find the Path of Man has been quietly acquiring the records of Vira the Maker for months. The great wheel-shaped harbor is being prepared for something. The free-spirited resistance to the Path of Man is disorganized and needs leadership. The PCs must find Vira's heir, understand what the ritual site is, and begin to pull the resistance together.

**Chapter 11 (Levels 11–12):** The Path of Man moves. The ritual sequence begins — a multi-stage process that must be completed in the harbor. PCs must fight to disrupt each stage while managing the chaos of Wheel's factions and the Harbor's complicated geography. Satvan the First is present, increasingly certain and increasingly dangerous.

**Chapter 12 (Level 12):** The final confrontation. The 33rd Rune hangs in the balance. Satvan must be stopped, reasoned with, or confronted with the truth of what the Shadow Lords have made him into. The ending depends on what the PCs have done, what they chose, and how they choose to end it.

## Possible Endings

### Ending 1 — The Wish Is Never Made (Good Ending)
The PCs stop the ritual before the 33rd Rune can be cast. The Wish is never spoken. The Void seal holds. The Path of Man's connection to the Adi — the Shadow Lords — is exposed, either through Priya's recovered research, through the PCs' confrontation of Satvan with the truth, or through both. The Path does not collapse overnight, but its authority is broken and its divine power source is severed. Satvan the First faces what his obsession became. Whether he accepts it, fights it, or breaks under it is a final scene that varies by PC approach and what was revealed.

The world does not end. The hard work of building something new begins.

### Ending 2 — The Wish Is Subverted (Partial Success)
The 33rd Rune is cast, but the PCs have gathered enough understanding — through Research Points and the alliances they built — to disrupt the Shadow Lords' channel at the critical moment. Satvan makes his wish. It does not do what the Shadow Lords intended. The Void seal takes damage but does not break; the Shadow Lords' influence seeps through a fracture but they cannot enter Talumh bodily. Satvan, confronted with evidence of what the Adi are, is shattered. The Path of Man survives in fragmented form. The world is changed, and not entirely for the better.

The PCs have bought time. The fracture will need to be healed. That is a story for another day.

### Ending 3 — The Wish Is Granted (Dark Ending)
The 33rd Rune is cast. Satvan makes his wish for human liberation. The Adi answer it in their own terms: the Void seal — the sacrifice that the great spirits gave their lives to create — is undone. The Shadow Lords can now affect Talumh directly. The Lord and Lady are driven back to Trí-na-nóg. The Tuthá courts fracture. A new dark age begins.

Satvan gets exactly what he wished for — freedom from the Lord and Lady's covenant — and something far worse than he ever imagined.

The PCs survived. The world they knew did not.

## Key Mechanics to Design

### Path of Man Influence Tracker
A community-by-community measure of the Path of Man's control, rated on a scale of 1–10 per community (Window starts at 8, Shade at 4, Wheel at 5). PC actions — investigations, alliances, public confrontations, uncovering corruption — can reduce the score. Certain Path of Man events advance it. The influence score at each community affects:
- What help (or hostility) the PCs receive locally
- Whether a given ending track is accessible
- Narrative flavor of each community during PC visits

### Rune Research Mechanic
Priya's scattered notes, hidden caches, and research fragments are the key to understanding both the true nature of the 33rd Rune and the true nature of the Adi. The PCs accumulate **Research Points** across all four acts. The total at the start of Act IV determines:
- Whether the PCs know that the 33rd Rune is a Wish spell — and that the wish will be corrupted at the source by the Adi (the Shadow Lords)
- Whether the PCs can prove to Satvan what the Adi truly are (a high Research score gives them the evidence; a low score means they can assert it but not prove it)
- How much the PCs know about the ritual's stages and requirements (giving them a tactical advantage in Ch. 11)
- Whether Ending 3 can be averted, or only survived

### Shade Elder Council Alignment
Seven seats; at the start of Act III, two favor the Path of Man, two oppose, three are neutral. Each scene in Shade can earn or lose Council votes based on PC actions. The final tally at the Chapter 9 vote determines whether Shade becomes an ally, an obstacle, or a battleground in Acts III–IV.

### Wheel Influence / Resistance Tracker
Unlike Shade's formal council mechanic, Wheel's tracker is a measure of the free-spirited resistance's organization and morale. It starts low. PCs can raise it by finding leaders, supplying resources, and winning small victories in the Act IV chapters. A high tracker score means the resistance fights alongside the PCs in Chapter 11. A low score means the PCs face the final confrontation with less support.

### Milestone Leveling
PCs level at the completion of each chapter cluster:
- End of Ch. 2 → Level 2
- End of Ch. 3 → Level 3
- End of Ch. 4 → Level 4
- End of Ch. 5 → Level 5
- End of Ch. 6 → Level 6/7
- End of Ch. 7 → Level 7/8
- End of Ch. 8 → Level 8/9
- End of Ch. 9 → Level 9/10
- End of Ch. 10 → Level 10/11
- End of Ch. 11 → Level 11
- End of Ch. 12 → Level 12

## Institutions and Factions

### The Path of Man
**Public face:** A religion of human dignity and self-determination. Worships "the Virtues" — an idealized divine force representing the highest aspirations of humanity. Preaches that Men must no longer rely on the Tuthá's divine patrons, the chaos of Void Magic, or the capriciousness of the Lord and Lady. Hierarchical structure with regional leaders (called Pillars) and a senior council of Voices. Satvan the First holds no official rank but is venerated as its founder.

**Secret truth:** The Path of Man's divine power is supplied by the Shadow Lords, who have been cultivating it for generations as a tool to break the Void seal. Most practitioners — including Satvan — do not know this. The path began as a genuine human philosophical movement and was co-opted gradually. The corruption is deep but not total; there are genuine believers throughout its ranks.

**In this adventure:** The primary antagonist organization. Fully controls Window; advancing in Shade and Wheel; covertly present in Fountain.

### The Path of Light (Spiritualism)
The dominant human folk religion — a relatively unstructured system worshiping a singular Creator based on beliefs brought by Men from their world of origin. Its practitioners include wise women, spirit doctors, and local clergy. Followers attribute humanity's passage to Talumh to the Creator's grace, a belief they hold sincerely. What they do not know — what no human knows — is that their divine power is supplied by **the Exiles**: a separate faction of banished spirits who pulled Men through the Void centuries ago for their own purposes. The Exiles are not allied with the Shadow Lords (they have competing goals), and they are not allies of the Lord and Lady either. They are a third force, quietly sustained by the Path of Light's prayers. Fountain is a Path of Light stronghold.

### The Lord and Lady
The two guardian spirits who maintain the covenant with the Tuthá and watch over Talumh. The Lord (Chaotic Neutral) is trickster and protector; the Lady (Chaotic Good) is fierce and nurturing. Their power is vast but they do not act directly in mortal affairs unless the Balance is threatened. They are aware of the Shadow Lords' activities but are constrained by the same cosmological rules that prevent the Shadow Lords from entering directly. If the Void seal breaks, everything changes.

### The Tuthá Courts
**Radiant Court** (Queen Glaréad): More open to Men; has advocated for coexistence since the Settlement. The Lady's court.
**Glimmering Court** (King Dwynnan): More suspicious of Men; bitter over the Great War. The Lord's court.

Both courts watch the Path of Man's rise with deep unease — and have been watching Priya the Wanderer's research for longer than the PCs realize. The Tuthá are allies in this adventure, but cautious ones: centuries of mutual antagonism with humanity means that trust is extended carefully and on Tuthá terms. The PCs will not be explained to, guided by hand, or rescued without cost. What the courts offer is intervention at critical moments, the occasional cryptic warning, and the knowledge — once earned — that they are not alone in this.

The Lord and Lady operate through the courts with deliberate indirection. Their involvement is deniable by design: they are constrained by the same cosmological rules that prevent the Shadow Lords from entering directly. They can push, warn, and intervene at moments of extremity. They cannot simply solve the problem.

### The Greensingers
A druidic sect devoted to Talumh's natural world. Their relationship with the Lord and Lady is independent; they serve the land itself. They have been tracking Path of Man activities in the Dune Sea with growing alarm and may surface as allies in Act III.

### The Shining Path
A monastic sect pursuing personal enlightenment through mastery of mind and body. No external divine patron — their power comes from internal discipline. Politically neutral, but genuinely opposed to both the Shadow Lords' manipulation and the Path of Man's ideology. Individual Shining Path practitioners may appear as unexpected allies or moral foils across the adventure.

## Calendar and Holidays

*The following calendar is proposed for Talumh — subject to revision during DM Guide development.*

The Talumh calendar runs on a 13-month year of 28 days each, plus a single uncounted day called the **Still Day** at the year's midpoint. The thirteen months follow the cycle of the thirteen great spirits who gave themselves to seal the world.

**Significant dates for this adventure:**
- **The Awakening** (first day of the first month): The new year; also the anniversary of the Lord and Lady's covenant with the Tuthá. A significant holy day for both Tuthá courts and the Path of Light; ignored by the Path of Man.
- **The Still Day** (midyear intercalary): A day with no date, traditionally a day of rest, silence, and reflection. The Path of Man considers it a day of preparation. Summoners believe the Void is closest to the world on this day — rituals have greater resonance and greater risk.
- **The Dimming** (last month): The anniversary of the War of the Spirits' end, when the great spirits sacrificed themselves to seal Talumh. A solemn day observed by all peoples. Void Magic is traditionally not practiced on this day. The Path of Man does not observe this prohibition.
