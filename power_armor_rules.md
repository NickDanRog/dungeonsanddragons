# Power Armor Rules for D&D 5e
The purpose of this document is to outline the rules for use of power armor platforms developed for the Mors Stellaris campaign module. This document consists of three sections. The first section is designed to outlined the combat rules for power armor platforms, which are deisnged to compliment the standard D&D 5e combat rules. The second section outlines the character options and contains details on a special character class, created for use with these platforms in a scifi setting. The third section outlines the various power armor platforms, weapons, and special equipment designed for use in the Mors Stellaris campaign. 

-----

## Section 1: Combat Rules
The standard D&D 5e combat rules form the base of the combat rules for power armor platforms, with additions and adjustments as outlined here. 

## Movement
- Power armor platforms move the same way as standard D&D characters
- In general, each platform has a standard (ground) speed, and a flying speed. 
- Some platforms have alternate movement profiles such as a burrowing speed, a climbing speed, etc. These alterate movement profiles will be listed for each individual platform (in section 3). 
- Due to a difference in scale from standard D&D rules, power armor units may stack infinately within the same square (see weapon ranges below)

### Flying 
- When flying, power armor platforms do not need to land at any point in their movement. 
- They must use 10 feet of flying movement to hover. 
- They may freely change altitude or land as part of their movement. 
- They may not use multiple forms of movemenet in a single turn, unless this is accomplished by a special maneuver (see section 2) or through special equipment.

## Making Attacks
- Power armor units make attacks with onboard weapons
- Character may make a number of attacks based on their character class (see section 2)
- A different weapon system must be used for each individual attack, unless the weapon system has a specific ability that allows it to be used multiple times in a turn. 
- Weapons are grouped into types - weapon types govern which attribute bonus is used for attacking with it, and characters may only add their proficiency bonus when they are proficient with a weapon they are attacking with. 
- Each weapon deals one or more standard 5e damage type (Piercing, bludegoning, fire, etc.)
- Range is more important than in standard D&D combat - all weapons have a maximum effective range, and some have a minimum range (i.e. they cannot be fired at targets at or closer than their minimum range) 
- Most weapons are direct fire - they use the same cover and line of sight rules as standard ranged combat. Indirect fire weapons are specifically noted and have special case rules (outlined below)

### Weapon Ranges 
- Power armor uses a larger square size --> 1 square = 10 meters
- Each weapon model (listen in section 3) will have a maximum effective range 
- There are three standard weapon ranges: short (0-5 squares), medium (6-15 squares), and long (16-30 squares) 
- There are two other weapon ranges used as well: close (only used for point-defense weapons - 0-3 squares), and extreme (anything beyond long range and within a unit's detection range - see detection special case rules)
- Firing at targets beyond short incurs a standard range penalty for each range increment  
- Melee weapons (see weapon types) may only be used on units within the same square 

### Weapon Types
- Artillery

#### Artillery Weapons
- Fire large projectiles at long ranges
- Has a minimum range 
- Can use special munitions
- Can fire indirectly 
- Uses the independent munitions rules for missiles and torpedoes, but uses your attack roll instead of that of the munition (see special case rules)
- Relevent Stat: INT - requires calculation to hit a target 
- Requires 1 turn to reload (as a free action) after firing 

#### Ballistic Weapons
- Fires fast streams of projectiles 
- Typically short range and high damage 
- Can fire multiple times in a round by default (if the character has extra attacks)
- Can knock targets prone (or out of the air)
- Relevent Stat: STR - requires the ability to compensate for recoil 

#### Barrage Weapons
- Takes the form of rocket launchers, mortars, or canister munitions 
- Saturates an area with fire
- Short to medium range  
- Has a minimum range
- Can be used for indirect fire 
- Can be equipped with special munitions
- Requires at least 1 turn to reload (as an action) 
- Relevant Stat: WIS - requires patience, judgement, and intuition 

#### Laser Weapons
- Fires a beam of directed energy 
- Doesn't require ammunition
- Short to medium range 
- Larger models require time to recharge (usually 1 turn as a free action)
- Relevent Stat: DEX - requires aiming and trigger discipline

#### Missile Weapons
- Fires a single independent, self-guided warhead 
- Multiple types of munitions 
- Long range weapon
- Can fire at targets outside of long range 
- Requires at least 1 turn to reload (as an action)
- Takes at least 1 round to hit a target 
- Makes an independent attack roll
- See independent munitions special case rules 
- Relevent Stat: n/a - device makes its own attack roll 

#### Melee Weapons
- Function as standard D&D melee weapons 
- Can take on multiple forms - all use the same proficency 
- Can only be used to attack adjacent targets
- Some specific models do additional damage types 
- Relevent Stat: STR - uses muscle power 

#### Particle Weapons
- Fires a stream of charged particles
- Larger scale weapons, uncommonly used with power armor 
- Medium to long range
- Doesn't require ammunition 
- Larger models have a minimum range 
- Can stun enemy power armor 
- Requires time to recharge - typically 2 rounds (as a free action)
- Relevant Stat: WIS - requires judgement and patience 

#### Point Defense Weapons
- Designed to be used to shoot down enemy munitions 
- Takes the form of rapid-fire machine guns or fast pulsing lasers
- Can also be used to attack at very short ranges
- See point defense fire special case rules
- Relevent Stat: INT - requires pinpoint accuracy and split second decision making

#### Railgun Weapons
- Accelerates munitions toward a target using magnetic rails
- Large but powerful 
- Some models are rapid fire (can be fired more than once in a round)
- Varies from short to long ranges
- Larger moduels can knock targets prone or out of the air 
- RElevent Stat: DEX - requires careful aim  

#### Torpedo Weapons
- Similar to missiles but fires a larger and slower moving independent warhead 
- Rarely mounted on power armor platforms
- Multiple munition types
- Very high damage, has a large area of effect 
- See independent munitions special case rules 
- Relevent Stat: n/a - device makes its own attack roll 

#### Unarmed Attacks
- Unarmed attacks are physical attacks made against a target 
- The target must be in the same square as the attacking unit
- Unarmed attacks deal a base 1d4 bludegoning damage, which can be further enhanced by specific techniques 

### Special Case Rules - Direct vs. Indirect Fire 
- When firing at a target that you can see within a weapon's range, use standard line of sight and cover rules
- If a weapon is of a type (or model) that allows indirect fire, you may fire at a target within range but must first make a detection roll 
- Detection rolls use a power armor unit's sensors to determine the location of a target - this is resolved as a standard Perception check  
- The base DC for a detection roll is 15 - add +2 for each range increment outside of short range 
- If you succeed on this detection roll, you may fire at the target as if you had line of sight
- When firing indirectly, you must still use standard cover rules, but you may treat full cover as three-quarters cover instead 

### Special Case Rules - Independent Munitions 
- Independent munitions include missile and torpedo weapon types 
- When fired, these weapons are propelled indepenently, arriving at the target suqare or hitting the target unit in subsequent rounds
- Missiles attack targets at medium or long range, and torpedoes targets at medium range, at the start of your next turn. 
- At the start of each of your subsequent turns, the munition advances to the next range increment 
- For munitions that fire into extreme range, an additional round is added after the first round the munition enters extreme range for every additional 30 squares
- Missiles and torpedoes that fire into extreme range have no effective range limitation, as long as the target can be detected by some method (see detection ranges special case rules below). 
- Each munition type (see entries in section 3) has a set attack bonus, which is used to make the attack roll 
- Independent munitions may strike specific squares instead of units, but only if they inflict AoE damage (see the weapon listings in section 3)
- If you are attacking a specific square, roll against a base AC of 12, adding +3 for every range increment outside of the first (calculated from the weapon's minimum range)
- If you miss this attack roll, the munition strikes an adjacent square instead (roll 1d8 to determine a random direction).
- If you are attacking a specific unit, roll against AC 10, adding the unit's DEX bonus, if any - again, add +3 for every range increment outside of the first.
- If you miss an attack against a specific unit, it strikes the square that unit is currently in, dealing damage if it has an AoE effect, or no damage if not
- Artillery weapons use these rules as well, with the following exceptions: these weapons strike targets (or squares) at long range in the first round, and targets at extreme range(s) in subsequent rounds, and they use the character's attack roll instead of one provided by the munition, along with standard range increment penalties. 

### Special Case Rules - Point-Defense Fire 
- Point-defense weapons can be used to negate attacks from weapons with a weapon type of artillery, barrage, missile, or torpedo that target units or squares within close range (0-3 squares) of you. 
- Point-defense fire uses a reaction instead of an action. 
- To target an attack with point defense, make an attack roll with your point-defense weapon. If this attack roll meets or exceeds the attack roll of the attack you targeted, the attack is negated.

## Damage Rules
- Each power armor platform has a base HP rating, representing the frame's onboard armor 
- Some platforms are also equipped with a powerful electromagnetic field to defelect or absorb damage, called shields 
- Shields have a total HP equal to their rating x10 (e.g. a platform with a shield rating of 1 would have 10 shield HP)
- When an attack hits the unit, deduct HP first from its shields (if any), and then its armor.  
- Some character classes allow units to add additional HP to armor and/or shields (see section 2). 
- Special equipment can also tune shields or reinforce armor to give it resistance against specific damage types (see special equipment in section 3). 

### Special Case Rules - Ballistic and Railgun Damage 
- When hit with an attack from a weapon with a weapon type of Ballistic or Railgun, the affected character must make a CON save to avoid being knocked prone
- The Base DC for this check is equal to 10, +1 for every 5 points of damage inflicted (rounded down)
- A unit that fails this roll is knocked prone 
- A flying unit that fails this roll looses 3 squares of altitude - on their next turn they may use their movement in an attempt to stabilize themselves by making a DEX save against the same DC - success means they are now stable, but may not make any further movement this round, failure means they loose another 3 squares of altitude. Units that reach alitude 0 are subject to standard falling damage.

## Character Options
- Characters designed for use with power armor rules use the special Pilot character class 
- Character design and leveling is the same as standard 5e, with exceptions listed here.
- Characters receive the standard ability score improvement or feats at the appropriate levels (not noted in the class details below)
- Equipment proficiencies have been added, which represent the ability to use different types of special equipment (see section 3)
- Character HP and proficiency rules remain the same
- A weapon or amrmor porficiency confers the ability to use the same type of non-power armor weapons or armor (e.g. if a character has proficiency in the laser weapon type, they may use personal scale laser weapons as well)
- in lieu of using subclasses, the Pilot character class receives a new proficiency or technique each time they reach a new level, allowing each character to fill a highly customizable role  
- Many techniques have pre-requisites 
- Many techniques mirror the class abilities of standard 5e character classes 

### Class Features 
- Hit Dice: 1d10
- Armor Proficiencies: Light Power Armor
- Weapon Proficiencies: Choose any three
- Equipment Proficiencies: Choose any two 
- Tool Proficiencies: Choose any one
- Skills: Choose any two 
- Saving Throws: Choose any two 

### Proficiencies 
- Proficiencies are passive class features that allow a character to use a differnt type of armor, weapon, special equipment, skill, or tool
- When selecting an armor proficiency, select a new type of armor for your character to become proficient in - note that the heavy armor proficiency requires a character to have proficiency in medium armor first 
- When selecting a weapon or equipment proficiency, add proficiency in a single weapon or equipment type
- When selecting a tool or skill proficiency, your character gains proficiency in two tools or skills of your choice - you may not mix and match between tools and skills this way (i.e. you must select two skills or two tools)

### Techniques 
- Techniques replace the class features of standard character classes. Many techniques mirror existing class features from standard 5e character classes.

*Defensive Techniques*

#### Armor Training 
- Add 1 to your base armor HP for every character level 

#### Advanced Armor Training 
- Pre-Requisites: Armor Training, CL 9th 
- Add 1 to your base armor HP for every character level for a total bonus of 2

#### Armor Specialization
- Pre-Requisites: Advanced Armor Training, CL 15th, Training in the armor type you select
- Select a specific armor model. When using this power armor, you gain an addition +15 to your base HP - this is in addition to the bonus you receive from advanced armor training  

#### Damage Reduction 
- Pre-Requisites: Focus technique, CL 6th, CON 13 
- When using the Focus technique, reduce damage from all incoming attacks by 3
- Damage reduction gained from this technique does not stack with that from other abilities or special equipment, unless specifically noted

#### Improved Damage Reduction
- Pre-Requisites: Damage Reduction technique, Focus technique, Proficiency in medium armor, CL 11th 
- When using the Focus technique, reduce damage from all incoming attacks by an additioanl 2 points
- This stacks with the damage reduction gained from the Damage Reduction technique 
- You must be equipped with medium or heavy power armor to use this technique 

#### Defensive Specialization 
- Your character gains proficiency in a single type of saving throw 
- You may choose this technique multiple times, gaining a new saving throw proficiency each time 

#### Evasive Action 
- Pre-Requisites: DEX 13
- When you use this technique as an action, you can attempt to dodge incoming attacks until your next turn
- If you are attacked when this technique is active, you can choose to make an attack roll, adding your DEX and proficiency bonuses to the roll - if your attack roll is higher than the attackers, you receive no damage from the attack
- When used, you may dodge a total number of attacks equal to your proficiency bonus 
- This technique may only be used with light or medium power armor - when used with medium power armor, you take a -2 penalty to your attack roll when attempting a dodge 

#### Improve Evasive Action 
- Pre-Requisites: Evasive Action technique, DEX 15
- Add a +2 bonus to your attack roll when attempting to dodge an attack when using evasive action 

#### Reactive Dodge 
- Pre-Requisites: Evasive Action technique, DEX 15
- Use your reaction to attempt to dodge 1 incoming attack. You may only use this technique once per round. 

#### Indomitable
- Pre-Requisites: CL 
- Mirrors the Indomitable ability (Fighter) 

#### Point-Defense Specialist 
- Pre-Requisites: proficiency with point-defense weaponry 
- You can make an attack with a point defense weapon or use the point-defense fire action as a bonus action instead of a standard action or a reaction 

#### Sensor Ghost
- Pre-Requisites: proficiency with ECM equipment, CL 3rd
- Use your action to create multiple sensor ghosts to fool enemy targeting scanners. 
- You create 1 sensor ghost for every 3 character levels after level 3 (e.g. 1 at level 3, 2 at level 6) to a maximum of 5 at level 15
- When you are hit with an attack, roll a die equal to the number of active sensor ghosts (use a d6 if you have 5, treating a 6 on the die as a roll again) - if you roll a 1, the attack hits you as normal. If you roll another number, reduce the number of active sensor ghosts by 1.
- This ability can be used a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus, and recharges after a short rest  
- Your power armor must be equiped with an ECM generator to use this technique 

#### Warding Flare 
- Mirrors Warding Flare ability (Cleric)

*Offensive Techniques*

#### Action Surge 
- Mirrors the Action Surge Ability (Fighter)

#### Extra Attack 
- Pre-Requisites: CL 5th 
- You may make an additional weapon or unarmed attack when using the attack action 
- You must use a different weapon system to make this second attack, unless the weapon system you're using has the rapid-fire special ability 

#### Improved Extra Attack 
- Pre-Requisites: Extra Attack technique, CL 11th
- As Extra Attack, but you may make a third attack when using the attack action. 
- The same restrictions apply as in the Extra Attack technique  

#### Focus
- You enter a highly focused state in combat, directing all your energy at removing your opponents from the battlefield 
- When you enter this state it presists for 1 minute 
- You may enter this state once per day, adding additional an additional use for every three character levels
- When in this state, you may not use any special equipment, or use any skills that require a WIS, INT, or CHA check 
- When Focused, you add a +1 bonus to all ranged attack and damage rolls, and and +2 bonus to all melee or unarmed attacks and damage - these bonuses increase by an additional +1 for every 6 characer levels

#### Unwavering Focus 
- Pre-Requisites: Focus Technique, CL 6th

#### Reckless Attack
- Mirrors Reckless Attack ability (Barbarian)

#### Syncronized Technique 
- Pre-Requisites: CL 12th, Proficiency with used weapon and equipment types
- When you use your action to activate a special equipment equipped on your power armor, you may also make a single weapon attack as part of this action. 

#### Weapon Specialization 
- Pre-Requisites: CL 6th, proficiency in selected weapon type 
- Select a specific model of weapon for a weapon type you have proficiency in 
- When attacking with that weapon, you recieve a +1 bonus to hit, and +2 to all damage rolls 
- You may select this technique multiple times, selecting a new weapon model each time 

#### Weapon Mastery 
- Pre-Requisites: CL 12th, weapon specialization in selected weapon type 
- Double the attack and damage bonuses you receive from weapon specialization for the selected weapon 
- You may select this technique multiple times, selecting a new weapon model each time 

#### Improved Critical 
- Pre-Requisites: Weapon specialization in the selected weapon
- Reduce the number needed from a critical hit on a specifc weapon model by 1
- You may select the technique multiple times, choosing a new weapon each time

#### Superior Critical 
- Pre-Requisites: Improved Critical technique for the selected weapon, CL 15th 
- Reduce the number needed from a critical hit on a specifc weapon model by 1
- You may select the technique multiple times, choosing a new weapon each time

### List of Character Classes
- Barbarian --> Vanguard: A close-combat specialist that can enter a state of hyper-focus, dealing additional damage and soaking up extra punishment  
- Cleric --> Engineer: A specialist trained to repair damage to friendly units in the chaos of combat and providing defensive bonuses to their team 
- Druid --> Operator: A specialist trained to command drones that give their squad an imporant edge on the battlefield 
- Fighter --> Soldier: A generalist skilled in many differnt types of weapons who knows how to coax the best performance out of their power armor  
- Monk --> Skirmisher: A specialist trained to be always on the move, approaching opponents from multiple angles in a constant barrage of fire and movement 
- Paladin --> Guardian: A defensive specialist who anchors the allied line and absorbs more punishment than other units while remaining operational 
- Ranger --> Tactician: A sepcialist trained to seize tactical advantage through clever use of local terrain and conditions    
- Rogue --> Scout: A maneuver specialist trained to attack behind enemy lines and use special stealth equipment
- Sorcerer --> Grenadier: A specialist who uses short range area of effect attacks to pound enemy positions 
- Warlock --> Sentinel: A specialist trained to spot for indirect fire, call in strikes from off field, and use advanced sensory equipmen 
- Wizard --> Technician: A specialist who uses long range attacks to keep the enemy off balance, and a wide range of special equipment to aid allies and hinder opponents
