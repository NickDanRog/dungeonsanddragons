# DARK GOSPEL
## Dungeon Master's Guide

*A D&D 5th Edition adventure for characters of levels 3–12*

---

# INTRODUCTION

## About This Adventure

**Dark Gospel** is a full-length campaign adventure set in the Kingdom of Artaxarta — an ancient mountain realm, one of the great successor states of the fallen Bandorian Empire, whose rugged terrain and warrior culture have defeated every conqueror for a thousand years. The adventure begins with the player characters drawn into royal service, charged with protecting a young prince and executing missions on behalf of a crown that is about to cease to exist. By the end, they will have fought their way back into a burning capital to face the man who burned it, knowing that even their victory will not be peace.

The dark secret at the heart of this adventure is not the cult. The cult is well understood — the Kingdom of Artaxarta has spent generations suppressing the worshipers of Umgul, whom they rightly regard as servants of an evil god. What no one understands is that the cult has nothing to do with Umgul, or with any recognized deity. The entity driving the incursion is the **No-God**: something older than the Twelve Divines, something that predates every theology in the known world, a nihilistic force with a single imperative — the destruction of all existence. It does not want power. It does not want worship. It forms pacts with mortals through the seductive coherence of its philosophy, and those pacts escalate until there is no exit. The kingdom's religious establishment has been persecuting the wrong threat for three hundred years, and the right one has been hiding in plain sight behind the misidentification ever since.

The primary instrument of the No-God's incursion is **Orestes Sarkhazi**, Lord of the Magisterium, a member of the royal council who has spent thirty years quietly building the cult's network throughout the kingdom's power structure. His story is not one of ambition or hatred — it began with intellectual curiosity, proceeded through a killing he believed to be righteous, and arrived at corruption through the slow escalation of a pact he did not fully understand when he accepted it. He is the adventure's most dangerous figure and its most tragic one. Whether he dies a monster or breaks the pact at the cost of everything he has left is one of several questions whose answer depends entirely on the choices your players make.

Player agency is the structural foundation of this adventure. What the party completes, what they neglect, what they say to a grieving child in a mountain fortress, and what they choose when there is no good choice — all of it accumulates into the shape of the ending they reach. Three distinct outcomes are possible. None of them is easy.

---

## How to Use This Guide

This book is organized as follows:

- **The Realm** covers everything you need to understand and portray Artaxarta, its people, its institutions, its history, and its secrets. Read this section thoroughly before running the adventure — the setting is the adventure's emotional foundation, and players must come to love it before it can hurt them.
- **Running the Adventure** covers all mechanical and narrative systems: milestone leveling, the Faction Relationship Tracker, the Corruption Mechanic, the Act II Mission System, and the Multiple Endings Framework. Read this section before Chapter 1 and keep it accessible throughout.
- **Chapters 1–10** present the adventure act by act. Each chapter begins with a summary and DM guidance, followed by scene-by-scene descriptions with read-aloud text, DM notes, encounter information, and skill check DCs.
- **Appendices A–D** contain all custom stat blocks, magic items, and random tables.

**Read-aloud text** is presented in blockquote format:

> *This text is meant to be read aloud or paraphrased to your players. It describes what characters see, hear, and experience — not what they conclude.*

**DM notes** appear in italics and address pacing, likely player divergences, and conditional outcomes. Where the text says *"if the party does X,"* treat that as conditional logic, not prescription.

## Adventure Synopsis

**Act I: The Black Flame (Chapters 1–3, Levels 3–6)**
The characters are called into royal service and dispatched south to investigate a force of unknown identity that has laid siege to the Brotherhood of Light's mountain fortress. The road provides the first evidence that something organized is moving through the uplands. They arrive to find the siege active and help the Brotherhood drive it back — and in the aftermath, discover that the cult they just fought bears no connection to Altair, the god it is supposed to serve. They have no time to make sense of this before a message arrives from the capital. They return to Alcidea on the night of the Jinsoona, the kingdom's most sacred festival, to find it already falling. King Telamon Aristedes Alcimidias II is dead. The city cannot be held. They extract through fire and chaos, find the prince on the road to Mazrok, and fight off pursuit through the mountain dark. Act I ends when they reach the Citadel.

**Act II: The Remnant Kingdom (Turns 1–12, Levels 6–9)**
Operating from the Citadel of Mazrok — a mountain fortress that has never fallen to siege — the characters become the instrument of a crown that exists on paper and in the loyalty of a grieving general. Act II is structured as a mission pool: twelve operations across four difficulty waves, revealed through an intelligence-gathering system that the party's own choices drive. Some missions will expire before they can be reached. The enemy has been inside the kingdom's institutions for thirty years, and every piece of intelligence that surfaces makes the situation look worse. Across these months, a name eventually emerges — Orestes Sarkhazi — and the investigation becomes personal. Simultaneously, the young prince they are protecting is showing signs that attentive players will recognize and inattentive ones will miss. Act II ends when all missions are resolved. The Sadars, who have been trickling toward Mazrok across the campaign, are finally present in sufficient numbers to call the Jurga.

**Act III: The Dark Gospel (Chapters 8–10, Levels 9–12)**
The Jurga convenes. The Sadars vote on the prince's coronation, their starting dispositions shaped entirely by what the party accomplished in Act II. The night after the vote, the cult attacks the encampment. The military outcome of that attack scales with the party's Act II record, but the result is the same regardless: the prince is taken to Alcidea, and the majority of his retainers are taken to a nearby cultist stronghold scheduled for execution by nightfall. The party faces an impossible choice — save their comrades or race to the prince — and that choice determines the corruption level they bring into the climax. The adventure ends in a multi-stage assault on Alcidea and a final confrontation with Orestes Sarkhazi, whose arc concludes based on the relationship the party built or failed to build across the entire campaign.

## Themes and Tone

**Dark Gospel** is a dark fantasy investigation with political and philosophical dimensions. The adventure's central threat is not a monster or an army — it is an idea. The No-God does not manifest physically in this campaign; it operates through the coherence of its argument. Its tenets are genuinely seductive to people in sufficient pain, and it finds those people systematically. This is the design: the cult expands not through fear or force but through philosophy, reaching individuals at their most vulnerable and offering them a framework that, for a time, genuinely helps them make sense of their suffering. The horror is the escalation that follows, and the discovery that by the time the escalation becomes visible, it is already too late to exit.

Aim for weight and moral complexity throughout. The Brotherhood of Light has spent generations fighting the wrong enemy with sincere dedication — they are not corrupt, and their error is understandable. Orestes Sarkhazi was a man before the pact made him what he is, and echoes of that man remain. The prince is a child carrying grief he cannot articulate, and his vulnerability is not weakness — it is the entirely predictable result of what has happened to him. The Sadars are not obstacles; they are people with legitimate concerns about committing their followers to a cause led by an eight-year-old boy in a mountain fortress.

The setting should feel like a real place that people love before it is taken from them. The Artaxartoi are proud, competent, and fiercely loyal — qualities the cult has learned to weaponize. Every institution the party encounters should feel like something worth saving before it is threatened.

## Adjusting for Your Party

**Dark Gospel** is designed for a party of four to five characters. For other party sizes:

- **Three characters:** Reduce enemy hit points by 25% and lower encounter DCs by 1–2. The night attack in Chapter 9 should be run with more emphasis on escape and rescue than on holding the perimeter. The Act II mission system does not require adjustment, but consider alerting the party more visibly when missions are at high expiration risk.
- **Six characters:** Add hit points to named antagonist encounters throughout. The cultist assault in Chapter 9 should include a second assault wave after the first is repelled. The final confrontation with Orestes Sarkhazi should include at least two elite cult guards as persistent combatants alongside him.

**If the party rushes Act I:** The road to the Brotherhood's fortress contains encounter hooks that can be skipped without breaking the story, but Chapter 2 (The Siege) should not be shortened — the evidence discovered in the siege's aftermath is the party's first window into the No-God's true nature, and it needs space to register. If the party clears the siege unusually fast, give the Brotherhood's surviving adepts time to speak.

**If the party stalls in Act II:** The mission expiration system creates organic pressure. If a party is indecisive about mission selection, allow the expiration rolls to run normally — missions will begin to close, which is the intended consequence of delay. Do not rescue parties from their own strategic choices. The system is designed to produce failure; failure is part of what this adventure is about.

**If players engage deeply with the prince:** This is the intended experience. Eurimedos rewards attention. His conversations with the party, his questions about the deaths they have witnessed, his slowly developing understanding of the situation he is in — all of these have mechanical consequences through the Corruption Tracker, but they are also simply good drama. Give him space.

**If players disengage from the prince:** Do not force the interaction. The Corruption Tracker will reflect the neglect, and the available endings will narrow accordingly. Ending 3 is not a punishment — it is a legitimate outcome that follows naturally from a particular kind of play.

# THE REALM

## The Kingdom of Artaxarta

Artaxarta is one of the oldest and most formidable states in the known world — a mountain kingdom whose network of high river valleys has fed civilizations for millennia, defeated conquerors for just as long, and produced a people who regard competence as a moral virtue and failure as a wound that heals slowly. The Bandorian Empire took twenty years and a catastrophic portion of its military strength to conquer it, and even then the conquest required treachery as much as force. No one else has managed even that.

The kingdom occupies a complex of mountain ranges, river valleys, and high upland plateaus that sprawl across the former Empire's eastern borderlands. To the east, the Yashim Mountains form a wall so severe that they constitute less a border than an endpoint — the world simply stops there, and the narrow passes through the peaks lead to near-uninhabited terrain before eventually reaching the Kelish tribal territories, with whom Artaxarta has essentially no contact. The kingdom's other borders are managed affairs with real diplomatic weight: Kyrexos to the northwest is the most significant military concern; Morna to the west is a simmering nuisance with territorial ambitions the Artaxartoi regard as delusional; Kesh to the north is a trading partner the Artaxartoi find culturally baffling but economically useful. Kentana to the south is secretive and wealthy from its gem trade. Asta to the southwest is a religious irritant — its people worship Altair, whom the Artaxartoi equate with their ancient enemy Umgul, and the near-impassable mountain border between them has never done anything to warm the relationship.

The culture that emerged from millennia of isolation and then centuries of Xoshan and Bandorian influence prizes three things above sentiment: merit, martial excellence, and loyalty. These are not abstract virtues — they are enforced by a social system that rewards demonstrated competence and punishes failure publicly. The aristocracy exists, but its members are expected to outperform commoners at their own roles or face the social consequences of falling short. The Artaxartoi find most foreign cultures bewilderingly slack about this.

### Physical Features

**The Yashim Mountains** form the eastern border of the kingdom and define its character. These are not hills or even ordinary mountains — they are an extreme alpine environment, with passes that close for months each year and peaks that have never been climbed by recorded history. The kingdom's most famous defensive advantage flows from the Yashim: every invasion force that has ever attempted to enter from the east has either turned back or died in the attempt. The Bandorian Empire, which came from the west through Durasi-controlled corridors, remains the only power that has successfully invaded, and even they needed twenty years.

**The Twelve River Valleys** are the kingdom's economic and agricultural heart. Flowing outward from the high Yashim peaks, these river systems carve through the mountain terrain in descending networks that create hundreds of subsidiary valleys at various elevations. The Artaxartoi have developed a sophisticated system of terraced agriculture over centuries of Xoshan and Imperial engineering, and the result is a kingdom that produces significantly more food than its apparent terrain should support. The valleys vary considerably: the lower, northern valleys are warm, wide, and densely settled; the higher, more eastern valleys are narrow, colder, and predominantly given over to herding.

**The Five Cities** are Artaxarta's urban centers, all situated in the more accessible northern region where the river valleys open into broader terrain. Originally established as Xoshan military colonies — rigid, planned, laid out in the Xoshan grid style — they have long since accumulated centuries of organic growth atop their original bones. Alcidea is the largest and the capital; the other four range from significant regional centers to cities that exist primarily because of their position along major trade routes. All five have a characteristic quality: the old Xoshan skeleton is still visible in the straightness of the central avenues and the regularity of the civic buildings near the original forum, while everything built since sprawls in the irregular, human-scale pattern of a city that grew by need rather than plan.

**The High Uplands** are the pastoral highlands above the valley floors — a world apart from the cities and farming villages below. The people who live here are descended from the original pre-Xoshan tribes, and while they have nominally adopted the religion and political structures of the larger kingdom, their day-to-day lives look more like their ancient ancestors than like their urban countrymen. They herd the large warhorses for which Artaxarta is famous, practice a form of Ilura's worship that includes animal sacrifice and ancient rites absent from urban temples, and regard lowlanders with a combination of polite contempt and genuine cultural pride. The Zavra-Prag draw primarily from these communities; the Sadars are their leaders.

**The Citadel of Mazrok** rises from a cliff face directly above Alcidea, 600 feet overhead, accessible only via a single massive staircase cut into the rock and sealed at the top by a gate that can only be opened from within. It contains its own aquifer, years of stored provisions, and defensive positions that have defeated every assault in its history — the single exception being a famous mountaineering raid during the Bandorian siege, which succeeded only because a gifted group of climbers scaled the cliff face at night and forced the gate from the inside. The Citadel is where Act II of this adventure takes place and where the remnant crown makes its base.

### Regions

**The Northern Valleys and the Five Cities** form the kingdom's political and commercial core. The terrain here is the most accessible in the kingdom — wide valley floors, reliable roads, dense settlement. The culture here is the most cosmopolitan in the kingdom, shaped by centuries of Xoshan colonial influence and subsequent Bandorian administration. In the adventure's present, the fall of Alcidea has severed the administrative web that connected this region; the other four cities are either under cult control or scrambling to establish independent authority in the power vacuum.

**The Central Valley Network** is the agricultural heartland — hundreds of farming villages spread across the middle elevation valleys, supported by the terraced farming system that makes Artaxarta a major food exporter. The cult has penetrated this region primarily through civic infiltration rather than military force — corrupting local magistrates, replacing community leaders with sympathizers, quietly strangling the loyalist networks that might otherwise organize resistance. Civic missions in Act II draw heavily from this region.

**The Southern Highlands** are wilder and less settled than the central region — higher elevation, thinner soil, communities that exist primarily to support the herding economy and the Brotherhood of Light's operations. The Brotherhood's fortress is built into the southern mountain terrain, isolated enough to operate outside ordinary royal oversight but close enough to the populated valleys to deploy effectively. This region is the setting for Act I's first two chapters.

**The High Uplands** stretch across the elevated plateaus above the valley networks — a vast, cold landscape of seasonal pastures, small fortified clan settlements, and ancient sacred sites that predate the Xoshan colonization. The Sadars govern here through the authority of tradition and personality. The Zavra-Prag are called from these communities when the king requires military force. The cult has been slower to penetrate the uplands than the cities — the tight-knit clan structure resists outside infiltration more effectively than urban institutions — but several Sadars have been targeted, and at least one has been compromised.

**The Western Marches** are the borderlands between Artaxarta and its western neighbors — Kyrexos to the north, Morna to the south. The Mornai territorial claim on portions of this region makes it a recurring diplomatic irritant; the Kyrexan threat makes it a military concern. Whether Kyrexos sees the fall of Alcidea as an opportunity to press its claims is one of the background diplomatic pressures that Act II's military missions are partly managing.

### The Crisis (DM Only)

The cult's infiltration of Artaxartoi institutions was not a rapid operation. Orestes Sarkhazi spent thirty years building it, and the result is a penetration that is deep, patient, and precisely targeted at the systems that would otherwise detect and respond to it. The Kryptea — the kingdom's intelligence service — was the most dangerous obstacle, and it was eliminated first: over the decade before the Jinsoona, Orestes identified and either turned or marked every Kryptea operative he could find. By the time the strikes happened on the festival night, the service was compromised to its core. Delana Geminia Sabinus survived only because her cover as a lady's maid kept her off the operational rolls that Orestes had accessed.

The cult's outer layer — the rank and file who light the fires, guard the ritual sites, and make up the siege force in Act I — genuinely believe they are serving Umgul, the dark god of ancient Artaxartoi tradition. Most of them found their way to the cult through genuine suffering and the genuine comfort of the No-God's philosophy in its early, seductive form. They do not know what they are actually serving. The inner circle — those who have made direct pacts or been elevated to Orestes's confidence — know exactly what they serve and what the pact requires. These are the most dangerous, and the most tragic.

## Alcidea — The Capital

Alcidea is built at the foot of the cliff that holds Mazrok, spread across the broad mouth of the central valley in the grid pattern of a Xoshan military colony that has been accumulating centuries of organic growth. The straight central avenues and the rectangular civic forum near the original Xoshan heart are still visible from any elevated vantage point; the neighborhoods that grew up around them follow the natural logic of a city that built outward by habit and necessity. The effect is a city that is simultaneously ordered and alive — legible from a distance, labyrinthine on the ground.

The population before the Jinsoona disaster was perhaps eighty thousand, a number that included a significant Durasi-descended community whose ancestors were the Imperial garrison absorbed into the kingdom three generations ago. They constitute their own tribal designation and still favor Durasi-style names while being otherwise culturally indistinguishable from their neighbors. The city is also home to a substantial merchant class, the professional legionary families stationed at the barracks, the Magisterium's scholarly community, and the artisan guilds that service all of them.

Since the fall, Alcidea is under cult control. The streets are patrolled by cultist forces. The institutions have been decapitated, occupied, or co-opted. The royal palace is the cult's operational headquarters. The library — and the vault beneath it — is sealed and guarded. What was a living city is now a demonstration: this is what the No-God's incursion looks like when it wins a round.

### Key Locations

**The Royal Palace** occupies the northern quarter of the city, built into the base of the cliff immediately below the Mazrok staircase. In its original form it was the Xoshan military governor's residence; additions over centuries of Artaxartoi kingship have wrapped it in wings and courtyards that sprawl east and west from the original structure. The great hall where King Telamon received petitions is the largest interior space in the city. The palace is now Orestes Sarkhazi's headquarters and the seat of the cult's administration of the occupied capital.

**The Royal Library** stands adjacent to the palace — connected by a covered colonnade — and is the largest repository of knowledge in the kingdom. Orestes worked here as a young man. The vault his mentor showed him still exists, accessible through a passage in the restricted archives. The vault contains the original Umgul texts that began the entire chain of events, along with additional materials accumulated over the decades of Orestes's tenure as Lord of the Magisterium. It is now guarded. It is also one of the most important locations in the adventure's climax.

*DM note: The vault contains at minimum: the original Umgul texts; a thirty-year record of the cult's internal communications in Orestes's hand; and a set of ritual materials for No-God pact ceremonies. If the party reaches it, this is the most damning evidence available — and potentially the thing Orestes would most want to prevent them from reaching. Destroying it is one of the conditions for Ending 1.*

**The Temple of Ilura** is the city's largest religious structure and the most emotionally significant building in the capital to most of its citizens. Built in the Artaxartoi style rather than the Xoshan — rougher stone, native iconography, mounted statues of the goddess in her warrior aspect — it occupies a full city block near the original forum. The head priest and most of the temple's senior clergy were killed on the Jinsoona night. A skeleton staff remains, some of whom are cult sympathizers and some of whom are simply terrified. During Act III, the temple is a potential rally point for civilian resistance and a location with significant symbolic weight for the final assault.

**The Magisterium** is housed in a converted Xoshan administrative building three blocks from the library — a long, colonnaded structure whose scholars and scientists constituted Artaxarta's intellectual elite. Most were not told what their leader was. Several have been killed; others have been pressed into service by the occupation. A few may be reachable as sources of intelligence or assistance during Act III.

**The Legionary Barracks** occupy the eastern quarter of the city, a walled compound large enough to house two full legions with their auxiliary support. The barracks fell quickly on the festival night. The compound is now a prison and a cult military staging area.

**The Agora** is the city's primary market, built along both banks of the stream that runs through the center of Alcidea. Before the fall it was the loudest and most cosmopolitan space in the kingdom. It is quieter now — the foreign merchants are gone, many of the local vendors have been replaced by cult operatives using commercial cover, and the remaining citizens shop quickly and speak quietly.

**The Cliff Stairs** rise from a fortified gate at the palace's northern edge, cut directly into the cliff face, ascending 600 feet to the Citadel of Mazrok. The staircase is the only route between the city and the Citadel; it is defensible at multiple points, and the gate at the top can only be opened from inside the Citadel. The stairs are where the party's extraction occurs at the end of Chapter 3, and they are one of the primary tactical considerations in the Act III assault on the capital.

## The Power Structure

### The Crown

The kingdom is an absolute monarchy. The king rules through a council of fifteen ministers who manage the practical business of government, each heading a ministry with its own bureaucratic organization. Ministers serve at the king's pleasure. The Artaxartoi model of government is notably lean — form follows function, organizations are smaller than their counterparts elsewhere, and performance is evaluated against results rather than ceremony.

**King Telamon Aristedes Alcimidias II** was the reigning monarch when the adventure begins. He was killed on the Jinsoona night. He was considered a capable administrator rather than a great military figure, which made his close friendship with General Heskalo — who is the latter emphatically — one of the court's defining partnerships. He appointed Orestes Sarkhazi as Lord of the Magisterium based on the genuine quality of Orestes's scholarship, which was, by any objective measure, extraordinary. His son Eurimedos was the center of his hopes for the kingdom's future; the boy's intensity and intelligence clearly delighted him, and he believed the child would become a great king.

### The Dark Secret (DM Only)

The Kryptea was not just penetrated — it was rebuilt around Orestes. Over the course of a decade, he identified operative after operative through careful cultivation of court relationships and access to records his ministerial position gave him. Those he identified were either turned or marked for elimination on the Jinsoona night. The list the cult operated from on that night was Orestes's list. Delana Geminia Sabinus is the only known survivor because she was deep-cover: her operational file showed her as a lady's maid, and her actual status as an active Kryptea officer was known only to her handler, who is dead.

The extent of cult penetration across the other ministries is not fully known even to Orestes. He built a network of networks, each isolated from the others. This means the party will not be able to fully map the infiltration even with complete access to Orestes's records — he does not know everything his network has done, by design. The question of what pieces of the Artaxartoi government are salvageable is one that will outlast this campaign.

## The Brotherhood of Light

### Public Face

The Brotherhood of Light is one of the most respected institutions in the kingdom — a holy order of fighting monks devoted to Ilura whose reputation for martial excellence and personal integrity has endured for generations. Their 250 active adepts are recruited exclusively from male orphans donated to the temple as infants; they survive a series of trials of extraordinary difficulty to earn full membership, and even then must wait for a slot to open before joining the active rolls. The survivors of this process are among the most formidable individual combatants in the known world.

The Brotherhood operates independently of the crown's chain of command. They answer to their own hierarchy and ultimately to the High Priest of the Temple, not to the king. Their five Knight Commanders govern the order by seniority, and all must consent before an aspirant joins the active rolls. The Brotherhood's fortress in the southern highlands is a significant military installation — designed to be defended by a small number of highly capable fighters, provisioned for long sieges, and positioned to deploy quickly into the surrounding region.

The Brotherhood has suppressed Umgul cult activity in the kingdom for centuries and considers itself the institutional guardian of the faith against its oldest enemy.

### The Dark Truth (DM Only)

The Brotherhood's account of the Umgul cult is wrong in important ways, and they do not know it. Their historical records document centuries of suppressing what they believe to be worshipers of a dark aspect of the native Artaxartoi religion — the entity called Umgul, identified by Xoshan missionaries with the Twelve Divines' dark aspect Altair. This identification was made three hundred years ago by scholars who had good reason to equate what they observed with what they already understood, and it was never seriously revisited.

The No-God is not Umgul. It is not Altair. It is not any deity the Brotherhood has a theology for. Marcus Ivor, the Brotherhood paladin the party can recruit in Act I, is the member of the order most likely to eventually understand this — his prior history in the city of Vista exposed him to enough unusual theology to recognize when a theological category is simply wrong. But even he will need evidence before he will say so clearly.

The Brotherhood's incorrect account of what they are fighting is one of the adventure's central ironies: the institution most dedicated to stopping the No-God's incursion is also the one most completely wrong about what the incursion is.

## Key Factions

### The Crown (Prince Eurimedos / General Heskalo)

The nominal authority the party serves. In practice: a grieving eight-year-old with a gift for military theory and no political capital, and a combat veteran in his late fifties who is trying to hold himself together by force of professional habit. Their legitimacy is genuine — the prince is the rightful heir, Heskalo is the ranking military officer, and the legal apparatus of the kingdom recognizes them. Their practical power is precisely what the party can build for them.

**What they want:** Survival, first. Legitimacy, second. The army they need to retake the capital, third. All three require the party.

### The Brotherhood of Light

Badly damaged by the Act I siege but not destroyed. Perhaps 180 of the order's 250 adepts survived; the fortress held, but the assault took a toll. Their knowledge of Umgul cult history is the party's best early window into the No-God's nature — flawed knowledge, but better than nothing. Their five Knight Commanders are divided on the question of committing fully to the prince's cause versus maintaining the order's traditional independence.

**What they want:** To understand what they actually fought in Act I, and to respond appropriately. Marcus Ivor's presence with the party is the most direct link between the Brotherhood's leadership and the truth.

### The Kryptea

Functionally destroyed. Delana Geminia Sabinus is the thread from which any rebuilt intelligence capacity must grow. She has some contact with former Kryptea assets who are either in hiding or operating autonomously, but the network's coherence is gone. Rebuilding it is the narrative function of the Act II Intelligence bonus.

**What they want:** Delana wants two things: to rebuild the Kryptea, and to understand the full scope of how thoroughly it was penetrated. The second may be more painful than the first.

### The Legions

Seven professional legions, descended from the Bandorian Imperial forces absorbed into the kingdom three generations ago. Their loyalty flows to whoever can credibly claim legitimate authority. The fall of Alcidea scattered the legionary command structure; individual commanders are either dead, captured, under cult coercion, or independently trying to hold their formations together. Consolidating surviving legionary officers around the prince's authority is a background task of Act II with significant payoff in Act III.

**What they want:** Clear legitimate authority to follow and a credible plan for retaking the kingdom.

### The Zavra-Prag and the Sadars

The tribal militia and their leaders. The Zavra-Prag constitute the bulk of Artaxarta's fighting force by numbers; the Sadars hold their loyalty. Neither is automatically available to the prince's cause — the Sadars require real reasons to commit their clans to a fight led by an eight-year-old boy operating from a mountain fortress. The Jurga in Act III is the formal mechanism for winning or losing their support; Act II's missions build the foundation.

**What they want:** Protection for their people, honest information about what is happening, and leadership they can respect.

### Arslan Zedan's Warband

A young tribal chieftain of considerable cavalry reputation and a border-on-villainous personal history, depending on who is telling the story. He brings approximately 300 highly capable mounted warriors if recruited, along with tactical creativity that has repeatedly surprised commanders who thought they knew what cavalry could do. His motivation is personal — the cult killed people he loved — and his commitment would be genuine.

**What they want:** Access to the cult. Everything else is negotiable.

### The No-God's Cult

The primary adversary. Not a religious movement in any traditional sense — a three-decade infiltration operation that now controls the capital and significant portions of the kingdom's institutional infrastructure. The cult is organized in isolated cells; the outer layer believes it is serving Umgul; the inner circle knows it is serving the No-God and understands what that means.

**What they want:** The destruction of the royal line, the elimination of the Brotherhood of Light as an organized institution, and the use of Artaxarta as a staging ground for the No-God's broader agenda. The prince is not a target for assassination — he is a target for conversion.

## Key NPCs

### Prince Eurimedos Toxamones Alicimidia
*8 years old. The crown's heir. The party's primary charge.*

**Appearance:** Small even for his age, with his father's dark coloring and a quality of focused attention in his face that makes him look older than he is until something catches him off-guard and he looks exactly eight. He dresses in whatever he is given; he has no strong feelings about clothes. He almost always has a book nearby.

**Personality:** Eurimedos is the product of a childhood spent more in libraries than in training yards, and it shows. He is intellectually voracious — specifically obsessed with military history and tactical theory, a fixation his tutors found embarrassing and his father found promising — and he applies this knowledge in conversation with an accuracy that startles adults who have forgotten they are talking to a child. He is not socially confident; genuine warmth from adults is something he approaches with caution, as though unsure whether it is real. With people who prove consistent — Marcus Geminius especially, the party if they put in the time — he opens fully and the intelligence underneath becomes even more apparent.

**Role in the adventure:** He is the party's responsibility and their connection to legitimate royal authority. He is also — unknown to him, and ideally for some time unknown to the party — the No-God's identified candidate for the next pact. His grief at his father's death, his intellectual hunger, and his isolation from peers make him susceptible in ways the cult is working to exploit. Whether he arrives at the campaign's end as an unbroken boy who will be a great king, or as a child who has already been reached, depends on choices the party makes across the entire campaign.

**Secret:** The cult is not trying to kill him. They are trying to reach him. Orestes Sarkhazi has been assigned his conversion personally, and the prince's time in custody during Act III is spent in careful philosophical conversation rather than in a cell.

**Corruption Tracker:** Eurimedos begins the adventure at Corruption 0. See the Corruption Mechanic in Running the Adventure for the full system.

### General Tanos Mitradanes Heskalo — *The Laughing Lion*
*Late 50s. Commanding General of the Royal Guard.*

**Appearance:** Enormous frame — genuinely large, in the way of men who were built that way and then spent forty years making it worse through military exercise. A great blonde beard shot through with gray, kept neat by habit rather than vanity. Battlefield scars on both hands and the left side of his neck, covered by his collar in formal settings. The name came from his battlefield habit of laughing in situations that make other men pale — it still happens, but since the king's death the laugh doesn't always reach his eyes.

**Personality:** The Laughing Lion is the most straightforward person in the party's orbit: competent, honest, loyal, and operating on a combination of professional discipline and personal grief that is becoming harder to maintain as the campaign progresses. He has been the king's closest friend for thirty years. He has buried that friend without a proper ceremony, in the chaos of an extraction, and he has not had time to stop since. The grief surfaces in erratic moments — excessive force in a training session, an unusual silence in a council meeting, occasionally something that was almost an order to do something tactically inadvisable. The party may need to protect him from his own worst impulses as much as from external threats.

**Role in the adventure:** The party's commanding officer and the senior military authority in the prince's circle. Fully trustworthy and experienced, but increasingly erratic.

**Secret:** He suspects, at a level he has not yet consciously acknowledged, that someone on the royal council was responsible for the Jinsoona attacks. He does not yet have a name. When he gets one, his response will be significant.

### Delana Geminia Sabinus
*Junior Kryptea officer. Sole known survivor of the service's destruction.*

**Appearance:** Deliberately unremarkable — medium height, features that are pleasant without being memorable, the kind of face that does not stick in other people's recollection. This is partly natural and partly practiced; she has spent years cultivating the specific quality of not being noticed. She carries herself with the controlled ease of someone who is always reading the room.

**Personality:** Meticulous, patient, and intensely loyal to the people she has decided to trust — which is a short list developed slowly and defended fiercely. She is not warm on first meeting and does not pretend to be. She is also carrying the full weight of watching an institution she dedicated her career to be dismantled overnight by an enemy who was inside it for years, and she has not had a moment to process that because the work is continuous and her replacement is not coming. The party will learn this gradually, if they pay attention.

**Role in the adventure:** The party's intelligence resource. Everything they learn about the cult's infiltration of the kingdom passes through or is organized by Delana. Rebuilding the Kryptea is her project; the party's missions generate the intelligence that makes it possible.

**Secret:** She knows the Kryptea was penetrated far more deeply than anyone has officially acknowledged, and she has a working theory about how it was done. She has not shared this theory because it implicates someone the General trusts. She is waiting until she has evidence rather than inference.

### Kaske Heskalo
*Cavalry officer. Daughter of the General.*

**Appearance:** Built like her father but leaner, with the particular physical confidence of someone who spends most of her time on horseback. Her mother's coloring — darker than the General — and an expression that defaults to assessment. She is almost never still.

**Personality:** Decisive and capable, with the specific self-possession of someone who has had to prove herself in systems that were not inclined to give her credit. She earned her Kataphractoi slot after being relegated to the auxiliary corps; she then won the tournament to qualify for the active rolls. She has done everything twice over and stopped being surprised by it. Her relationship with her father is close and complicated by military hierarchy — she loves him and respects his capability, and she sometimes finds herself acting as his de facto second-in-command at moments when he is not quite himself.

**Role in the adventure:** Capable cavalry officer and one of the party's most reliable tactical assets. One of two NPCs who escape the Act III night attack and remains with the party through the climax.

### Marcus Geminius Sabinus
*Former legionary officer. Delana's twin brother.*

**Appearance:** Quiet where his sister is watchful — they share the same features, but his carry a different quality, more inward. Built for a legionary: solid, economical in movement. He has the manner of someone who has learned to take up less space than he is actually occupying.

**Personality:** Contemplative and deeply loyal to the people who have earned his trust. His bond with the prince is genuine on both sides — they have spent long hours together discussing military history and tactical theory — and it has given Marcus a quiet influence over Eurimedos that no one else quite matches. He does not speak about why he left the legions. Whatever it was, it cost him something he has not gotten back.

**Role in the adventure:** Companion to the prince; bridge between the prince's world and the party's. His capture in Act III is the loss the prince will feel most directly, and his execution — in the Option B scenario where the party races to Alcidea first — is the permanent cost of that choice.

### Fleur Dontillion
*Former mercenary. One-eyed. The only foreigner in the royal guard.*

**Appearance:** From the Kingdom of Murundus, far to the west — her features mark her as distinctly foreign in Artaxarta. The missing eye is her left; she wears nothing over it. The scar that caused the loss extends from her temple to her cheekbone. She is not small, and she carries herself as though she expects to need the space.

**Personality:** Fleur does not explain herself and does not invite inquiry. Her skills are well beyond what they need to be for a royal guard position, which is the question nobody asks directly because nobody wants to hear the answer without context. She is not unfriendly — she is simply economical about what she shares. The party is likely to spend some time around her before they learn anything about her past, and when they do learn something, it will raise more questions than it answers.

**Role in the adventure:** Combat capability and the particular value of an outside perspective — she sees Artaxartoi institutions without the assumptions of someone raised inside them. Her capture in Act III adds a personal dimension to the rescue decision for any party that has spent time with her.

### Abala Kadar
*Son of a tribal chieftain. The prince's personal bodyguard.*

**Appearance:** Young — early twenties — and built for his single purpose: large, physically capable, trained from childhood in close protection. Upland coloring: darker skin than the city-born Artaxartoi, with the characteristic mountain tribesman's broad build. He carries himself with the alert economy of someone who is always tracking the room.

**Personality:** Abala has no ambiguity about his purpose and no interest in acquiring any. He was raised for this role; he is good at it; he considers the protection of the prince to be the correct use of his existence. This is his greatest strength and, in the right circumstances, a vulnerability — someone with sufficient leverage over the prince could potentially use that leverage on Abala, because his devotion is to the person rather than to any abstract principle of service.

**Role in the adventure:** The prince's shadow. His capture in Act III removes the prince's most dedicated close-range protection at exactly the moment the prince needs it most.

### Marcus Ivor
*Paladin of the Order of Light. Former Knight of Vista.*

**Appearance:** A foreigner — Vista is a city-state far to the west with a distinctive cultural heritage, and Marcus carries it in his features, his accent (present but controlled), and the particular way he holds himself in religious spaces, which is slightly different from the Artaxartoi norm. He is in his forties, with the physical conditioning of a man who has maintained a demanding regimen for decades. His armor, when he wears it, is the Brotherhood's white-and-gold.

**Personality:** Marcus Ivor is the most theologically flexible person in the party's orbit, and the most knowledgeable about what the party is actually facing — though it takes time for his knowledge to reach its own accurate conclusions. His faith is genuine. His ability to update his theological understanding in response to new evidence is the quality that makes him uniquely valuable in this adventure.

**Role in the adventure:** The party's window into the Brotherhood's knowledge of the Umgul cult's history, and eventually the person most capable of articulating what the No-God actually is when the evidence accumulates.

**Secret:** His transition from Vista's Knights to the Brotherhood followed from something he witnessed in Vista that his previous faith could not account for. He has never found an adequate theological category for what he saw. The No-God, when it finally becomes visible, fits that category.

### Arslan Zedan
*Young tribal chieftain. Cavalry tactician.*

**Appearance:** Mid-twenties. The upland build — broad, physically capable — combined with the manner of someone who is used to being underestimated and has stopped minding because it keeps being useful. He is not physically remarkable in any single dimension; his reputation precedes him, which means the first meeting is usually a surprise in some direction.

**Personality:** Intelligent, tactically creative, and operating on a genuine desire for revenge that he makes no effort to disguise. He is not reckless — his reputation for unconventional tactics reflects genuine strategic creativity rather than mere aggression — but his motivation is personal in a way that makes him a complicated ally. He will be useful, committed, and occasionally difficult to manage when his personal objectives and the campaign's operational objectives diverge.

**Role in the adventure:** Available for recruitment via a Military mission in Act II. He brings approximately 300 cavalry and tactical creativity that is a genuine asset in the kind of mobile warfare Act II and Act III require.

**Secret:** His "villainous" reputation among several Sadars is based on real events; what those Sadars omit from their account is the context that makes his actions understandable, if not unambiguous. Whether the party investigates and what they do with what they find has diplomatic consequences at the Jurga.

### Orestes Sarkhazi
*Lord of the Magisterium. Priest of the No-God. Primary antagonist.*

**Appearance:** Late sixties, with the appearance of a man who has spent forty years at a desk — not frail, but not physically imposing either. His hands are notable: long-fingered, ink-stained even now, the hands of someone who has written constantly for his entire adult life. His eyes carry a quality of full attention that students and colleagues always described as flattering and that, at close range, reads as something else entirely. He dresses as a scholar even in the occupied palace.

**Personality:** Orestes is the product of a mind that was genuinely exceptional before the pact and has become something more and worse since. He is patient, precise, and intellectually honest in a way that is disturbing — when he tells you something is true, it usually is, even when that truth is being used as a weapon. He does not hate. He does not feel contempt for the people he has destroyed. He has made his peace with what he is, and that peace is perhaps the most unsettling thing about him. He can still feel the shape of the person he used to be — the young man who killed his mentor in an act of genuine piety, who spent years in the library reaching for something he could not name. Whether that person can be reached depends on whether the party gives it a chance.

**Role in the adventure:** The primary antagonist and the final confrontation. He is the architect of everything that has happened to the kingdom, and how that confrontation resolves determines which ending is available.

**Secret:** He knows that breaking the pact will kill him. He has known this for years. The question the party has to answer is not whether he knows this, but whether they can make him choose it anyway.

*Stat block: Appendix B*
