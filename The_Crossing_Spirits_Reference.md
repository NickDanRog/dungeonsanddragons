# HOW SPIRITS WORK

## Belief-Spirits vs. Old Gods

There are two fundamentally different categories of spirit in Encarnia.

**Old Gods** are primordial. They predate mortal civilization and are bound to the physical world itself. Their power does not depend on worship. Their health is tied to the land, sea, and sky they inhabit — when they suffer, the world suffers. They are not created by belief. They simply are.

**Belief-spirits** are created and sustained by mortal belief. They are the spiritual embodiment of ideas, ideals, emotions, and concepts that mortals invest with meaning. They are real — genuinely powerful and capable of independent action — but they have no primordial connection to the world. They are passengers, not foundations.

## Belief-Spirit Mechanics

**Names:** Most belief-spirits have names distinct from the ideal that empowers them. **Hope** is a rare exception — her name is the same as her belief. This is unusual enough that it warrants comment.

**Forms and names change over time.** As mortal understanding of a concept evolves, so does the spirit embodying it. A spirit of justice defined by one civilization's laws may become barely recognizable to another civilization's prayers for the same thing.

**Spirits can split.** When a belief expands to encompass distinct sub-beliefs, a new spirit can emerge. Example: a spirit of War might generate a spirit of Strategy, a spirit of Soldierly Virtue, or even a spirit specific to a type of military unit. The originating spirit retains a paramount relationship to these offshoots, but the new spirits are distinct beings.

**Spirits have relationships with each other.** These relationships are as complex as the connections between the beliefs themselves — siblings, rivals, lovers, enemies, parasites, dependents.

**Past vs. present:** Many of these spirits were once worshiped as specific deities, which gave them more defined form, clearer personalities, and stronger embodiment. As organized faith has declined (especially in the Unity), many have become less distinct — more spirit than deity, somewhat confused about their own nature and place in reality. They remain genuinely powerful because belief does not require worship to generate spiritual energy. But they are less *themselves* than they were.

**Indirect worship:** The Unity outlawed divine worship but did not eliminate belief. The ideals the Unity is built on — law, public order, public good, knowledge, unity itself — are held with something approaching religious fervor by its citizens. This empowers a cluster of belief-spirits within the Unity's spiritual landscape, even though no one prays to them.

**Archipelago spirits:** The Perfect Isles, with their many factions and active spiritual traditions, generate their own cluster of belief-spirits tied to the community's particular values and practices.

**Sacred Lands:** The peoples of the Sacred Lands worship the Old Gods directly and give regular offerings and gifts to major and minor nature spirits throughout the continent. Their spiritual ecology is the healthiest in the world.

# BELIEF-SPIRITS

## Hope
*Belief domain: Hope*

**Names:** Hope (unusual — her name is the same as the belief that empowers her)

**Nature:** One of the more stable and coherent belief-spirits, possibly because hope is a concept that has not changed dramatically in meaning across civilizations. She takes the form of a young girl sitting beside a fountain. The fountain's fullness reflects the current state of hope in the world.

**Domain (spirit world):** An abandoned city square at twilight. The geometry of the buildings is subtly wrong — not impossible, but uncomfortable. A central fountain, its flow reduced to a trickle. The girl sits beside it. As hope strengthens in the physical world, the fountain fills, the city begins to populate with people, and the twilight progresses toward dawn.

**The crossing passage:** A long series of dark passageways filled with lost items in various states of disrepair. No non-magical light source allows clear vision. An ethereal light moves ahead of the party; they must follow it.

**Relationships:**
- **Lon (Despair):** Sibling, best friend, and rival. The two are inextricably linked — neither can truly exist without the other. Their relationship is one of deep intimacy and constant tension.
- **Revna (Rebellion):** Complex love/hate relationship. Hope and Revna are each other's most frustrating mirror. Hope will introduce the party to Revna before Act II.

**Role in the adventure:** First spirit the Circle visits. The revelation from this crossing — that the Old Gods are deliberating the end of mortal life — comes through Hope's domain. She is the adventure's guide-spirit in Act I.

**Acceptable offering:** TBD — to be reviewed during DM Guide writing.

As the world has worsened — the land dying in the west, the colonial violence in the east — Lon has grown stronger. ## Revna
*Belief domain: Rebellion*

**Names:** Revna

**Nature:** The spirit of rebellion, resistance, and the refusal to accept what is. A younger spirit than Hope and Lon — rebellion as a concept requires something to rebel against, and she grew more distinct as the Unity's suppression became more complete. She is volatile, passionate, and not easy to work with.

**Domain (spirit world):** TBD

**Relationships:**
- **Hope:** Complex love/hate. They are each other's most frustrating mirror — rebellion without hope is nihilism, and hope without rebellion is passivity. Hope will introduce the party to Revna before Act II.
- **Maritas (War):** Her uncle — specifically the brother of her father. She asked the party not to mention him to Placida, and will not explain why until after the Congress interlude. She knew Placida would recognize the name and react poorly.

**Role in the adventure:** Introduced to the party by Hope before Act II. Gives the party Revna's scroll (introduction to Placida) and a key for Maritas's cell. Asks them specifically not to mention her uncle to Placida. A key figure in the Unity thread — the spirit of rebellion within a society that has outlawed rebellion.

**Acceptable offering:** Blood — one party member cuts their palm and bleeds willingly into Revna's stone bowl. A token gift or an unwilling offering will be returned. The act must be voluntary.

## Maritas
*Belief domain: War*

**Names:** Maritas

**Nature:** Ancient beyond any other belief-spirit the party will encounter — his origins predate the Unity by a vast span. The Congress made use of him during their war against the One True Way, then weakened him by building a civilization nearly free of war and tricked him into captivity in the theatre beneath the Congress's seat of power. He remains fully formed and entirely himself — captivity has not diminished him, and he knows it. He is a great bear of a man wearing fine chainmail, close-cropped dark beard and long hair, face permanently flushed — a complexion, not exertion. He is direct, occasionally blackly funny, and entirely without self-pity.

**Domain (spirit world):** Not yet visited. Encountered in captivity within Placida's domain — the Congress theatre.

**Relationships:**
- **Revna (Rebellion):** His niece — the daughter of his brother, whose name he claims he cannot recall. Perhaps he ate him. He finds this funny.
- **The Congress:** Deep and mutual enmity. The Congress considers him dangerous; he considers them cowards who built a world of enforced quiet and called it peace.
- **Novus (Progress):** The third leg of the alliance that freed him — Revna, Placida, and Novus. Novus provided the key to his cell. When Placida discovers what happened and withdraws, Novus will not. War is good for progress; their interests are aligned.

**Role in the adventure:** The identity of Revna's uncle — withheld from the party until the Congress interlude. Held captive in a cell behind the Congress table in the Congress's theartre. The party must decide whether to free him. If freed, he grants each party member a boon (Might / The Secret of Steel / Retribution) and can thereafter be contacted via the Communing ritual. He does not pick sides, but has cause to hate the Unity and will tip the scales in the party's favor from time to time. His freedom is one of the most consequential decisions in the campaign.

**Acceptable offering:** A worthy fight — one party member must volunteer to face a challenger in Maritas's arena and conduct the bout with genuine effort. The outcome is irrelevant; the effort is not. An uninspired performance earns no passage.

# THE UNITY SPIRIT COMMITTEE

The Unity's outlawing of magic and divine worship did not eliminate the spiritual energy generated by its citizens' deeply held beliefs. The ideals the Unity is built on are held with something approaching religious fervor. This has empowered a cluster of belief-spirits — a loose committee of beings who embody the Unity's foundational values. These spirits are not worshiped and are largely unaware of themselves as a group. They have a fuzzy understanding of their own nature and place in reality. They are nonetheless genuinely empowered.

**Members of the committee:**

| Spirit | Domain | Form | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lexan** | Law | Frail old man leaning on a staff; powerful voice | The eldest member by far — evolved from pre-Unity western deities of Law; more coherent and self-aware than most Congress spirits; old antagonist of Novus |
| **Placida** | Common Good | Smiling, matronly woman; glowing blonde hair | The most self-aware member; exists among the Archipelago peoples as well, giving her unusual coherence; has allied with Revna; unaware of Revna's uncle scheme; contactable via the Communing ritual near the Congress's seat of power |
| **Concordia** | Public Order | Identical form to Placida; dark hair instead of light | Placida's near-twin; focused on the social fabric rather than formal rules; relationship with her sister-spirit is one of quiet tension |
| **Ratia** | Reason | Thin woman in voluminous robes; often in a thinking pose | One of the Unity's founding drives; among the most powerful Congress members |
| **Sentia** | Knowledge | Woman in thin silk; skin appears to be made of marble | Well-fed by the Unity's emphasis on learning and technology; cool and precise |
| **Caluma** | Oppression | Comely man with bronze skin; perpetual glower | Does not think of himself as Oppression — his self-concept is Necessity; growing stronger as suppression intensifies |
| **Praefecta** | Unity (the ideal) | Skin and hair shine like the sun; almost too perfect to look at directly; rarely appears | Nominal leader of the Congress; troubled — built on something real, she can feel the widening gap between the ideal and the reality |
| **Labora** | Labor | Sturdy working woman; calloused hands; practical | The Unity's work ethic and labor-as-tax system feed her; uncomfortable closeness with Caluma |
| **Novus** | Progress | Young man always in motion; slightly blurred | The Unity's technological drive feeds him; restless and forward-looking; perpetual antagonist of Lexan |
| **Mnema** | Memory and Official Record | Elderly archivist; moves slowly; rarely speaks first | Holds the Unity's sanctioned history, which is partly false — she knows this; the most quietly troubled member and potentially the most dangerous ally |
| **Pax** | Peace | Diminished; hollow-eyed; quiet | The Unity was founded on ending war; can feel the difference between genuine peace and enforced quiet — and what she feels is the latter; she and Praefecta rarely speak |

# NATURE SPIRITS

Nature spirits are a third category of spirit, distinct from both belief-spirits and Old Gods. They are the animating presences of specific physical places and features — a stream, a valley's farmsteads, a grove, a mountain pass. They are not created by mortal belief and they predate organized worship, but they are far smaller in scope and power than the Old Gods. They are of their place and rarely range far from it. When they take mortal forms, traces of their true nature persist.

Nature spirits are most present and active in the Sacred Lands, where the spiritual ecology is healthiest. They exist in the Perfect Isles as well. In the Unity, most have been reduced to insubstantial remnants by centuries of development and suppression, surviving only in the most undisturbed places.

## Silver
*Nature spirit of the Silver Stream*

**Mortal form:** A very elderly gnome. The extreme age reflects the stream's long history in the valley.

**Role in the adventure:** Attends supper at Hellie's cottage at her invitation to help evaluate the arriving Circle. He is assessing whether the party is what the spirits have been told to expect. Whether the players ever learn who he is depends on their conduct and the DM's judgment.

## Beam
*Nature spirit of the Parna Valley farmsteads*

**Natural form:** A scarecrow. When he takes a mortal form, the traces persist — straw clings to his clothing and he carries the slightly-out-of-step quality of a field left to its own devices.

**Role in the adventure:** Present at supper, evaluating the party for his own reasons independent of Hellie's assessment. The farmsteads he represents have their own stake in what happens to the valley and, by extension, the wider world. Whether the players learn who he is depends on their conduct and the DM's judgment.

## Kelpie
*Nature spirit of the unnamed island in the Middle Sea*

**Mortal form:** A halfling-like mermaid. Speaks in the third person.

**Domain:** A small, remote island in uncharted waters of the Middle Sea. The island's flora and fauna exhibit unusual behaviors, strange colorations, and signs of higher-than-normal intelligence — all reflections of Kelpie's presence. A grotto on the north shore contains a small shrine that serves as her place of power.

**Current situation:** The island's shrine was profaned and its inhabitants slaughtered by a group of deformed Sahuagin, who converted the grotto into their lair. Their violence gave rise to a belief-spirit, **Alvolea**, sustained by the slaughter. After the party destroys the Sahuagin and repairs the shrine, Alvolea rapidly declines in power and Kelpie reports she has him firmly in hand.

**Relationships:** She refers to regular visitors — now absent — as "the other two legs of the sea." These were **Tritons**, humanoid sea-dwellers who visited the island periodically. They have long since left the region and play no further direct part in the story. Kelpie does not know why they stopped coming; she only knows it has been "quite some time."

**Role in the adventure:** Encountered during the Act II Middle Sea crossing. Contactable via the Summoning ritual once her shrine is repaired. Cannot offer much of direct strategic use but answers questions honestly. Grants the party open access — they may call on her whenever they wish, though her power is limited. Gifts them the **Voice of the Sea** (a large conch shell; minor artifact; statistics in DM Guide appendix) and asks that they return it to her someday.

## Alvolea
*Belief-spirit of the Sahuagin tribe; domain — the unnamed Middle Sea island*

**Nature:** A belief-spirit generated by and sustained by the collective belief of the deformed Sahuagin community that took up residence on the island. He is the spiritual embodiment of what the tribe believed in — whatever that was. After the party destroys the majority of the Sahuagin, Alvolea rapidly declines in power. Kelpie reports she has him firmly in hand.

**Role in the adventure:** Background presence; not directly encountered. Mentioned by Kelpie as context for the island's situation. Serves as a small-scale illustration of how even a small, isolated community generates a belief-spirit of its own.

# OLD GODS

*The Old Gods are not belief-spirits. They are primordial forces, bound to the physical world. For full mechanical and narrative context, see the Design Document.*

## Structure: Witches and Covens

The Old Gods are not organized. They communicate freely — more often with those whose regions overlap — and are as often at odds with one another as not. Their relationships shift constantly.

Each Old God is represented in the physical world by a chosen **Witch** — a mortal practitioner with whom the Old God has formed a direct bond. Witch covens exist within each Old God's region; these covens tend to the relationship between the Old God and the mortal communities of the area.

A **greater coven** exists to which each regional coven sends a single representative. It meets rarely. Its proceedings filter back to each region's Old God. As with everything in the Sacred Lands, this structure is a guideline rather than a rule — the Old Gods do not enforce it.

The **Witch of the Silver Stream** is one such practitioner, but her specific Old God affiliation (if any) and her location are unknown. The Oracle knows of her only because the spirit of Belief told her: she can help with the Crossing. Nothing more.

## Carnuth, the Stag of the New Moon

*Old God of the Wildwood (great western temperate rainforest of the Sacred Lands)*

**Forms:**
- *Daytime:* A giant stag, sleeping peacefully in a forest meadow. Quiescent; harmless unless threatened.
- *New moon (power at peak):* A gargantuan multi-legged stag of great magical and destructive power. Most dangerous; most active in the war against colonists.
- *Full moon (power at ebb):* A giant stag-headed battle sorcerer. Physically reduced; magically formidable; capable of mortal-legible communication.

**Nature:** Follows natural thought patterns, not mortal ones. Focused on the Wildwood. Kind and generous when approached respectfully with an appropriate gift; brooks no quarter for those who despoil his lands.

**Relationships:** TBD — connections to neighboring Old Gods and their regions.

**Role in the adventure:** Leads the eastern resistance against Unity colonization. Most conflicted member of the Old God community regarding the fate of mortals — has seen what mortals can become. His death is a major failure condition in Act III. Potential internal advocate at the Act IV deliberation.

**Acceptable offering:** TBD — Carnuth's entry notes that he is "kind and generous when approached respectfully with an appropriate gift"; the nature of that gift is to be determined during DM Guide writing for Act IV.

## The Old Man of the Mountain
*Old God; identity and domain TBD*

**Mortal form:** A foul-mouthed old man, encountered at his mountaintop shrine above the Parna Valley.

**Witch:** Hellie — she serves him directly. He is the Old God whose territory encompasses the Parna Valley and surroundings. She directs the party to him after teaching them the nature spirit summoning ritual, explaining that only he can teach them the Crossing ritual (which she calls "old magic" — Witches do not enter the spirit realm unless invited).

**Nature:** Has been observing the party throughout their time in his lands. By the time they reach his shrine, he has already formed a judgment of them based on their conduct: whether they have respected the land, been courteous to the spirits of the region, and avoided unnecessary killing. This judgment determines the terms on which he shares the ritual.

**What he provides:** The Crossing ritual itself, and the name of the first spirit seeking the Circle: **Hope**. He explains the distinction between Hellie's nature spirit summoning (performed in a spirit's place of power) and his own ritual (performed anywhere, but requiring the spirit's true name).

**The undead task:** A powerful undead creature has taken up residence on the other side of his mountain. He considers its presence an offense. If the party has not acquitted themselves well, he demands they clear it before he will share the ritual. If they have acquitted themselves well, he asks it as a post-audience favor.

## Huma, the Storm Whale

*Old God of sea and storm; formerly of the western continent*

**Nature:** A primordial spirit of the ocean and its weather. Survived the Unity's suppression longer than other western Old Gods because the sea cannot be developed. Eventually captured in a weakened state by a Unity cabal.

**Current status:** Held captive by the cabal's device. His energy is being channeled to power the northern coastal region of the Unity — the only highly productive area left in the west. The cabal views this as proof of concept.

**Condition after captivity:** Outcome-dependent on loss of life during freeing.

*Minimal damage and loss of life:* Huma returns to himself within days. His natural domain — the sea and its weather along the northern coast — reasserts itself within weeks. He is grateful to the party in the manner of a very old being: not effusive, but genuine. He grants them open access via the Communing ritual and, in the best outcome, offers each party member a boon chosen from:

- **Breath of the Sea** — *Air Bubble* 3/day
- **Call the Storm** — *Lightning Bolt* (cast at 5th level) 2/day
- **Divine Wind** — *Warding Wind* 3/day

Each boon is a permanent innate ability, usable without spell slots or material components.

*Catastrophic freeing (significant loss of life):* The deaths caused in part by the manner of his release wound him in a way that is not physical. He goes into isolation. He will speak with the party if sought out for a final conversation and will be honest about what happened. He does not offer the Communing connection or a boon. His isolation has natural world consequences in Act IV that the DM may develop.

**Role in the adventure:** Freeing Huma is Act III's central objective. He can be contacted via the Summoning ritual once the party reaches the pressurized chamber — fragmented and confused before the projectors are disabled, coherent after. If freed with minimal harm, he allows the party to contact him via Communing whenever they wish and may offer a boon. He is a witness and potential advocate at the Act V deliberation.

**Acceptable offering:** TBD — Huma is contacted via the Summoning ritual rather than the Crossing; the nature of an appropriate gift when petitioning him for aid is to be determined during DM Guide writing for Act III.
