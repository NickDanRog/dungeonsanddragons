Original Notes on the Nine Worlds

# AUZRA — The Realm of Air

Environment: Three tiers of floating islands suspended in open sky. The lowest tier is shrouded in toxic gases (cataclysm damage), the middle tier is wrapped in thick mist populated by dangerous avian predators, and the highest tier rises above the mist into perpetual twilight and extreme cold. Australia-scale landmass distributed across the tiers.

Primary Race: Grey Elves — pale skin, silver/white/gray hair, varied eye colors. Settled the larger islands by mutual agreement with the Hawkmen after arriving post-Hawkmen. Now the dominant population across all three tiers.

Secondary Race: Hawkmen — pre-cataclysm natives. Prefer smaller, open islands. Now live in tribal bands on small islands deep in the mist layer, actively resisting the Warlock's rule. Deeply saddened by the broken relationship with the Grey Elves, who turned against them through the Warlock's propaganda.

Society:
Lower tier: Single large island. Domed settlements due to toxic atmosphere. Labor population grows crops and manufactures goods for the upper tiers. Criminals and exiles from other tiers are sent here as punishment. Harshest conditions, lowest status.
Middle tier: Five islands chained together to prevent drifting apart. Periodic earthquakes when islands pull at their chains. Police state under the Directorate. Home to many universities focused on culture, art, and philosophy — all run by the Warlock's cronies. Learning is the highest social capital. Inter-island travel is dangerous (mist, avian predators) and magical transport is heavily regulated.
Upper tier: Single island above the mist. Serves the academy and the Warlock directly. The most promising arcane talent from across the realm is taken here — the academy functions as both training ground and prison. Castle Perilous, the Warlock's palace, dominates this tier.

Warlock — The Gray Eminence (Tana White-Lightning)

Female-presenting non-binary Aasimar Wizard
Formerly a great teacher, scholar, and philosopher who shared knowledge freely and took many apprentices
Now extremely paranoid, obsessed with hoarding and controlling all knowledge, especially arcane
Cannot memorize spells since full corruption — relies entirely on scrolls and magic items produced by the academy, which they hoard obsessively
Rules through the Directorate, a sinister bureaucracy that uses magical compulsion on mid- and upper-level agents to ensure loyalty
Lever: Tana's original spellbook, secreted away by an Elven resistance leader who died in the final confrontation when the resistance was destroyed, taking the secret to their grave. The spellbook is hidden in a ruin on the lower tier. Recovering it and presenting it to Tana allows them to remember who they were, opening the possibility of turning them.

Mythic Place of Power — The Overlook

A scenic clifftop at the highest point of the upper tier, from which one can see clearly into any part of the realm
Located within Tana's private garden at the apex of Castle Perilous
Tana can no longer call upon its power but refuses to let anyone else use it
Access strictly controlled and guarded by the Warlock's most elite defenders

# TANIS — The Realm of Earth

Environment: A great orb-shaped cavern with the top sliced open. The walls grow progressively steeper before folding inward toward the opening — no one has ever successfully climbed them. The sun passes over the opening for only a few hours each day, leaving the realm in near-perpetual darkness. Formerly a land of green, growing things; now the majority of the cavern floor is covered by the Contamination — a zone of twisted plants, mutated creatures, and infectious fungi emanating from the central ziggurat. Australia-scale landmass.

Primary Race: Dwarves — originally lived in partially underground cities on the cavern floor. As the Contamination has advanced, communities have been forced progressively outward, abandoning city after city. Many have taken to drilling tunnels into the cavern walls and building partially vertical cities, believing elevation will protect them.

Secondary Race: Gnomes (Forest Gnomes) — a smaller population native to this world. The druidic tradition is specifically theirs. Gnomish druids work to hold back the Contamination's advance on remaining communities, but they are fighting a losing battle.

The Contamination: Twisted plants, mutated creatures, and infectious fungal growth spreading outward from the central ziggurat. Those who breathe in the spores are transformed into mutated zombies. Originated from the ichor of the Warlock's unhealable wound.

The Healer's Road: A safe path from the outer communities to the ziggurat, but still dangerous without escort. Twisted agents of the Warlock approach communities at random intervals, asking if anyone can heal the Warlock's condition. If someone volunteers, they are escorted to the ziggurat. If no one volunteers, the agent attacks in a rage, killing until it is put down.

Warlock — Howler (Garl the Even-Handed)
Forest Gnome Druid, native to this world
Originally a kind and good ruler; his increasing madness from an unhealable wound drove away those who tried to help him
Wounded by a demon lord's spear during the war — the wound never healed. Ichor from it is the source of the Contamination
Spends much of his time screaming in pain (hence "Howler")
Obsessed with curing his condition; will latch onto any attempt, no matter how outlandish. Many have tried. All have died.
Can only assume mutated, tortured forms through wild shape. No access to druidic spells. Greatly enhanced strength and damage absorption.
Lever: His animal companion, the grizzly bear Rerox, contained within a wondrous figurine token. Garl cast the spell to give Rerox time to heal during the demon war. An old friend retrieved the figurine and brought it to Garl's childhood home, but died before communicating this. The figurine is extremely difficult to recover. If presented to Garl, he finally understands his wound is incurable, renounces his mythic power, and asks for the mercy of death. The Contamination begins to wither when he dies.

Mythic Place of Power — Gaia's Navel
Located in a cavern beneath the ziggurat
A circular worked stone pool glowing with golden light, surrounded by a garden of healthy, dense vegetation despite the lack of sunlight
Those who step into the pool are healed of all wounds
The Warlock is denied its power by his corruption, but his pain lessens in its proximity — he is often found here

# VATRA — The World of Fire

Environment: A circular world arranged in three concentric rings. The outer ring consists of mountains, progressively more impassable toward the edges, with many volcanic peaks on the inner face. The middle ring holds cities full of workshops and magical laboratories, fed by a large canal system that channels lava from the volcanoes to power the forges — these cities were reduced to ruin during the cataclysm and are now only partially inhabited. The inner ring was once a great rainforest with lakes and resort villages behind stone walls, protected by a dome that filtered volcanic fumes — the dome was destroyed in the apocalypse and the rainforest was later burned entirely by the Warlock. The world is covered in near-constant ash and low-scuttling smog clouds. Australia-scale landmass.

Primary Race: Tieflings — population is very low. Most toil in the mines. Those with magical talent are conscripted to produce magic items. People survive on near-starvation rations from magitek food and water machines, which are slowly breaking down.

The Burned Forest: The inner ring is completely destroyed. It is haunted by incorporeal undead — spirits of those killed when the forest burned. They endlessly repeat fragments of their former lives and will seek to destroy the living if disturbed, but can be brought to lucidity through proper communication and potentially bargained with. They are unable to move past the remains of the wall at the forest's edge.

The Construct Hierarchy:
Majestics — few in number, oversee the bureaucracy that keeps the realm running
Techpriests — oversee production of magic items
Enforcers — punish offenders
All constructs are controlled by the Overmind, a hive-mind intelligence made from the souls of those fed to the construct-creating machine, bound to a magitek apparatus. Blackfire rarely consults it, so it often operates on outdated instructions — making it both oppressive and erratic.
Haplo the Prophet: Blackfire's sole surviving apprentice. Effectively runs the realm on Blackfire's behalf. Created a false religion to legitimize the regime. Kept alive via a complex magitek life support apparatus that has twisted his body and mind. A central tenet of this religion forbids any mention of the past — not merely historical events, but any event that occurred before the current day.

Warlock — Blackfire (Kerlin Whitemane)
Tiefling Magus, once a great inventor of magitek devices, weapons, and armor
After the apocalypse, did not seek to rule — built machines to create food and water to save the starving population. Trained many apprentices.
Corruption caused him to lose his knowledge and grow jealous of his students. His greatest apprentice, Renara, became his lover.
Killed Renara in a jealous rage after she forged a powerful magic sword he could not replicate. His corruption became complete.
Forced remaining apprentices to build a machine that converts living beings into enslaved constructs, then fed them all to it.
Crushed a popular uprising and chased survivors into the rainforest, which he burned.
Has lost his magitek abilities; can only produce the simplest magic items.
Once a year, chooses the most skilled magic artisan in the land as an apprentice. Eventually grows jealous of their work and feeds them to the construct machine.
His only goal is to create something new again, which he hopes his apprentices' work will inspire.
Lever: Renara's Blade — the powerful relic forged by his lover before he killed her. Seeing it will force him to confront what he lost. The blade was hidden in a secret vault whose location Blackfire has forgotten, guarded by a dragon construct built for that purpose.

Mythic Place of Power — The Flame Imperishable
An ancient temple deep within the forges of Vatra's largest city
At the center: a pure white flame with no obvious source, floating freely in the air
Once powered a forge in the adjacent workshop (now Blackfire's personal workshop) capable of creating items imbued with mythic power
Denied to Blackfire by his corruption

# DIASPOLA — The World of Water

Environment: A circular world, half land and half sea, fading to endless mist at the edges. The land side was once replete with small lakes, picturesque villages, farms, and fishing settlements along the shoreline. The sea side is a great freshwater body full of fish and coral reefs, containing dozens of islands that once served as personal retreats for powerful adventurers and mythic heroes from other worlds. Rain is frequent but the weather was always warm and pleasant. Now the entire realm is covered in ice and snow. Australia-scale landmass.

Primary Race: Halflings — left mostly to their own devices but struggling to survive in the frozen land.

Secondary/Antagonist Race: Corrupted Fey — led by the Nixie witch Khaga. Corrupted by the demonic presence during the war, they now torment the population and effectively rule the realm through Sona. Fey who are knowledgeable about can be bested by those who understand fey etiquette.

Non-Aligned Fey: In hiding on the fringes of the land side or on islands throughout the sea side. Not initially friendly to outsiders, but can be approached by those who have something to offer or who understand how to deal with fey. Potential allies.

The Wild Hunt: Once a year, the Snow Queen rides a magnificent sleigh at the head of a carnival procession of fey. The event is more entertaining than dangerous and is anticipated by the population. During the Hunt, the Queen selects two girls and two boys as royal guests for a week at her castle. These children are secretly replaced by fey changelings and transformed into magical animated items that serve the Queen and fey at the castle. They can be freed by the PCs.

Warlock — The Snow Queen (Sona Brightspring)
Female Halfling Witch, native to this world. Still holds her mythic power.
Returned home after the cataclysm to help rebuild.
The fey, corrupted by demonic presence during the war, quietly sabotaged all rebuilding efforts under the leadership of Khaga, disguising their interference as the work of evil spirits — supposed spiritual remains of the invading demons. These spirits tormented Sona extensively.
After several years, Khaga approached Sona with an offer of help and manipulated her into using ice magic to combat the spirits, which always seemed to escape at the last moment.
Sona's continued use of ice magic gradually froze the entire realm.
Now withdrawn, paranoid, and terrified of the evil spirits that torment her. Has effectively ceded rule to Khaga and the malicious fey.
Lever: The Ivory Music Box — an intricate music box featuring the figure of a stately ice dancer, made by Sona's father. Hearing its tune breaks Khaga's spell, allowing Sona to be turned. She will turn on and banish the fey and destroy Khaga. The music box is hidden in Sona's old tower on one of the far islands, guarded by fey sentinels placed by Khaga.

Mythic Place of Power — The Mother of Rivers
A great magical spring of pure water at the center of the land side, surrounded by a grove of trees within a sacred enclosure
Once the source of the Crystal River, which fed the freshwater sea — now frozen solid
Khaga and the fey placed a powerful curse on the surrounding grove: the trees are frozen crystals, and the grove slowly saps the strength of anyone who enters, with effects intensifying deeper in
The grove also holds captive the spirits of fey who opposed Khaga, corrupted and imprisoned here
The curse can be broken by a magical key carried by Khaga, or it will slowly fade after her death

# ILLIUM — The Realm of Light

Environment: A circular floating continent with varied terrain and climates. The sun spends more time passing over Illium than any other realm, producing long days, short nights, and extended twilight and dawn. During the cataclysm, the northwestern third of the continent split off into a separate island. Frequent seismic disruptions persist throughout the realm. The main continent is well-developed with a few small cities and many towns and villages. The northwestern island has grown wild, reclaimed by nature since the split. Australia-scale landmass.

Primary Race: Half-Elves — descended from extensive human and elven populations that merged over time.

Religion Note (Campaign-Wide): The dominant religious practice across the Nine Worlds venerates the Host of Heaven — the celestial beings of Heaven collectively, not any single deity. They are commonly referred to as "the gods." The practice goes by different names on each world but has largely fallen away due to the waning power of the celestials, the general decline of civilization, the influence of the Warlocks, and a widespread feeling that the gods have abandoned the worlds.

Current State:
The Church Ascendant, led by Tamaron, rules the majority of the realm. The realm appears peaceful and prosperous, but signs of wrongness emerge on closer inspection — the population is unnervingly eager to serve the church, with all adults tithing heavily and performing works for it.
The Crown of Ascension — a powerful relic created by Tamaron as his last act of divine magic at the demon's behest — is used in the coming-of-age ceremony where people formally join the church. It places a powerful geas compelling the wearer to support the church above all other concerns. Its true power is not publicly known.
The Rebellion occupies the fortified northwestern island. They are aware of the Crown's power and run a resistance network to spirit away those questioning the status quo before the geas can be placed on them. Their claims are dismissed as propaganda. The rebels are mixed — they started with genuine cause but the long war has hardened them, producing both sympathetic members and extremists.
The realm remains at war between the Church and the Rebellion.

The Northwestern Island & Rebellion:
The island's fortifications are primarily magical rather than physical — the original rebellion included many arcane spellcasters
Arcane magic has been outlawed by the Church Ascendant. A key goal of the mainland resistance network is smuggling arcane practitioners to safety on the island
The island is largely wild, with former urban areas and infrastructure partially reclaimed by nature
Home to many magical beasts, fey creatures, and other magical beings — persecuted by the church, the rebellion has worked to rescue as many as possible
The rebels will be initially hostile to the PCs, assuming them to be church agents. Significant effort will be required to earn their trust

Warlock — Tamaron the Just
Half-Elf Cleric. Has kept his name despite his corruption.
During the final battle of the demon war, he unknowingly absorbed the dying soul of the demon lord Tekticula the Unraveler after destroying it. The demon spent years recovering before slowly exercising control, presenting itself as the "voice of the gods" in Tamaron's mind.
Returned home to find a realm split by civil strife — many blamed the gods for the cataclysm and rejected the church. His heavy-handed attempts to reconcile them, counseled by the voice, escalated into a crusade. Atrocities on both sides. Rebels were chased to the northwestern island.
Has lost all divine magic. The voice tells him this is punishment for allowing the rebels to escape. Lost in delusion and zealotry. Assigns grueling penances to himself at the voice's direction. The corruption was gradual — early suggestions sounded pious and logical; now he believes almost anything the voice says, and severely punishes himself whenever he manages to resist.
Tekticula the Unraveler: A demon lord of destruction and hate. Passed its dying soul into Tamaron as a last-ditch gambit. Unknown to the demon, it is permanently bound to Tamaron's soul and can never leave his body. Destroying Tamaron's body kills both, but the demon is briefly released and may attempt to possess a nearby person — this becomes more difficult the more powerful the target. If the demon is killed in Tamaron's mindspace, it is irrevocably destroyed with no possession attempt.
Lever: The Sign of the Host — Tamaron's original winged holy symbol from when he first joined the church. Lost during the war. Found by one of the last pilgrim groups before the Rainbow Road closed and brought to an obscure reliquary now in rebel territory. Hiding in plain sight — a PC with Religion knowledge might identify it on inspection, but finding it requires extreme diligence. To turn Tamaron, the PCs must place the Crown of Ascension on his head and present the Sign — this allows the gods to focus divine power through the Sign into the Crown, granting access to the spiritual battlefield where the party can fight and destroy Tekticula.

Mythic Place of Power — The Well of Light
A massive stained glass window in a sequestered shrine at the apex of the Cathedral of Light, Illium's main center of worship
Tamaron keeps his offices on the same floor of the cathedral
The window glows with sacred power when the sun passes at midday — in former times, the gods sent visions to the most pious at this hour
Unable to use its power, Tamaron has had the Well sealed off since the end of the war with the rebels

# NOCTURNE — The Realm of Darkness

Environment: A series of interconnected valleys and canyons amidst a great mountain range, with mountains becoming increasingly impassable away from the center. The sun never reaches this realm, traveling along its edges and shrouding it in perpetual gloomy twilight. A brief period of "near dawn" occurs for a few hours each day, but the sun never crests the horizon. In former times, five main valleys each held a city renowned for art — literature, painting, poetry, music, opera, dance, and other forms were highly venerated. The realm also featured extensive magitek infrastructure, including hydroponic farms powered by magitek sunlight lamps. Australia-scale landmass.

Primary Race: Fetchlings (Kayal) — native to this realm of shadow. In former times, Nocturne had a significant multicultural population. Only the native Fetchlings were affected by the curse — all non-Kayal residents died.

The Curse / "The Dream":
A demon-caused plague devastated the realm after the cataclysm. Baselia, bereft of divine power, turned to the Cabal (outlawed sorcerers, by then demon-corrupted) for help. They twisted her preservation spell into a curse.
The population is caught between the mortal world and the afterlife — neither living, dead, nor undead. They appear as slightly ghostly versions of their former selves.
They do not realize they have been affected. They call this state "the Dream" and believe it is how life naturally is.
They no longer need to eat, drink, or sleep, and find nothing strange about this.
They cannot create anything new — deeply frustrating for this formerly inventive culture — but rationalize it away. Instead they produce the best renditions they can of existing works of art.
Any attempt to confront them about their state is met with disbelief regardless of evidence.
If a person "dies" within the Dream, they are immediately reconstituted in another region of the realm but without their memories. Over centuries, this means everyone has lost their original personalities.

Getting to Lady Sorrow:
The five valley-regions were connected by magitek light bridges spanning bottomless chasms, powered by orbs.
Powerful spirits of slain Cabal sorcerers have taken strongholds in each region and hidden the orbs.
The PCs must defeat or otherwise reclaim the orbs from the Cabal spirits to reactivate the bridges and reach Lady Sorrow's castle.

Warlock — Lady Sorrow (Baselia the Undaunted)
Female Half-Elf Paladin, now an Anti-Paladin
Former lover of Tamaron, the Warlock of Illium. They had a great falling out at the end of the demon war when Baselia renounced her vows, blaming the gods for allowing the catastrophe.
Returned home to a realm ravaged by a demon-caused plague. Worked tirelessly but was powerless without divine magic. Every solution failed.
Turned to the Cabal for help. They corrupted the spell, cursing the population into their half-life state. Enraged, she hunted down and destroyed every sorcerer of the Cabal over the course of many years.
Now broods in the Citadel of Lament, surrounded by the spirits of the slain Cabal and other undead guardians. In her obsession with returning to normalcy, she forces these creatures to perform macabre renditions of famous works of art, but finds no joy in them.
Lever: Tamaron himself. If Tamaron was turned on Illium, he can help Baselia return to her former self and retake her vows. He will not accompany the PCs on the full quest — he has his own realm to rebuild — but if they open the way to the Citadel and return with him, he will fight the undead guardians alongside them and confront his former lover. If Tamaron was killed on Illium, Baselia is unturnable. The PCs must fight her.

Mythic Place of Power — The Womb of Darkness
A mysterious levitating orb of pure darkness in a vault beneath the Citadel of Lament
Passing through the orb transports a person to a place beyond space and time. They exist there for as long as they will — only a fraction of a second passes in real time. Within the Womb, thoughts take on ghostly physical dimensions.
Formerly used by scholars, artists, and religious devotees as a place of contemplation, with access controlled by a strict application process.
Baselia can use the Womb but finds it unhelpful — within it, she can only reflect on her past mistakes.

# ARLUNE — The Realm of the Moon

Environment: An endless desert called the Twilight Sea. The central regions feature normal desert terrain — mesas, badlands, mountains, hills, canyons — but flatten to featureless sand toward the edges, which fade into the mists of the Ethereal Plane. The sun is never seen; instead, the moon passes slowly across the sky each day. The sands glow softly in moonlight, giving off residual multicolored light during the few hours the moon is below the horizon. Australia-scale landmass.

Primary Race: Gnomes (approximately 50% of the current population). Gnomes in this setting do not experience the Bleaching. In former times, Arlune was the trade hub of the Nine Worlds, giving it a large multicultural population — remnants of that diversity persist.

Infrastructure: Extensive magitek systems, necessary for survival in a realm where water is scarce and food cannot be easily grown. Magitek-powered sandships are the primary mode of transport between settlements.

Current State:
Arlune is effectively ruled by seven major criminal organizations, held in check only by mutual fear of each other and the mysterious Trademaster.
Below the cartels: an ever-shifting patchwork of guilds, street gangs, labor unions, and similar groups with constantly changing alliances.
Debt and obligation are facts of life — everyone owes something to someone. Many would go hungry, unhoused, or thirsty without a network of charity organizations (secretly funded by the Trademaster).
The Nightmarket is the largest settlement: a small city at its core, surrounded by a massive, erratic bazaar of tents and temporary shelters. Several smaller towns exist throughout the desert.
Independent sandship captains are the freest people of Arlune — moving cargo and people between settlements. The most daring hunt titanic creatures of the deep sands (sand leviathans, silt whales) which fetch exorbitant prices at the Nightmarket.
Sandship pirates plague the trade routes, hunt the desert hunters, and harass enemies for pay. Most operate from oases deep in the desert.

Warlock — The Trademaster (Garm the Honest)
Dwarf Rogue. Unique among the Warlocks in that he has not been fully corrupted and actually wants to turn.
Returned home to find the realm in turmoil — overwhelming traffic and structural damage had destabilized the Rainbow Road. Populations were fleeing to other realms.
Successfully repaired much of the magitek infrastructure to prevent starvation and dehydration. Made Road access contingent on a bureaucratic application process, stabilizing the exodus before the Road's final shutdown.
The loss of trade — which the realm depended on — caused continued unrest. Economic solutions were stymied by a rebellious populace. Government collapsed around him, leaving a power vacuum filled by criminal cartels.
Assassination attempts drove him underground. He re-emerged years later as the Trademaster, using the cartels against each other from the shadows through his own organization, creating a state of equilibrium.
A popular legend holds that Garm will someday return to defeat the crime lords and free the people.
Extremely slow to trust and nearly impossible to find. No one alive can honestly claim to have met the Trademaster in person.
If contacted and convinced, Garm will have the PCs perform difficult tasks on his behalf. After completion, he publicly reveals himself and works to establish democratic council rule.
If forced into confrontation, he is a very dangerous opponent — still in possession of mythic power, surrounded by powerful minions and magitek constructs.
Lever: The Lucky Coin — shows a laughing Dwarf (Garm's face) on one side and a pile of treasure on the other. Made by his best friend Casomir (ruler of the hub realm) after their first adventure together. Passed down through Garm's family. The PCs receive it from Garm's great-great-grandson, captain of an anti-pirate sandship, as part of an optional side quest available soon after arriving in Arlune. The coin provides a solid introduction and bypasses many of Garm's initial reservations.

Mythic Place of Power — The Moonstone
A massive opalescent monolith in a hidden valley deep in the desert
The Trademaster has carefully eliminated references to its location through his agents; diligent PC investigation can uncover it
The Trademaster watches the site — approaching before interacting with him will trigger a confrontation he is prepared for
If the PCs earn Garm's trust and complete his tasks, he freely reveals the location and escorts them personally
When the moon passes overhead each night, the stone glows with unearthly light. Anyone within 30 feet has all diseases, curses, level drain, and ability damage permanently removed.

# CANDELA — The Realm of the Stars

Environment: The smallest and most unusual of the realms. Candela consists of sphere-like constructs suspended in a featureless void. Twelve larger spheres serve as central hubs, each surrounded by smaller peripheral spheres connected directly to them. Spheres are grouped by purpose — residential, commercial, etc. Each larger sphere has a central platform with buildings, parks, and magitek kiosks for ordering floating platforms to reach the peripheral spheres (homes, private businesses). The spheres glow with soft ambient light and are opaque from outside, making clusters resemble stars from afar. Gravity is subjective within each sphere — wherever you stand is down. Larger spheres are connected by a tram system: bubble-like transports moving through twisting tube structures with no sensation of acceleration. At the outer edges, smaller central spheres connect via long covered walkways to private spheres — the domains of tenured faculty and notable visitors, protected by layers of magical defenses.

Primary Race: Half-Orcs (Dromar) — served as the primary faculty of the Arcanium and caretakers of the realm. Population was never high. The majority were evacuated to the hub world during the cataclysm.

The Arcanium: The greatest university in the Nine Worlds, and the home of magitek research. Its campus encompasses the largest sphere group at the realm's center. Partially ruined by a demon incursion that was ultimately stopped before reaching the sealed core research labs.

Current State: Completely depopulated except for the Warlock. As the realm subjectively closest to the Underworld, Candela was heavily affected by demonic corruption and evacuated as a precaution at the onset of the apocalypse. A small team of caretakers remained behind with orders to destroy the university if necessary to keep its research from demon hands.

Warlock — Overmind (Ourania Lachos)
Half-Orc Arcanist
After the demon war, returned with a small team of specialists to assess the situation and find the caretakers
Found the Arcanium partially in ruins — demons had breached multiple defense waves before being cut down. Core research labs were sealed and intact. Caretakers were condemned to slow death by demonic curses.
Volunteered to enter the Overmind control room to assess defenses. Immediately assaulted by demon spirits lying in wait — she destroyed them easily in their weakened state. However, her use of magic in the chamber caused the automated defenses to malfunction, killing the rest of her team. She discovered she was fused to the machine.
Has been trapped in this state for 500 years. Thoroughly insane.
Through the Overmind interface, she perceives all interactions with magitek machinery throughout Candela. Her control increases with proximity to the center. She is aware of the PCs from the moment of arrival and progressively escalates automated defenses and magitek constructs against them as they approach.
Lever: Dew in the Morning Grass — Ourania's favorite volume of poetry, a much-abused paperback gifted by her maternal aunt who raised her. Found on her desk in the library of her personal sphere. Locating the sphere likely requires foreknowledge about Ourania — any previously turned Warlock can provide this if asked. Casomir (ruler of the hub realm) was her close friend and kept the key to her sphere in his study — he will not be able to reveal this to the PCs. If the PCs bring the book to the Overmind control room and read a poem to her in person, she can break the interface. Horrified by everything she has done and still deeply insane, she will seek to self-terminate. Only very powerful magic to end her insanity combined with significant convincing will fully turn her.

Mythic Place of Power — The Stellar Oracle
A magitek machine created by the university and blessed by the gods
Uses the power of the Rainbow Road to tap into the collective unconscious of all beings across the Nine Worlds
The Oracle has its own personality and judges worthiness by unknown criteria. If a user is deemed worthy, they may ask three questions, which the Oracle answers cryptically — or sometimes not at all.
The Oracle comes fully online when the PCs access Candela's place of power, which opens the realm along the Rainbow Road

# NEXUS — The Heart of the Nine Worlds

Environment: A small continent surrounded by the mists of the Ethereal Plane. Temperate, with varied terrain features found on prime material worlds. The great city of Nexus sits at the center — the largest city in the Nine Worlds. Near the realm's edges, equidistant from each other, are eight gate towns, each centered around a Rainbow Road terminus to one of the other worlds. A perfectly circular road of rainbow tiles connects each gate town to its neighbors; those with mythic power find their speed on this road dramatically increased, shortening journeys from days to minutes. Each gate town also has an inward-facing rainbow road connecting it to the city. The gate towns have been abandoned since the apocalypse. The city was formerly surrounded by a broad belt of farmlands and villages, now destroyed. In ancient times, Nexus also contained portals to other planes throughout the multiverse — all have gone dark since the apocalypse. The Nine Worlds are effectively cut off from the wider planes until the Rainbow Road is fully restored.

Primary Race: Humans — a significant portion of the population, but members of any race from the other worlds can be found here, along with races from across the planes who were trapped after the apocalypse.

Campaign-Wide: The Rainbow Road
The Road is currently shut down. PCs can travel to specific worlds through alternative means (detailed in DM Guide).
Each time the PCs access a place of power on a world, that realm is unlocked along the Rainbow Road.
Full restoration of the Road is progressive — each world conquered opens new travel options.

Current State:
Over several years, Casomir and his followers eliminated major demon concentrations and defended the city. Researchers determined demons were either reproducing or continuing to arrive from the Underworld in small numbers.
Demon attacks have continued but in smaller, more tactical numbers — multiple simultaneous small raids across the city to keep Nexus off balance. In truth, the demons are secretly amassing forces for a massive attack, the largest since the end of the war.
The population converted two full city districts to farmland, which feeds the now-reduced populace. Magitek foundries (restored by Casomir) keep the population well-armed; they generally handle demon raids without difficulty.
People are terrified of Beast Blavin and barricade during full moons. Some have hunted the creature against Casomir's orders; all have died.
Researchers have noted demon attacks increase after the Beast's appearances. The truth: Blavin is drawing on Casomir's power to actively gate demons into Nexus. His gating capacity is limited, which has prevented the numbers from becoming overwhelming — but the demons have been hiding their true numbers and preparing for a decisive assault.

Warlock — Casomir Zukra / Beast Blavin
Human Magitek Knight (custom prestige class). Leader of the original ten heroes. Proclaimed King of Nexus after rallying the city's defense against demon remnants — an office he did not seek but tries to honor.
Aware of the corruption affecting all former heroes. Used alchemy to proactively contain it. The attempt partially succeeded but created a catastrophic side effect: his corruption manifests as the Beast Blavin, a gigantic mutated creature. Transformation builds whenever he uses magic or mythic powers.
Takes elaborate precautions — "hunting trips," a hidden prison in his citadel's basement — but the Beast knows everything he knows and always finds a way out. Always kills.
Cannot be turned. Has no lever. He is horrified by what the Beast has done and knows there is no way out.
Serves as the campaign's starting point: assigns the PCs two quests (detailed in DM Guide), one of which proves they are the heroes chosen by the gods. After this, he asks them to kill him.
Has left instructions for his seneschal to open the passage to the first place of power after his death.

Mythic Place of Power — The Great Ancillary
An ancient temple at the very center of Nexus — the central point of the Rainbow Road
Well-fortified and tightly sealed; accessible only with a key given to the PCs after their final encounter with Casomir
Contains a great magitek orrery: eight spheres (representing the outer worlds) orbit a central sphere (Nexus), with a tenth sphere above (Heaven). Light shines down from the Heaven sphere.
A great diagram of magic runes on the floor beneath the central sphere glows with arcane light. When the PCs step into this circle, they are taken outside space and time to meet representatives of the gods. Their adventure begins.

Campaign Background:
The Nine Worlds and the Rainbow Road were created for a specific purpose: to find worthy mortal champions of good and imbue them with mythic power to fight evil across the multiverse. No one now remembers this except the Warlocks.
Before the apocalypse, worthy candidates sought confirmation from the Nexian Oracle (an ancient being on Nexus who serves as the arbiter of worthiness), then traveled to the Ancillary for their first portion of mythic power, then journeyed across the planes performing heroic acts to prove themselves worthy to walk the Road and gain more power. Note: The Stellar Oracle on Candela is a separate entity — a magitek machine intelligence that serves as that realm's mythic place of power.
The legends the PCs grew up hearing about ancient heroes are true. Their stories all began here — as does the PCs' story.

Endgame — The Battle of Nexus:
Demon forces amass on Nexus throughout the campaign, growing as the Rainbow Road opens
Tornsoul uses the reopening Road to move demons via prepared ritual sites outside the city — first a trickle, then larger numbers
PCs are unaware of this threat until most of the way through the campaign
PC actions on each world can gain allies for this confrontation
An ally-tracking mechanic should be designed (PCs are tangentially aware of gathering strength but don't see the numbers)
Before the final battle, PCs can return to each world and draw allies into Nexus along the Rainbow Road
This battle occurs just before the descent to the Underworld

# HEAVEN — The Artificial Sun
A giant clockwork and magitek sphere that moves between the realms in sequence, providing light
Inhabited by three races of celestials who collectively created the Nine Worlds: Archons, Angels, and Agathions
Not true gods, but heavenly beings whose collective worship grants divine power to followers equivalent to that of a deity — this is the basis of the Host of Heaven faith across the Nine Worlds
Heavily damaged during the demon assault on Heaven. Current state is broken, though the celestials endure.
The PCs never visit Heaven directly. They meet representatives of the three celestial races twice:
First meeting: At the Great Ancillary, after stepping into the rune circle. The celestials confirm their mission and grant their initial mythic power.
Final meeting: At the Ancillary after the complete reopening of the Rainbow Road. The celestials grant the PCs their final portion of mythic power (mythic tier 10) and offer to open a portal to the Underworld when they are ready.

# THE UNDERWORLD — Prison of the Asuras

Origin: When the three celestial races were constructing the Nine Worlds, creatures of hate and ruin — exiles from Hell — learned of the project and infiltrated it while still in progress. The goodness of the mortal populations rejected these entities, and the gods responded by forming a prison for them: the Underworld. Unknown to the gods, these creatures had already touched the Nine Worlds, and while they could not leave their prison, their lingering influence allowed them to affect mortalkind across the worlds in a manner similar to the gods themselves.

The Demons: The people of the Nine Worlds call these creatures demons — there are no other infernal races in the setting, and the term "asura" is unknown in-world. For game purposes, all demons use Asura stat blocks from the PF1e Bestiary. New demons are formed whenever a mortal willingly corrupted by their influence dies across the Nine Worlds and their soul descends to the Underworld instead of passing to their chosen afterlife. Demons have plagued the Nine Worlds throughout their history — sponsoring cults, vile acts, and wars among mortalkind. Many mythic heroes built their reputations fighting demonic influence.

Asura Ranas: Unique named asura demigods. No generic stat block published in PF1e — these are lore entities that serve as the rulers of the Underworld. Named ranas in Pathfinder lore include Aghasura, Daissiad, Holika, Vritra, and others. For the campaign, Asura Ranas serve as Tornsoul's chief subordinates and the demon lords referenced in the Underworld section. Custom stat blocks should be designed for any Asura Rana that appears as a named encounter.

Note: The 8 custom types fill every CR gap from 1–16. Combined with the 9 SRD types, the full roster provides 17 asura types spanning CR 1–20. Full stat blocks for all custom types will be provided in the Stat Block Appendix.

Potential Additional Types (Not Yet Designed):

CR 17–19 gap: The current roster jumps from Ramjagodar (CR 16) to Asurendra (CR 20). Candela (level 19) relies primarily on automated magitek defenses rather than asura encounters, so this gap does not affect Phase 2. However, the Underworld descent (Chapter 13) needs progressively escalating asura encounters leading up to Tornsoul, and 1–2 types in the CR 17–19 range would serve as Tornsoul's elite guard / final conventional combat before the boss encounter. Design these during the Stat Block Appendix phase.

Support/buffer type (suggested CR 5–7): The roster currently has no mid-range asura that enhances other asuras — the Ramjagodar at CR 16 is the only force multiplier. A lower-CR support type that heals, shields, or coordinates nearby asuras would make demon groups more tactically interesting throughout Phase 2 and teach players to prioritize targets ("kill the support first or the others won't stay down"). This creates a recurring tactical pattern that pays off when the Ramjagodar appears and does the same thing at far greater scale.

Trap/terrain specialist (suggested CR 9–11): An asura that makes environments more dangerous rather than fighting directly — corrupts ground, creates hazardous zones, warps local geometry. Useful for asura-held territory sequences (the Demon Road, Vatra's burned forest, Candela's outer approaches). Fits the asura theme of corrupting creation itself — the creature doesn't just occupy a space, it ruins it.

Swarm-type (suggested CR 2–3): A mass of tiny malformed spirit-fragments functioning as a swarm rather than individual creatures. Not full asuras with regeneration and DR — lesser entities formed from the residue of asura activity or spiritual runoff of corrupted souls that didn't fully coalesce. Low-level environmental hazard for flavor encounters; explains what happens in areas with long-term asura presence (e.g., the territory around the Oracle's Sanctum).

The Apocalypse: The ten heroes sought to destroy demonic influence permanently by opening and then severing the connection to the Underworld using a powerful magitek artifact. The plan backfired, allowing demons to physically enter the worlds. Over 95% of the demon population was destroyed during the ensuing war, with the vast majority of casualties occurring in their assault on Heaven.

Environment: A generally featureless grey plain. Powerful demon lords (Asura Ranas) can create features — bleak mountain ranges, serrated hills, or other wicked formations — that serve as their strongholds.

The PCs' Visit: Brief and intense. They must fight through progressively more powerful groups of demons to reach Tornsoul's stronghold.

The 10th Warlock — Tornsoul (Sarena Tal)
Human Summoner
Willingly became the Warden of the Underworld after the war. She died in the casting of the spell to open the gate to the Underworld, but this was part of the Plan. Each of the other nine heroes granted her a portion of their mythic power, hoping this reservoir would protect her from corruption.
Before descending, she merged her form with her Eidolon, transforming into a being of immense power.
The demons corrupted her through this vector — spiritually scratching away portions of the Eidolon's soul over a century to create an opening. This had unintended consequences: seeing the corruption taking hold, Sarena began torturing demons endlessly, consuming their soul essence to maintain her mythic abilities, but sinking deeper into corruption with each century.
Now a being of pure revenge and hate, possessed of untold mythic power. Plans to return to the Nine Worlds at the head of a demon army to destroy the plane once and for all.
The ultimate encounter of the campaign. She calls upon the expanded power of her Eidolon, demonic essence she has consumed, and her own vastly overpowered mythic abilities. A near-insurmountable challenge.
The PCs can attempt to reason with her, but success is next to impossible. The only true way to defeat her is destruction.
Death in the Underworld is permanent — for Tornsoul and for the PCs. No resurrection.
Her death saps significant strength from the remaining demons and leaves them leaderless. The Underworld endures, but its denizens will be unable to affect the Nine Worlds again for centuries
