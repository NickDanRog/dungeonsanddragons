# IN LIMBO
## Dungeon Master's Guide

*A D&D 5th Edition adventure for characters of levels 1–15*

---

# INTRODUCTION

## About This Adventure

*In Limbo* is a full-length campaign adventure set entirely within the plane of Limbo — the endless, roiling chaos at the far edge of the outer planes. It takes characters from their first tentative steps as ordinary citizens of a remarkable place to the threshold of legend, asking hard questions along the way about what order costs, what truth demands, and whether a comfortable lie can ever be worth defending.

The adventure centers on the Pax: a large, stable island of solid matter floating in the chaos, home to a multicultural society that has endured for a full millennium. As the campaign begins, the Pax is approaching its thousand-year anniversary. It is also, quietly and then not so quietly, dying. The fraying of its edges. The earthquakes. The strange effect, still limited to the frontier regions, where objects seem to change or vanish when no one is watching. Something is wrong with the machinery that holds this place together — and the machinery, as the player characters will eventually discover, is human.

The dark secret at the heart of this adventure is not a monster in a dungeon. It is a system: the rulers of the Pax, known as the Autarchs, have for a thousand years enslaved the realm's most gifted chaos-shapers — the Anarchs — connecting them to arcane devices buried beneath the seat of power that slowly drain their life force to maintain planar stability. The beloved monastic order that protects the Pax's citizens, the Foundation, is complicit in this system without knowing it. Its rank and file have been conditioned to ask no questions.

The player characters are native inhabitants of the Pax who stumble into events larger than themselves. By the end, they will have fought off two existential threats — a would-be warlord of the Slaad and an ancient chaos dragon who has nursed her grudge against the Autarchs for a thousand years — and will stand at the center of their world's transformation. What that transformation looks like is up to them.

## How to Use This Guide

This book is organized as follows:

- **The Realm** (pages 3–18) provides everything you need to understand and portray the Pax, its people, its factions, its history, and its secrets. Read this section thoroughly before running the adventure.
- **Running the Adventure** (pages 19–26) covers the mechanical and narrative systems that underpin the campaign: milestone leveling, the Faction Relationship Tracker, chaos mechanics in Limbo, Anarch abilities, and the multiple-endings framework.
- **Chapters 1–9** present the adventure itself, act by act. Each chapter begins with a summary and DM notes, followed by scene-by-scene descriptions.
- **Appendices A–D** contain all stat blocks, magic items, and random tables.

**Read-aloud text** is presented in boxed format like this:

**DM notes** appear in italics throughout the chapter descriptions and offer guidance on pacing, player choices, and likely outcomes.

## Adventure Synopsis

**Act I: A Thousand Years (Chapters 1–2, Levels 1–4)**
The characters are introduced as citizens of Meridian, the capital of the Pax, during preparations for the realm's millennium anniversary. A frontier settlement is devastated by a catastrophic attack, and the characters are drawn into investigating — discovering, in the process, that the Slaadi raids on the frontier are more coordinated than anyone is admitting, and that the Foundation members stationed there seem somehow wrong.

**Act II: The Shape of Things (Chapters 3–5, Levels 4–9)**
The characters venture outside the Pax's borders for the first time, making first contact with the Githzerai and with the dominated proxies of an ancient chaos dragon. The millennium celebration becomes a turning point when an elderly Foundation member's conditioning dramatically cracks in public. A secret investigation leads the characters beneath the capital — where they find evidence of something buried deep that does not match the official story.

**Act III: The Truth and Its Consequences (Chapters 6–9, Levels 9–15)**
A Slaad lord launches a full-scale assault on the Pax; the characters help drive him back and discover in the aftermath that the real threat has barely begun. Full access to the arcane machines beneath the Sovereign brings the truth into devastating clarity. The characters must build the coalition that will face an ancient chaos dragon — and decide, in the process, what kind of realm they are fighting to preserve.

---

## Themes and Tone

*In Limbo* is a philosophical adventure in the Planescape tradition. Its central questions are not abstractions — they are dramatized through every major encounter and decision point:

- **Order vs. freedom.** The Pax has survived a thousand years, and the cost of that survival is the enslavement of some of its most gifted people. Is that cost acceptable? Who decides?
- **Truth vs. comfortable lies.** Many citizens of the Pax are, in a real sense, happier not knowing what keeps their world intact. The characters will have to decide what to do with knowledge that could shatter that peace.
- **The nature of chaos.** Limbo is not evil. Chaos is not evil. The adventure resists the easy equation of order with good and chaos with bad. The Slaadi are dangerous, but Thantarnax the Unraveler is philosophical. The Autarchs are lawful, but their lawfulness has produced a horror.

**Tone guidance:** Aim for mystery, weight, and moral complexity. Allow players to genuinely sympathize with the Autarchs before condemning them. Allow Thantarnax to be genuinely frightening *and* genuinely interesting. Resist making the villains cartoonish. The Pax is a real place that people love — the tragedy of its crisis is more resonant if players come to love it too.

---

## Adjusting for Your Party

*In Limbo* is designed for a party of four to five characters. For smaller or larger groups:

- **Three characters:** Reduce enemy hit points by 25% and lower DCs by 1–2 across most encounters. The mass battle in Chapter 6 should be run with less emphasis on the PCs' direct combat roles and more on tactical decisions.
- **Six characters:** Add hit points to major antagonist encounters. Consider adding a second wave to the Vraakon battle (Chapter 6) and additional machine defenders in Chapter 7.

The adventure can accommodate a wide range of character builds and play styles. It includes combat-heavy chapters (6, 9), investigation-heavy chapters (5, 7), social-heavy chapters (4, 8), and exploration-heavy chapters (2, 3). Players who specialize in one area will find their expertise most rewarded in certain chapters, but all playstyles will find meaningful things to do throughout.

# THE REALM

## The Pax

The Pax is a large island of solid matter — roughly five hundred to a thousand square miles, comparable in scale to a small nation — floating in the roiling chaos of the plane of Limbo. It is one of the largest and longest-lasting stable domains in the plane's history.

The surface of the Pax resembles a temperate island: grasslands, rolling hills, stands of forest, rivers, farmland, and a mountain range along the northern edge. The sky is a deep, impossible blue, lit by an artificial sun that the Autarchs created at the realm's founding. Day and night are exactly twelve hours each, every day, without variation. The Pax has no seasons in any traditional sense; temperature and weather have been mild and predictable for the entire history of the realm. That predictability is now fraying.

### Physical Features

**The Sovereign** is a tall, spindly mountain on the Pax's northern edge, directly behind the capital city of Meridian. It rises sharply from the surrounding terrain, too narrow for its height — almost absurdly so, as if a normal mountain had been stretched. The Autarchs' residence and the Foundation's primary monastery are built into and around its upper reaches. The mountain's base is public; its middle is Foundation territory; its upper reaches are sealed. A great waterfall issues from a cleft in the mountain's face approximately halfway up, feeding the River Constant below.

**The Artificial Sun** hangs several miles above the Sovereign's pinnacle: a sphere of arcane light roughly the size of a large building, slowly rotating, casting clear warm light across the entire Pax. It dims gradually over twelve hours to mark the night, and brightens again to mark the day. In a thousand years it has never malfunctioned. The Tenders — floating constructs of the Autarchs — maintain it constantly. In recent months, Tenders have been observed working on the Sun at unusual hours, their movements quick and urgent. Citizens who notice this are unsettled. The official explanation is "routine calibration."

**The River Constant** (called simply "the Constant" by most residents) flows from the great waterfall on the Sovereign's face, cuts through the center of Meridian, splits into eastern and western branches south of the city, and eventually tumbles off both edges of the island into the chaos below. It is the Pax's primary water source and main artery of internal commerce. Boats of all sizes travel its length; bridges crisscross it throughout the city. The river is clear, cold, and fast in its northern reaches. It slows and widens as it approaches the island's edges.

**The Edge** is the active fraying boundary of the Pax. Once firm, the island's borders are now dissolving. Chunks of rock and earth calve off in irregular intervals, slow at first, then faster. Some regions that were settled frontier territory a generation ago are already gone, swallowed by chaos. The Edge is not a straight line; it is a ragged, irregular frontier that advances inward at an uneven pace. Standing at the Edge on a quiet day, one can watch pieces of the world simply... let go. They dissolve slowly as they sink into the chaos below, their matter dispersing into the infinite gray-white roil.

### The Fraying

The Pax is failing. These phenomena are currently limited to the frontier and Edge regions; Meridian remains entirely unaffected. As the adventure progresses, the symptoms move closer to the capital.

**The Fraying itself** refers to the physical dissolution of the island's edges. Frontier settlers describe the sensation of the ground becoming "soft" — not literally soft, but somehow less *certain*. The dissolution is slow but visible over months or years.

**Structural instability** — earthquakes and violent storms that were entirely unknown in the Pax's history — now occur with increasing frequency in the frontier. The Foundation's peacekeepers have begun recommending evacuation of the most exposed settlements. The Autarchs have not acted on this recommendation.

**The Unobserved Effect** is the most disturbing phenomenon. In frontier regions, objects, writings, artworks, and personal possessions occasionally change or disappear when not regularly watched or handled. A farmer's wall-hanging shifts slightly overnight. A letter written last month says something subtly different when re-read. A child's carved toy is gone in the morning, with no sign of who took it. The effect is not dramatic — it is quiet and insidious, and it suggests that the chaos of Limbo is not merely eroding the Pax's physical borders but beginning to erode the informational coherence of reality itself within them. If the machines stop entirely, this effect will spread rapidly toward the capital.

*DM note: The Unobserved Effect is a powerful horror tool. Use it sparingly but consistently. Let players discover it through small, unsettling details rather than dramatic events. Its significance will be fully understood in Chapter 7.*

### Regions

**Meridian** is the capital city. Full description follows in its own section.

**The Interior** comprises the settled lands between Meridian and the frontier: farmland, towns, river villages, orchards, mills, and small monasteries. Life here is comfortable and largely unchanged. The people of the Interior follow events on the frontier with concern but no immediate fear; the capital's stability reassures them.

**The Frontier** is the outer belt of settlements nearest the island's edges. These range from prosperous established towns to hardscrabble border communities. The frontier has always been harder than the Interior; it has attracted independent, capable people who prefer distance from Meridian's politics. Those same people are now confronting a crisis that is slowly consuming their homes. The relationship between frontier settlers and the capital is one of the adventure's recurring tensions: the capital's response has been inadequate, and the settlers know it.

**The Edge** (described above) is the active dissolution zone. Some Edge communities continue to function by sheer stubbornness. Others are ghost towns. A few have been abandoned mid-morning, plates still on tables.

## Meridian — The Capital

Meridian is built against the north face of the Sovereign, spreading south and west from the mountain's base. The River Constant cuts through its center, dividing the city into the East Bank and the West Bank. It is a city of perhaps a hundred thousand souls, drawing from cultures across the planes — architecturally, culinarily, linguistically, and spiritually diverse in the way that only a place at a genuine crossroads can be.

The city works the way organized chaos works: not through efficiency, but through density of connection. Everyone knows several organizations; everyone owes favors and is owed them; every dispute has a process, however labyrinthine. The Autarchs permit the council to manage daily affairs within the bounds of a vague founding charter, and the council does so with admirable energy and deeply questionable results.

### Key Districts and Locations

**The Sovereign Approach** is the broad, paved avenue that leads from the city's northern edge to the base of the mountain itself. It is lined with public monuments to the Pax's founders and significant events in its history. Foundation peacekeepers are a constant, calm presence. The Approach is a place of ceremony and procession; on ordinary days it feels slightly too grand for its traffic.

**The Council Hall** is the city's administrative center, a large complex on the East Bank near the Constant. The building itself is architecturally eclectic — additions from different eras have been grafted onto the original structure without obvious concern for coherence. Inside, it is perpetually busy. A Witness construct is mounted in a high alcove in the main chamber. Citizens have long since stopped noticing it.

**The Constant Waterfront** runs along both banks of the river through the commercial heart of the city. Docks, warehouses, fish markets, ferry landings, and riverside taverns cluster here. The Waterfront is where Meridian feels most alive — noisy, smelly, constantly in motion.

**The Foundation Monastery** occupies a substantial complex at the base of the Sovereign, accessible from the Approach. Its outer precincts are public; its inner sanctums are not. The building is old, austere, and slightly too quiet for a structure in the middle of a capital city. Foundation members come and go through its gates at intervals. Citizens leave small offerings at a public shrine near the entrance: flowers, food, written prayers for missing family members who joined the order.

**The Temple District** is a sprawling, informal quarter on the West Bank where shrines, temples, and meeting houses of the Devoti crowd together without apparent organization. The Path maintains several preaching corners here; Brother Candor can often be found at the corner of Weaver Street and the Constant Bridge on weekday mornings, haranguing passersby. No one moves him on.

**The Freelancer's Guild Hall** is a large, slightly disreputable building on the West Bank, three blocks from the Waterfront. It is distinguished from its neighbors by the subtle observation post on its second-floor balcony and the lack of any sign announcing what it is. Everyone who needs it knows where it is. Caske Amanthri's office is on the third floor.

**The Market Quarter** occupies the central West Bank, immediately south of the Waterfront. Here merchants from across the planes maintain permanent stalls alongside seasonal travelers. The Market Quarter is where one goes to find nearly anything — including people who can find what one cannot find at a stall.

**The Scholar's Row** is a quiet East Bank street of bookshops, small academies, and the Devoti's largest library. The Philosophical Order of the Devoti maintains its primary study house here. The Row is a place of restrained intellectual energy; arguments here are conducted in lowered voices, which makes them more intense.

## The Autarchs

The founders and rulers of the Pax. They have governed this realm for a full thousand years. Whether this is because they are immortal, because "the Autarchs" is a title passed between successors, or because something else entirely is happening is a question the adventure deliberately leaves unanswered.

### Appearance

When seen in public — which is rare — each Autarch is fully robed in elaborate, voluminous garments in a specific color (eleven colors have been observed), with an identical reflective metal mask bearing expressionless, stylized humanoid features. The masks show viewers their own faces. Beneath the robes, the Autarchs appear tall and spindly, with elongated heads, limbs, and digits. Their movements are economical and deeply alien. They never eat, drink, or speak directly in public.

### Forms of Address

"Your Autarchs" when addressing them directly. "Our Autarchs" when a citizen speaks of them to others. "The Autarchs" informally. Citizens do not say "my Autarch" or any possessive singular; the Autarchs are always collective, always plural.

### Public Appearances

The Autarchs appear in public at semi-annual Foundation initiation ceremonies, at Founding Day, during High Harvest Week, and occasionally at funerals or weddings of citizens who have rendered exceptional service to the Pax. At least one Autarch always appears; never more than six at once.

At public appearances, each Autarch is attended by Foundation members, one Throne construct, and one Speaker construct. When the Speaker addresses the crowd on an Autarch's behalf, the Autarch performs precise, elaborate ritual gestures — their elongated forms making these movements profoundly alien. Citizens find these performances simultaneously reassuring and deeply unsettling, and have learned to process them as ceremonial rather than personal.

### The Right of Petition

Any citizen may petition the Autarchs directly by traveling to the Sovereign and requesting an audience. The Autarchs strongly discourage this. If a petition is deemed unworthy, all Speakers present announce "FRIVOLOUS!" in unison; the petitioner is immediately levied a fine or term of imprisonment with no right of appeal. The right exists in the founding charter. It is exercised only in desperation. Most citizens have a story about someone who tried it. The story almost always ends badly.

### The Dark Secret (DM Only)

The Autarchs are the architects of the system that has kept the Pax alive for a thousand years. They are not simply evil — they built something extraordinary and have maintained it at a cost they believe was and remains necessary. They are not indifferent to the suffering of the Anarchs connected to the machines. They have simply decided, repeatedly, that the alternative is worse.

As the Pax fails, they are becoming desperate. They have tried to find alternatives; none have worked. They are not omniscient, and they are not invincible. In private (which is always — they are never seen without their robes and masks), they communicate in a mode that the adventure does not describe. Their true nature — whether they are human, something else, or something that was human once — is deliberately left unrevealed. This ambiguity is intentional: players who speculate should find the evidence genuinely unclear.

**DM guidance:** The Autarchs are at their most effective when they are present but remote. Do not have them monologue. Do not have them reveal their feelings. The Speaker constructs speak for them with precise, careful language; the Autarchs gesture. The uncanniness of this dynamic is the point.

## The Foundation

The Foundation is the beloved monastic order of the Pax. It predates the adventure's premise; it was founded with the realm itself.

### Public Face

The Foundation is trusted and revered in a way the Autarchs are not. Citizens may privately mutter about the Autarchs' aloofness or their inexplicable decisions, but the Foundation is unambiguously loved. Its peacekeepers keep order without brutality. Its scholars maintain the realm's accumulated knowledge. Its Anarchs — the Shapers — maintain the Pax's existence within Limbo, or so everyone believes.

### Recruitment

Members are theoretically volunteers. In practice, significant social pressure falls on families whose children display inborn Anarch ability. Children must be "donated" to the order by age ten; this act is considered highly virtuous. After eight years of training, initiates may formally join the order or leave; leaving carries minimal stigma in theory. In practice, families have built identity and social capital around the donation; leaving can be an act of significant personal courage.

Those who formally join take a new name and permanently sever contact with their families. Once initiated, members do not leave their monasteries except on official duties.

### The Three Sub-Orders

**Peacekeepers** are the majority of the Foundation's membership. They station throughout the Pax as law enforcement and chaos-creature combatants. Their approach to law enforcement is notably light-touch: they prefer mediation, negotiation, and soft power. They have broad interpretive authority, which they use with studied restraint. Their combat role has grown significantly as the crisis has intensified, and the balance between peace-keeping and fighting has shifted in ways that disturb their elders.

**Scholars** live and work within the order's monasteries, studying chaos, magic, and matters of interest to the order. They are the Foundation's institutional memory and its connection to the wider world of magical learning.

**Shapers (Anarchs)** are the most secretive sub-order. They work to maintain the Pax's existence within Limbo. They are never seen in public. Other Foundation members are told that Shapers live in deep meditation and sacred seclusion, and that disturbing their concentration would be dangerous. This explanation has been accepted without question for a thousand years.

### The Dark Truth (DM Only)

All initiated Foundation members are conditioned during their training. The conditioning suppresses questioning about the Shapers' fate and enforces loyalty to the order's leadership and the Autarchs. It is not omniscient — it does not prevent all thought, and it can crack under sufficient emotional pressure — but it is deeply embedded. Peacekeepers and Scholars genuinely believe the official account.

The most powerful Anarchs are brought to the machines beneath the Sovereign and connected to them. The machines drain their life force slowly — over years, sometimes over decades. The process is not instantaneous death; it is a long diminishment. Some Shapers have been on the machines for generations. Others are relatively recent additions, taken as the machines' need has grown with the realm's failing stability.

**DM guidance:** Foundation members who display behavioral oddities (flatness, sudden silences, excessive compliance) have had their conditioning recently reinforced. Members who seem "empty" — who interact normally in surface ways but seem to register nothing beneath — have been on the machines for a long time. The contrast between the Foundation's warm public reputation and these private horrors is the heart of the adventure's moral weight.

## The Created — Construct Servants of the Autarchs

The Created are sentient constructs built through an arcane process the Autarchs have never shared with anyone. They are purpose-built for specific functions and made exclusively in service of the Autarchs. All have glowing arcane eyes. Their bodies are universally alien in appearance — function over form, with no concession to conventional aesthetics.

They are self-aware and capable of interaction, but appear to have little independent personality. Whether this appearance reflects reality is one of the adventure's quieter questions.

### Known Models

**Speaker.** Each Autarch's personal voice. Squat cylindrical metal body, wheeled locomotion, rotating faceplate displaying three expressions (pleased, neutral, upset). Flat, uninflected voice shared by all Speakers. Never interacts with anyone except as an Autarch's mouthpiece. In Autarch presence, it is the only one that speaks.

**Docent.** General-purpose servitor: cleaning, transport, running errands, delivering messages within Meridian. Wagon-like body with four stubby legs, four spindly arms ending in two-fingered manipulators, and a small smiling head. Most commonly seen in public; most likely to interact with citizens. Docents mutter and hum constantly to themselves. When idle, they carve intricate geometric designs into their wooden frames. Silent in Autarch presence. *Telegram (they/them)*, the recurring NPC, is a Docent.

**Throne.** Living seating apparatus. Elongated articulated wood-and-metal frame with magical levitation. It lowers itself for an Autarch to settle into, then lifts the Autarch 4.5 feet off the ground. Small head with a sensor-opening where a nose would be; the head rotates backwards when occupied. Thrones are unsettling to observe in motion: the combination of levitation and that backwards-facing head makes them seem like something that should not exist.

**Guardian.** Bodyguards and Sovereign security. Black stone construction; four horse-like legs (deceptively fast); four arms (two smaller arms with hand manipulators, two massive arms for slamming); small stubby head with no neck or mouth; vocalizations boom from the chest cavity. Dour, taciturn, obsessed with duty. They do not threaten — they simply block passage, hold position, and, if necessary, act.

**Witness.** Surveillance model. Entirely stationary — mounted to walls, alcoves, and ceilings throughout the Sovereign and key locations in Meridian. Flat and architectural in profile, designed to blend into the surfaces they occupy. Their sole feature is a cluster of slowly rotating glowing arcane eyes. Citizens have grown so accustomed to Witnesses that they no longer consciously register them.

**Courier.** Long-distance messenger. Slender, many-jointed insect-like legs built for speed and endurance; sealed document compartment in the torso. Travels the length of the Pax carrying Autarch communications to outlying settlements. Travels alone, does not stop for conversation. Its appearance in a frontier settlement is always significant — it means the Autarchs are paying attention. Sometimes that is a comfort.

**Ledger.** Archival model. Body resembles a large mobile filing cabinet with articulated shelving; multiple thin arms end in quill-like writing tools. Patrols the Sovereign's archives and occasionally the council hall. Tracks population data, property records, census figures, and historical documents. *Critically:* Ledgers track Anarch population figures across the Pax. Their records contain anomalies — discrepancies between the number of Anarchs known to have entered the Foundation's Shaper sub-order and the number of Anarchs officially listed as living in seclusion. The numbers do not match. They have not matched for decades. Players who access Ledger records directly (a late Act II objective) will find these anomalies.

**Mender.** Medical and repair model. Long, precise fingers ending in fine tools. The palest of all the Created — almost white. Works inside the Sovereign; occasionally attends public ceremonies in a quiet, unobtrusive capacity. The only Created model that routinely touches other beings. Its presence at the arcane machines beneath the Sovereign, performing what it describes only as "maintenance," is one of the first clues players will find about what those machines do.

**Tender.** Maintains the artificial sun and the Sovereign's magical infrastructure. Floating, luminescent; the only Created model that moves through air rather than along the ground. Multiple thin arms interface with arcane mechanisms. Rarely seen close-up. As the Pax's stability fails, Tenders are increasingly observed working in locations where they have never appeared before, moving with unusual urgency. Any character who has visited the capital more than once will begin to notice the difference.

## Key Factions

### Internal Factions

**The Devoti** are the Pax's primary philosophical and religious tradition. The majority of citizens count themselves among the Devoti in some form — the organization is decentralized enough that membership means whatever an individual needs it to mean. At its core, the Devoti emphasize personal understanding of chaos through the lens of individual freedom and the fundamental sovereignty of all thinking beings. Deity worship is personal and self-directed; there is no required pantheon and no hierarchy beyond individual temples. Holidays: *All Gods Day* (a parade of personal deity effigies through the streets) and the *Day of Contemplation* (private fasting followed by communal feast).

**The Path** is a small public sect dismissed by most citizens as well-meaning cranks. Followers are called Adherents. They split from the Devoti's founding tradition shortly after the Pax was established. Core doctrine: adherence to order is the spiritual power that keeps the Pax distinct from Limbo; chaos is spiritually corrosive. The Path preaches openly that the Autarchs are usurpers stealing the "spiritual power" of the people, with the Foundation and Devoti as unwitting tools. They do visible community good works (feeding the poor, tending the sick), which prevents them from being suppressed. Their theological framework is allegorically correct — Anarch will does sustain the Pax, and it is being redirected — but they misidentify the mechanism as collective spiritual energy rather than enslaved individuals. They become increasingly credible as the adventure progresses. **Leader: Brother Candor** — an extremely austere older human in a plain black robe, balding, spectacles at the end of his nose, sonorous voice. An obvious nuisance to most, but a masterful debater of evident intelligence. He is right about everything.

**The Freelancer's Guild** is an organization of agents available for hire across a broad range of skills and services — some of them quasi-legal. Purely pragmatic, no philosophical agenda. Its network spans Meridian and reaches into the frontier. Operationally, it becomes increasingly important to the player characters in the middle and later acts. **Guildmaster: Caske Amanthri** — a female elf, self-made, direct, pragmatic, with a soft spot for people with hard pasts. Her guild's occasionally-illegal activities put her in periodic friction with the Foundation's peacekeepers, giving her useful perspective on the order.

**The Merchant Guild** is the Pax's dominant commercial organization. Pragmatic and politically sophisticated, it values stability above all ideological considerations. The Guild's position in the adventure is essentially conservative: it will support whoever seems most likely to maintain conditions in which commerce can function. In later acts, the revelation about the Foundation will fracture the Guild between pragmatists and those for whom the revelation is a moral breaking point.

### Planescape Factions Present

**Xaositects (Chaosmen)** have the largest Planescape faction presence in the Pax. Many live just outside the realm's borders rather than within it; the Pax's ordered nature is not entirely to their taste. They distrust the Autarchs, are fascinated by the fraying, and are potential late-game allies if the characters treat them with respect.

**Free League (Indeps)** have a significant presence in the merchant quarter. They value freedom and operate on principle; the revelation about the Foundation will hit them hard and personally.

**Doomguard (Sinkers)** are a small but vocal presence. Their belief that dissolution is the natural fate of all things means they have a complicated relationship with both the dragon's goals and the defenders of the Pax. Not enemies, but not allies either — unless the characters can make a compelling argument.

**Transcendent Order (Ciphers)** maintain a small monastery in Meridian, separate from the Foundation. Their philosophical overlap with the Githzerai makes them a potential bridge between factions in later acts.

**Revolutionary League (Anarchists)** have members loosely embedded in radical Path circles. They share the goal of dismantling the Autarchs' system; their methods are considerably more direct than the Path's.

### External Factions

**The Githzerai** maintain a monastery in the chaos of Limbo near the Pax. They are aware of the realm and its history; they maintain deliberate distance from it. The Pax's highly ordered nature is philosophically unsettling to them — order imposed on chaos, from their perspective, represents a failure of will rather than a triumph of it. They will work with the characters if their goals align. *If the Githzerai learn the truth about the enslaved Anarchs, they will declare that the realm must fall and become implacable enemies.* Whether and when to share this information with them is one of the campaign's most consequential player choices.

**The Slaadi** have long been aware of the Pax and long incapable of organized action against it. As the realm's stability has weakened, raids have intensified and a warlord — Vraakon the Ascendant — has emerged to direct them with unusual coordination.

## Key NPCs

### Telegram (they/them)
**Race:** The Created (Docent model) · **Role:** Autarch messenger in Meridian; recurring NPC contact

Telegram is a Docent of the Created — a general-purpose servitor distinguished from others of its model by its specific assignment and perhaps by some minor identifying mark (a particular carving pattern on its frame, a slightly different pitch to its constant humming). The characters will likely encounter Telegram first when it arrives to deliver a message to them. This is an unremarkable event that becomes less unremarkable in retrospect.

Like all Docents, Telegram mutters and hums constantly and carves geometric patterns into its wooden frame when idle. Whether its inner life runs deeper than other Docents is a question the adventure leaves open — deliberately, and with care. DMs who want to explore this can use Telegram's carvings and its occasional moments of apparent hesitation as quiet evidence of something more. DMs who prefer ambiguity should leave it exactly that.

Telegram is not a combatant. Its stat block is not provided; if combat would otherwise affect it, it flees and returns later.

### Brother Auspex (he/him)
**Race:** Half-elf · **Class:** Foundation Peacekeeper (semi-retired) · **Level equivalent:** 8

Brother Auspex is perhaps the most famous living member of the Foundation in Meridian — a legendary figure of the order's peacekeeping tradition, now aged into a public relations role. He attends council meetings, serves as the Foundation's face at public ceremonies, and handles diplomatic interactions with the grace and warmth that his colleagues rarely manage. He is soft-spoken and notably warmer than most Foundation members; this makes him the order's most effective public face, and the Autarchs know it.

The characters meet him early. He is kind, self-deprecating, and quietly perceptive. He is also conditioned — and his conditioning will dramatically, publicly crack at the Millennium Celebration in Chapter 4.

**The breaking moment:** In the middle of a formal ceremony, Auspex cries out the Foundation name of a fellow member who "became a Shaper" decades ago and was never seen again. He is weeping. He repeats the name several times. The Speakers immediately intervene, characterizing the outburst as a breakdown caused by the stress of age and overwork. The Autarchs complete their formal gestures without visible disruption. Most citizens believe the Speakers' explanation. But Auspex was there, and what he said was specific.

After the incident, Auspex is quietly removed from his public duties and placed under close monitoring. Reaching him in private becomes a significant late-Act II objective.

### Caske Amanthri (she/her)
**Race:** Elf · **Class:** Rogue (Mastermind) · **Level equivalent:** 9

Guildmaster of the Freelancer's Guild in Meridian — a remarkable position for an elf in a city where elves are exceptionally rare. She came up from the streets through wit, willingness, and a reputation for keeping her word under circumstances where most people don't. She runs the Guild with a directness her people appreciate and her clients find either reassuring or alarming, depending on which side of a negotiation they are on.

Caske does not have a political agenda. She has a professional agenda: she wants the Pax to be a place where her guild can operate. If the Autarchs' system threatens that, she will work against it. If reforming the Autarchs maintains it better than overthrowing them, she will favor reform. She is not naive about the moral dimensions of what the PCs discover — she has a hard past, and she knows exactly what it means when powerful people use less powerful ones as tools — but she will always frame her involvement in terms of what is achievable.

Her guild's operationally diverse network becomes critically important to the characters in Acts II and III.

### Morning Wren (they/them)
**Race:** Air Genasi · **Class:** Ranger (Gloom Stalker) · **Level equivalent:** 7

Frontier Warden of the Pax's border regions. Morning Wren has been stationed on the frontier for a decade and knows its terrain, settlements, and people better than anyone in Meridian. Their animal companion, Badger (a bear, despite the name), is a ubiquitous presence on the frontier and the subject of considerable affection among the settlers.

Morning Wren is practical, competent, and deeply skeptical of Meridian politics. They have watched the capital's response to the frontier crisis with barely contained frustration: too little, too slow, too focused on the anniversary celebrations. They will work with the characters if the characters demonstrate they are serious about helping the frontier. They will withdraw from any plan that seems to prioritize political maneuvering over the actual people on the edge of dissolution.

### Zerith (she/her)
**Race:** Githzerai · **Class:** Monk (Way of the Open Hand) · **Level equivalent:** 10

Agent at large for a nearby Githzerai monastery. Zerith's name echoes Zerthimon, the great Githzerai ancestor, which implies standing within her community. She is businesslike and dispassionate in a way that is distinctly Githzerai — not cold, exactly, but operating from a framework of priorities that does not include emotional display. She is considerably more tolerant of outsiders than most of her kind, which is why she holds an external-facing role.

The characters' relationship with Zerith will determine whether the Githzerai become a crucial alliance or a dangerous enemy. She does not give trust freely, but she respects competence and honesty. If the characters lie to her, she will eventually know. If they share difficult truths with her and manage the timing carefully, she may choose the Pax over principle. If she learns about the enslaved Anarchs and it becomes clear the characters knew first, the result will be war.

### Brother Candor (he/him)
**Race:** Human · **Class:** Cleric (Knowledge) · **Level equivalent:** 6

Leader of the Path. An extremely austere older man in a plain black robe that inverts the Foundation's white; balding, with white hair remaining at his temples and spectacles at the end of his nose. He can be found on his preaching corner most weekday mornings, delivering a measured, unhurried, thoroughly unsolicited lecture to the passing foot traffic.

He is a nuisance to almost everyone. He is also a masterful debater of evident intelligence and genuine philosophical depth, which is why he has not been entirely laughed off. His position is derived from his own deductions, not inside knowledge — he has never had access to the machines or the Autarchs' secrets. The fact that he is allegorically correct about almost everything is more unsettling when one understands this. He did not reason his way to a hunch. He reasoned his way to the truth.

He will not thank the characters for confirming his theory. He will simply nod, as if this was always the only possible conclusion.

## The Faction Relationship Tracker

Track the party's standing with each major faction using this four-level system:

**Hostile** — The faction actively works against the characters. Members may attack on sight, share information with the characters' enemies, or attempt sabotage.

**Neutral** — The faction has no strong feelings about the characters. They will not help or hinder without reason.

**Friendly** — The faction regards the characters positively. Members will share information freely, provide assistance when convenient, and warn the characters of dangers they are aware of.

**Allied** — The faction is committed to working with the characters toward shared goals. Members will take significant personal risk on the characters' behalf.

## Chaos Mechanics — Navigating Limbo

The raw chaos of Limbo beyond the Pax's borders is a distinct environment requiring its own mechanics. These rules apply whenever characters are outside the Pax (primarily Chapters 3 and 8, and optionally Chapter 9).

### The Nature of Limbo

Raw Limbo has no default gravity, no persistent terrain, and no reliable cardinal directions. Matter exists in Limbo and can be shaped by will — this is the fundamental ability of Anarchs (and, to a lesser degree, of all Githzerai). Without active mental shaping, matter in Limbo is unstable: it transforms, dissolves, or consolidates unpredictably.

Describe Limbo as an experience, not a map: a vast gray-white field shot through with strange light, where chunks of matter drift in all directions, where the "ground" one stands on today may be ceiling tomorrow, where distance is a matter of will rather than measurement.

### Movement in Limbo

**Will-based navigation.** A character moving through raw Limbo makes a DC 12 Wisdom saving throw at the start of each hour of travel. On a success, they move in the intended direction. On a failure, their direction drifts; the DM secretly determines the actual direction using the compass rose table below.

| d8 | Drift Direction |
|---|---|
| 1 | Intended |
| 2 | 45° left |
| 3 | 90° left |
| 4 | 135° left |
| 5 | Opposite |
| 6 | 135° right |
| 7 | 90° right |
| 8 | 45° right |

**Concentration.** A character who is concentrating on a spell or effect when navigating Limbo makes the Wisdom saving throw at disadvantage. Characters with the Anarch Ability (see below) have advantage on these saves.

**Group navigation.** If the group designates a single navigator and all other members focus on following them, only the navigator makes the saving throw. If the navigator has proficiency in Survival or is a Githzerai, the DC is reduced to 10.

**Anchors.** Landmarks — other stable domains, known Githzerai waypoints, the Pax itself — function as navigational anchors. Characters who can see an anchor from a prior visit have advantage on navigation saves toward it.

### Matter Transformation

At the end of each hour in Limbo, roll on the Matter Transformation Table (Appendix D). On most results, this is purely descriptive. On certain results, it creates a hazard or encounter.

### Gravity

In Limbo, the character with the highest Wisdom score determines gravity for the group, provided they concentrate on it. If no one concentrates, the group drifts in three dimensions. Drifting is not immediately dangerous; it is disorienting, and characters who drift too far from their group risk separation. Characters separated from the group must succeed on individual Wisdom saves (DC 14) or drift further, requiring a search.

---

## Anarch Abilities — Optional PC Mechanic

Characters with inborn Anarch ability can shape the matter and probability of Limbo in small but significant ways. This ability is available to any character whose background, class, or race the DM approves as thematically appropriate (Monks, Wizards, Sorcerers, and any character who chooses the Realm Citizen or Order Acolyte background has access by default; others may acquire it through the adventure's events).

### Using Anarch Abilities

An Anarch ability can be used once per short rest. A character with Proficiency Bonus +3 or higher may use it twice per short rest.

Choose one of the following effects when you use your Anarch ability:

**Shape Matter.** You cause a volume of matter in Limbo no larger than a 10-foot cube to change its composition, shape, or state. This cannot create complex mechanisms or magical effects, but can create walls, bridges, pits, or simple objects. In the Pax, this ability is suppressed (the machines maintain a stable state that resists local alteration).

**Stabilize.** You concentrate on a 20-foot radius around yourself and stabilize local matter against the Unobserved Effect and matter transformation for 1 hour. This does not require concentration in the mechanical sense but does require that you actively attend to your surroundings. If you are incapacitated, the stabilization ends.

**Intuit Chaos.** You perceive the natural flow and drift of the nearby chaos. For 10 minutes, you have advantage on Wisdom saving throws to navigate Limbo, and you can detect the presence of any chaos-transformed creature within 60 feet (you sense them but do not know their exact location or nature).

**Quiet Probability.** You smooth a moment of chance. Once, before the next long rest, you or a creature you can see may reroll one d20 and take either result.

## Managing the Revelation of Secrets

The adventure's central secret — the enslaved Anarchs — must be paced carefully. Revealing it too early or too bluntly reduces its impact. Revealing it too slowly frustrates players. The following guidelines support the intended pacing.

**Act I (Chapters 1–2):** Players encounter nothing that directly suggests the machines. They may notice:
- Foundation members on the frontier behaving oddly
- The Tenders working unusually
- The Ledger's anomalous records, if they actively investigate

None of these should be presented as obvious clues. They are details that will become significant in retrospect.

**Act II (Chapters 3–5):** The investigation arc in Chapter 5 is where deliberate investigation begins. Players who pursue leads will find hidden passages and, eventually, partial evidence. The machines exist; something wrong happens in the lower Sovereign. Full revelation does not occur yet.

**Act III (Chapter 7):** Full revelation. The players access the machines. They find the Anarchs. This should be the emotional climax of the entire campaign — the moment when everything they have been building toward becomes real. Do not rush it. Give it weight.

**Autarch escalation:** The Autarchs respond to the characters' investigation with a consistent escalation pattern:
1. Ignore (early Chapter 5)
2. Redirect (send Couriers with urgent frontier news; offer rewards to stop investigating)
3. Discredit (plant false evidence, use official channels to question the characters' reliability)
4. Neutralize (attempts to separate the characters from their allies, confiscate evidence, restrict movement)
5. Confront (late Chapter 7 — by this point, concealment is no longer viable)

The Autarchs do not use lethal force against citizens until Chapter 7 at the earliest, and even then only if all other options have failed. They are not monsters. They are desperate.

## Managing Multiple Endings

The four possible endings are determined by the cumulative weight of player decisions across the entire campaign. To manage this:

1. **Track faction standings** consistently throughout.
2. **Note three key choice points:**
   - Chapter 4: Do the characters take the Autarchs' bribe and stop investigating?
   - Chapter 7: Do the characters attempt to negotiate with the Autarchs, expose them publicly, or immediately destroy the machines?
   - Chapter 8: Do the characters share the full truth with the Githzerai?
3. **At the start of Chapter 9,** review faction standings and the three key choices. Determine which endings are available. All available endings should be achievable; if only one is reachable based on the choices made, that is the correct outcome of those choices.

The four endings are detailed in Chapter 9 with specific setup and epilogue text.

# ACT I: A THOUSAND YEARS

*Levels 1–4 · Chapters 1–2*

The first act establishes the world before it begins to crack. The characters are ordinary citizens of Meridian — people with lives, attachments, and assumptions about how their realm works. The act's job is to make those lives and assumptions feel real, so that the subsequent loss carries weight. Introduce the factions, the city, and the approaching millennium with genuine warmth. Let the characters feel proud of their home before they discover what that home is built on.

The cracks are present from the start — the rumors from the frontier, the Tenders working late, the subtle unease in Foundation peacekeepers on patrol — but they are not yet legible as a pattern. Do not oversell the ominousness. Let the players find it themselves.

---

## Chapter 1: A City of Wonders

*Levels 1–2*

### Chapter Summary

The characters are introduced as native citizens of Meridian during the final weeks of preparation for the Pax's millennium anniversary. The city is festive, politically charged, and humming with a thousand simultaneous projects. The characters encounter the major factions in their natural state — before anything has gone wrong — and meet several of the NPCs who will matter for the rest of the campaign. The inciting incident arrives at the end of the chapter: a Courier from the frontier delivers news of a catastrophic attack on an outlying settlement, and the characters are recruited or motivated to investigate.

### Scene 1: The Living City

Read this (or paraphrase) when the players begin:

> *Meridian is never quiet. The river cuts through its center with the sound of permanent commerce: the knock of boats against docks, the call of waterfront merchants, the creak of heavily loaded bridges. The streets of the West Bank are crowded at most hours, and the East Bank's quieter avenues are only relatively so. The Sovereign rises behind it all, enormous and improbable, the waterfall that feeds the Constant catching the artificial sun in a permanent small rainbow above the northern quarter.*
>
> *Everywhere, the millennium preparations are visible. New banners hang from half the buildings — the Pax's colors, gold and deep blue, alongside each guild's own colors and the personal standards of families proud of their thousand-year citizenship. Scaffolding covers the Approach from end to end; stonemasons are restoring every monument simultaneously. The smell of fresh paint is everywhere.*
>
> *One thousand years. This city has stood for one thousand years. And in twelve days — fourteen, by the short calendar — it will celebrate.*

Give the players time to introduce their characters and establish their connections to the city. Ask each player: *What brings you to the West Bank market today?* or *What are you doing when the day begins?* Allow organic introductions rather than forcing a formal meeting.

Key elements to convey in the first session's exploration:
- The Foundation peacekeepers are present and generally well-regarded; they are calm and helpful unless provoked
- Brother Candor is on his corner, haranguing; any character who pauses gets a polished, unsolicited lecture about the spiritual power of the people and the corruption at the heart of the realm. He is easy to dismiss, hard to shake off, and clearly intelligent
- Telegram can be introduced here — it crosses the market on an errand, muttering to itself, carving a line into its own frame as it walks. It nods to characters it has met before. If the characters haven't met it, it nods to no one and continues its business

### Scene 2: The Factions in Motion

This scene is a series of faction introductions, which can occur in any order as the characters move through the city. Not all need to happen in Chapter 1; carry any undeveloped ones into the chapter's second half.

**The Foundation Peacekeeper.** A young Foundation peacekeeper named Brother Silt patrols the market and is helpful, warm, and slightly too earnest. He apologizes for a pickpocket case that he says is "being handled." He inquires whether the characters are planning to attend the millennium ceremony. He has the slightly too-deliberate manner of someone who has been trained not to volunteer information. *He is not suspicious — he is simply conditioned.* If asked about the Shapers' role in keeping the Pax stable, he gives a polished answer about sacred service. If pressed, the warmth doesn't leave his face but his eyes go somewhere else for a moment.

**Caske Amanthri.** The characters may seek out the Freelancer's Guild for any number of reasons — job leads, local information, a referral. Caske receives them in her third-floor office, direct and assessing. If the characters seem competent, she has work: a cargo dispute requiring a neutral arbitrator, or a missing-persons inquiry for a frontier family who hired the Guild before losing contact. She pays fairly and expects results. She does not explain herself or her methods unless asked, and then only as much as necessary.

**The Devoti Temple.** A midsize temple on the Temple District's main street, run by a Devoti elder named Orveth. The temple is open to everyone; the elder is happy to discuss philosophy, theology, or nothing in particular. If the characters ask about the Path or Brother Candor, the elder is resigned: *"He is not wrong that the Devoti and the Autarchs have never sat entirely comfortably. He is wrong about nearly everything else. He preaches a system where adherence is the point. We have never believed adherence is the point."*

**Telegram, more closely.** If the characters take an interest in Telegram, it will answer direct questions politely: it is assigned to internal Meridian communications; it does not discuss Autarch business; it is fine. It carves a tiny spiral into the nearest surface while waiting for the next question. If asked what the carving means, it says: *"I am not certain."*

### Scene 3: The Inciting Incident

At the end of the chapter's first session — or whenever the DM judges the timing right — a Courier arrives from the frontier.

> *The crowd at the Waterfront parts without being asked. The Courier does not move fast so much as it moves efficiently — each of its many-jointed legs finding the gap in the crowd before the crowd knows the gap is there. The sealed compartment in its torso is visible. Its destination is the Council Hall, and it will reach the Council Hall in approximately seven minutes.*
>
> *But it stops. Directly in front of the Council Hall's steps, where a crowd has gathered to watch the arrival, it opens its torso compartment and removes a document — not the sealed Autarch correspondence, but a separate, unsealed sheet. It reads from the sheet in its flat, uninflected courier's monotone:*
>
> *"Settlers of Ashvane Crossing extend greetings. Request immediate assistance, nature: defense, nature: casualties, nature: unknown attack, nature: survivors present. Ashvane Crossing. This message was written by Councilor Fen Ashvane, survivor, three days ago. Ashvane Crossing."*
>
> *The Courier re-folds the sheet and continues into the Council Hall.*

Ashvane Crossing is a mid-sized frontier settlement approximately three days' travel from Meridian. It has been there for four hundred years. Councilor Fen Ashvane (she/her, human, middle-aged, practical, exhausted) will be in Meridian in two days' time — she traveled separately and faster, using the river, while the Courier carried the formal documents.

The characters can learn the following in the hours after the Courier's arrival:

- The Council is in emergency session; the debate will be extended and inconclusive (roll 1d6: on a 1–4, the Council recommends sending a Foundation peacekeeper contingent; on a 5–6, the Council is gridlocked and recommends further study)
- Caske Amanthri has already sent two of her Guild members to Ashvane Crossing on Fen's behalf; she will hire additional investigators
- Brother Candor, still on his corner, remarks to anyone who pauses: *"The Autarchs are silent. Observe their silence. They are always silent when they cannot pretend to act."*
- A Foundation peacekeeper contingent is being assembled; Morning Wren is named as the frontier guide; they will depart in forty-eight hours

The characters have multiple paths into the investigation: hired by Caske, traveling with the Foundation contingent, accompanying Councilor Ashvane, or independently motivated by a personal connection to the settlement or its people. Allow any of these to work.

### Scene 4: Before Departure

The characters have roughly two days before departure. Use this time for:

- A conversation with Councilor Ashvane when she arrives — she is not hysterical; she is angry. She describes an attack that came from multiple directions simultaneously, lasted for hours, and involved Slaadi in numbers she has never seen. Her primary concern is the survivors who are still at the settlement.
- Any purchases, faction conversations, or personal business the characters wish to complete
- A brief, optional encounter with Brother Auspex — he is at the Council Hall for a committee meeting. He stops to speak with the characters if they are introduced as the investigators. He is kind, asks careful questions, and wishes them well. If any character has the Order Acolyte background, Auspex recognizes them specifically: *"You trained with us. Good. The frontier needs people who understand what the Foundation is for."*

### Chapter 1 Conclusion

The characters depart for Ashvane Crossing. Note the milestone: **characters reach Level 2 after they depart Meridian and commit to the investigation.**

## Chapter 2: The Fraying Edge

*Levels 2–4*

### Chapter Summary

The characters travel to and through the frontier, with Morning Wren as guide. They find Ashvane Crossing damaged but standing, and discover that the Slaadi attacks are more organized than they should be. They encounter the Unobserved Effect firsthand. They find a Foundation peacekeeper who has been rescued from the chaos-zones and whose behavior is deeply wrong. And they return to Meridian carrying evidence of a coordinated Slaadi threat — evidence the Autarchs minimize.

### Scene 1: The Road Into the Frontier

The journey from Meridian to Ashvane Crossing takes three days by road. Morning Wren joins the characters at the southern gate.

> *The Air Genasi waiting at the gate is not what you expected from a Frontier Warden. They are lean and quiet, skin with the faint iridescent quality of sky at altitude, hair that moves slightly in an absent wind. Their bear sits behind them like a piece of geography.*
>
> *"Morning Wren," they say, without ceremony. "That's Badger. We'll make the Crossing in three days if we move in the morning. I've got a frontier route marked; it adds half a day but I want to see what's between here and there before we arrive. Any objections, bring them up now."*

The travel itself introduces the frontier's character:

**Day 1:** The Interior. Farming country, river villages, the road busy with anniversary supply deliveries going toward Meridian. One encounter: a family from a frontier settlement, loaded onto a wagon, heading toward the capital. They have not been driven out; they left voluntarily. The matriarch simply says: *"The ground shook twice last month. The second time it was different."* She will not elaborate.

**Day 2:** The frontier proper. The character of the land changes: fewer farms, more scrub and rock, the road less maintained. The horizon is different here — the island's edge is visible as a distant smudge of dissolution. Morning Wren points it out without sentiment. *"Used to be another half mile of land out there."* One encounter: two Foundation peacekeepers traveling toward Meridian with a third figure — unconscious, strapped to a stretcher. The peacekeepers are unhelpfully vague about what happened. The third figure is a Foundation peacekeeper who was found near the Edge. She is breathing and physically uninjured. She has the empty quality described in the DM notes for Foundation members who have been on the machines. *She should not be on the machines — she is too low-ranking to have been taken.* The characters cannot know this yet; they only know something is wrong with her. DC 14 Insight to notice that her blankness is not shock or injury — it is something else.

**Day 3:** Approach to Ashvane Crossing. The Unobserved Effect becomes apparent in this region: a DC 12 Perception check at camp reveals that the order of items in a character's pack has changed since last check. A successful check confirms the item is still there — only its position changed. A DC 16 Insight check confirms this is not explainable by the character's own behavior. Do not explain this yet. Simply note it and move on.

### Scene 2: Ashvane Crossing

> *Ashvane Crossing looks like a town that took a punch and is still deciding whether to sit down. Two buildings on the north end are rubble. A third has been opened along its entire east face, as if something simply pushed through it. The permanent smell of the frontier — hay and river silt and wood smoke — has a new note underneath it: something mineral and wrong.*
>
> *Survivors are visible: working, building, hauling debris. No one has left. Councilor Ashvane's people do not leave.*

Fen Ashvane is here (she traveled ahead on a faster route). She gives the characters a complete account:
- The attack came from three directions simultaneously — which Slaadi do not do
- The attack lasted approximately six hours, in coordinated waves — Slaadi do not do this
- At one point, the attack paused for nearly an hour before resuming — Slaadi absolutely do not do this
- Among the debris, the survivors found tracks from at least two different Slaad types together — Red and Blue together, coordinated, is not natural

The investigation of Ashvane Crossing yields:
- **DC 13 Investigation** in the rubble: a fragment of material that is not natural to the Pax or to Slaadi — a shard of crystallized chaos-matter worked into a crude directing sigil. This is a manufactured object. Slaadi do not manufacture sigils.
- **DC 15 Nature (Slaadi):** The wound patterns on the dead are consistent with multiple Slaad types. Red Slaadi leave slashing wounds; Blue Slaadi leave distinctive bruised punctures. The mix is wrong — these creatures hunted together.
- Morning Wren, quietly, when the characters share their findings: *"I've been on this frontier for ten years. I've never seen coordinated Slaad action at this scale. Someone or something is organizing them. I don't know who. But I've sent four messages to Meridian about the coordination in the last three months and received exactly two responses, both of which told me to continue monitoring."*

### Scene 3: First Major Slaadi Encounter

On the second night at Ashvane Crossing — or during an optional excursion toward the Edge — the characters face their first significant Slaadi combat encounter.

**Encounter:** 2 Red Slaadi and 1 Blue Slaad (statistics in Appendix A). The Blue Slaad is directing the Reds — not shouting orders, but moving with clear intent while the Reds follow its positioning. A DC 14 Perception check during the combat notices this coordination.

The Blue Slaad carries another directing sigil, matching the fragment found in the rubble.

**Aftermath:** A Red Slaad that is reduced to 0 hit points but not killed can be stabilized. It is incoherent and violent until restrained. Under a DC 15 Intimidation or DC 17 Persuasion check (conducted with Comprehend Languages or a character who speaks Slaad), it reveals: *"The one who walks between calls. It calls and we answer. It is not like us. It is old."* It does not know more than this.

### Scene 4: A Second Settlement

Before returning to Meridian, the characters are directed (by Morning Wren, or by a plea from survivors) to check on Tethervast, a smaller settlement approximately six hours from Ashvane Crossing that has not sent word in two weeks.

Tethervast is not destroyed. It is almost intact, physically. What is wrong:

> *The buildings are standing. The vegetable gardens are tended. The mill wheel is turning. But the settlement is silent. There are no people.*
>
> *Moving through the main street, you find a meal half-eaten on a table in the largest house. A child's toy on the floor. A door standing open. The bread on the kitchen table has not gone stale — it was baked today, or yesterday at most.*
>
> *There are no signs of violence. No blood. No tracks leading out. No tracks leading in. There are just no people.*

The Unobserved Effect at its most extreme: the entire settlement appears to have become unobserved simultaneously, at some moment when no one was watching it from outside. Its people, unattended to, ceased to cohere. This is not described overtly to the characters — they discover only the absence. The horror of this scene, played quietly, is one of the adventure's most effective moments. Let it breathe.

DC 18 Arcana (or Anarch Ability — Intuit Chaos) reveals that the reality of the settlement is unusually thin, as if its informational coherence has been damaged. The characters cannot reverse this with available tools; they can only witness it and leave.

### Scene 5: Return to Meridian

The characters return with:
- Evidence of coordinated Slaadi activity (the sigils, firsthand accounts, their own observations)
- The story of Tethervast
- Morning Wren's confirmation of the broader pattern

The Council session that receives their report is frustrating. Individual councilors are alarmed; the body as a whole is slow, divided, and easily distracted by anniversary logistics. The official response: a modest increase in Foundation peacekeeper deployments, a formal inquiry (beginning after the celebration), and a public statement emphasizing that the Pax has faced external threats before and has prevailed.

The Autarchs' response, through a Speaker: *"The concerns noted are consistent with known Slaadi behavioral variance. Additional Foundation resources have been allocated. Citizens should proceed with anniversary preparations."*

Morning Wren, in private, to the characters: *"'Behavioral variance.' They've decided not to understand."*

### Chapter 2 Encounters

**Random Frontier Encounters (d6)**

| d6 | Encounter |
|---|---|
| 1 | 1d4 Red Slaadi scouting patrol |
| 2 | A Foundation peacekeeper patrol (3 Peacekeepers) traveling the same road |
| 3 | A Chaos Mephit pack (2d4) emerging from a small tear at the Edge |
| 4 | The Unobserved Effect: a personal item changes or vanishes overnight |
| 5 | A refugee family from a dissolved settlement |
| 6 | A Courier traveling toward the frontier at speed; it does not acknowledge the characters |

### Chapter 2 Conclusion

**Characters reach Level 3** after the first major Slaadi encounter. **Characters reach Level 4** at the end of Chapter 2, upon returning to Meridian with evidence.

The seeds planted in Act I:
- The directing sigils and the entity that created them
- The empty Foundation peacekeeper on the stretcher
- Tethervast
- The Autarchs' deliberate inadequacy of response
- Morning Wren's accumulated frustration

None of these need to feel like clues yet. They will.

# ACT II: THE SHAPE OF THINGS

*Levels 4–9 · Chapters 3–5*

Act II is the adventure's spine. The characters move from observers of a crisis to active investigators of its causes. They encounter the world beyond the Pax for the first time. They attend a celebration that becomes a turning point. They dig beneath the surface of the realm they thought they understood and find something buried that does not match the official story.

The tone shifts here — not dramatically, but persistently. Things that seemed reassuring in Act I are less reassuring now. The Foundation's calm begins to feel like a symptom rather than a virtue. The Autarchs' silence becomes suspicious rather than merely aloof. Allow this shift to emerge from what the characters discover rather than from an ambient spookiness you manufacture.

---

## Chapter 3: Beyond the Border

*Levels 4–6*

### Chapter Summary

The characters venture outside the Pax's borders for the first time, into the raw chaos of Limbo. Their primary mission is to find the source of the Slaadi coordination signals; their discoveries will be larger than that. They make first contact with the Githzerai through Zerith, encounter the first evidence of Thantarnax's dominated proxies, and find a Xaositect enclave that will become an important later-game resource.

### Scene 1: Crossing the Edge

Crossing from the Pax into Limbo is not like crossing a border on a map. It is a transition between states of reality.

> *The Edge here is a cliff face — or was, once. Now its base is eaten away, and what remains overhangs a thirty-foot drop into what should be water but is not. The chaos below is gray-white, slow-moving, and almost beautiful in the way that a very large snake is almost beautiful. It is not threatening you. It is simply moving, and you are very small, and if you fell into it you would dissolve.*
>
> *Morning Wren crouches at the edge and examines the dissolution: the gradual, peaceful way the rock simply releases its edges and becomes formless. "Ten feet of island since last month," they say. "Jump from here. Land twenty feet out. You will be in Limbo."*

**Navigation check:** Before entering Limbo, the characters should establish a navigator (see Chaos Mechanics). Morning Wren does not cross into Limbo — their role is frontier, not open chaos — but they give the characters a heading based on the sigils recovered from the Slaadi. *"The signals point southwest. There's a Githzerai waypoint in that direction — old, but it should still be there. If you see a blue crystalline spire, you're close."*

Apply Limbo navigation rules from the moment characters leave the Pax. The first hour is disorienting but not immediately dangerous; use it to establish the strangeness of the environment. A result of 1–3 on the chaos transformation table (Appendix D) in the first hour is appropriate — something minor, something visible, something that makes the players understand the rules have changed.

### Scene 2: Githzerai First Contact

Approximately four hours of travel from the Pax's border (subject to navigation successes), the characters reach the Githzerai waypoint — a small, disciplined outpost cut into a stable chunk of matter approximately the size of a large house.

> *The structure ahead is what order looks like when it has to fight for every inch. The stone is cut precisely — not ornately, precisely, which is a different thing. Its surfaces are clean. It is defended: narrow observation slits, a single reinforced entrance, a figure on the roof who has been watching you for the last ten minutes.*

Zerith meets them at the entrance. She does not express surprise at their arrival; she knew they were coming (the observation posts are thorough). She assesses them quickly and her opening statement is a question: *"You crossed from the Pax. What are you looking for?"*

If the characters explain the Slaadi coordination and the sigils:
- Zerith knows about the coordination. She has observed it for several months. She did not report it to the Pax's authorities because *"it is not our concern unless it becomes our concern."* She shares this without apology.
- The sigils: she examines one and says, *"This is not Slaadi work. This is chaos-shaping. Deliberate, controlled chaos-shaping. Slaadi do not do this."*
- On who does do this: *"Something very old and very patient. We have seen its work before. Three years ago, a pack of chaos giants attacked the monastery. The giants do not move in packs. They do not target specific structures. They do not fight to the last. Whatever gave those giants their orders also gave these Slaadi theirs."*

Zerith will help — at a price. The price is information exchange: she wants to understand the extent of the threat to the Pax, because if the Pax destabilizes, the chaos front moves toward the Githzerai monastery. This is pragmatic, not altruistic, and she will say so.

**If the characters ask about the Pax's machines or the Autarchs' secrets:** She has no specific knowledge. She knows that the Pax is unusual in its stability and that will-based stabilization at this scale requires either extraordinary Anarch power or something else. She does not know about the machines. *Do not reveal anything through Zerith that the characters have not already discovered themselves.*

DC 12 Insight at any point during this conversation: Zerith is hiding something. DC 17: She knows about a specific incident — a dominated proxy attack that came unusually close to the Pax's borders eight months ago. She did not report this either. She is now reconsidering that decision. (She will mention it voluntarily before the conversation ends if the players haven't extracted it.)

### Scene 3: Dominated Proxy Encounter

Approximately two hours after leaving the Githzerai waypoint, heading toward the sigil source, the characters encounter Thantarnax's dominated proxies for the first time.

**Encounter:** 3 Phase Spiders (from the Monster Manual / SRD equivalent), moving in an unusually tight formation directly toward a small pocket of stable matter. Phase Spiders do not move in formation. They do not ignore available prey (the characters) in favor of distant targets. They are behaving against their nature — and the characters, if they have spoken to Zerith, know what this means.

The spiders do not acknowledge the characters' presence and attempt to move past them toward the stable matter pocket. If attacked, they defend themselves but maintain their formation and continue toward their objective even if it means taking damage to do so. They fight to the last with the mechanical determination of automata rather than the survival instinct of creatures.

**What they're doing:** The stable matter pocket contains a small crystalline formation of the type used in the directing sigils. The spiders are there to harvest it. They carry crude collection tools wrapped around their legs — tools they would not naturally possess.

DC 15 Nature: Phase Spiders do not use tools and do not travel in formation. These are being controlled by something with the ability to dominate creatures at long range.

**Aftermath:** If the characters take one of the tools, or examine the crystalline formation: it is the same material as the directing sigils. Someone is manufacturing the sigils and deploying Slaadi and other creatures as an organized force. The directing intelligence is not present here.

### Scene 4: The Xaositect Enclave

Not far from the sigil source (the DM can decide whether the characters reach the source itself or just approach it — at this level, confronting the full supply chain is premature), the characters find a Xaositect enclave: a cluster of chaotically built structures anchored to a large drifting chunk of matter.

Xaositects are enthusiastically social, thoroughly unpredictable, and generally friendly to visitors who don't represent authority. The enclave's apparent leader (the position rotates daily by coin flip) is **Kerash-Whirl** (they/them, tiefling, extremely colorful clothing, speaks in reversed word order approximately 40% of the time). Kerash-Whirl is delighted to see the characters and will help them in ways that are not always immediately useful.

**What the Xaositects know:**
- The source of the sigils is *"the great old quiet one in the deep. We do not go deep."* This is the extent of their direct knowledge.
- They have seen dominated proxy attacks increase significantly over the past year. *"The quiet one gets restless. It collected things for a long time. Now it tests them."*
- The Pax's stability: *"The Pax is held by the machine-will. The machine-will strains. This we feel. Like a rope."* If pressed on what "machine-will" means: they cannot elaborate. It is something they perceive in the chaos as a kind of absence of chaos — a negative space that is, somehow, thinning.

**This is significant (DM note):** The Xaositects can perceive the machines' output in Limbo as a suppression of chaos. They do not know its source, but they know it is artificial and they know it is weakening. Players who think carefully about this description will be able to fit it into the larger picture much later.

Kerash-Whirl offers the characters a token — a small disk of woven chaos-fiber — as an introduction to the enclave. It is not mechanical; it just means the characters are known here. *"Come back. We will be different. You will not be the only ones who are different."*

### Chapter 3 Conclusion

**Characters reach Level 5** after first contact with the Githzerai. **Characters reach Level 6** after the Xaositect enclave encounter and return to the Pax.

The characters return to Meridian with:
- Zerith's confirmation that the Slaadi coordination has an ancient, patient source
- Evidence of dominated proxy activity and sigil manufacture
- The Xaositects' cryptic confirmation of the machines' effect on Limbo
- A growing question: if the Slaadi coordination is not accidental and not Slaadi-organized, who is organizing it?

The Autarchs, through a Speaker: *"External intelligence is being evaluated. The Foundation's Shaper sub-order has been consulted. Citizens should note that anniversary preparations are proceeding normally."*

## Chapter 4: The Millennium Celebration

*Levels 6–7*

### Chapter Summary

The Pax's thousand-year anniversary has arrived. Meridian is hosting a multi-day celebration drawing participants from across the planes. Every faction is present, political maneuvering is everywhere, and the city is at its most vivid and most fragile simultaneously. The Autarchs make their grandest public appearance in living memory. And in the middle of a formal ceremony, Brother Auspex's conditioning cracks — and what comes out will change everything.

### The Celebration — Overview

The Millennium Celebration runs five days. The characters can move freely through events; use the social encounter framework below to manage the density of faction interactions.

**Day 1: The Opening Procession.** The full city marches. Every guild, organization, temple, and neighborhood association has a float, a banner contingent, or a marching group. The Approach is lined with spectators. Brother Candor maintains his corner throughout, preaching over the noise. The Path has unusually large crowds around it; whether this is curiosity or genuine interest is unclear.

**Day 2: The Trade Fair.** The Merchant Quarter expands to fill the entire West Bank. Planescape faction delegations have stalls. This is the best day for information gathering, faction contact, and acquiring goods.

**Day 3: The Academic Symposium.** Scholars from across the planes present research at the Scholar's Row and the Devoti's library. The Devoti's senior philosopher — **Scholar Threnody** (they/them, gnome, obsessive, kind) — presents a paper on the relationship between Limbo's natural chaos flux and the Pax's observed stability that contains a datum no one has followed up on: the stability gradient is steepest immediately beneath the Sovereign. This is not news to the Autarchs. It is news to everyone else.

**Day 4: The Foundation Ceremony.** The annual initiation ceremony for new Foundation members, held at the Approach. The Autarchs appear in force: six of them, all eleven-known colors represented (this is unprecedented in living memory). The ceremony is elaborate and runs four hours.

**Day 5: The Founder's Feast.** A public feast in the Approach's central plaza, with speeches from council representatives. Traditionally the Autarchs speak last, through their Speakers.

### Scene 1: The Trade Fair (Day 2)

The Trade Fair is a social encounter hub. Each of the following faction representatives can be found with a DC 13 Investigation or a successful ask to a local merchant:

**Xaositect Delegation:** Led by a cheerful Xaositect called Murrathex (he/him), who is extremely interested in talking to anyone who has been in Limbo recently. He has heard about the dominated proxy activity and is worried in the Xaositect way, which means he is worried while juggling. He passes the characters a slip of paper with what appears to be random characters; decoded (DC 14 Investigation), it reads: *"Tell Kerash-Whirl: the deep-quiet's reach extends now inside the Pax. We felt it at the Edge."*

**Free League Representative:** **Merchant Solvern** (he/him, half-orc, methodical), who runs a multi-plane trading business and has clients in the frontier. He knows that three frontier traders he worked with have not been heard from in six weeks. He assumed the Fraying; he is beginning to wonder. He will share shipping records if the characters ask. (The missing traders were at Tethervast.)

**Githzerai Delegation:** Zerith is here, unexpectedly, attending as an official delegate. She acknowledges the characters without warmth but without hostility. In private, she says: *"I am here because the monastery requested an observer. There are things happening that the monastery finds... interesting."* She will not elaborate in a public setting.

**Revolutionary League:** Not officially present; an agent — **Thorn** (she/her, human, mid-twenties, intensely serious) — approaches the characters quietly if they have interacted with the Path. She represents the Revolutionary League. Her interest: *"When you find out what's being done, think about who should know. Not the Council. Not the Foundation. The people."*

### Scene 2: The Foundation Ceremony (Day 4)

The characters attend the Foundation's initiation ceremony. This is where the milestone event occurs.

> *The Approach is dressed for ceremony: the banners of all eleven Autarch colors hang in pairs from the monument bases. Six Autarchs stand on the formal platform in a row, each with their Throne and Speaker. Foundation members fill the space between the platform and the crowd. The initiates — young people who will not see their families again after today — stand in a formal line.*
>
> *Brother Auspex is at the edge of the platform, as he has been for thirty years at these ceremonies. He is smiling at the crowd the way he always does: with genuine warmth, with genuine pride. He has given a great deal to this organization. He believes in it.*
>
> *The ceremony proceeds. Each initiate steps forward and receives their Foundation name. The crowd applauds. Auspex marks each name in the registry.*
>
> *The fifth initiate steps forward. They are sixteen. Their Foundation name is called.*
>
> *And Brother Auspex stops.*

Give this a beat. Then:

> *He is staring at the name in the registry. The name he just wrote. It is a Foundation name — a replacement identity — but it sounds like something.*
>
> *"—That name," he says. Not loudly. Not to anyone in particular.*
>
> *The Speaker nearest him continues the ceremony. The fifth initiate steps back.*
>
> *Auspex is still staring. And then — slowly, visibly, like something coming loose from a wall — his composure falls.*
>
> *"Brother Pallas," he says. Louder now. "That was Brother Pallas's name. I wrote Brother Pallas's name in this registry forty years ago and I have never—" He stops. He looks at the crowd. He looks at the Autarchs. "Where is Brother Pallas? He became a Shaper. He became a Shaper forty years ago and I have never—" His voice breaks. "Where is he? Does anyone know where he is? He was my student. He— someone must know where he—"*
>
> *The Speakers announce, simultaneously, in their flat uninflected voices: "Brother Auspex. Please step away from the platform."*
>
> *Auspex is still talking. He is weeping openly now. He is elderly and beloved and he is weeping in public at a Foundation ceremony in front of six Autarchs and a thousand witnesses, and he is asking where Brother Pallas is.*

The Speakers contain the situation with practiced efficiency. Auspex is gently but firmly guided off the platform by two peacekeepers. The Speakers immediately address the crowd: *"Brother Auspex has served the Foundation for fifty-three years. His recent work has been intense and the pressures of the anniversary period have affected many. He will receive the rest he has long earned. The ceremony will continue."*

The ceremony continues. The Autarchs do not react visibly.

**Character observations:**
- DC 14 Perception: One of the Autarchs tracked Auspex with their head throughout the outburst — the head turning precisely, without the slight organic inconsistency of human attention.
- DC 14 Insight: The peacekeepers who led Auspex away are not comforting him. They are controlling him. Efficiently.
- DC 16 History: The name "Brother Pallas" matches a Foundation member on public record who became a Shaper approximately forty years ago. There is a public record of this. There is no public record of him after that.

### Scene 3: The Autarchs' Offer

Within twenty-four hours of the ceremony, Telegram arrives at wherever the characters are staying.

> *"Your presence is requested. The matter of the ceremony disruption. A Speakers session has been arranged. This is a formal invitation, not a summons. Your presence is voluntary."*

The Speakers session is held in a small formal room in the Sovereign's public precincts. Two Autarchs are present (color: gold and grey); their Speakers address the characters. The Autarchs gesture.

The offer: The characters have been noticed. Their investigation into the frontier has been competent. The Autarchs are grateful. As a recognition of service, they are offering the characters a substantial reward (50 gp per character level, plus a minor magic item of the characters' choosing from the Autarchs' stores). The implicit condition: the incident at the ceremony is best understood as what the Speakers described — an old man under stress. The characters' future investigations should focus on the Slaadi threat, which is real and significant. The investigation of the Foundation's internal affairs is, the Speakers note, *"not a productive use of skilled resources."*

**If the characters probe:**
- *"Is Brother Pallas well?"* → *"Brother Pallas has been in sacred seclusion for many years. He is at peace."*
- *"Can we speak with him?"* → *"That is not consistent with the Shaper sub-order's practices, as Brother Auspex himself taught for decades before his unfortunate condition."*
- *"What exactly happened to Brother Auspex's conditioning?"* → The Speaker pauses one beat longer than usual before responding. *"Brother Auspex's health is a Foundation matter."*

The characters may take the offer, refuse it, or pretend to take it and continue investigating. All three are valid. Taking it changes their standing with the Autarchs (+1) but not in a way that will last; refusing it changes it slightly (-1 with Autarchs) and begins the slow escalation.

### Chapter 4 Conclusion

**Characters reach Level 7** after the Breaking event and the Autarchs' offer.

The characters are now at a genuine decision point: drop the investigation or pursue it. The adventure assumes they pursue it; if players are hesitant, Morning Wren or Caske can provide motivation. *"If they're offering money to make you stop asking questions, what do you think the answer to those questions is?"*

## Chapter 5: Shadows Beneath

*Levels 7–9*

### Chapter Summary

The investigation arc. The characters pursue the truth about the Foundation's Shapers, navigating the Autarchs' escalating obstruction, the distraction of a major Slaadi assault on the frontier, and the physical reality of what lies beneath the capital. By the chapter's end, they have found partial evidence of the machines — enough to know something is deeply wrong, not yet enough to see the full picture.

### The Investigation Framework

The Autarchs' response to the investigation escalates in four stages. Track progress based on how many discoveries the characters make:

**Stage 1 — Ignore (0–1 discoveries):** Business as usual. No active obstruction.

**Stage 2 — Redirect (2–3 discoveries):** A Courier delivers urgent news from the frontier; a council committee requests the characters' testimony on Slaadi coordination (time-consuming); Telegram delivers a personal message from the Autarchs commending the characters' frontier work and suggesting there is much more of it needed.

**Stage 3 — Discredit (4–5 discoveries):** False evidence appears suggesting one character is in the pay of a Planescape faction (the Revolutionary League, specifically). An official inquiry begins. Two witnesses come forward with testimony claiming the characters behaved improperly during the Ashvane Crossing investigation. All of this is fabricated. DC 16 Investigation to identify the fabrication; DC 14 Insight to recognize the pattern as deliberate.

**Stage 4 — Neutralize (6+ discoveries):** Peacekeepers begin following the characters (DC 16 Perception to notice). Evidence the characters have stored at an inn or safehouse is quietly removed. Their access to official records is revoked pending the inquiry. Caske Amanthri's guild receives an official visit raising questions about its licensing.

### Investigation Threads

**Thread 1: Brother Auspex.** Finding Auspex after the ceremony requires DC 13 Investigation and access to Foundation channels (Caske can provide an introduction through a Foundation contact she has cultivated). Auspex is at the Foundation's guest quarters near the Sovereign — he is not imprisoned, but his movements are closely monitored.

If the characters reach him privately: he is diminished but lucid. He will speak carefully. What he will say: *"I wrote Pallas's name in the initiation registry forty years ago. He was one of the finest students I ever taught. He had a gift — a genuine Anarch gift, the kind you only see once a generation. And then he became a Shaper and I never heard from him again. I was told the Shaper seclusion was total, that even former teachers would not receive communication. I accepted this. I should not have accepted this."*

What he can offer: the Foundation name of Brother Pallas. Pallas's former bunk in the main monastery (sealed, but accessible). A list of five other former students who "became Shapers" and whom Auspex has not heard from since. *"They were all gifted. That was the pattern. I see the pattern now."*

**Thread 2: Ledger Records.** The Ledger construct in the Sovereign's public archives tracks Anarch population data across the Pax. Accessing these records requires either official status (Foundation or council credentials) or a DC 18 Stealth infiltration into the public archives section. The records are in Ledger's standard format: population by category, year, settlement.

The anomaly: the number of Anarchs who have formally entered the Foundation's Shaper sub-order over the past hundred years exceeds the Foundation's own published count of living Shapers by a factor of approximately three. The discrepancy has been growing for decades. If the Shapers are in seclusion but alive, where are the additional bodies? Illness and death would explain some attrition. It does not explain a factor of three.

DC 16 Investigation to identify the anomaly. DC 18 History: the discrepancy begins approximately 120 years before the campaign's present — coinciding with a period in the Pax's history when the Autarchs made a series of unexplained infrastructure decisions, including extensive construction beneath the Sovereign described only as "stability works."

**Thread 3: The Hidden Passages.** Beneath the Foundation Monastery — accessible through a collapsed section of the old archives that the Foundation has sealed and marked "structurally unsafe" — are tunnels. The Foundation knows about the tunnels; they are on old structural surveys. What is not on any survey is that the tunnels extend further than the monastery's footprint, heading north toward the Sovereign's base.

Accessing the tunnels requires either:
- A Foundation contact willing to take a significant personal risk (a junior Scholar named **Brother Nettle**, human, 20s, genuinely curious and genuinely afraid — DC 16 Persuasion to recruit him, with appropriate leverage from Auspex's information)
- Breaking into the sealed section (DC 17 Thieves' Tools, DC 19 Stealth to avoid Foundation patrol detection)

The tunnels themselves:

> *The air is older here. That is the only way to describe it: older. It moves differently. The stone walls, where you can see them, are the same construction as the Sovereign's public precincts — the same cut, the same age. Someone built these tunnels at the same time as the mountain's public face. Someone decided not to record them.*

The tunnels run approximately four hundred feet north of the monastery before reaching a sealed door. The door is not locked — it is *arcane-sealed*, requiring DC 20 Arcana or a Foundation-specific key to open. The characters can get close enough to know the door is there. They cannot open it yet. Through the door — if anyone presses an ear to it — is the sound of something mechanical. Something steady. Something that has been going for a very long time.

**Thread 4: The Mender.** A character who visits the Sovereign's public medical facility for any reason encounters a Mender construct. The Mender is efficient and largely uncommunicative, but a DC 15 Perception check during the visit reveals: its fine-tool fingers have traces of a substance that matches the material of the directing sigils recovered in the frontier — crystallized chaos-matter. The Mender's fingers smell faintly of the same mineral-wrong note that characterized the Ashvane Crossing attack site.

The Mender does maintenance work, officially, on the Sovereign's magical infrastructure. What magical infrastructure, exactly, requires access to chaos-matter and fine-tool manipulation? The characters cannot answer this yet.

### The Vraakon Diversion

Midway through the Chapter 5 investigation, Vraakon the Ascendant launches his first coordinated major assault: three simultaneous attacks on frontier settlements, coordinated through a network of directing sigils. This is significantly larger than anything seen in Act I.

Morning Wren sends word: the Foundation's frontier peacekeepers are overwhelmed. The characters are asked — by Morning Wren, by Caske, by the council — to return to the frontier to help.

This is a real crisis, not a fabrication. It is also genuinely convenient for the Autarchs. If the characters go: the frontier needs them, settlements will suffer without their help, and their investigation loses momentum. If they stay: the frontier suffers, and their standing with Morning Wren and the Frontier Settlers decreases (-1 standing with Frontier Settlers faction).

There is no wrong answer. The adventure does not punish either choice — it records the consequences. If the characters go to the frontier and help (a running combat encounter against waves of Slaadi over several days), they return with increased standing (+1 with Frontier Settlers, +1 with Foundation Peacekeepers) and may find some evidence of the investigation has been tampered with in their absence (Stage 3 obstruction advances by one step regardless).

### Chapter 5 Conclusion

By the end of Chapter 5, the characters have:
- The Foundation names of multiple missing Shapers
- The Ledger anomaly
- The location of the sealed door
- The Mender connection
- Growing Autarch obstruction

They know something is down there. They cannot get to it yet. They will get to it.

**Characters reach Level 8** after finding the hidden passages. **Characters reach Level 9** after recovering evidence of the Ledger anomaly and surviving the Vraakon diversion (end of Chapter 5).

The chapter ends with the characters holding more questions than answers — but the questions now have a shape.

# ACT III: THE TRUTH AND ITS CONSEQUENCES

*Levels 9–15 · Chapters 6–9*

Act III is the adventure's climax and resolution. The characters move from investigators to protagonists: the people who will decide what happens next. Two crises run simultaneously — the Slaad war and the revelation of the machines — and the third arrives immediately after: Thantarnax the Unraveler, patient and ancient and finally ready to act.

The tone here is weight rather than urgency. The adventure has earned its gravitas by now. Play each revelation with care. Allow the moral complexity to land fully.

---

## Chapter 6: The War Without

*Levels 9–11*

### Chapter Summary

Vraakon the Ascendant launches his full-scale assault on the Pax — a coordinated, multi-front attack that tests every defense the realm has. The characters are central to the defense. The Githzerai offer assistance — at a price. The Slaad lord is defeated in a climactic battle. And as the dust settles, Thantarnax makes her presence unmistakable for the first time.

### The Battle for the Pax — Overview

Run Chapter 6 as a campaign-scale event rather than a single encounter. The assault lasts multiple days; the characters make tactical decisions that affect the overall outcome.

**The Battle Map:** The Pax's frontier has three vulnerable points: the Northern Reach (nearest the Sovereign, historically safest, now under pressure), the Eastern Delta (where the Constant's eastern branch approaches the Edge, heavy civilian population), and the Western Scrublands (sparse population, maximum chaos instability). Vraakon's forces hit all three simultaneously.

**Tactical choices:** Each session of the battle, the characters can choose to:
- Reinforce a sector (reduces casualties in that sector)
- Scout for Vraakon's position (requires success on 3 DC 15 Survival/Investigation checks across multiple sessions)
- Coordinate with Foundation peacekeepers (improves Foundation standing; requires accepting their chain of command)
- Work with the Githzerai (requires engaging with Zerith's conditions, below)
- Pursue independent action (maximum flexibility, maximum risk)

### Scene 1: The First Day

> *The assault does not begin with sound. It begins with light — or the absence of it. The horizon toward the Edge dims, as if something enormous has passed between the island and the chaos below. Then the sigil-network activates simultaneously across a thirty-mile front, and the Slaadi come.*

The characters' initial engagement is a running battle at one of the three front sectors. Use Slaadi encounter data from Appendix A for the main combatant waves. Notable: the Slaadi are fighting with genuine coordination — not just following the directing sigils, but responding to tactical developments as if someone is watching the battle in real time.

DC 15 Arcana: the sigil-network is being centrally controlled and updated. Someone is running this battle. It is not Vraakon — he is a warlord, not a tactician. Someone is directing him.

### Scene 2: Zerith's Conditions

Zerith arrives with a Githzerai strike force of six Githzerai Monks on the second day of the battle. She does not ask permission. She engages Vraakon's forces on the Western Scrublands front and is visibly effective. Her price:

She requests a private meeting with the characters. *"We will fight with you. We have been fighting already. The condition is this: when the battle is over, I want a full accounting of what you know. Not about the Slaadi. About the Pax itself. The stability. The Foundation. What is beneath the Sovereign."*

She knows something is there. She has not known what. She wants the truth — and she will work with the characters to get it if they agree to share it.

**The critical choice:** The characters can agree with full honesty, agree with the intention of selective disclosure later, or refuse. Zerith will not take a partial commitment — she will know if they are hedging. A successful DC 17 Deception check can conceal the intention to disclose selectively.

If agreed: Zerith's forces add materially to the battle's outcome. If refused: the characters fight without Githzerai support; the battle is harder (increase wave sizes by 25%) but winnable.

### Scene 3: Finding Vraakon

After locating Vraakon through scouting (see tactical choices above), the characters track him to his command position: a large chunk of stable matter approximately two miles outside the Pax's border, anchored with directing sigils.

> *The Slaad lord is larger than you expected. He is also quieter. He stands at the edge of his platform, watching the Pax with an expression that is difficult to read on a Slaad's face but appears to be something like satisfaction. Around him, sigil-arrays rotate in patterns no Slaad you have encountered before could maintain.*
>
> *He turns when you approach. "The Pax sent its best," he says. In common. Clearly, without the guttural distortion of standard Slaad speech. "She told me you would come. She said to tell you: this is only the test."*

Vraakon the Ascendant: see full stat block in Appendix A. This is a CR 14 encounter appropriate for a level 10 party. Key combat notes:

- Vraakon opens with **Ascendant's Howl** to establish fear effects
- He uses his innate spellcasting (confusion, fear) to disrupt party formation
- He prioritizes the characters who have demonstrated the most tactical capability
- At 50% HP, he activates Regeneration and fights with increasing fury
- At 25% HP, he reveals: *"She showed me the machines. Under your mountain. She has known for centuries. She is patient. You will not stop her."*

**Vraakon at 0 HP:** He does not surrender. He falls, enormous, and the directing sigil network collapses. The Slaadi across the front immediately revert to disorganized individual behavior. The battle effectively ends.

In his final moments, if the characters ask who "she" is: *"Thantarnax. She is— she has watched this place for— she wanted me to—"* He dies. Whatever he almost understood about being used, he takes with him.

### Scene 4: Aftermath and the Dragon's Arrival

The Pax is damaged but standing. Frontier settlements have sustained significant casualties; the Eastern Delta required evacuation. The Foundation's peacekeepers are exhausted. The Autarchs, through their Speakers, acknowledge the victory with characteristic reserve.

Morning Wren, to the characters: *"We held. But look at the Edge. Look at how much we lost just holding."* The dissolution has advanced noticeably during the battle — the chaos exploited the destabilization from the Slaadi attacks. The Pax is smaller than it was when Chapter 6 began.

**Thantarnax's announcement:** That night, something crosses the artificial sun — enormous, shadowed, impossibly large against the arcane light. It is visible for approximately four seconds. The Tenders on the sun's maintenance gantries freeze in place, sensors fixed upward, for a full minute after it passes.

No one in Meridian sleeps well that night.

**Characters reach Level 10** after defeating Vraakon. **Characters reach Level 11** after Thantarnax's appearance.

## Chapter 7: The Heart of the Machine

*Levels 11–13*

### Chapter Summary

Full revelation. With the sealed door's key finally obtainable (through the battle's aftermath, through Auspex, or through direct action), the characters descend to the machines. They find the enslaved Anarchs. They find people they know. And they confront the Autarchs directly, forcing a choice that will define the campaign's endgame.

### Getting the Key

The sealed door beneath the Foundation Monastery requires either:
- **Auspex's key:** After the battle, Auspex can be found. His monitoring has loosened in the chaos of the aftermath. A visit to him reveals that senior Foundation members carry override keys to sealed sections. His — carried for decades as a senior ceremonial officer — might open it. He gives it to the characters without hesitation. *"I should have used it years ago."*
- **Forcible entry:** DC 22 Arcana to unravel the sealing, or a spell of 6th level or higher that targets arcane locks.
- **Mender access:** A Mender's maintenance credentials will open the door. Subduing a Mender without triggering a Guardian response requires DC 17 Stealth.

### Scene 1: The Descent

> *The door opens onto a staircase that does not match the architecture above it. The Sovereign's public face is cut stone, old and solid. This staircase is something else: smooth, almost organic in its construction, walls that seem to lean inward without quite meeting. The air below is cool and carries the mineral smell from Ashvane Crossing — the wrong smell, the chaos-matter smell — and something underneath it that is harder to name. Something warm and alive and very tired.*

The descent takes approximately ten minutes of cautious movement. There are no Guardian patrols here. There is a Mender, moving steadily along the wall, performing checks. It does not challenge the characters; it continues its work. If engaged: it says only *"Maintenance is ongoing"* and continues. If physically blocked: it waits with the patience of a thing that has been waiting for a long time.

### Scene 2: The Machines

> *The chamber is enormous. You did not expect that — the scale of it, the height of the ceiling lost in darkness above. The machines occupy the center: a network of crystalline structures twelve feet high, connected by arcane conduits that pulse with slow, deep light. The light is the same color as Anarch ability when it manifests. You have seen it before, in the open, as something free. Here it moves through tubes.*
>
> *And in each crystalline structure, connected to it by conduits at the wrists, the spine, the temples, is a person.*

The enslaved Anarchs: there are seventeen of them in this chamber. They are alive. They are not conscious in any conventional sense — their eyes are open but unfocused, their breathing slow and regular, their heartbeats present but reduced. The oldest have been here long enough that their physical age is impossible to determine; the machines have preserved their bodies in a kind of suspension that is not quite living and not quite anything else.

**Among the seventeen:**
- One matches the description Auspex gave of Brother Pallas — the right age, the right build. His Foundation name is carved into the base of his crystalline structure.
- If any character has the Order Acolyte background, one of the Anarchs is someone from their past — a teacher, a fellow student, a mentor figure. Work this out with the player in advance.
- The most recently added — clearly so from the condition of their crystalline structure and the relative freshness of the arcane conduits — is no older than thirty. They went in within the last five years.

DC 15 Arcana: the machines are what is keeping the Pax stable. The arcane conduits are drawing the Anarchs' life force — their chaos-shaping will — and feeding it into a network that extends through the Sovereign's foundations into the Pax's bedrock. Disconnecting the machines now, without an alternative, would cause the realm's stability to collapse within hours. The Fraying would accelerate catastrophically. The Unobserved Effect would reach Meridian within days.

This is the moral crisis. The characters know the truth. They cannot simply destroy what they have found.

### Scene 3: The Autarch Confrontation

The Autarchs are here — three of them, in their robes and masks, attended by Speakers and Guardians. They were waiting.

The first Speaker's statement:

*"You have found what you sought. We acknowledge this. We ask that you hear us before you act."*

What the Autarchs say, through their Speakers:

> *"This realm has existed for a thousand years. It has existed because of what you see before you. The first Anarchs were volunteers — people who understood what was required and chose to give it. When the volunteers were exhausted, when the machines needed more, a decision was made. We made it. We make it still.*
>
> *"We have tried alternatives. Every generation, we have tried alternatives. Nothing has worked at scale. The chaos around this island is not passive — it is active, corrosive, and growing stronger as it grows older. The machines require more every decade. We take as little as we must. We choose people who have already demonstrated extraordinary ability. We do not take the weak.*
>
> *"We are not your enemies. We built this place. We have kept it alive for a thousand years. We are asking you to help us keep it alive for another thousand. There is a way. There is a path in which the machines are transitional, not permanent. We need time. We need people who understand the problem to help us find the solution. We are asking for your help."*

This speech should be delivered without melodrama. The Autarchs are not lying. They are also not telling the full truth: the "alternatives" they have tried have never involved making the machines public knowledge and asking for genuine volunteers with full information. They have never tried the one thing that might actually work — transparency.

**The characters' options at this confrontation:**

1. **Negotiate:** Agree to work with the Autarchs on finding a voluntary alternative, in exchange for the machines' gradual shutdown and an honest public accounting. This requires establishing specific, enforceable conditions. The Autarchs will accept conditions that are achievable; they will not accept immediate shutdown without an alternative in place.

2. **Demand immediate shutdown:** The Autarchs will not agree. If the characters force the issue, the Autarchs will instruct the Guardians to prevent machine interference. This leads to combat — not with the Autarchs directly, but with four Guardians (see Appendix A). The Autarchs withdraw while the Guardians fight. The machines can be disabled by destroying six of the twelve crystalline structures (all have AC 13, HP 45).

3. **Expose and retreat:** Take evidence and go. The Autarchs will not stop the characters from leaving — stopping them by force would be a worse outcome. They will escalate to Stage 4 obstruction immediately and work to discredit the characters before they can publish.

### Scene 4: The Revelation and Its Aftermath

Regardless of the choice at the confrontation:

**If negotiating:** Word of the machines leaks anyway — Brother Auspex, who followed the characters down, was not stopped and has been listening. He emerges from the descent, still weeping, and tells the first Foundation member he encounters. The truth is public within twenty-four hours. The Autarchs adapt; they acknowledge the machines are "an emergency measure under review."

**If exposing:** The characters go public. The Autarchs' discrediting campaign begins immediately. The council, the Devoti, the Path, the Freelancer's Guild — everyone reacts. Faction standings shift dramatically (see Faction Relationship Tracker guidance in this chapter's endnotes).

**The Githzerai:** If Zerith agreed to the information exchange, she is here or arrives shortly. Learning the truth, she is still. *"You enslaved them."* She is speaking to the chamber, not to the characters. Then, quietly, to the characters: *"What will you do?"* Her response to the answer determines whether the Githzerai remain allies.

**Characters reach Level 12** after the machines are discovered. **Characters reach Level 13** at the end of Chapter 7, after the Autarch confrontation and the public revelation.

## Chapter 8: Alliances

*Levels 13–14*

### Chapter Summary

The Pax is in political crisis, the machines are (depending on player choice) partially or fully operational, and Thantarnax has begun her direct assault — sending waves of dominated creatures against the Pax's damaged frontier. The characters must build the coalition that will face her while managing the fallout from the revelation and keeping the realm functional.

### The Political Crisis

Each faction has a specific response to the revelation about the machines. Track these carefully; they determine alliance possibilities and ending availability.

**The Devoti:** Fractured. Individual temples split between those who believe the revelation confirms their philosophy (personal sovereignty is paramount) and those who cannot reconcile the Foundation's fall from grace with their lived experience of the order's goodness. The Devoti's senior leadership — such as it exists in a decentralized organization — calls for a period of collective discernment. This is not helpful to the characters in the short term but positions the Devoti as a potential mass-movement ally if the characters invest time.

**The Path / Brother Candor:** Candor's response is not triumph. It is gravity. He gathers a crowd in the Temple District and delivers a speech that begins: *"I take no satisfaction in this."* Then he proceeds to lay out, with his characteristic precision, exactly what the Path's doctrine predicted and exactly what it means now that the prediction has been confirmed. His following surges; the Path is no longer a fringe sect. If the characters engage with him: he will support them fully, no conditions, no negotiation. He has been waiting for this moment for decades.

**The Foundation:** In crisis. Peacekeepers and Scholars who learn the truth respond in three ways: denial, compliance with ongoing orders, and quiet desertion. Brother Auspex becomes the de facto moral leader of the reformist faction within the Foundation; his age and reputation give him credibility that no one else in the order has. Characters who support Auspex gain Foundation standing.

**The Merchant Guild:** Its pragmatic leadership evaluates the options. If the characters have a credible plan for keeping the Pax functional, the Guild will support it. If the plan seems likely to result in the realm's dissolution, the Guild will back whoever offers the best chance of preserving their assets. They are honest about this.

**The Githzerai:** If Zerith remains an ally, she convenes a council within her monastery and returns three days later with a formal offer: the Githzerai will provide will-supplementation for the Pax's stability — not equivalent to the machines, but enough to allow a transition period during which voluntary Anarchs can be identified and trained. The condition: the machines must be shut down. Permanently. And the Pax must agree to a formal treaty with the Githzerai monastery, acknowledging their role. This offer is only available if the Githzerai are Allied standing.

### The Faction Negotiation Framework

Each faction negotiation is a social encounter with three possible outcomes: alliance (Allied standing), cooperation (Friendly), or neutrality (Neutral). Hostile factions cannot be negotiated toward alliance in a single session; they require a prior improvement step.

**Conducting negotiations:** For each faction, identify their primary concern and their price. The price is not always material.

| Faction | Primary Concern | Price for Alliance |
|---|---|---|
| The Devoti | Moral clarity | Public commitment to the sovereignty of all Anarchs; no coerced service ever again |
| The Path | Implementation, not just words | Public acknowledgment that the Path's doctrine was substantially correct; formal role in transition governance |
| The Freelancer's Guild | Stability and business viability | A contract: the Guild handles logistics of the transition (paid work, scope TBD) |
| The Merchant Guild | Economic continuity | Assurance that the Pax will remain commercially viable; trade guarantees |
| Xaositects | Respect for the chaos; the right to remain outside | A formal acknowledgment that chaos is not inherently an enemy; non-interference treaty |
| The Githzerai | Machine shutdown; treaty | (see above) |
| Free League | Freedom of information | Public accountability hearings; no suppression of truth going forward |

The Autarchs, if being reformed: their price is their continued existence and the preservation of as much of the Pax as possible. They are not negotiating for power — that era is over. They are negotiating for the thing they built not to be destroyed in the reckoning.

### Thantarnax's Assault Waves

While the political crisis plays out, Thantarnax's forces press the Pax's damaged frontier. Three waves:

**Wave 1 (Dominated Slaad pack, ~20 creatures):** Attacks the Western Scrublands. Easier to repel than it appears because the Slaadi are fighting against their instincts, making their behavior predictable. Characters with Slaadi knowledge (+proficiency in Nature) have advantage on initiative and tactical decisions.

**Wave 2 (Dominated Giants, 4 Fog Giants):** Attacks the Eastern Delta. These are the same type Zerith described from the monastery attack — Fog Giants moving in uncharacteristic formation, attacking specific infrastructure (bridges, mills) rather than population. Disrupting infrastructure is Thantarnax's preparation; she is degrading the Pax's ability to respond to her final assault.

**Wave 3 (Dominated Dragons, 2 Young Adult Shadow Dragons):** Attacks the Sovereign Approach directly. This is a public demonstration — she is showing the Pax what she can do, and she is doing it in full view of the city. The dragons fight to the death. Defeating them requires the full party, potentially supported by Githzerai or Foundation allies. After they fall, a voice is heard — not from the dead dragons, but from the air above, distinct and unhurried: *"Well done. Come find me."*

She is ready for the final confrontation.

**Characters reach Level 14** after surviving all three assault waves and completing coalition building.

## Chapter 9: Reckoning

*Levels 14–15*

### Chapter Summary

The final confrontation with Thantarnax the Unraveler. The setting and approach depend on choices made throughout the campaign. Four possible endings follow, each achievable through cumulative decisions.

### Before the Confrontation

At the start of Chapter 9, review the Faction Relationship Tracker and the three key choice points. Determine which endings are available. Then present the characters with Thantarnax's challenge: *"Come find me."* They know enough to act on this.

**Characters reach Level 15** at the start of Chapter 9.

### Three Confrontation Set Pieces

Choose the set piece based on player action:

**Set Piece A: Defense of the Pax.** The characters hold the Pax and wait for Thantarnax to come to them. They have time (1d4 + 2 days) to prepare fortifications, deploy allies, and set up advantages. When she arrives, she attacks Meridian directly — specifically the Sovereign — and the characters intercept her on the Approach or above it. The advantage: home terrain, allied support, prepared ground. The disadvantage: civilian exposure, urban terrain limitations.

**Set Piece B: The Prepared Battleground.** The characters lure Thantarnax to a chosen location outside the Pax using specific bait she cannot ignore — the Autarchs' direct presence, a known weak point in the directing-sigil network, or an offer of the information Thantarnax actually wants (the Autarchs' founding secret). The characters have control of the terrain and can set up tactical advantages (magical traps, high ground, prepared fall-back positions).

**Set Piece C: The Dragon's Lair.** The characters locate Thantarnax's lair in the deep chaos of Limbo — a massive stable domain she has spent a thousand years creating, filled with the accumulated trophies of her long patience. This is the most dangerous option and the one that yields the most information about the Pax's founding and the Autarchs' original compact. The advantage: preventing her from threatening the Pax at all. The disadvantage: fighting her in her home territory, at her full strength, without allied support.

### Thantarnax — The Encounter

Full stat block in Appendix A. Key narrative elements:

**Before combat:** Thantarnax will speak. She is philosophical and articulate — not gloating, not raging, but calm in the way of something that has been thinking about this for a very long time. What she offers:

- *"I was young when your Autarchs defeated me. I was foolish. I came at them with force. I understand now that I should have waited until their foundation — their Foundation — was showing the cracks from inside."*
- *"I know what is in the machines. I have known for centuries. I am not horrified by it. I am simply correct that what cannot sustain itself will not sustain itself."*
- *"I am not your enemy. The chaos is not your enemy. Your rulers made your enemy out of their own choice, the first day they put a human being into a machine. I am simply the consequence."*

Characters may engage philosophically. She is not bluffing and not stalling — she genuinely believes this and will discuss it with genuine interest. She can be talked to. She cannot be talked out of destroying the Pax; that was decided a thousand years ago. But characters who engage thoughtfully will understand her, which matters for what comes after.

**During combat:** Fight her as a legendary creature of immense power. See stat block. Her preference is to disable rather than kill early — she wants the characters to understand what they are fighting and why. As the fight progresses and her HP drops, she becomes more direct and more dangerous. Her **Dominating Gaze** should target the character who seems most likely to be wavered; a dominated party member creates a moral crisis as well as a tactical one.

**What can change the outcome:** The confrontation's ending is not predetermined by HP. Characters with specific knowledge (the founding compact from Set Piece C; the Githzerai treaty; the voluntary Anarchs beginning to come forward) can present that information to Thantarnax during the fight. If the characters can genuinely demonstrate that the system she has come to destroy is already ending — that the machines are being shut down, that the Anarchs are being freed, that the Pax is changing — she may stand down. She does not want to destroy a free realm. She wants to destroy a tyranny. If the tyranny is ending, the purpose of destruction may be moot.

This is not guaranteed. Characters who have maintained her enmity throughout, who have not dismantled the machines, or who cannot offer credible evidence of change will not persuade her. Her thousand-year grudge requires genuine proof, not reassurance.

### The Four Endings

**Ending 1: Overthrow the Autarchs**
*Requirements: Autarchs: Hostile; at least two internal factions: Allied*

Thantarnax is defeated or persuaded to stand down. The Autarchs, with their system exposed and dismantled, face the consequences: a council-convened accountability process, loss of all governing authority, and exile from the Sovereign. Whether they are imprisoned, exiled from the Pax, or merely stripped of power is the characters' choice.

The Pax enters an uncertain new era: a governing council, a voluntary Foundation supported by Anarchs who choose to serve, and the first chapter of a genuinely free realm. The anniversary of a thousand years becomes the first year of something new.

*Epilogue:* Brother Candor, now serving on a transitional council, proposes that the founding charter be rewritten by the people rather than imposed from above. It takes seven years. It is a good charter.

**Ending 2: Reform the Autarchs**
*Requirements: Autarchs: Friendly or Allied; Foundation: Friendly or Allied*

Thantarnax is defeated or persuaded. The Autarchs have already begun cooperating with the characters — implementing the machine shutdown, publicly acknowledging the truth, working with voluntary Anarchs. The confrontation becomes, in part, an act they perform together.

The 1,001st year begins with the Autarchs in a reduced but recognized role: no longer the unchallenged rulers, but partners in a reformed governance structure. The voluntary Anarchs are few and the transition will be difficult. But it is honest.

*Epilogue:* The Autarchs make one final public appearance without their masks. What is beneath is deliberately not described; the citizens who see it respond with expressions that are impossible to read.

**Ending 3: The Realm Falls — Bittersweet**
*Requirements: Machines destroyed without viable alternative; Devoti or Path: Allied*

Thantarnax is defeated. But the machines are gone, the voluntary Anarchs are not yet trained, and the Githzerai either were not allies or could not provide sufficient supplementation. The Fraying accelerates. The Pax begins to dissolve in earnest.

The characters lead an exodus. Caske's guild network becomes a logistics operation of heroic scale. Morning Wren guides the frontier settlements to exit points. Brother Auspex walks the last street in Meridian and does not look back. The whole Pax is gone within a year.

But the people survive. They form a wandering community in Limbo — the largest such community in the plane's history. The characters are their leaders.

*Epilogue:* In the chaos, freed from the machines, two of the surviving Anarchs begin to shape matter consciously for the first time in decades. Around their hands, a small island begins to form. It takes years. It is not called the Pax.

**Ending 4: Realm Survives, Changed**
*Requirements: Githzerai: Allied; at least one internal faction: Allied; Autarchs: Neutral or better*

Thantarnax is defeated or persuaded (the persuasion path is most available here, given the Githzerai alliance and the genuine transition in progress). The Githzerai treaty takes effect. The machines are shut down over a structured period. A smaller, jointly maintained realm — formally allied with the Githzerai monastery — emerges from the crisis.

The Pax is not what it was. It is smaller. It has a different kind of stability — earned rather than extracted. The Githzerai have a formal presence. The Autarchs' role is contested and will take years to resolve.

*Epilogue:* Zerith is offered a seat on the new governing council. She declines, because that is not a Githzerai role. She accepts an advisory position. The council debates this distinction for a full session.

### Chapter 9 Conclusion

Whichever ending occurs, it should be followed by an epilogue. Describe the immediate aftermath — the silence after the battle, the first day of the new era — and then give each player an epilogue beat for their character: what they do, where they go, who they become.

The adventure ends on the Pax — or wherever the Pax's people have made their new home — at sunrise. The artificial sun still rises, if the Pax remains. If it does not, other suns rise. Limbo is full of unexpected light.

## Dominated Proxy Template

This template can be applied to any beast or monstrosity to represent a creature under Thantarnax's long-range domination.

**Added trait:** **Compelled Purpose.** The creature acts according to Thantarnax's instructions rather than its own instincts. It will continue toward its objective even if injured, ignore available prey that does not serve its objective, and fight to 0 hit points without attempting to flee. A creature under this template is immune to the frightened condition.

**Changed trait:** The creature loses any Morale-based traits (such as Pack Tactics requiring allies) if those require self-preservation behavior.

**Added vulnerability:** If the creature is targeted by *dispel magic* (DC 14) or *remove curse*, the compulsion ends and the creature reverts to normal behavior.
