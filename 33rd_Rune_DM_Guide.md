# THE 33RD RUNE
## Dungeon Master's Guide

*A D&D 5th Edition adventure for characters of levels 1–12*

---

# INTRODUCTION

## About This Adventure

**The 33rd Rune** is a full-length campaign adventure set on the continent of Talumh — an island world cut off from the infinite ocean mist that surrounds it, home to seven fey races, a dozen scattered human communities, and a cosmological wound that has been slowly healing for two centuries. The adventure carries the player characters across four of those communities, from a small city built at the foot of a great waterfall to the wheel-shaped harbor of a pirate port at the edge of the known world, in pursuit of a scholar who disappeared and a secret that was never supposed to be found.

The central mystery of this adventure is not the rune. The rune is the mechanism. The real question — the one that will take twelve chapters to fully surface — is this: when a man who genuinely wants the best for his people is given exactly what he needs to achieve it, and that gift has been deliberately poisoned by the beings who gave it, what responsibility does he bear for the outcome? Satvan the First is not a monster. He is not trying to end the world. He is trying to free his people from what he sincerely believes is divine subjugation. The beings he trusts — the ancient Adi, humanity's spiritual patrons since before the Great War — have been feeding his obsession for generations, carefully cultivating his understanding of the 33rd Rune so that when the moment of casting comes, his most noble wish will be executed as an act of cosmological destruction.

The player characters are the found family of **Priya the Wanderer**, a rune scholar who got closer to the truth than anyone since the Settlement. When she disappears, they follow her trail. What they find will carry them across Talumh, into the heart of an expanding religious empire, through a desert that was old when humanity was new, and finally to a ritual site beneath a pirate harbor where the fate of the world waits for one man to speak.

Three endings are possible. None of them are simple.

## How to Use This Guide

This book is organized as follows:

- **The Realm** covers everything you need to understand and portray Talumh, its communities, its factions, its peoples, and its secrets. Read this section thoroughly before running the adventure. The world must feel real before its destruction can feel like a loss.
- **Running the Adventure** covers all mechanical and narrative systems: milestone leveling, the Path of Man Influence Tracker, the Rune Research Mechanic, the Shade Elder Council Alignment, the Wheel Resistance Tracker, and the Multiple Endings Framework. Read this section before Chapter 1 and keep it accessible throughout.
- **Chapters 1–12** present the adventure act by act and chapter by chapter. Each chapter begins with a summary and DM guidance, followed by scene-by-scene descriptions with read-aloud text, DM notes, encounter information, and skill check DCs.
- **Appendices A–D** contain all custom stat blocks, magic items, and random tables.

**Read-aloud text** is presented in blockquote format:

> *This text is meant to be read aloud or paraphrased to your players. It describes what characters see, hear, and experience — not what they conclude.*

**DM notes** appear in italics and address pacing, likely player divergences, and conditional outcomes. Where the text says *"if the party does X,"* treat that as conditional logic, not prescription.

## Adventure Synopsis

**Act I: The Wanderer's Trail (Chapters 1–3, Levels 1–4)**
Priya the Wanderer has been missing for three weeks. The player characters — her found family, her field agents, her adopted children — return to her home in Fountain to find the study searched, a contact dead, and coded notes pointing south. Investigation reveals that Priya had discovered something significant about the 33rd Rune, and that agents of the Path of Man — a human religious movement that has no known presence in Fountain — were watching her. The trail leads through the client towns of Grove and Portal and onto the southern road. Before the PCs depart Fountain, they receive a cryptic visit from a Tuthá emissary of the Lady, who offers a warning, a fragment of knowledge, and an acknowledgment that what the PCs are doing has been noticed. Act I ends as they board a ship for Window.

**Act II: House of Stone (Chapters 4–6, Levels 4–7)**
Window — the largest human settlement on Talumh, built on islands in the Asura River delta — has been under Path of Man control for a generation. The PCs arrive into a city of watched streets, orderly silences, and an underground that has been driven deep. They must find Priya's contacts, navigate the Path of Man's hierarchy, and turn a key defector — **Varun the Pillar**, Window's Path of Man regional commander, a man whose growing doubts have not yet become conviction. Through him they learn that Priya was taken and carried east, and that the 33rd Rune's location is somewhere in the ancient ruins of the Dune Sea, accessible from the canyon city of Shade. The act ends in a chase across Window's island bridges and out through the Asura delta before the Path of Man can close the trap.

**Act III: Desert's Edge (Chapters 7–9, Levels 7–10)**
Shade is the pivot point of the adventure. Its Elder Council is split — two seats for the Path of Man, two against, three watching — and the PCs must navigate canyon politics, desert expeditions, and the first direct encounter with **Satvan the First** himself. The Dune Sea holds an ancient ruin site that Priya was surveying before she was taken; the PCs venture into the desert to find her research cache and discover what the 33rd Rune truly is. They may find Priya herself, alive in Path of Man captivity. The act culminates in the Council vote and a moment of near-certain death, from which the PCs are saved by an intervention from the Lord — or an agent acting in his name — that is decisive, unexplained, and brief.

**Act IV: Broken Wheel (Chapters 10–12, Levels 10–12)**
The Path of Man has spent months quietly acquiring the records of **Vira the Maker**, the legendary magician who built Wheel's harbor above an ancient Void Magic ritual site beneath the Singing Sea. The ritual begins. The PCs arrive in a pirate city on the edge of revolution to find a wheel-shaped harbor being prepared for something that will change the world. They must locate **Vira's Heir** — a reclusive Feytouched sorceress who holds the key to understanding the ritual site — build the free-spirited resistance against the Path of Man, and finally descend beneath the harbor to face Satvan the First at the moment of casting. What happens next depends on what the PCs have done, what they know, and what they choose.

## Themes and Tone

**The 33rd Rune** is a heroic quest with political and investigative texture that escalates, over twelve chapters, into cosmic stakes. The adventure's emotional register is not horror or despair — it is the particular combination of urgency and moral weight that comes from pursuing a truth that matters, across places and people that are genuinely worth protecting.

Aim for three tonal registers across the adventure's arc. In Act I, the adventure should feel like a mystery: intimate, personal, rooted in grief for a missing person who matters. The worldbuilding is present but not overwhelming; the PCs are people following a trail, not heroes pursuing a destiny. In Acts II and III, the scale expands — community politics, organized opposition, a living world that is actively moving around the PCs — and the tone becomes more political, more complex, and progressively more dangerous. By Act IV, the adventure has reached its full scope: the cosmic stakes are visible, the antagonist is understandable, and the question is no longer what but how.

Throughout all four acts, resist the temptation to make Satvan the First a simple monster. He is the adventure's most important character after the PCs themselves, and he works only if he remains genuinely sympathetic until very late. His arguments should be compelling. His grief at what he believes humanity has suffered should be real. The PCs' task is to stop him, not to despise him — and the question of whether he can be saved, or whether saving him is even the right goal, should remain open until the final confrontation.

The world of Talumh is beautiful, wild, and deeply lived-in. Every community the PCs visit should feel like a place people love. The Path of Man's expansion is not pure evil arriving from outside — it is a human movement, sincerely believed in by most of its members, that has been hollowed out from within by something older and much worse. The tragedy is that the Path began as something legitimate. Some of what it preaches is not wrong. This is intentional; easy villainy is less dangerous than the kind that starts with truth.

## Adjusting for Your Party

**The 33rd Rune** is designed for a party of four to five characters of varied composition. For other party sizes:

- **Three characters:** Reduce enemy hit points by 25% across all encounters and lower skill check DCs involving direct confrontation (combat, intimidation) by 2. In Act IV's harbor battle (Chapter 11), reduce the number of simultaneous ritual stages by one. The Shade Elder Council tracker should begin with one additional seat in the opposition column, since a smaller party will find political action harder to sustain.
- **Six characters:** Add hit points to all named NPC antagonists. In Chapter 11, add a second assault wave after the first ritual stage is disrupted. **Kaal the Unmade** (the recurring antagonist) should receive bonus hit points on each appearance equal to twice the number of party members above four. The final confrontation with Satvan should include two Path of Man Pillars as persistent bodyguards.

**If the party rushes Act I:** The investigation scenes in Chapters 1 and 2 contain evidence that accumulates — do not skip the death of Priya's contact in Fountain, as it is the PCs' first concrete evidence that the Path of Man is dangerous rather than merely ideological. If the party moves through the investigation quickly, the coded correspondence should provide a clear and compelling lead that makes moving forward feel like urgency rather than railroad.

**If the party stalls in Act II:** Window is an unfriendly city, and player uncertainty is realistic. The Path of Man's increased surveillance of the PCs should create organic pressure — the longer they stay without making contact with the underground, the higher the surveillance rating, and the harder each subsequent action becomes. Do not rescue parties from slow play; the sense of pressure and risk is intentional.

**If the party engages deeply with Satvan:** This is the ideal. Give him space in every appearance. His arguments should be the best arguments the DM can construct for his position. Players who find themselves genuinely uncertain whether he is wrong are experiencing the adventure as designed.

**If players disengage from the Rune Research Mechanic:** Do not force engagement, but make the consequences of low Research Point totals visible at the start of Act IV. If the PCs don't know what the 33rd Rune truly is, their tactical options in Chapters 11 and 12 are significantly narrower. Frame this as the characters feeling outpaced — not punished.

**If players want to fight Satvan:** Allow it. He has a stat block. But the most satisfying version of Chapter 12 is one in which the confrontation is forced by Satvan's choices, not by the PCs refusing to engage. If the party moves directly to violence, have Satvan express genuine sorrow rather than anger — he does not hate them; he simply cannot stop now — and run the fight as written. Even ending with his death, the questions the adventure has raised should remain.

# THE REALM

## Talumh

Talumh is a large island continent enclosed on all sides by a boundary of infinite ocean mist — though most of its inhabitants have never seen it. The mist is not visible from land; it only becomes apparent once a ship has sailed out of sight of the coast, appearing first as a thickening of the horizon, then as a wall of grey that swallows the sky. Those who sail into it do not find another shore. They begin to fade — slowly at first, then faster, their presence growing thin and indistinct until they disappear from the world entirely, as though they are dissolving back into the dream from which everything came. No one who has entered the mist has returned. This is not a mystery that troubles most inhabitants; it is simply where the world ends.

Within the broader D&D cosmology, Talumh exists as a pocket dimension contained within the Ethereal Plane — a world dreamed into being within the consciousness of the sleeping One, separated from the outer planes by the mist's boundary. The Void is not a separate plane in this context but the space between the dream and the waking world: the absence that surrounds the dream, pressing against it from outside. The seal the spirits sacrificed themselves to create is the boundary between Talumh's pocket reality and the Void's pressure. The Shadow Lords exist at this boundary, anchored just outside the dream, unable to enter — until the seal breaks.

At the continent's heart, drawn into a sheltered mountain valley by the magic of its guardians, floats **Trí-na-nóg** — the eternal city of the Lord and Lady, home of the Tuthá courts and the seat of the great covenant that has kept the world in fragile balance since the end of the War of the Spirits. Trí-na-nóg is not accessible to mortals by any ordinary road. The Tuthá reach it by ways that are their own. Its existence is known to humans; its location is not.

The world is alive with magic in the way that a forest is alive with birds — constantly, variously, without ceremony. Four magical traditions are practiced in Talumh, each with its own source, its own practitioners, and its own relationship to the spirits that animate the world. Sorcery flows from the living land and is the province of the Tuthá and those touched by their blood. Elemental Magic is the wild practice of those who enter pacts with land spirits — Greensingers, Rangers, Summoners. Divine Magic is granted by great spirits to their chosen — Clerics, Favored Souls, Witches. Void Magic is the rune-based system revealed to Men by the beings they call the Adi: structured, analytical, powerful, and dangerous. Every Void Magic casting risks catastrophic failure and leaves a visible distortion in the air — a shimmer of darkness, a flash of cold light, a brief smell of nothing. Practitioners call this the Scar. It fades in minutes but marks the caster's location with unmistakable clarity.

Two centuries ago, the twelve human communities were placed in their current positions by decree of the Lord and Lady following the defeat of the human empire in the Great War. The **Settlement** — as this dispersal is formally known — was not a kindness. It was a strategic precaution: a unified humanity had come within reach of conquest, and the Lord and Lady were not prepared to trust that a second attempt would fail. The twelve communities are geographically remote from each other. Contact is increasing as the Settlement's grip loosens, but human civilization in Talumh remains fractured and interdependent in ways that the Path of Man finds intolerable and exploits with great skill.

### The Six Geographic Regions

**The Expanse** stretches across the continent's western territory in vast rolling plains and shallow river systems. Two of the twelve human communities are established here, as are the Yashim Mountains, home to the six clans of the Dwarfs. The Expanse is the most open terrain in Talumh — comfortable to travel, predictable in its seasons, and historically the region where human communities and Dwarf clans have had the most sustained contact. The Path of Man has a presence in the Expanse's human communities but has not yet established regional dominance.

**Wilderland** occupies the continent's central mass — a complex of mountain valleys, high passes, and fertile upland basins. This is the Halflings' homeland: **Goodlund**, in a broad valley that has been Halfling territory since before the Settlement, managed by the Tinker clans that move between its scattered settlements. The Sacred Valley in Wilderland's southern reaches is considered holy ground by both the Tuthá and the Halfling elders. The terrain is challenging for large organized forces, which has historically made Wilderland both difficult to conquer and difficult to administer.

**The Pale** covers the continent's cold northern reaches. The **Twilight Vale** shelters the largest concentration of Feytouched on the continent — those of mixed human and Tuthá descent who have built a quiet, self-sufficient culture in the shadow of the **Darkwood**, a forest so old and so deeply inhabited by spirits that most travelers give it a wide margin. The **Shining Plain** at the Pale's northern edge is cold, flat, and largely empty — a place where the distance between the world and the Void is said to be thinner than elsewhere.

**The Long Coast** runs along the continent's southern edge in a curve of grand beaches, river deltas, and the rich grasslands of the Kesla region. The **Asura River** empties here through a wide, multi-channeled delta that has created the most significant geography in this region: the island city of Window, built in the delta's largest channel cluster, connected by bridges and navigated by boat. The Long Coast is the most cosmopolitan region in Talumh, where human communities and trading vessels from up and down the coast have been in sustained contact for generations.

**The Sward** covers the continent's eastern interior — rough, transitional terrain that runs from scrubland through boulder fields into the vast expanse of the **Dune Sea**, an ancient desert that occupies the eastern reaches. The **Crystal Forest** at the Sward's northern edge is a Tuthá-inhabited region of peculiar beauty — trees that have mineralized over centuries, their branches translucent in certain light. The Dune Sea is hostile, inhospitable, and ancient in ways that the desert does not advertise. There are things buried here that are perhaps best forgotten. 

**The Knife** is the continent's southeastern peninsula, tropical and dense, dominated by the **Chula Jungle** and ringed by the **Singing Sea** — a body of water with a distinctive low harmonic vibration audible on calm days, attributed by locals to the unusual acoustic properties of the sea floor. The Knife is the most remote inhabited region of Talumh, accessible only by a coastal sea route or by cutting through the jungle interior. Its human community, Wheel, sits at the tip of the peninsula, looking out at the Singing Sea.

## The Four Communities

### Fountain

Fountain sits at the base of the great **Escarpment** — the cliff face that divides the flat Expanse from the elevated terrain of Wilderland, running north-south for hundreds of miles. Here, a massive waterfall drops nearly three hundred feet from the rim above and feeds the **Sona River**, which flows west across the Expanse in a broad, navigable course. The city takes its name from the waterfall, which is visible from nearly every street and audible everywhere within its walls.

Fountain is a place of color in a world that can be austere. The city's signature multicolored tile roofs — a tradition that began with a single wealthy merchant family three generations ago and spread because each family wanted to distinguish their home from their neighbors — give the city a patchwork appearance from above that delights visitors and locals alike. Religious banners of the Path of Light hang from major buildings alongside the trading house flags of the city's merchant class. The constant sound of rushing water is so omnipresent that residents stop hearing it; visitors from quieter places find it either soothing or maddening.

The city is organized in tiers corresponding to elevation and wealth. The upper tiers, closest to the escarpment and the falls, house the merchant aristocracy, the Path of Light's major temples, and the administrative offices of the city's governing council. The middle tiers are the commercial heart — markets, craftspeople, inns, the headquarters of the major trading houses. The lower tiers, spreading out toward the Sona River, are the province of the laboring class: dock workers, river boatmen, agricultural laborers who come in from the surrounding farmland during harvest season. The wealth gap between the tiers is visible and largely unremarked upon; this is simply how Fountain is, and it has always been this way.

The two client towns — **Grove** and **Portal** — sit below the escarpment, serving as commercial intermediaries between Fountain and the wider world. Grove is an inland trading village on the Sona River's upper reach, handling agricultural trade. Portal is a river port where the Sona broadens enough for seagoing vessels: the point at which goods going south and goods coming north change hands. The long journey from Portal south along the coast to Window takes twelve to fifteen days by river and coastal vessel; by road, faster but more exposed.

The Path of Man has no official presence in Fountain. The city's deep Path of Light tradition — reinforced by the major temples on the upper tiers — has made it resistant to the Path of Man's missionary efforts for two generations. But the wealth gap between the tiers is exactly the kind of structural inequality that the Path of Man's message of human dignity and self-determination finds purchase in. There are Path of Man sympathizers in Fountain's lower tiers. This is new and growing.

#### Key Locations in Fountain

**The Wanderer's House:** Priya the Wanderer's home and research base — a large, slightly chaotic building on the upper-middle tier, recognized by the colored glass lanterns she hung in the windows over twenty years ago and has never taken down. Three floors: the upper floor is her study and library; the middle floor is communal living space for whoever she has brought into the household; the ground floor has a large kitchen and the kind of comfortable, much-used furniture that suggests many people have spent time here. The study shows signs of a hasty departure and a professional search. See Chapter 1 for detail.

**The Temple of the Still Light:** The largest Path of Light temple in Fountain, situated on the upper tier. Its outer walls are white stone; the interior is famous for the colored light cast through its stained glass windows at midday, a deliberate architectural effect meant to represent the Creator's presence filtering into the world. The senior clergy here are respected civic figures. They are also, unknown to themselves, drawing their divine power from the Exiles — the banished spirits who brought humanity to Talumh — rather than from any Creator. This is not relevant to Act I but establishes the world's cosmological texture.

**The Counting House:** The combined headquarters of Fountain's three largest trading families, a large stone building on the commercial tier where accounts are kept, contracts registered, and disputes adjudicated. The trading families also maintain a covert intelligence network along the river trade routes — not for political purposes, but for commercial advantage. This network has noticed Path of Man agents moving through Grove and Portal and is quietly alarmed.

**The Dockside Quarter:** The lower-tier district where Fountain's labor class lives, works, and congregates. Several informal gathering places here — a large covered market, a cluster of riverside taverns — serve as communication hubs for the lower tiers. It is in one of these taverns that the PCs will find the first evidence of a Path of Man sympathizer cell in Chapter 1.

**The Lower Gate:** The point where the road descends from Fountain's lower tiers to the base of the escarpment and the road to Grove. Goods going up and down pass through here; it is also the point from which anyone departing Fountain on foot or by cart begins their journey south.

### Window

Window is built on a cluster of eleven small islands in the largest of the Asura River's delta channels, connected to each other and to the mainland shores by a network of arching stone bridges and supplemented by a constant traffic of small ferryboats that navigate the channels between islands. The buildings are constructed from dark river stone — a local basalt that the delta's river currents deposit in quantity — stacked in a distinctive layered style that gives Window's architecture a sober, stratified appearance. From the water, the city looks like a collection of dark towers rising from the mist.

Window is often shrouded in fog. The delta's warm water meeting the coastal air produces a low, persistent river mist that softens the city's edges and muffles its sounds. Citizens of Window take pride in navigating it without hesitation; visitors find it disorienting. The fog is thickest in the mornings and at the channel junctions between islands.

The city's history as a trade hub has made it diverse — more so than any other human community in Talumh. Its population includes Feytouched merchants from the Pale, Halfling Tinker families who maintain permanent residences alongside their traveling lives, Gnome artisans who came for the trade and stayed for the craft guilds, and Dwarf stone-workers whose ancestors came to build the bridges and whose descendants stayed to maintain them. The Path of Man's arrival and consolidation of power has compressed this diversity inward — the minority communities have grown quieter, more watchful, and more interdependent — but it has not eliminated it.

Under Path of Man control, Window is orderly, productive, and deeply suspicious of outsiders. The Path's hierarchy controls the major guilds through a system of "certification" that requires guild membership to be approved by a Path-aligned council. The city watch answers to a Path-appointed Commissioner. The civic councils that once debated policy are now advisory bodies whose advice is accepted or ignored at the local Pillar's discretion. Dissent exists. It is managed.

#### Key Locations in Window

**The Bridge of First Arrival:** The longest and most architecturally significant bridge in Window, connecting the mainland north shore to the first island. Its sides are lined with small alcoves, each containing a stone figure of a different Navigator — the explorers credited with discovering Talumh's major trade routes. Travelers from the mainland enter Window here. The Path of Man has added its own iconography to the Bridge without removing the Navigators; the juxtaposition is striking.

**The Portmasters' Hall:** The headquarters of Window's Portmasters' Guild — the institution that has managed the city's trade for two centuries. The Guild remains technically independent of the Path of Man, as it is too economically important to absorb without disruption, but its senior leadership was quietly replaced a decade ago with Path-sympathetic figures. The Hall itself is a large building on the second island, its facade carved with the symbols of the dozen communities whose goods pass through Window annually.

**The Deep Quarter:** The lower-level streets of the third and fourth islands, below the commercial tier — a dense, close-built district of workers' housing, small workshops, and the kind of establishments that don't appear on official maps. This is where the underground lives. Priya's contacts in Window are here. The Path of Man's surveillance is thinner at the Deep Quarter level — the streets are too narrow for organized patrols, and the community is too tight-knit for easy infiltration.

**The Pillar's House:** The Path of Man's Window headquarters, occupying a building on the fifth island that was previously the city's administrative center. **Varun the Pillar** lives and works here. The building has been modified to include a private chapel, an archive of Path of Man theological texts, and a secure chamber where the Pillar conducts sensitive conversations. It is guarded, but not as heavily as it should be — Varun's growing doubts have made him lax in ways he hasn't consciously acknowledged.

**The Stone Library:** Window's public archive and the closest thing the city has to a neutral institution. The librarians consider themselves above politics and maintain relations with all factions. Two of them are secretly Priya's contacts. The archive contains records relevant to Priya's research — specifically, cartographic records of Sward exploration routes and a set of pre-Settlement texts that Priya requested and studied three months before she disappeared.

**The Fog Gate:** The water-level entrance to the Deep Quarter, accessible by small boat through a low-arched channel that is nearly invisible from the bridge level above. The underground uses this entrance precisely because the Path of Man's patrols do not check boats that look like they're making routine deliveries.

### Shade

The canyon that holds Shade was cut by the **Lakhsa River** over a geological age too long for human reckoning — a deep, narrow gorge in the western edge of the Dune Sea where the river drops through a series of falls before emerging into the desert. The canyon walls rise two hundred feet on either side, and Shade's buildings are carved directly into them: doorways cut into the stone, windows framed by the native rock, staircases descending from one residential tier to the next along the canyon's natural ledges. Between the rim and the canyon floor, strung on cables the city's engineers have replaced every generation, hang the great canvas shades — kilometers of treated cloth in overlapping layers that block the direct sunlight and give the canyon a perpetual amber-tinted dimness. In the hottest months, walking Shade's streets feels like walking through light that has been filtered through honey.

The city's population clusters in three main tiers: the Rim, where the more affluent families and the Elder Council's meeting hall are situated; the Middle Tier, where most commercial activity takes place; and the Floor, where the Lakhsa River runs through the canyon base and the city's craftspeople and laborers live. Travel between tiers is via staircase — long, worn flights cut into the canyon walls, sometimes covered by overhanging ledges, sometimes open to the amber air. The Lakhsa is the city's water supply, its transportation artery for goods too heavy to carry up the stairs, and its waste disposal system. The relationship between Shade and its river is intimate in the way that only long co-habitation can produce.

Shade survives on trade. The great **Caravan Road** — a route connecting Shade to the Long Coast by a hard two-week journey through the Sward's scrubland — is the city's lifeline. Everything Shade cannot produce itself arrives by caravan: preserved food, finished cloth, metal goods, medicines. And the caravans go both ways — Shade exports the distinctive crafts that its canyon artisans produce (carved stone goods, pigmented glass, the treated canvas that supplies their shades and is prized across the continent) and the material wealth of the Dune Sea's edge (salt, certain mineral pigments, occasional pre-Settlement artifacts that surface after desert storms). This trade runs through Window. This dependency is the Path of Man's primary lever.

Beyond the canyon's eastern rim lies the Dune Sea: vast, hostile, and inhabited by nomadic fey tribes and desert humanoids with whom Shade has been in low-level conflict for as long as anyone can remember. The desert's far interior is genuinely unmapped by human cartographers. This is where the ruins are.

#### Key Locations in Shade

**The Council Hall:** Situated on the Rim, a large room cut directly from the canyon wall with windows looking out over the canvas shades below. Seven seats around a circular table of polished Lakhsa stone. The room is politically charged in ways that its neutral design tries to conceal. The current council: **Dhara the Stone** (opposition), **Piram the Elder** (opposition), **Kelva** (Path of Man), **Surath** (Path of Man), and three neutral seats — **Masa**, **Ondari**, and **Tev the Young**. See Running the Adventure for the full council tracker.

**The Shade Market:** The canyon's primary commercial space, occupying most of the Middle Tier's widest ledge. Open-air stalls under permanent canvas overhangs, smelling of spice and stone and the Lakhsa's particular mineral quality. The Market is the best place to gather information in Shade — the stall-keepers know everything, trust regulars, and talk to strangers for commercial reasons that make their conversation feel safe.

**The Caravan Yard:** The wide, flat space at the canyon's northern entrance where caravans assemble and depart. The Yard has its own small economy of guides, suppliers, animal handlers, and porters. The Greensinger **Mira of the Green Roads** often camps at the Yard's edge — she has a working relationship with most of the experienced caravan guides and uses their knowledge of Dune Sea approach routes for her own tracking operations.

**The Lakhsa Gate:** The canyon's southern opening, where the river exits and the Dune Sea begins. The gate is a structural arch that Shade's engineers built into the canyon mouth over a century ago, providing a checkpoint for desert traffic and a windbreak during the sandstorms that sweep in from the east. Priya's last confirmed sighting in Shade was at the Lakhsa Gate, departing with a small survey party and enough supplies for three weeks.

**The Deep Archive:** A set of chambers carved into the Floor level's eastern wall, housing Shade's historical records. More useful for this adventure: it also holds Priya's preliminary survey notes, left with a local archivist under a false name. The archivist, a small, fierce woman named **Ola**, has been keeping these notes secure since Priya's party failed to return and knows that something went wrong.

### Wheel

The harbor of Wheel is immediately visible from the sea, even through the Singing Sea's periodic fogs: a perfect circle of dark stone walls rising from the water, perhaps four hundred feet in diameter, with a single narrow channel cut through the western face. Ships enter through the channel — barely wide enough for two vessels abeam — and inside the walls the water is preternaturally calm. No storm has ever troubled the harbor interior. **Vira the Maker**, the legendary magician-engineer who created it, built the wheel-harbor two centuries ago using Void Magic of a scale that has not been repeated since. The city of Wheel grew up around the harbor over the following generations, and the harbor itself is the reason for everything: the protection it offers, the trade it generates, and the secret it sits above.

Wheel is a chaotic city in the way that only genuinely free places can be chaotic. Its official government consists of a **Portmaster** elected by the major trading houses for one-year terms, with authority that extends precisely as far as the harbor's commercial regulations and no further. The city's streets operate on a combination of guild influence, personal reputation, and the understood threat of organized violence from any of several competing neighborhood associations. Nothing is formally illegal in Wheel that does not directly interfere with commerce in the harbor; enforcement of even that limited standard is inconsistent.

The city's population is cosmopolitan in a rougher key than Window's — sailors from every community in Talumh, merchants operating outside the Path of Man's guild certification system, a significant Tuthá Satyr and Faun community drawn by the city's tolerance of their culture, Halfling Tinkers who find the permanent market more productive than the road. The Chula Jungle begins a mile south of the city, and the jungle's fey and humanoid inhabitants trade at Wheel's southern market in a state of commercial neutrality that has held for fifty years through dedicated mutual pragmatism.

The Path of Man arrived in Wheel not through coercion but through money and community. In a city that has always struggled with violence and disorder, the Path's offer of stability, mutual aid, and a sense of collective purpose proved genuinely attractive to a significant portion of the population — particularly families with children, small merchants who wanted security, and anyone who had been victimized by the neighborhood associations. A large minority has converted. An equally large minority considers the Path of Man an existential threat to everything that makes Wheel worth living in. The tension between them has been building for three years, and the PCs arrive at the moment it is about to break.

Beneath the harbor floor, at a depth of forty feet, lies a pre-Settlement site of Void Magic power — a convergence point where the boundary between the world and the Void is permanently thin. Vira the Maker found it during harbor construction, recognized it for what it was, and built the harbor above it deliberately. The wheel shape of the harbor's walls focuses the acoustic energy of the site — the Singing Sea's characteristic hum is the site's Void resonance audible through the stone. Vira buried the knowledge of the site's nature and location in his personal records, accessible only through a cipher that he gave to his heir. The Path of Man has been seeking those records for four years.

#### Key Locations in Wheel

**The Wheel Harbor:** The harboring itself — the circular stone-walled enclosure with its unnaturally calm water. Ships at anchor here feel the faint vibration of the Void site below. Most attribute it to the harbor's acoustic geometry; experienced Void Magic practitioners who visit sometimes feel something they cannot name. At low tide, the harbor floor is visible through Wheel's unusually clear water, and the pre-Settlement stonework beneath the sandy bottom is faintly visible to anyone who knows to look.

**The Portmaster's House:** A large, conspicuously comfortable building on the harbor's north side, currently occupied by **Otha the Current**, the incumbent Portmaster. Otha is genuinely trying to remain neutral between the Path of Man's money and the resistance's pressure, a position that becomes harder to maintain with each week of the adventure.

**The Resistance Quarter:** No formal name — it is referred to by locals as "the Saltings," a district of packed residential streets and small workshops on the harbor's southern arc. This is where the free-spirited opposition to the Path of Man organizes, argues, and occasionally accomplishes something. It is also where the PCs will find their first genuine allies in Wheel.

**The Maker's Archive:** The location where Vira's heir, **Sena of the Still Water**, has kept Vira's personal records for the past sixty years. The Archive is not labeled as such — it is the back room of a small chandlery on a side street two blocks from the harbor. Sena has lived above it for sixty years, wants nothing to do with anyone who comes asking, and has made the building deeply inconspicuous through a combination of deliberate neglect and Feytouched glamour.

**The Path of Man's Wheel Pillar House:** Situated in a former guild hall on the harbor's east side, the Path of Man's Wheel operations are run from here by **Arjan the Voice** — a True Believer with genuine charisma and no doubts whatsoever, which makes him significantly more dangerous than Varun the Pillar in Window. Arjan has been coordinating the search for Vira's records for months and is within days of finding them when the PCs arrive.

**The Submerged Approach:** Accessible from the harbor bottom at low tide through a descending stone passage that Vira cut during construction and sealed with a Void Magic lock. The lock responds to Void Magic of sufficient power. Priya's research describes the lock but not its precise location; Sena knows both. The passage leads to the ritual site below.

## The Void Beneath the World *(DM Only)*

This section contains the full cosmological truth of the adventure — information the player characters may approach across all four acts but should only fully possess by Act IV, and only if their Research Point total warrants it.

### The War of the Spirits and the Seal

Long before humanity arrived on Talumh, the pocket dimension that houses the continent was the domain of two great empires and the many spirits that inhabited the dream beneath the sleeping One. The War of the Spirits — a conflict of such magnitude that it nearly unraveled the fabric of the dream entirely — ended with the defeated spirits banished to the Void outside the dream's boundary, and the remaining spirits sacrificing themselves to seal that boundary permanently. The Lord and Lady survived because they were not combatants; they were guardians, and guardians are bound differently. The Exiles survived because they were already halfway through the boundary when the seal was placed, and managed to anchor themselves at the edge rather than being fully expelled into the Void beyond.

The seal is not a physical structure. It is a cosmological fact — a permanent alteration of the boundary between Talumh's pocket reality and the Void that surrounds it, maintained by the sacrifice that created it and reinforced by the continued existence of the covenant between the Lord and Lady and the Tuthá. It is not fragile, but it is not inviolable. Something of sufficient power, channeled at a location where the Void presses close against the dream's edge, could unmake it. Such locations are rare; the site beneath Wheel's harbor is the most significant on the continent.

The Void site beneath Wheel's harbor is exactly such a location — a convergence point where the world's boundary with the Void is permanently thin. Vira the Maker discovered it and recognized its danger. He built the harbor above it as both a permanent marker and a warning: the circular walls focus the site's resonance, making it audible (the Singing Sea's hum), and the harbor's architecture creates the acoustic geometry necessary to contain rather than amplify the Void's output. Vira's intent was preservation, not weaponization.

### The Shadow Lords and the Adi

The Shadow Lords — the corrupted spirits who escaped their banishment — did so through a combination of the Exiles' initial passage through the boundary (which created a brief instability they exploited) and the assistance of a specific individual: **Penumbra**, the most cunning and patient of their number, who found a way to anchor a fragment of her consciousness at the boundary's edge without crossing it. From this position, Penumbra can observe, communicate, and influence — but she cannot enter.

The name "the Adi" — the ancient, the primordial, humanity's spiritual patrons — was created by Penumbra. The runes of Void Magic were not a gift; they were a designed dependency. Every piece of theological understanding that the Path of Man possesses about the Adi is a construction. Penumbra has spent two centuries cultivating this narrative, ensuring it is consistent, internally coherent, and emotionally satisfying to its adherents. She is very good at this.

### What the 33rd Rune Actually Is

The 33rd Rune is real. It is the final and most powerful runic structure — one that, when combined with the other thirty-two, enables a single, world-altering act of Void Magic. It is, functionally, a Wish spell of cosmic scope: whatever the caster intends, at the moment of casting, the Void channels their intent as a miracle.

The rune's location was hidden following the Great War by the same scholars who realized its danger. It was encoded across multiple artifact sites in different regions of Talumh, requiring a practitioner with access to all of them to reconstruct. Priya the Wanderer identified this encoding pattern. She never reconstructed the rune itself — the Path of Man found her and took her notes before she completed it — but her research caches contain enough fragments that the PCs can understand what is happening even if they cannot read the rune themselves.

Penumbra's plan: Satvan makes his wish — for human liberation from divine subjugation — through the 33rd Rune at the Void site beneath Wheel's harbor, channeled through the Adi's power. At the moment the wish passes through the Shadow Lords' channel, Penumbra reshapes it. The seal breaks. The Shadow Lords enter Talumh. This is the dark ending.

Penumbra's vulnerability: the channel can be disrupted. If the PCs understand the mechanism (sufficient Research Points), they can interfere with the channel at the moment of casting. This produces Ending 2. If they can stop the casting entirely and expose the Adi for what they are, the plan collapses entirely. This is Ending 1.

## Key Factions

### The Path of Man

The Path of Man is two centuries of accumulated human resentment given theological shape. Its core argument — that humanity was brought to Talumh as a pawn, punished for a war it did not choose, and scattered by powers that never asked for consent — is not entirely wrong. Satvan the First did not invent the grievance; he articulated it with a clarity that made the movement inevitable. His contribution was to give the grievance a direction: not simple anger, but a structured philosophy of human dignity and self-determination with practical political implications.

The Path of Man's theology shares a foundational belief with the Path of Light: that the Creator brought humanity through the Void to Talumh. The crucial divergence is in what that act means. Where the Path of Light reads the passage as an act of grace — humanity saved from a dying world, given a new one — the Path of Man reads it as a catastrophic error, or worse, a deliberate instrumentalization. Humanity was pulled into a conflict it did not understand, used as a weapon in someone else's war, and then punished for the result. The Creator, in Path of Man theology, either made a mistake or was complicit in a crime. Either way, humanity must not look to divine power for salvation — it must look to itself.

The Path of Man's public structure is hierarchical: **Voices** are senior theological leaders, **Pillars** are regional commanders, and **Advocates** are local organizers and missionary clergy. Below the Advocates is a large base of ordinary believers — people who joined because they found the Path's message of human worth and collective purpose genuinely meaningful, and who have no reason to question where their divine power comes from. The Path's divine magic is real, functional, and indistinguishable from any other form of divine practice.

The Path of Man's dark truth is that its divine power comes from the Shadow Lords, channeled through the Adi mythology that Penumbra constructed. Most practitioners — including Satvan — do not know this. The corruption exists in degrees: the ordinary believer is simply wrong about the source of their power; the senior Pillars and Voices know that the Adi are real patrons who require results; and a very small inner circle — perhaps a dozen people — understands that "the Adi" are not what the theology describes and has made peace with that knowledge. **Kaal the Unmade** is the clearest example of the latter category.

In this adventure, the Path of Man is the primary antagonist organization. It fully controls Window; it holds two of seven council seats in Shade and is applying economic pressure for more; it has significant membership in Wheel and is actively working to control access to Vira's records; and it has a covert cell in Fountain that is still too small to matter but growing. Its expansion has been methodical, patient, and calibrated to each community's specific vulnerabilities.

### The Path of Light (Spiritualism)

The dominant human folk religion — an unstructured tradition worshipping a singular Creator whose grace, believers hold, brought humanity through the Void to Talumh. The Path of Light is not an organization in any formal sense: it has no hierarchy, no orthodoxy, and no central authority. Its practitioners include wise women who read signs and dreams, spirit doctors who mediate between individuals and the unseen world, and temple clergy who maintain the major shrines in communities like Fountain. Its rites center on gratitude, community, and the acknowledgment of a larger order whose nature is not fully knowable.

The Path of Light's divine power comes from the Exiles — the banished spirits who pulled humanity through the Void in the first place, who use the Path's prayers as a channel to maintain their own limited influence in Talumh. This is not known to any mortal, and the Exiles are not sinister in the way the Shadow Lords are — their goals are their own survival, which is served by humanity's continued existence and practice. They are not allies of the Lord and Lady, but neither are they enemies. They are a third force, operating quietly.

### The Lord and Lady

The two guardian spirits of Talumh. The **Lord** (Chaotic Neutral) is a trickster and a protector in one — wild, unpredictable, and deeply serious beneath the performance. The **Lady** (Chaotic Good) is fierce, nurturing, and direct in ways the Lord is not. Both are constrained by cosmological rules that mirror the Shadow Lords' constraints: they can push, warn, and intervene at moments of extremity, but they cannot simply solve the problem. Their interventions in this adventure are deliberate and measured: the Lady's Agent in Chapter 3, the Lord's intervention in Chapter 9. Both are genuine, neither is fully explained, and the PCs should feel the weight of being noticed by powers of this scale without being guided by hand.

The Lord and Lady do not walk in Talumh directly. They operate through the Tuthá courts and through mortal agents chosen for specific purposes.

### The Tuthá Courts

The Tuthá are twelve tribes of fey people — the original inhabitants of Talumh, bound to the land and its spirits in ways that humanity is not. They are not a unified people; the twelve tribes span an enormous range of size, temperament, magical nature, and relationship to the mortal world. What unites them is the covenant with the Lord and Lady, renewed across generations, which grants the Tuthá their role as stewards of Talumh's living world.

The twelve tribes are organized loosely into two courts, though membership is a matter of alignment and disposition rather than rigid authority — individual Tuthá may travel freely between the courts:

**The Radiant Court** serves the Lady and is led in the mortal world by **Queen Glaréad** — an immortal Elf who serves as the Lady's representative among the Tuthá, having held that role for longer than most human communities have existed. The Radiant Court is more open to coexistence with Men and has advocated for measured trust since the Settlement. Its tribes: **Elves, Faeries, Nymphs, Dryads, Pixies**.

**The Glimmering Court** serves the Lord and is led by **King Dwynnan** — an immortal Elf whose long memory of the Great War shapes the court's cautious, sometimes bitter relationship with humanity. The Glimmering Court is more withdrawn and more protective of the old ways. Its tribes: **Brownies, Gremlins, Spriggans, Sprites, Nixies**.

Two tribes — **Satyrs** and **Fauns** — are not strongly aligned with either court and move between both, following their own dispositions. They are often the most visible Tuthá in human communities, drawn by markets, music, and their own complicated curiosity about Men.

Both courts have been watching the Path of Man's expansion with deep unease. The Path's explicit theology — that humanity must free itself from the Tuthá's divine patrons — is not abstract to them; it is a direct threat to the covenant structure that makes their existence in Talumh stable. The Radiant Court is more inclined to act through human allies; the Glimmering Court prefers to watch and wait, trusting the Lord's timing.

### The Greensingers

The druidic tradition of Talumh — practitioners who have entered pacts with the land itself and the elemental spirits that inhabit it, and who serve the natural world independent of any political authority. Greensingers regard the Path of Man's expansion into the Dune Sea with particular alarm, as the ancient ruins contain spirit presences that the Path's ritual activities are disturbing. **Mira of the Green Roads** has been tracking Path of Man agents in the desert for months before the PCs arrive. She is the Greensingers' representative in this adventure and a potential ally in Act III.

### The Shining Path

A monastic tradition of personal enlightenment through physical and mental discipline. No external divine patron; its practitioners believe that the path to truth runs through the self. Politically neutral, the Shining Path nevertheless produces individuals of remarkable capability and genuine philosophical clarity. Individual practitioners appear across the adventure as unexpected allies and moral foils. They are not organized enough to be a faction in any practical sense, but they are never entirely absent.

## Key NPCs

### Priya the Wanderer
*Scholar. Rune researcher. The PCs' mentor and found family.*

**Appearance:** A woman in her late fifties who looks at first like she has been ignoring her appearance and on second look like she has simply stopped thinking about it entirely — her attention is always somewhere else, on a text or a problem or a person. Her hair is grey-streaked and pinned in a style that started careful and became habitual. She wears practical clothing, usually travel-stained, with pockets that contain an implausible variety of objects. Her hands are always moving — gesturing, touching objects, writing invisible notes on flat surfaces.

**Personality:** Priya is the kind of person around whom interesting things happen, and she has spent thirty years making sure that the right people are nearby when they do. Her found family — the PCs — are not accidents; each was chosen because she saw something in them that the world had not yet appreciated. She is warm, demanding, and genuinely curious in a way that makes conversation with her feel like being taken seriously for the first time. She does not explain herself when she expects someone to figure it out, which means people who work with her spend a great deal of time figuring things out.

**Role in the adventure:** The inciting absence. Priya is missing at the start of Chapter 1. The PCs follow her trail across Acts I–III and may find her in Chapter 8. If rescued, she joins Act IV in an advisory and supporting role. If not rescued before Chapter 9, the PCs continue without her, though she survives if Ending 1 or 2 is achieved.

**Fate:** Priya is alive. She was taken by Kaal the Unmade's team at the Dune Sea ruins when she came too close to reconstructing the 33rd Rune's complete structure. She is held in a Path of Man detention site within the desert ruins — not tortured, because her knowledge is valuable and she won't break under pain anyway, but kept in conditions of significant discomfort while the Path tries to extract what she knows through persuasion. She has not told them anything.

**Secret:** Priya is further along in her research than anyone knows. Before she was taken, she had already identified the 33rd Rune's complete encoding structure. She encoded this knowledge in her own body — a form of Void Magic that the Path of Man cannot detect — as a final precaution. If she is present in Act IV, she can provide critical guidance at the moment it is most needed.

---

### Satvan the First
*Philosopher. Founder of the Path of Man. Primary antagonist.*

**Appearance:** A tall, lean man in his late sixties who carries his age in his hands and his eyes rather than his posture. He is not physically imposing. He dresses simply — practical robes in dark colors, minimal adornment — and moves with the deliberate economy of someone who has thought about what his appearance communicates and made a considered choice. He looks like a scholar, because he is one.

**Personality:** Satvan's most dangerous quality is that his arguments are good. He has spent forty years refining his understanding of humanity's position in Talumh, and the result is a philosophical system with real internal coherence: clear premises, careful logic, and conclusions that follow from the premises in ways that are genuinely hard to refute without access to the information the PCs are working to accumulate. He is patient with disagreement — not dismissive, not contemptuous, not threatened.

He is also, by the time the PCs encounter him, someone who has been choosing not to look at certain things for a long time. He turns away from reports of his followers' excesses. He tells himself that these are costs of a larger project. By Act IV, this is visible — not dramatically, not in monologue, but in the things he doesn't say and the way he doesn't quite meet the PCs' eyes when certain topics arise.

**Role in the adventure:** A rumor in Acts I–II, a presence in Act III (first encounter in Chapter 9), and the final confrontation in Chapter 12. Every time the PCs hear his name before they meet him, it should be from people who genuinely believe in what he stands for.

**Arc:** Satvan enters Chapter 9 as the most reasonable person the PCs have encountered since Fountain. He asks good questions. He listens. He finds counterarguments. He is wrong — but he doesn't know that, and the PCs may not be able to prove it until their Research Points confirm what they believe. The discovery of what the Adi actually are should hit him like the world shifting under his feet. What he does with that discovery is the adventure's final question.

**Secret:** Satvan has had doubts about the Adi's true nature for years, suppressed through a combination of intellectual pride and genuine fear. The PCs are not the first people to tell him something is wrong with the Adi; they are the first people who might be able to prove it.

*Stat block: Appendix B*

### Kaal the Unmade
*Path of Man Enforcer. Shadow Lord-touched. Recurring antagonist.*

**Appearance:** A human figure of indeterminate age with the quality of a person from whom something essential has been removed. His features are regular, his build unremarkable, his coloring nondescript. He carries no visible weapons. A rune branded onto the palm of his left hand is partially visible when his sleeve falls back. The rune is not one of the thirty-two.

**Personality:** Kaal does not experience anything the PCs would recognize as ambivalence. He was once a Path of Man Pillar with genuine beliefs, and the process by which the Shadow Lords extended their power through him — in stages, across years, each stage feeling like a gift — consumed those qualities along the way. He is not cruel; cruelty requires investment in the object of cruelty. He is efficient.

**Role in the adventure:** The recurring antagonist — the face of the Shadow Lords' direct influence. Three appearances across Acts II–IV, each at higher power:
- **Chapter 6:** Leads the trap in Window. Serious threat; can be escaped.
- **Chapter 8:** Pursues the PCs in the Dune Sea with a significant force. Has Priya. Direct confrontation is very dangerous; survival is the goal.
- **Chapter 11:** Appears during the Wheel harbor battle, now fully expressing the Shadow Lords' power. This is the definitive confrontation.

**Secret:** Kaal can be disrupted rather than simply destroyed. PCs with sufficient Research Points can target the Shadow Lord bond rather than the host body. A successful disruption may leave the former Pillar behind — damaged, amnesiac, and potentially someone who can be questioned.

*Stat block: Appendix B*

### Varun the Pillar
*Path of Man Regional Commander, Window. Having doubts.*

**Appearance:** A heavyset man in his late forties with a careful, considered manner that reads as confidence but is actually control. His Path of Man robes are well-maintained. His eyes are the tell: they linger on things slightly longer than certainty would require.

**Personality:** Varun rose through the Path of Man on genuine belief and genuine organizational talent. He built Window's Path infrastructure over twelve years. The doubts started three years ago, when two separate scholars were detained by Path operatives and neither was released. He did not investigate. He has been quietly doctoring Path records in Window for eighteen months — removing names from detention lists, losing paperwork for minor offenders — without knowing what he's building toward.

**Role in the adventure:** The defector the PCs must turn in Act II. He provides the lead on Priya's fate, the 33rd Rune's general location, and internal Path of Man intelligence that is otherwise inaccessible. Turning him requires patience; a clumsy approach sends him back to the Path with knowledge of the PCs' identities.

*Stat block: Appendix B (if turned hostile)*

### Dhara the Stone
*Shade Elder Council, Opposition. PCs' primary local ally.*

**Appearance:** A compact woman in her sixties with the physical density of someone who has spent a life doing physical work. She was a stone carver before the council and still takes on small commissions. Her hands are calloused; her posture is direct; her expression defaults to assessment.

**Personality:** Eleven years on the Elder Council, watching the Path of Man's approach progress through every stage from philosophical curiosity to economic pressure to bloc formation. She knows exactly what they are doing and has said so to anyone who would listen. She is not looking for validation; she is looking for evidence that will persuade the three neutral seats. She has been let down by well-intentioned outsiders before and does not offer help automatically.

**Secret:** Dhara knows more about the Dune Sea ruins than she has admitted to the council. A carving expedition twenty years ago found something in the deep desert that she has never told anyone about. The right questions will surface this and provide the PCs a significant advantage in Chapter 8.

---

### Mira of the Green Roads
*Greensinger. Desert tracker. Unexpected ally.*

**Appearance:** A woman who could be any age between thirty-five and sixty, weathered by outdoor life. Her skin is deeply tanned; her hair is kept short for practical reasons. A Tuthá-made bird sits on her shoulder — not a familiar in any formal sense, but an elemental bond. She is almost always approaching from an unexpected direction.

**Personality:** Comfortable with long silences. Gives information in the manner of someone who has already calculated what the recipient can use — precise, contextual, without elaboration unless asked. She has been tracking Path of Man operations in the Dune Sea for four months, has found things she did not expect, and is looking for people who might understand what she found.

**Role in the adventure:** Appears in Chapter 7 at Shade's Caravan Yard. She has intelligence the PCs need: the ruins' location, Path of Man troop movements in the deep desert, and the fact that Priya was taken alive. She will accompany the Act III desert expedition if asked.

---

### Otha the Current
*Portmaster of Wheel. Trying to stay neutral.*

**Appearance:** A broad, comfortable-looking man in his early fifties who radiates deliberate ease. He wears the Portmaster's bronze chain at all times and treats it as both a comfort and a burden. He has been Portmaster for seven months in what was supposed to be a quiet term.

**Personality:** Not a coward — he has stayed neutral because he genuinely believes economic neutrality protects a trading city, and because he has family in Window and knows what the Path of Man does to opposition families. His neutrality is pragmatic, not cowardly, and he is not comfortable with how thin the distinction is becoming. A direct appeal to his self-interest — specifically, that the Path of Man's control of the harbor will end Wheel's commercial independence — is more effective than ideology.

---

### Sena of the Still Water
*Vira's Heir. Feytouched sorceress. Deeply private.*

**Appearance:** A small woman who appears to be in her thirties but is somewhere above a hundred. Feytouched features — attractive in a way that doesn't quite map onto human beauty standards, an uncanny grace, eyes that are slightly the wrong color for the light they're in. Almost always dusty from the chandlery work she uses as cover.

**Personality:** She has been protecting Vira's records for sixty years and has turned away treasure hunters, scholars, Path of Man agents, and at least two sincere visitors who lacked sufficient understanding. She is not hostile; she is completely uninterested in being reached by people who haven't earned it. Reaching her requires finding her (non-trivial), convincing her the PCs can be trusted (harder), and giving her evidence that the Path of Man is days away from finding the records themselves (which is both true and the argument most likely to move her).

**Secret:** Sena knew Priya the Wanderer. They corresponded for years. One of Priya's research caches is in Sena's possession — Priya sent it as a precaution when she realized she was being watched. The PCs finding this out is a reunion of a kind, even if Priya is not present.

## Path of Man Influence Tracker

The Path of Man's control over each community is tracked on a scale of **1–10** per community. At the start of the adventure, influence is:

| Community | Starting Influence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Window | 8 | Near-total control; city watch, guilds, and civil council all compromised |
| Shade | 4 | Two council seats; economic pressure applied; no street presence yet |
| Wheel | 5 | Significant membership; Arjan the Voice is aggressive and effective |

Fountain has no tracker — the Path of Man has no meaningful presence there and the PCs' actions in Act I do not affect it.

### What Influence Does

**At 7–10 (High Influence):** The Path of Man has significant institutional control. PCs face active surveillance, hostile city watch, and difficulty operating openly. Key allies are underground or inaccessible without work. Path of Man assets can mobilize quickly.

**At 4–6 (Contested Influence):** The Path of Man has a presence but has not locked down the community. PCs can operate with caution. Some institutions are compromised; others are not. Neutral parties can be approached.

**At 1–3 (Low Influence):** The Path of Man's local operations are disrupted. The PCs have room to maneuver openly. Local institutions are available as allies. Path of Man agents are cautious and reactive.

**At 0 (Exposed):** The Path of Man's operations in this community are effectively ended. Its agents have withdrawn or gone to ground. This level may not be achievable in all communities within the adventure's timeframe.

### Changing Influence

Each scene in Window, Shade, and Wheel either presents an opportunity to reduce influence (by 1 per successful PC action) or an event that increases it (if the PCs fail or do not engage). Chapter introductions and conclusions specify when the tracker should be checked and adjusted. The influence score at each community affects:

- What help (or hostility) the PCs receive locally
- Which endings are accessible (see Multiple Endings Framework)
- Narrative flavor of each community during PC visits

*Keep this tracker visible at the table. Players who understand the mechanic will make more meaningful choices about how to spend their time in each community.*

## Rune Research Mechanic

Priya's scattered notes, hidden caches, and research fragments are the key to understanding both the true nature of the 33rd Rune and the true nature of the Adi. The PCs accumulate **Research Points (RP)** across all four acts. Track this total openly.

### Gaining Research Points

Research Points are awarded when the PCs find and successfully process one of Priya's caches or other significant sources of rune-related intelligence. Each source is identified in the chapter text.

| Source | RP Value | Location |
|---|---|---|
| Priya's coded correspondence (Fountain study) | 1 | Chapter 1 |
| Priya's Fountain archive (hidden compartment) | 2 | Chapter 2 |
| The Stone Library records (Window) | 1 | Chapter 4 |
| Varun the Pillar's defector information | 2 | Chapter 5 |
| Priya's travel notes (Grove/Portal contacts) | 1 | Chapter 3 |
| Shade Deep Archive (Priya's survey notes) | 2 | Chapter 7 |
| The Desert Ruins cache (primary discovery) | 3 | Chapter 8 |
| Sena's Priya cache (Wheel) | 2 | Chapter 10 |
| Priya herself (if rescued) | 2 (ongoing) | Chapter 8+ |

Maximum possible Research Points: **16**. A thorough party should reach 12–14.

### What Research Points Do

| RP Total | Effect |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | PCs know the 33rd Rune is real and significant; nothing more |
| 4–6 | PCs understand the 33rd Rune is a Wish spell of vast scope |
| 7–9 | PCs understand that the Adi channel the wish and can reshape its intent |
| 10–12 | PCs have evidence (not just assertion) that the Adi are the Shadow Lords |
| 13+ | PCs understand the disruption mechanism; can attempt to break the channel during the casting |

**In Chapter 12:**
- **RP 7+:** PCs can confront Satvan with a compelling argument about the Adi's nature (he may not believe it, but he cannot dismiss it)
- **RP 10+:** PCs can show Satvan physical evidence that the Adi are the Shadow Lords (this is the condition for Ending 1's most complete version)
- **RP 13+:** PCs can attempt to disrupt the Shadow Lords' channel during casting (the condition for Ending 2's active version)

| Ondari | Neutral | Primary concern: the desert conflict; respects military competence |
| Tev the Young | Neutral | The most persuadable; genuinely troubled by what he has seen |

**Council State at start of Act III: 2 for the Path of Man, 2 opposed, 3 neutral.**

A majority is 4 of 7. The PCs need to flip at least two neutral seats to achieve a vote that opposes Path of Man alignment. They can also attempt to flip Surath (Path of Man but pragmatic) if their evidence of the Path's true nature is strong enough.

### Moving Council Seats

Each scene in Shade can produce a council outcome — a vote gained or lost based on PC actions. Chapter 7 and Chapter 9 specify which actions move which seats. Summary:

| Action | Seats Moved |
|---|---|
| Publicly exposing a Path of Man deception in Shade | Tev the Young shifts toward opposition |
| Successfully defending Shade's desert border (encounter) | Ondari shifts toward opposition |
| Providing trade route intelligence that benefits Shade | Masa shifts toward opposition |
| High Research Points (10+) presented as evidence | Surath shifts toward opposition or abstention |
| Failing a scene that exposes the PCs to public discredit | A neutral seat shifts toward Path of Man |
| The Lord's Intervention occurs visibly | All neutral seats shift toward opposition (dramatic; happens regardless of prior results) |

### The Vote

The Council votes at the end of Chapter 9. Outcomes:

| Result | Consequence |
|---|---|
| 4+ votes opposing Path of Man | Shade becomes an ally; sends an emissary and material support to Wheel |
| 3 votes each (tie) | Shade remains officially neutral; individual opposition members support PCs informally |
| 4+ votes for Path of Man | Shade aligns with Path of Man; the PCs must leave; Shade's caravan route to Wheel is now hostile territory |

## Managing the Revelation of Secrets

This adventure contains several revelations that should land with weight. Pacing them correctly is the DM's primary structural task.

**The Adi are the Shadow Lords.** This is the adventure's central secret. The PCs should have growing suspicion from Act II onward (Priya's research starts pointing in this direction) and be able to prove it by Act IV if their Research Point total warrants it. Do not reveal this through a single dramatic monologue — reveal it through accumulation. Each research cache adds a piece. Satvan's reaction when confronted with the complete picture is the payoff; it only works if the revelation has been earned across multiple sessions.

**Priya is alive.** Do not confirm this until Act III. In Act I, Priya is simply missing. In Act II, the PCs learn she was taken alive (Varun knows this). In Act III, Mira confirms she was taken alive and can name the approximate location. The rescue in Chapter 8 should feel like a genuine discovery, not a scripted beat.

**The 33rd Rune is a Wish.** This revelation should come in stages. Act I establishes it as a weapon. Act II suggests it is more complex. Act III's Dune Sea cache contains Priya's notes describing it accurately. By the time the PCs face Satvan, they should understand what he is about to do and why it will not do what he believes.

**What Satvan doesn't know.** The most delicate revelation in the adventure. If the PCs tell Satvan the truth about the Adi and he believes them, the shape of Chapter 12 changes entirely. Do not prevent the PCs from attempting this. The conditions that make him receptive are: high Research Points (the evidence is credible), having met him in Chapter 9 (he remembers the PCs and has thought about what they said), and approaching him at a moment of sufficient gravity. A Satvan who is mid-ritual and being fought is much less receptive than a Satvan who has a moment's pause before the final casting.

---

## Multiple Endings Framework

The adventure's ending is determined by two factors: the **Research Point total** at the start of Chapter 12, and **PC choices in Chapter 12**.

### Ending 1 — The Wish Is Never Made

**Conditions:** Research Points 10+. The PCs reach Satvan before the final casting and successfully confront him with evidence that the Adi are the Shadow Lords. This requires an opposed Persuasion check (DC 18, advantage if RP is 13+) or sufficient narrative justification through play. Satvan stops. He does not believe immediately — but he cannot dismiss the evidence. The ritual collapses without its caster's intent. The Path of Man's leadership is exposed. The adventure ends with Satvan facing what his obsession cost, in a scene that varies by PC approach and what was revealed.

**Result:** The Void seal holds. The Shadow Lords' plan collapses. The Path of Man is politically broken; its divine power source is severed. Satvan is alive, possibly imprisoned, possibly something harder to categorize. The world does not end. The hard work of what comes after begins.

### Ending 2 — The Wish Is Subverted

**Conditions:** Research Points 13+. The PCs are unable to stop the casting but understand the disruption mechanism. During Chapter 12's ritual sequence, the PCs take a specific action to break the Shadow Lords' channel at the moment the wish is spoken. Satvan makes his wish. The channel breaks. The Void seal takes damage but does not fully break; the Shadow Lords' influence seeps through a fracture but they cannot enter Talumh bodily. Satvan, confronted with evidence of what the Adi are, is shattered.

**Result:** The PCs have bought time. The fracture exists and will need to be healed. The Path of Man survives in fragmented form. Satvan is broken — not dead, but a man whose life's work has been revealed to him as a construction. The world is changed, and not entirely for the better.

### Ending 3 — The Wish Is Granted

**Conditions:** Any combination of low Research Points, failed approaches, or PCs who are too late to reach Satvan before the casting completes. The 33rd Rune is cast. Satvan makes his wish for human liberation. The Adi answer it in their own terms: the Void seal is undone. The Shadow Lords can now affect Talumh directly. The Lord and Lady are driven back to Trí-na-nóg. The Tuthá courts fracture. A new dark age begins.

**Result:** Satvan gets exactly what he wished for — freedom from the Lord and Lady's covenant — and something far worse than he ever imagined. The PCs survived. The world they knew did not. This ending is not a failure state: it is a legitimate outcome that follows from a particular kind of play, and it should be run with the full weight of what it means. Some stories end this way.

# ACT I — THE WANDERER'S TRAIL

## Chapter 1: The Empty Study *(Levels 1–2)*

### Chapter Summary

The PCs return to Priya the Wanderer's home in Fountain after an absence of several weeks to find her missing. The study has been searched — professionally, by someone who knew what they were looking for — and a member of Priya's wider network of contacts is dead. Initial investigation establishes three things: Priya left in a hurry, she was being watched before she left, and the people watching her are connected to the Path of Man, which has no known presence in Fountain. The chapter ends with the PCs finding a coded message Priya left specifically for them: a sequence of symbols that resolves to a name and a location pointing south.

### Scene 1: Coming Home

The PCs return to the Wanderer's House — Priya's home on the upper-middle tier — to find it empty and not in the way a house is empty when its occupant is simply out.

> *The colored glass lanterns in the windows are dark — she always leaves at least two lit when she goes out, a habit she developed in the early years when people borrowed her books without asking. The door is unlocked. Not forced; the lock is intact, the frame is undamaged. Unlocked in the way of a person who left in a hurry and did not think of the door.*
>
> *Inside: the kitchen is cold and the last fire in the hearth went out several days ago. The common room has been disturbed — not ransacked, but examined. Cushions have been checked under, books shifted. Someone was looking for something with more care than urgency. The study upstairs is worse. Priya's desk has been cleared: her working notes, her current research, her correspondence log — all gone. The bookshelves have been checked, not removed. They were looking for specific things, not everything.*
>
> *She is not here. She has not been here for at least a week. Possibly three.*

*The study is the primary scene for investigation. Let the PCs explore without rushing. Key details:*

*The books themselves are intact — the searchers were not interested in published material, only Priya's own work and correspondence. This tells the PCs something about what was sought.*

*A faint smell of something chemical near the desk — DC 13 Investigation or DC 11 Arcana: residue of a quick-acting alchemical ink dissolvent, the kind used to remove writing from paper. Someone dissolved something that was written on the desk's surface. It could not have been more than a day or two after the study was searched.*

*Behind a loose brick in the fireplace surround — DC 15 Investigation or automatically if a PC specifically examines the fireplace's upper surround: a small leather packet containing Priya's coded correspondence. This is a secondary archive. The searchers checked the brick but apparently couldn't open the packet, which is sealed with a Void Magic lock that responds only to Priya's touch — or to a specific spoken phrase that she told each member of her found family. The phrase varies by PC; each one she gave a different one.*

**Research Points available:** 1 RP for successfully opening and beginning to decode the correspondence packet. Full decoding takes time — the PCs will get the first layer of information in this chapter and more layers in Chapter 2.

**Investigation checks:**
- DC 11 Investigation (general study): The search was professional and methodical. Not a theft; a targeted intelligence-gathering operation.
- DC 13 Perception (throughout the house): A second pair of boots has been in the kitchen recently — large, military, not Priya's. Someone else was here after the initial search and did not take anything. They may have been waiting.
- DC 14 History or Investigation (the correspondence): The code is one Priya's found family would recognize from her other correspondence. The first layer of decoding (this chapter) reveals a name: **Ovar**, a rune historian who works in Fountain's Dockside Quarter. And the word: *Watch.*

---

### Scene 2: The Body

Ovar is dead. He was found three days ago in the Dockside Quarter. The PCs can learn this from neighbors if they go looking, from the city watch if they inquire, or from the Counting House's informal intelligence network if they have contacts there.

> *The house is small, one room above a print shop, on a street that smells of ink and river water. The door was forced — recently, by the damage pattern on the frame — and inside, the evidence of what happened is still present because no one has come to clean it yet. He was surprised at his desk. The work he was doing when interrupted is still spread across the table: a set of pre-Settlement rune diagrams, hand-copied from an older source, with his own annotations in the margins.*
>
> *Whatever he knew, it cost him.*

*Ovar was a minor rune historian with no significant public profile — the kind of scholar who moves through his specialty without causing disturbance. His work on Priya's behalf was secondary to his primary academic interests, but they had corresponded for years. The people who killed him knew enough to target him, which means they had access to Priya's correspondence network.*

*The rune diagrams on Ovar's desk are incomplete and not dangerous in themselves, but they are clearly related to the same area of research Priya has been pursuing. DC 12 Investigation: the annotations reference a source text that Ovar does not name but describes as "the primary Expanse account" — a document that apparently no longer exists in any library Ovar could access.*

**Investigation checks:**
- DC 12 Investigation (the room): Ovar's personal correspondence has been removed. His published notes are untouched. Same pattern as Priya's study: targeted intelligence gathering.
- DC 13 Medicine (the body, if the PCs examine): He was killed cleanly. Trained practitioner, very likely Path of Man's enforcement arm. This was not opportunistic.
- DC 14 Perception (the street): A neighbor noticed two people in dark traveling robes watching Ovar's building a week ago. They did not look like scholars.
- DC 16 Investigation (Ovar's desk, with the rune diagrams): The diagrams represent an attempt to complete a partial runic structure. The partial is missing its central organizing element — whatever Ovar was working toward, he didn't reach it. But the shape of what's missing is suggestive to anyone with knowledge of Void Magic theory (Arcana DC 14): it looks like the frame for a higher-order runic form. Not one of the thirty-two.

---

### Scene 3: Path of Man in Fountain

Following the threads from Scenes 1 and 2, the PCs find evidence of a small but active Path of Man cell operating in Fountain's Dockside Quarter — something that should not exist in a Path of Light stronghold.

*This scene can play out through a variety of investigative approaches: questioning Dockside residents, using the Counting House's network, following the people the neighbor identified watching Ovar's building, or investigating the Path of Light's own awareness of an outside religious presence.*

*The cell consists of five to seven members, organized around a Dockside meeting house disguised as a storage concern. They have been operating here for approximately eight months. Their purpose is not missionary work — they have made no effort to recruit — but intelligence gathering: tracking Priya's network, identifying her contacts, and reporting to a handler in Window.*

**Encounter (optional, if the PCs confront the cell directly):**
The cell is not expecting confrontation. Two **Path of Man Advocates** (use Spy stat block, Appendix A) and three **Path of Man Devotees** (use Cultist stat block, Appendix A). They will attempt to flee rather than fight if seriously threatened, with one attempting to send a message to Window via a pre-arranged signal.

*If the party captures a cell member:* DC 13 Persuasion or DC 12 Intimidation extracts that they have been watching Priya for "months," that Priya "went south" before they could act, and that someone named "the Pillar of Window" has been directing the operation. They do not know where Priya went specifically. The handler in Window is **Varun the Pillar**.

**Investigation checks:**
- DC 12 Investigation (the meeting house): Documents indicating regular reports sent to Window by courier, with a cipher the PCs can partially decode. The most recent report was sent two weeks ago; it references "the target moving south."
- DC 14 Insight (observing the Dockside community): The Path of Man's presence has not gone entirely unnoticed — two Dockside residents, independently, have noticed something odd about the storage house but haven't said anything because they have no framework for what they were seeing.

---

### Scene 4: The Coded Message

The PCs decode the second layer of Priya's correspondence. This can happen at the Wanderer's House or wherever they take the packet.

> *The second layer of the code reveals itself slowly — Priya's ciphers were never designed to be fast, only to be reliable. What emerges, over an evening's work, is not a detailed instruction but a pattern: names and locations, each with a symbol beside it. The symbols are an index. Cross-referenced against a map of the continent — she always kept maps; there are three on the common room wall — the pattern resolves into a route. South from Fountain. Through Grove and Portal. To Window. And somewhere in the notes, two words in a different hand from Priya's: Not Safe.*
>
> *She wrote this for you specifically. She knew you would find it. She wanted you to follow.*

*The PCs now have: confirmation that Priya went south intentionally, knowledge that something was wrong before she left, names of contacts in Grove and Portal, and the beginning of a research trail. They also have 1 RP from the correspondence packet.*

*Give the PCs time to decide what to do with this information. They should feel the weight of the choice — Fountain is home, and leaving it means committing to something they don't fully understand yet.*

### Chapter 1 Conclusion

Award **Level 2** as the PCs prepare to leave Fountain. Priya went south. The Path of Man was watching her. A contact is dead. The trail leads through Grove and Portal to Window. The adventure has begun.

## Chapter 2: What She Knew *(Levels 2–3)*

### Chapter Summary

Deeper investigation before departure — the PCs have a lead and a direction, but not yet a full understanding of what Priya was researching or why it cost Ovar his life. This chapter develops those answers through Priya's remaining Fountain network: a small group of scholars and practitioners who shared parts of her research without knowing the whole. The Path of Man's agents in Fountain, realizing the PCs are actively following Priya's trail, attempt to shut down the investigation. The chapter ends with a direct confrontation that forces the PCs out of Fountain faster than they planned.

### Scene 1: Priya's Circle

Priya maintained relationships with three other practitioners in Fountain who contributed to her research at various points. None of them know the full scope of what she was investigating, but each knows a piece.

**Lena the Spirit Doctor:** A Path of Light practitioner who sometimes assisted Priya with texts that touched on divine magic history. She is shaken by Ovar's death, which she heard about through the neighborhood. She knows Priya had been asking about pre-Settlement Void Magic theory — specifically, about the original sources for the thirty-two runes, and whether the historical record is complete. *"She kept saying there was something missing from the primary accounts. Not just a gap — something that had been removed."*

**Farren the Scribe:** A professional copyist who transcribed several of Priya's working documents for her and noticed, without being asked, that Priya's recent correspondence used a different cipher from her usual system — more complex, more defensive. He has a partial copy of one document he was asked to transcribe and kept (out of professional habit and mild paranoia) a copy of the text. He doesn't know what it says. The PCs can. *DC 13 Arcana to begin decoding: the document discusses the conditions under which a Void Magic casting of unusual scope would require a specific geographic location with Void-proximity. +1 RP.*

**Torval the Lame:** An elderly Magician — one of Fountain's few practitioners of Void Magic — who met with Priya twice in the month before her disappearance. He is frightened and does not want to talk. He will talk anyway, because the PCs are Priya's family and he owes her a debt. *"She came to me asking about the thirty-third position. I told her it was a myth. She showed me something that made me less certain."* He cannot reproduce what she showed him, but he describes it: a runic structure fragment, partial, that did not match any of the thirty-two. He had never seen it before. +1 RP.

---

### Scene 2: The Path of Man Strikes

The cell in the Dockside Quarter knows the PCs are investigating. The handler in Window has ordered intervention. This scene can occur during or after Scene 1, depending on pacing.

*Two **Path of Man Advocates** and a **Path of Man Pillar's Agent** (use Veteran stat block, Appendix A) make a coordinated move against the PCs — not in Priya's house, not in a public space, but at a transitional moment: when the PCs are moving between the Dockside and the upper tier.*

> *The street is narrow at this hour and you have gotten used to the quiet. The three figures that step out of the side alley are wearing traveling clothes, not Path robes, and they have the deliberate economy of movement that means they have done this before.*

*Tactics:* The Pillar's Agent attempts to separate the party while the Advocates contain specific members. They want to capture if possible, eliminate if not. They know the PCs' names. One of them says: *"The Wanderer's trail ends here."*

*If captured (briefly):* The PCs are held in the storage house for a short period before escaping or being released. In captivity, they are not harmed — the Agent wants information, not revenge — and the questions asked reveal that the Path of Man is specifically looking for Priya's research caches, not just Priya herself.

*If the Advocates escape:* They will report to Window. The PCs' identities are known to Varun the Pillar.

---

### Scene 3: Priya's Hidden Archive

The third layer of the correspondence packet, decoded under pressure, reveals a hidden archive — not in the Wanderer's House, which was searched, but in a location only the found family would know: a specific spot that references a private joke, an old story, a place that means something only to them.

*Work with whatever makes sense for your specific group's relationship with Priya. The archive could be in a hollow tree in Fountain's upper garden, under a loose stone at the base of the waterfall overlook, or behind a false panel in the reading room of the local temple where Priya used to sit for quiet work. The details are yours to build into your group's shared history.*

*Contents of the archive: a bundled set of Priya's working notes on the 33rd Rune, incomplete but substantial. Key contents: a diagram of the runic encoding structure she believes the 33rd Rune uses, spread across multiple geographic sites; a list of the sites she has identified; and a note addressed to the PCs specifically, written in the last week before her departure: "If you are reading this, something went wrong. The work is real. The thirty-third position is real. Do not trust anyone who tells you the Adi are a safe patron. Follow the list south."* +2 RP.

---

### Scene 4: Flight South

The Path of Man's Dockside cell, alerted by the confrontation in Scene 2, moves to seal off the PCs' departure routes from Fountain. The PCs must reach the Lower Gate and get out of the city before the cell can coordinate a clean blockade.

> *The Lower Gate is visible from the upper tier — a clean run downhill if the streets stay clear. They are not going to stay clear. You can see the movement in the Dockside two blocks ahead: figures repositioning, the wrong kind of purposeful. They know you're coming.*

*This is a chase scene rather than a direct combat. The path from the upper tier to the Lower Gate passes through three distinct zones, each with a different obstacle:*

*Zone 1 (upper tier): Two Advocates blocking the primary staircase. Athletics DC 12 to force through, Acrobatics DC 14 to find an alternate route over the balcony rail, or Deception DC 13 to bluff past as merchants moving cargo.*

*Zone 2 (commercial tier): The Pillar's Agent has anticipated the Lower Gate and is waiting at the commercial tier's main junction with two Devotees. This is a fight if the PCs don't find a route around — Stealth DC 15 to avoid, or a deliberate distraction from one PC (who faces the fight alone) while the rest get through.*

*Zone 3 (lower tier): Clear, because the cell doesn't have enough people to cover all exits — but a group Perception DC 11 reveals that one figure is following at a distance, getting the PCs' descriptions for later reporting. Catching or losing this follower is the final beat.*

### Chapter 2 Conclusion

The PCs leave Fountain with Priya's archive, partial knowledge of what she was investigating, and a clear trail south. Award **Level 3** as they reach the road to Grove. The Path of Man knows who they are. The adventure is moving.

## Chapter 3: The Road to Window *(Levels 3–4)*

### Chapter Summary

The journey south through Grove and Portal — the two client towns that serve as Fountain's commercial satellites — and onto the coastal road to Window. The PCs gather additional Research Points and allies from Priya's contacts in Grove and Portal. The Path of Man sends a serious road encounter to stop them. At Portal, as they prepare to board a ship for Window, a Tuthá emissary of the Lady finds them — the first sign that their quest is being observed by powers they had not anticipated.

### Scene 1: Grove

Grove is a trading village on the Sona River's upper reach, a two-day journey from Fountain. It smells of sawdust and river mud and the particular domesticity of a place that processes agricultural goods for transport. Priya's contact here is a woman named **Amsa**, who runs one of Grove's smaller trading houses and has been receiving and forwarding Priya's correspondence to southern contacts for years.

Amsa is alive and alarmed. She received a message from Priya three weeks ago — the last message — and has been waiting for news since. She has a piece of information she does not know is important: Priya's message mentioned that she was going to "the salt library" before heading south, and that Amsa should tell anyone who came asking that "the Shade archive holds the survey." Amsa doesn't know what this means. +1 RP.

> *"She was always careful," Amsa says. "More careful than usual, these last months. She said something once — only once — about the people looking for the same thing she was looking for. She said they had been looking longer."*

*In Grove, there are also two Path of Man Advocates asking questions — posted here after the Fountain cell's alert. They are watching for the PCs and will report their passage south without confrontation unless directly encountered. If the PCs deal with them, they find a message for Window confirming that "the targets are on the road south."*

---

### Scene 2: Portal

Portal is a river port town where the Sona River is wide enough for seagoing vessels — the point where the river's commercial character gives way to coastal trade. It is louder than Grove, more cosmopolitan, and more anonymous. Ships bound for Window and the southern coast leave here twice a week.

Priya's Portal contact is a retired cartographer named **Vesh**, who holds a set of Priya's travel notes from an expedition in the Sward three years ago. Priya left these with him during that trip and never retrieved them. They are geographic and survey-oriented rather than theoretical, but they reference a specific canyon system — the Lakhsa River approach to Shade — and describe "anomalous Void resonance" in the deep Dune Sea east of the city. +1 RP.

Vesh also knows something Amsa didn't: Priya was followed from Fountain to Portal on her last journey south. She knew it. She told Vesh she was going to Window anyway because she had no choice — the Window contact had something she needed.

*The PCs can book passage on a coastal vessel here. The journey to Window takes twelve to fifteen days, hugging the Long Coast southward.*

---

### Scene 3: The Road Encounter

Before the PCs reach Portal — on the stretch of road between Grove and the river port — the Path of Man makes a serious attempt to stop them permanently. This is not the Fountain cell; this is a dispatch from Window, acting on the cell's report.

> *The road south of Grove runs through a stretch of low forest where the trees are old enough to crowd the verge and the light filters down in pieces. You are a half day from Portal. The forest is quiet in the way forests are quiet when they have been quiet for some reason, not the ordinary quiet of a morning road.*

*A **Path of Man Pillar's Agent** (Veteran stat block) and four **Path of Man Devoted** (guard stat block, 30 HP, Appendix A) are positioned in an L-shaped ambush across the road and into the tree line. They are prepared. They have orders to eliminate.*

*Tactics:* The Agent hangs back, directing the Devoted while watching for spellcasters or unusual abilities. The Devoted advance in two pairs, using the trees for cover. The Agent carries a sealed dispatch — not relevant to the ambush's purpose, but readable afterward: orders from Varun the Pillar in Window, confirming that the PCs are to be stopped, signed with the Path of Man's seal.*

*If the party is seriously injured:* A Greensinger passing through the forest (an opportunity to introduce Mira of the Green Roads very early, or a different unnamed Greensinger) hears the combat and assists, disappearing afterward without explanation.*

*If the Agent escapes:* He reaches Portal ahead of the PCs and attempts to delay their departure. This creates a secondary complication at Portal's docks — port authority being uncooperative, a ship's departure being mysteriously delayed — that the PCs must navigate.*

---

### Scene 4: The Lady's Agent

At Portal's docks, as the PCs are making arrangements for their passage to Window, they are approached by a Tuthá — a member of the Radiant Court, the Lady's court. This is the Lady's Agent, though she will not say so directly.

*She is an Elf — tall, with the Radiant Court's characteristic silver-gold coloring — dressed in traveling clothes that seem to belong to no human community and leave no impression of wealth or poverty. She finds the PCs without effort and approaches without hesitation. She speaks quietly.*

> *"Priya the Wanderer sent word before she could no longer send word. The Lady heard it." She pauses, looking at each of you in turn. "The thirty-second year of the Settlement ended this spring. The thirty-third begins in three months. I do not tell you this to explain it — you will understand when the time comes. I tell you so that when it does, you remember that you were warned."*
>
> *She sets something on the dock beside you — a small token, a piece of polished dark wood with a rune worked into it — and steps back. "She is alive. Follow the trail she left." She does not explain who she means. She does not stay to be questioned.*

*The token is the **Lady's Mark** — see Appendix C. It functions once, at a moment of the bearer's choosing.*

*If the PCs pursue her:* She is gone before they reach the end of the dock. There is no evidence of how.*

*If the PCs try to get more information before she leaves:* She answers exactly two questions, with answers that are true but incomplete: "Is Priya alive?" — yes. "What is the thirty-third year?" — "The time in which what was done in the first thirty-two years is either completed or undone."*

---

### Chapter 3 Conclusion

The PCs board their ship for Window. Award **Level 4** as the ship clears the harbor. The Lady knows what they are doing. Priya is alive. The trail leads to Window. Act I is complete.

# ACT II — HOUSE OF STONE

## Chapter 4: The Orderly City *(Levels 4–5)*

### Chapter Summary

The PCs arrive in Window by sea and feel the city's weight immediately. Window under Path of Man control is not a city of obvious cruelty — it is a city of managed compliance: orderly streets, purposeful citizens, watchful eyes. The PCs must navigate this environment carefully, finding Priya's underground contacts without alerting the city watch to their presence or their purpose. The chapter establishes Window as a genuine challenge and activates the Path of Man Influence Tracker for this community (starting at 8). By chapter's end, the PCs have made contact with the underground and have a base of operations in the Deep Quarter.

### Scene 1: Arriving in Window

The coastal vessel enters the Asura delta through the outer channels and ties up at Window's main harbor dock on the third island.

> *The delta fog is thick enough at this hour that the city arrives in pieces: first a bridge, its stone arch emerging from the grey; then the sound of the harbor, ropes and water and the particular low murmur of commerce conducted in deliberate quiet; then the buildings themselves — dark stone, stacked and layered, rising from islands that are connected by arching bridges that look from below like the ribs of something vast and patient. At the top of the Bridge of First Arrival, silhouetted against the morning fog, the Navigators stand in their alcoves. Someone has placed Path of Man iconography between them: simple medallions, the Virtue's symbol in polished brass. The Navigators do not appear to have objected.*
>
> *The dock is busy in the way of a supervised place: everything moving, nothing wasted, nobody saying more than necessary. Two members of the city watch — distinctive in their Path-regulated grey — stand at the harbor exit, checking papers. They do not appear particularly alert. They do not need to be. Everyone in this city already knows the rules.*

*The PCs will need travel documents or a convincing cover to pass the harbor check without immediate complication. Options:*

*Cover as merchants: DC 12 Deception, supported by any goods they can plausibly have been carrying. Success: they pass with a careful look and a reminder of "new visitor registration requirements" at the civic hall. Failure: they are detained for "additional verification" and must make a secondary DC 14 Persuasion check or escape attempt before being released. Either outcome puts them in the city.*

*Legitimate papers: If the PCs acquired papers from Varun's agents (captured in Fountain) or through Portal's harbor, they pass automatically. The papers are for different names. DC 10 to maintain the fiction under a casual question.*

*Entering via the Fog Gate (if the PCs identified it from Vesh's notes or other intelligence):* A DC 13 Survival or DC 12 Investigation check to find the right channel approach in the fog. On success, they enter Window through the water-level entrance without any document check. This is the approach most members of the underground use.

---

### Scene 2: The Weight of the City

A scene of exploration and orientation. The PCs move through Window's commercial tier, calibrating the city's atmosphere, discovering what life under Path of Man control looks and feels like.

> *The commercial tier is full in the middle of the morning — Window does not stop working — but the noise is different from a free city's noise. In a free market, the sound is argument and laughter and the competitive assertion of quality. Here it is transaction: precise, efficient, complete. The prices are posted in advance. The disputes are minimal. The absence of haggling, on a street full of traders, is the strangest thing you have heard in months.*

*Key observations available without checks:*
- Path of Man Advocates move through the market in pairs, not as enforcers but as presences — visible, calm, available. Their role is social: they speak with merchants, ask after families, provide small assistance. They are genuinely helpful. They are also noting who talks to strangers and for how long.
- The minority community presence (Feytouched, Gnomes, Halflings) has shifted toward the lower commercial areas. Not excluded — nothing so overt — but the upper commercial tier is predominantly human. This happened gradually.
- The Portmasters' Hall on the second island has new signage. The Guild crest is still present, but below it: a Path of Man medallion. The same size, prominently placed.

**Skill checks:**
- DC 11 Insight (observing the street): The compliance here is real, not performed. Most of these people genuinely believe in what the Path of Man is doing. Or they have made peace with it. The ones who haven't are very quiet about it.
- DC 14 Perception (watching the Advocates' patrol patterns): A specific area in the Deep Quarter — the third island, lower level — is checked less often than everywhere else. The patrol route skips three streets.

---

### Scene 3: Finding the Underground

Priya's correspondence named a contact in Window: a woman called **Ren**, who works at the Stone Library. The PCs must reach her without alerting the Path of Man's surveillance network.

*The Stone Library is on the fourth island — accessible, public, and staffed by people who consider themselves above politics. Reaching Ren requires getting to the library (easy), identifying which of the staff is Ren (DC 12 Investigation or Perception — she is the one who notices the PCs before they notice her), and making contact without triggering the Advocate who sits in the library's reading room every afternoon.*

*Ren is a slight, watchful woman in her forties with the careful manner of someone who has spent years operating under observation. She will not speak openly. She will, when she passes the PCs in the archive stacks, leave a book on the shelf beside them. Inside the book is a note with an address and a time: tomorrow evening, the Fog Gate approach.*

*If the PCs attempt to speak to her directly:* She is perfectly pleasant, helpful with library navigation, and completely unreachable. She does not acknowledge the correspondence names. She waits for the Advocate to leave the reading room, then makes a point of being in the same aisle as the PCs for thirty seconds longer than necessary while saying nothing useful. The PCs who are paying attention will understand.

*If the PCs go to the address:* See Scene 4.

---

### Scene 4: First Contact

The underground's meeting point is in the Deep Quarter — one of the streets the patrol route skips. A basement room behind a cooperage that looks abandoned and functions as a community gathering space for the three families who have maintained connections despite the Path of Man's consolidation.

> *Six people in a space that could hold twelve comfortably. They have not put lights near the windows. The one who speaks first is an older man — Halfling, with the Tinker family's particular quality of looking like he has been comfortable in uncomfortable places for a long time — who introduces himself as Perrin and wastes no time. "You're looking for Priya the Wanderer's trail. We know. Ren told us you were coming. We don't know where she is. We know she was here, and we know she left — or was taken — three weeks ago. And we know why, which is more than she told us."*

*What the underground knows:*
- Priya was in Window for five days before she disappeared. She was working in the Stone Library and had a meeting with someone in the Pillar's House — a meeting she arranged by sending a letter that Perrin thinks was to Varun the Pillar directly.
- Three days after that meeting, Priya left Window by boat, heading south, with a small group. The city watch was not alerted; whoever let her go had enough authority to prevent it.
- The underground suspects — without proof — that Varun the Pillar let her go. They do not know why.
- The Path of Man, two days after Priya's departure, conducted a search of Priya's lodgings in Window. They took nothing. They were angry.

*The underground cannot give the PCs what they most need: access to Varun the Pillar. That is the next chapter's work.*

*RP available:* If the PCs ask about what Priya was researching and push for specifics: Ren, brought in for this meeting, confirms that the Stone Library records Priya accessed included pre-Settlement cartographic records of the Sward. The library has a copy of an expedition journal from 140 years ago that describes "unusual atmospheric phenomena" in the deep Dune Sea. +1 RP.

### Chapter 4 Conclusion

The PCs are established in Window with a base of operations. Award **Level 5** at the end of this chapter. The underground is their network. Varun the Pillar is the next target. The Influence Tracker for Window begins at 8; initial PC navigation of the city (avoiding major confrontations) reduces it by 1 automatically — it is now at 7.

## Chapter 5: The Weight of the Pillar *(Levels 5–6)*

### Chapter Summary

The PCs work to reach Varun the Pillar — the Path of Man's Window commander who the underground suspects of letting Priya go. This requires understanding the Pillar's House's routines, finding an approach that doesn't trigger the Path's surveillance, and then conducting a conversation with a man who is both potentially their most valuable ally and an active enemy commander whose cooperation is not guaranteed. If successfully turned, Varun provides the lead on the 33rd Rune's location and on Priya's fate. He also begins to come apart in ways that are uncomfortable to watch and are not, in the end, the PCs' problem to solve.

### Scene 1: Learning the Pillar's Routines

Before approaching Varun, the PCs need to understand his patterns. This is an investigation montage — the PCs spend several days in Window gathering intelligence on the Pillar's House and Varun's behavior.

*Methods available: observation of the Pillar's House exterior (watch teams can gather DC 12 Perception and DC 11 Investigation results); conversation with commercial tier merchants who deal with the Pillar's House (DC 13 Persuasion to get useful detail); accessing Stone Library records about Pillar's House staffing (Ren can help, DC 12 Investigation); or a more direct approach of posing as Path of Man supplicants at the Pillar's House's public receiving hours (DC 14 Deception).*

*Information available:*
- Varun receives public petitions three mornings per week at the Pillar's House. He is accompanied during these sessions by two Advocates and a senior administrative assistant named **Kovi**, who manages his calendar.
- On two evenings per week, Varun walks the Fourth Island's residential bridges alone — not a security failing, just a habit. The Path of Man considers the city safe enough that the Pillar's evening constitutional is not a concern. The PCs know better.
- Kovi the assistant is more observant than he appears. DC 14 Insight watching him at the public sessions: he reads the room carefully and positions himself differently depending on how he assesses each petitioner. He is performing the surveillance function without appearing to.
- DC 15 Investigation (deeper research): Varun's administrative record includes two anomalies in the last eighteen months — a detention file for a Void Magic scholar that was marked completed but has no discharge record, and a border passage authorization issued in Varun's name three weeks ago that does not correspond to any known official travel. That authorization is what let Priya leave.

---

### Scene 2: The Evening Bridge

The PCs approach Varun during one of his evening walks — a bridge on the Fourth Island's residential western arc, quiet and fog-softened.

> *He is alone, as he always is on these walks, and he walks slowly in the way of a man who is using the walking as an excuse for something else — thought, or the absence of thought. The bridge lamps are lit but the fog has made them into small bright circles that illuminate nothing beyond themselves. He sees you before you reach him. He does not run.*
>
> *"I was wondering," he says, with the precise calm of a man who has been expecting something like this for several months, "when someone would come."*

*Varun knows who the PCs are. He has known since the Fountain cell's report. He has not acted on the information. This is, in itself, telling.*

*Running Varun:* He is not going to give the PCs what they need immediately. He is going to test them — not aggressively, but carefully. He will ask what they know. He will assess whether they are trustworthy enough to receive what he has. He is also, underneath the careful manner, genuinely frightened: not of the PCs, but of what agreeing to help them means.*

*Key things Varun will share, under the right conditions:*

- **Priya is alive.** He let her go. She came to him because she had information — about the Path of Man, about the Adi, about what the 33rd Rune actually does — and offered to share it in exchange for passage south. He let her go because what she told him was the most disturbing thing he had heard in twelve years with the Path. (He will not say what, yet.)
- **The 33rd Rune's general location** — somewhere in the ancient ruins of the Dune Sea, accessible from Shade. Priya identified it from the Shade archive's survey records. The Path of Man also knows this; they sent a team south six weeks ago.
- **What Priya told him** — he will share this only under significant Persuasion (DC 16 or DC 14 with advantage if the PCs have demonstrated their research knowledge): she told him that the Adi are not ancient patrons. She told him they are the beings who were banished in the War of the Spirits. She told him that the divine power the Path of Man uses comes from them, and that the 33rd Rune, if cast, will be their way in. He did not believe her. He does not disbelieve her. He needs more time. He does not have more time. +2 RP.

**Key Persuasion benchmarks:**
- DC 12: Varun confirms he let Priya go and that she is alive. He gives a general direction (south, toward Shade).
- DC 14: He confirms the 33rd Rune's location is in the Dune Sea ruins east of Shade.
- DC 16: He shares what Priya told him about the Adi.
- DC 18 or clear evidence from Research Points: He provides the border passage records — which the PCs can use to trace Priya's route more precisely — and admits that he has been doctoring Path records. He does not ask for protection. He asks only that the PCs finish what Priya started.

*If the PCs threaten or coerce Varun:* He will close. He has been managing threats for twelve years. The approach that works is honesty and patience, not pressure. A PC who leads with the Lady's Mark or demonstrates knowledge of the rune research (RP 4+) gains advantage on all Persuasion checks in this scene.*

---

### Scene 3: What the Pillar's House Holds

With Varun's cooperation — or without it, if the PCs choose a more direct approach — there is additional intelligence available inside the Pillar's House itself.

*If Varun is cooperating:* He provides after-hours access to his archive, accompanied, with the condition that the PCs leave before dawn. The archive holds the detention records for the two scholars (confirming the pattern Priya identified), the border authorization records for her departure, and a set of internal Path communications referencing "the desert operation" — the team sent to the Dune Sea ruins six weeks ago. +1 RP from the archive.

*If the PCs enter without Varun's cooperation:* DC 16 Stealth (a group check) to reach the archive without triggering Kovi, who sleeps in the building and is a light sleeper. On success, the same archive is accessible. On failure, Kovi raises an alarm, and the chapter's conclusion happens ahead of schedule.

*In either case, the key document is a sealed communication from a figure referred to only as "the Voice of the Adi" — Satvan the First, in the Path's internal designation — authorizing the desert operation and requesting all Pillar-level personnel ensure "unrestricted access" to the Shade caravan route. The seal is distinctive. The PCs will recognize it in Wheel.*

---

### Chapter 5 Conclusion

The PCs have the lead. Priya is alive, carried south, probably held at or near the Dune Sea ruins. The 33rd Rune's location is in the desert east of Shade. Satvan the First's hand is directly visible for the first time. Award **Level 6** at the end of this chapter. The Path of Man's Influence in Window can be reduced by 1 if Varun has been turned (it is now at 6). The city is not safe; the trap is coming.

## Chapter 6: Out of Window *(Levels 6–7)*

### Chapter Summary

The Path of Man moves to close the trap before the PCs can leave Window. This chapter is largely kinetic — a chase through Window's island geography that tests the PCs' knowledge of the city they've spent the last two chapters learning. The chapter ends with the PCs escaping through the Asura delta on a boat toward the southern coast. The aftermath scene on the water gives space for the PCs to take stock of what they know and what it means.

### Scene 1: The Trap Springs

The trigger can come through several channels: Kovi noticed something; a surveillance report from the Advocates flagged the PCs; or Varun, under pressure from the Path of Man hierarchy that has noticed his anomalous record behavior, was confronted and inadvertently revealed the PCs' location. In all cases, the result is the same.

> *You are in the Deep Quarter when the patrols change. It is a small thing — the patrols have been changing on a consistent schedule for three days, and this rotation is twenty minutes early. Perrin, who has been watching the street, comes back from the window with an expression that needs no translation. "They know," he says. "The fourth island bridge is already closing."*

*Window's geography is both the obstacle and the solution. The PCs have twelve bridges to choose from, a network of water-level channels, and the knowledge they've accumulated about the city's patrol patterns. What they do not have is unlimited time — the Path of Man is coordinating a net-closing operation that will seal all bridge exits within twenty minutes.*

*The tactical situation:*
- The Bridge of First Arrival (mainland access, first island) is already guarded.
- The fourth island's residential bridges are partially blocked.
- The Fog Gate water-level approach is still available — the patrol net doesn't cover it because the Path of Man doesn't believe the PCs know about it.
- Varun the Pillar (if turned) has arranged for a boat to be at the Fog Gate at this specific hour. He knew this was coming.

---

### Scene 2: Crossing the Bridges

A structured chase sequence across Window's island bridges.

*The PCs must cross from the Deep Quarter (third island, lower level) to the water-level Fog Gate channel (between the second and third islands). This requires crossing one bridge openly or finding the water-level route through the channel.*

**Route A — Open Bridge:**
> *The bridge between the third and second islands is still passable — there are guards at the far end but they haven't blocked it yet, just posted. Thirty meters of open bridge, two guards who are paying attention, and the fog that helps you and helps them equally.*

Athletics DC 12 (running the bridge) and Deception or Stealth DC 13 (passing or avoiding the guards). On success: clear passage. On failure: the alarm goes up and two additional Advocate pairs close in from the second island.

**Route B — Water Level:**
Acrobatics DC 11 (descending from the lower Deep Quarter to water level) and Athletics DC 12 (moving through the low channel toward the Fog Gate). The channel is tight, cold, and dark, but unguarded. Success: the PCs reach the Fog Gate without triggering the net. Failure (on the Acrobatics check): one PC falls into the channel, requiring a DC 12 Athletics check to avoid being carried off by the current, and the noise alerts a patrol on the bridge above.

---

### Scene 3: The Delta Run

The boat — a flat-bottomed river craft, capable of navigating the delta channels — waits at the Fog Gate. Varun's contact is at the oars. The Path of Man has a faster vessel in the harbor.

> *The fog on the Asura delta at night is a different kind of fog from the city's — thicker, moving, full of sounds you cannot source. The delta channels split and rejoin and split again; the correct route to open water requires either a guide or good luck, and your guide is an old woman named Tera who has been running contraband through these channels since before the Path of Man was a memory and who treats the whole pursuit with a personal insult she seems to find energizing.*

*The Path of Man's pursuit vessel — a light harbor skiff with six aboard — enters the delta two minutes behind the PCs. This is a navigation-and-stealth chase rather than a combat:*

*Three delta channel decisions, each requiring a different check:*

*Channel 1:* DC 12 Survival (reading the delta) or DC 11 Perception (following Tera's signals). The correct route is a tight turn into a shallow channel the pursuit vessel cannot follow at speed.

*Channel 2:* DC 14 group Stealth (moving quietly through a stretch of open water where the fog is thinner). On failure: the pursuit vessel spots the PCs and closes. Tera can create a distraction (capsizing a moored barge across the channel) if the PCs give her a reason to trust them — DC 12 Persuasion.

*Channel 3:* DC 13 Athletics (controlling the boat through a fast-water section near the outer delta). The pursuit vessel, unfamiliar with these channels, grounds on a sandbar here if the PCs reached Channel 3 without being caught. If caught, this becomes a brief ship combat — the pursuit vessel comes alongside and three Path Advocates board.

*Open water:* Once past the delta, the PCs are in the Singing Sea. The pursuit vessel does not follow into open water. Tera takes them south, hugging the coast, toward a waypoint where a larger coastal vessel can be arranged.

---

### Scene 4: On the Water

A quieter scene on the coastal passage south — a night or two at sea, time for the PCs to process what happened in Window.

> *The Long Coast at night is just the occasional light from a coastal settlement and the sound of water. Tera sleeps when she is not needed and the stars are clear. You have time, for the first time since Fountain, to think about what you know.*

*This is a character scene. Give it space. What has the experience of Window done to the PCs? They have seen a human community fully under Path of Man control, and it was not entirely terrible — the city functions, the people are fed and safe, and many of them are genuinely happy with the arrangement. The Path of Man is not pure evil arriving from outside. This is important to let land.*

*Varun the Pillar, if turned, sends a message via Tera: a sealed letter for the PCs. It contains the border authorization records, a personal note (*"I do not know if I believe what she told me. I am less certain I disbelieve it. Be careful in Shade — the two who sit for us there are mine, not Satvan's, which means they are cautious. The others are not"*), and a signet he has pulled from his own ring, which the Shade council opposition will recognize as a token of Window legitimacy. This is a practical gift. It also represents Varun crossing a line he cannot come back from.*

**Path of Man Influence in Window:** Final reduction from this chapter. If the PCs turned Varun and escaped cleanly: reduce by 1 (now 5). If they escaped without turning Varun: no change (still 6 or 7). If they burned significant ground cover: DM's discretion.

### Chapter 6 Conclusion

The PCs are at sea, heading for the southern coast and the caravan route to Shade. Award **Level 7** as the coast of the Long Coast comes into view. Act II is complete. The PCs know Priya is alive, know the 33rd Rune's approximate location, and carry Varun's intelligence. What they don't yet know is what they'll find when they get there.

# ACT III — DESERT'S EDGE

## Chapter 7: The Canyon City *(Levels 7–8)*

### Chapter Summary

The PCs arrive in Shade via the overland caravan route from the Long Coast — a hard two-week march through Sward scrubland that arrives at the canyon's northern mouth. Shade is a different kind of challenge from Window: not controlled, but contested. The Elder Council is split and watching carefully. The PCs must establish themselves in the city, navigate its politics, make allies among the council opposition, and begin to understand what the Dune Sea holds. The chapter ends with the PCs positioned for the desert expedition and the council tracker active.

### Scene 1: The Caravan Road

The journey from the Long Coast to Shade by caravan takes approximately twelve to fourteen days through the Sward's scrubland. This stretch of road is experienced rather than dramatized — a montage of hard walking, gradual landscape change, and the first hints of the desert ahead.

> *The scrubland begins to tell you something is different before the landscape confirms it. The vegetation changes first — the coastal grasses thinning, the trees growing shorter and further apart, the ground shifting from soil to stone. Then the light changes: harder, more direct, shadows with edges instead of gradients. Then the smell changes: mineral, dry, ancient. By the tenth day out, you are walking through terrain that has not been comfortable for anything in a long time.*
>
> *The canyon mouth is visible two hours before you reach it — a vertical darkness in the stone face ahead, the beginning of something cut rather than shaped. And then the sound: voices, the creak of canvas in the upper air, water somewhere below. The caravan road descends into the earth.*

*The descent into the canyon is one of the more dramatic geographic moments in the adventure. Take time with it. The sense of entering a different world — cooler, amber-lit, enclosed — should land.*

*On the road, the PCs can gather initial intelligence from their fellow caravan travelers:*
- DC 11 Persuasion (talking with an experienced caravan hand): The council has been arguing about the Path of Man for six months. Two seats have been openly Path-aligned for a year. The current debate is about whether to formalize the caravan arrangement with Window — which would give the Path of Man economic leverage, but also solve Shade's supply problem.
- DC 13 Insight (watching a caravan guard): Some of the caravan guards are Path of Man Advocates in plain clothes — not unusual, but more of them than a year ago. Someone is paying for additional security on this route.

---

### Scene 2: The Elder Council

The PCs' first full day in Shade should include an early encounter with the Elder Council — not a formal session, but an encounter with individual council members that establishes the political landscape.

*Dhara the Stone* seeks out the PCs within hours of their arrival. She has been told they are coming by Varun's signet token (if Varun was turned) or by Mira of the Green Roads (if the PCs haven't met Mira yet, Dhara mentions her). She is direct.

> *"I know why you're here," she says, not unkindly. "Priya the Wanderer has been in my Deep Archive for two years. Her survey notes are here, under a name she invented. I kept them because she asked me to and because she was right to ask." She looks at each PC in turn. "I need to know if you're going to finish what she started or if you're going to make things worse."*

*This is Dhara's test of the PCs. She does not need reassurance; she needs specific information. Let the PCs demonstrate what they know (RP helps here), what they've done, and what their intent is. If they satisfy her — and they should, if they've been paying attention — she becomes their guide to the council's internal mechanics.*

*Dhara's council briefing:*
- Kelva and Surath (Path of Man seats): Kelva is a true believer who will never move. Surath is a pragmatist who aligned with the Path three years ago because their money solved a real problem. Surath might be persuadable with strong enough evidence; she is not going to act on assertion.
- Masa and Ondari (neutral): Masa's concern is trade; Ondari's is the desert conflict. Both are moveable.
- Tev the Young (neutral): The most persuadable. He has been troubled by what he has seen and is looking for a framework to understand it.
- Piram the Elder (opposition): Old and consistent. Follows Dhara. Reliable.

*Dhara also raises the desert:* She has not told the council what she found twenty years ago on her pigment expedition. She describes it now, for the PCs: a structure in the deep Dune Sea, pre-Settlement, that she recognized as Void-resonant from old descriptions. She didn't report it because she didn't know who to report it to and was afraid of what attention it might bring. She has thought about it since. She gives the PCs the best geographic description she can.

**Council tracker activated.** Current state: 2 for Path of Man (Kelva, Surath), 2 opposed (Dhara, Piram), 3 neutral (Masa, Ondari, Tev). See Council tracker for how to move seats.

---

### Scene 3: Mira of the Green Roads

*If the PCs have not met Mira yet, she finds them at the Caravan Yard on their second day in Shade.*

> *She comes across the Yard with the specific directness of someone who has decided to stop waiting. The bird on her shoulder — a small, dark creature that turns its head to look at each of you in sequence — settles as she stops. "You're looking for Priya the Wanderer," she says. Not a question. "I've been looking for what happened to her. I think we should compare notes."*

*Mira has been in the Sward for four months, following Path of Man agents who have been making multiple expeditions into the deep Dune Sea. She has not found the ruins site — she stayed at the perimeter, tracking rather than approaching — but she has a detailed map of the Path of Man's approach routes and a count of their forces at the desert's edge: approximately thirty agents, well-supplied, with a base camp established at the ruins. She also knows that Priya's party was taken alive approximately six weeks ago and brought into the ruins site. She watched it happen from a distance and could not stop it.*

*Mira's intelligence:*
- The ruins site is approximately four days' march into the deep Dune Sea from the Lakhsa Gate. The route involves two specific navigation challenges: a section of soft sand that is only passable in the first hours of morning and a canyon approach that the Path of Man has posted with watchers.
- The Path of Man force at the ruins is not their combat elite — they were sent to excavate and survey, not to fight. They have Advocates and Devoted but no Pillar-level commander. This may have changed since Kaal the Unmade's movement toward the region.
- Mira offers to guide the PCs into the desert. She knows the approach routes and the patrol rhythms. She will not go into the ruins site — "that is Void-resonant territory beyond what my skills address" — but she can get the PCs to its threshold.

+1 RP from Mira's intelligence about what the Path of Man found and Priya's condition.

---

### Scene 4: Path of Man Pressure — The Border Incident

While the PCs are in Shade, the two Path of Man council seats (Kelva and Surath) push for a formal vote on "security cooperation" — authorizing Path of Man agents free movement through Shade's territory, including the Lakhsa Gate. This would give the desert operation formal cover.

*The PCs can interfere with this vote by:*
- Presenting evidence to Tev the Young of the Path of Man's actual desert activities (DC 14 Persuasion with any supporting research). This delays the vote by three days, during which the PCs can go to the desert.
- Approaching Ondari with a direct offer: if the PCs can neutralize the desert humanoid raid that has been threatening Shade's eastern water supply route, Ondari will vote to postpone the security cooperation measure. This is a combat encounter (desert humanoid raiding party, Appendix A) that takes one day's travel outside the canyon.
- Approaching Masa with commercial intelligence from Window — specifically, the document from Varun's archive showing that the Path of Man intends to use the Shade-Window caravan route as a controlled conduit. DC 15 Persuasion; if the PCs show the document, DC 12. This shifts Masa toward opposition.

*If the PCs do not interfere:* The vote passes 4-3 (Kelva, Surath, and one neutral), and the Path of Man can now move openly in Shade's territory. The desert expedition becomes more dangerous.

*Council tracker update:* Actions taken in this scene can move Ondari or Masa toward opposition, or lose a neutral seat if the PCs do nothing.

### Chapter 7 Conclusion

The PCs are positioned for the Dune Sea expedition. Award **Level 8** at the end of this chapter. Dhara is their ally. Mira is their guide. The council tracker is active. The Path of Man knows the PCs are in Shade but cannot move overtly against them yet.

---

## Chapter 8: Into the Dune Sea *(Levels 8–9)*

### Chapter Summary

The Dune Sea expedition is the adventure's most physically demanding sequence — four days of desert travel, navigated by Mira, into increasingly ancient and inhospitable terrain. At the ruins site, the PCs find Priya's research cache and possibly Priya herself. Kaal the Unmade arrives with a significant Path of Man force to prevent the PCs from leaving with what they found. This chapter is the adventure's Research Point climax — the cache contains the most significant single collection of research material in the adventure — and Priya's rescue, if achieved, changes the character of Act IV.

### Scene 1: The Desert

> *The Dune Sea is nothing like the Sward that precedes it. The Sward at least has ambition — scrubby growth, the occasional lizard, the sense of a landscape that hasn't decided to stop yet. The desert has decided. It is immense and mineral and indifferent, and its beauty is the kind that does not acknowledge the viewer. By the second day, the stone formations rise around you in shapes that predate every taxonomy you know — pillars and arches and collapsed geometries that were not made by wind alone. Mira stops at intervals to check something she reads in the sand that you cannot read, and keeps moving. She has not been wrong yet.*

*The desert passage is organized as a series of environmental challenges:*

**Day 1 — The Soft Sand Passage:** Mira leads the party through the only safe crossing of a wide soft sand field in the early morning hours before it heats and destabilizes. DC 11 group Athletics to maintain pace; any member who fails falls behind and must be assisted (costing 20 minutes). If the group takes too long, the second crossing (afternoon) requires DC 14 due to heat and softening sand.

**Day 2 — The Canyon Approach:** The approach to the ruins is through a canyon that the Path of Man has posted with two watchers at the rim. DC 14 group Stealth to pass undetected, or the PCs can attempt to neutralize the watchers (one Advocate, one Devoted, both equipped with signal horns). If the alarm sounds, Kaal the Unmade's force is alerted to the PCs' approach an hour ahead of when they would otherwise arrive.

**Day 3 — The Deep Desert:** Increasingly intense heat and disorientation. Each PC makes a DC 12 Constitution saving throw; failure gives one level of exhaustion that Mira can address with her elemental medicine at a cost of one hour's rest. At the end of Day 3, the ruins are visible: angular stone shapes emerging from a dune field, older than anything around them.

---

### Scene 2: The Ruins of the First Magicians

The ruins are a pre-Settlement site — a structure built by the first human Magicians to learn Void Magic from the Adi, used as a research and ritual space before the Great War. Its stones are carved with runes — but not the thirty-two. They are earlier, simpler forms, and they predate the settled runic system by generations. The site radiates a faint Void resonance that Void Magic practitioners can feel as a slight pressure behind the eyes.

> *What you are looking at is not a temple. It is a workshop — or was. The stones are arranged with the deliberate precision of people who needed a specific spatial relationship between the carved elements, and the relationship has survived the centuries better than the stones have. Some of the rune-carvings are intact; some are eroded to impressions; some are missing entirely where the stone has fallen. The Path of Man camp is at the site's eastern edge — canvas awnings, supply boxes, a water cache. Eight or nine people. They are working in the ruins, not guarding it.*

*The Path of Man expedition team is engaged in documentation — copying rune inscriptions, measuring spatial relationships, trying to reconstruct what the site was built to do. They are scholars first, Advocates second. They are also, notably, not doing this work correctly — they are missing the significance of the spatial arrangement entirely, which tells the PCs something important: the Path of Man does not fully understand what they have found.*

*Approaching the camp:*
- DC 13 Stealth (avoiding the camp and reaching the ruins' interior directly): The camp's attention is on their documentation work; a careful approach through the ruins' stone structure avoids direct contact.
- DC 14 Deception (presenting as additional Path of Man expedition members): Works until someone asks which Pillar sent them, at which point DC 16 Deception is required.
- Direct confrontation: 6 **Path of Man Devoted** (guard stat block) and 3 **Path of Man Advocates** (spy stat block). Not hardened fighters; this can be handled tactically or through the environment.

---

### Scene 3: Priya's Cache

The cache is in the ruins' interior, in a specific location that Priya's notes from the Shade archive describe in code — a spatial description that only makes sense if you know the rune-language well enough to read the carved stones as directional markers. DC 14 Arcana (or automatic if the PCs have RP 6+) to navigate the interior correctly and find the cache location: a hollow beneath a specific carved stone, sealed with the same Void Magic lock as the Fountain archive packet.

> *The space inside the hollow is small but dense. She packed this carefully. Three packets of notes, a preserved sample of something crystalline (Void-resonant, clearly), a map with markings that will take time to decode, and a letter sealed with her handwriting: "If you found this, you're far enough along. Read the notes before you read the letter. The letter will make more sense."*
>
> *The notes take two hours to read. The letter takes ten minutes. You read the notes first.*

*Contents of the cache:*

**The notes** (+3 RP — the largest single cache in the adventure): Priya's complete reconstruction of the 33rd Rune's encoding structure. She has identified all twelve artifact sites across Talumh that together contain the runic form. She describes the 33rd Rune's function in precise technical language: a Void Magic casting of vastly expanded scope, capable of expressing a single intent at the cosmological level. She then describes what she has discovered about how the wish is processed — that the Adi's channel reshapes the intent at the moment of casting, not before, meaning that even a perfectly good wish becomes whatever Penumbra needs it to be. The notes include a diagram of the channel mechanism and, critically, a theoretical description of how the channel could be disrupted: at the moment of channeling, a sufficiently powerful counter-resonance at the casting site would break the connection. This is the disruption mechanism for Ending 2.

**The letter** (Priya to the PCs, personal): She is afraid. She has been followed for weeks. She has encoded the complete runic structure into her own body — she will not say how — as insurance. She names Kaal the Unmade as the person who has been following her ("a man who was a Pillar once and is something else now"). She ends with something she rarely says directly: *"I am proud of all of you. That is not why I brought you in — I brought you in because you were going to be lost without something to find, and I was right about that too — but it is still true."*

---

### Scene 4: Finding Priya

*If the PCs investigate the ruins site beyond the cache location, they will find Priya — alive, held in a section of the ruins that the Path of Man expedition has converted into a detention space. She is guarded by two Devoted and monitored by an Advocate who conducts daily conversations trying to extract what she knows.*

> *She is in better condition than you feared. She is also, on observation, conducting the Advocate's interrogations rather than being subjected to them — answering questions with questions, explaining things in ways that are technically accurate but strategically meaningless, giving the impression of cooperation with no actual content. When she sees you, her expression does not change. Her eyes do. She does not move toward you. She mouths, very carefully: "Not yet. Two more minutes."*
>
> *Two minutes later, the Advocate leaves for the latrine. She moves.*

*The rescue is a coordination challenge, not a combat:*
- The two guards must be neutralized quietly before the Advocate returns (approximately 3 minutes). DC 12 Stealth to approach; the guards can be incapacitated with Stealth attacks, spells, or the Advocate's interrogation notes (which Priya can use as a distraction — she knows his schedule).
- Priya, once free, is physically capable but has not eaten properly in two weeks. She will push herself regardless. Ask each PC to make a DC 10 check of their choice — the collaborative nature of getting her moving and functional is what matters, not the numbers.

*If the PCs cannot rescue Priya at this point (Kaal's force arrives before the rescue can be completed):* She is taken with the withdrawing Path of Man force, still alive, still held. The PCs know where she was. Act IV includes a secondary rescue track if this occurs. Do not seal this door permanently.

---

### Scene 5: Kaal the Unmade

Kaal arrives at the ruins with a force of twelve **Path of Man Devoted** and two **Path of Man Pillar's Agents**, approximately six hours after the PCs reached the ruins — either because the canyon watchers alerted them or because Kaal anticipated the PCs' approach from Shade.

> *The figure that walks into the ruins ahead of the others is not announcing itself. It has the quality of something that has been moving for a long time in a direction you have been moving away from. The force behind him is organized — not rushing, not responding to a specific alert, just positioned. They came expecting to find you here.*

*Kaal is the second appearance of the recurring antagonist. He is significantly more dangerous than the Fountain or Window encounters suggested — this is the first time the PCs face him directly, and the encounter should establish that he is not a standard adversary.*

*CR for this encounter: 9 (see Appendix B). He does not fight to capture; he fights to end the investigation. But he also does not fight without intelligence — he will not commit fully until he has assessed what the PCs took from the cache.*

*If the PCs have Priya:* Kaal prioritizes recovering Priya over destroying the PCs — she is valuable and the Shadow Lords' investment in her knowledge is significant. This gives the PCs a brief tactical window to separate themselves while his forces are oriented toward Priya.

*The goal of this encounter is escape, not victory.* The PCs have a four-day walk back to Shade; they cannot fight their way through a force of twelve. Mira of the Green Roads (if she accompanied the party) has a contingency route through a secondary canyon approach that bypasses the main path — DC 13 Athletics to maintain pace through difficult terrain. If the PCs bought Mira's trust in Chapter 7, she activates this route automatically. If not, convincing her in the moment requires DC 14 Persuasion.

*If the party attempts a direct fight with Kaal:* He is CR 9. He is difficult but not invincible at this level. If they manage to defeat or significantly damage him, he withdraws — he does not die here. Note their approach for the Wheel encounter, where he appears with increased power. If they are defeated, they are captured briefly; Priya engineers their escape within the same night.

### Chapter 8 Conclusion

The PCs return to Shade with the cache contents. They have the most significant Research Point gain of the adventure (+3 RP from the cache, potentially +2 more if Priya was rescued). Award **Level 9** on return to Shade's canyon. The 33rd Rune is now fully understood. The next challenge is the council vote.

## Chapter 9: The Vote *(Levels 9–10)*

### Chapter Summary

Return to Shade and the culmination of Act III's political thread. The PCs present their evidence to the Elder Council, make their final case, and the vote is taken. Before the vote, they encounter Satvan the First — here in Shade, or at the desert's edge, making a personal appearance to advocate for the security cooperation measure. The chapter closes with a moment of near-certain death from which the PCs are saved by an intervention of the Lord — or a champion acting in his name — that is decisive, brief, and unexplained. Departure for Wheel follows immediately.

### Scene 1: Return and Evidence

The PCs return to Shade with the cache. Dhara the Stone immediately calls a private council session — not a formal vote, but a presentation. The PCs have two to four hours to make their case before the vote is formally called.

*What the PCs can present depends on their Research Point total and what they recovered:*

| RP Total | Presentation Strength | Likely Effect on Council |
|---|---|---|
| 6–8 | The 33rd Rune is real and dangerous; the Adi's role is suspicious | Tev the Young shifts to opposition; Masa and Ondari consider |
| 9–11 | The Adi are demonstrably not what the Path of Man claims; divine channel is corrupted | Tev shifts; Masa persuadable with DC 13; Surath disturbed |
| 12+ | Complete picture: Adi = Shadow Lords, disruption mechanism, full scope of the plan | Tev and Masa shift; Ondari shifts if desert evidence presented; Surath shifts on DC 11 |

*Priya (if rescued) can speak directly to the council. Her presence is worth +2 effective RP for persuasion purposes — she is a recognized scholar with specific technical authority that the PCs may lack.*

---

### Scene 2: Satvan the First

Satvan is in Shade. He arrived three days ago, quietly, to observe the council's deliberations personally. He has been staying in the Shade Market district as a private traveler. The PCs may encounter him before the vote (if they move through the market), during the vote (he is not present but his Pillar's agents are), or in a deliberate meeting that Dhara can arrange.

*The first meeting with Satvan should be one of the adventure's most careful scenes. He is the most reasonable person the PCs have encountered since Fountain, and he is genuinely trying to understand their position.*

> *He is older than the descriptions suggested — the philosophy of the Path of Man is a young person's image, and the man behind it is not young. He is not imposing. He is simply completely present: his attention, when he speaks with you, is the full kind, the kind that does not leave room for distraction. He asks what brought you to Shade before you can ask him. He listens to the answer. Then: "You believe the Adi are dangerous. Not their followers — the Adi themselves. I want to understand why."*

*Run Satvan as a genuinely curious interlocutor. He will challenge the PCs' evidence (not dismissively, but carefully — he has thought about these questions for decades and has considered answers). He will share his own evidence (his theological certainty, the genuine results the Path's divine magic produces, his personal experience of the Adi as beneficent). He will not be moved in this meeting — but he will remember it.*

*Key things Satvan should communicate in this first meeting:*
- He is not motivated by hatred of the Tuthá, the Lord, or the Lady. He is motivated by a genuine belief that humanity's subjugation to divine powers was unjust and unasked-for.
- He has been having dreams recently that he does not fully understand. He does not share this volunteered, but DC 14 Insight will notice that something is troubling him beneath the philosophical confidence.
- He knows who the PCs are and what they have been doing. He is not threatening them. "You are following your mentor's work. That is admirable, even if the conclusions you are reaching are wrong."

*If the PCs have RP 10+:* They can present physical evidence in this meeting. Satvan does not dismiss it. He is shaken. He does not change position — not yet — but something in him shifts and the PCs who are watching closely will see it.*

---

### Scene 3: The Council Vote

The formal vote is called at the Council Hall. Seven seats. The PCs may attend as petitioners (Dhara has arranged this) or may observe from outside the chamber.

*The vote proceeds according to the tracker state established in Chapters 7–9:*

> *The Council Hall is quiet in the way of a room where a decision is about to be permanent. The seven seats are occupied. The canvas shades above filter the afternoon light to amber. Dhara the Stone speaks first, as is traditional for the senior opposition member. She is brief. She presents the PCs' evidence — or summarizes it — and asks the question plainly: "What is the Path of Man doing in our desert, and what have they found?" Then it is time to vote.*

*Run the vote according to the tracker. Each council member's vote is the result of the PCs' accumulated work in Act III.*

*If the vote fails (Path of Man majority):* The council has aligned. Shade's Lakhsa Gate is now Path of Man-controlled territory. The PCs must leave. Dhara and Piram will not abandon their opposition — they vote no — but they are now a political minority in a hostile city. They can still provide support to the PCs informally and will eventually leave Shade for Wheel if the situation permits.

*If the vote ties or passes:* The council's resolution varies in strength, but Shade's formal neutrality is maintained or the opposition has won. The PCs leave with whatever support the council can offer — at minimum, a caravan route guarantee; at maximum, a formal letter of support that will matter in Wheel.

---

### Scene 4: The Lord's Intervention

*This scene occurs in the context of the Path of Man's response to whatever the council vote produced — or, if the vote failed, as the PCs are attempting to leave a now-hostile Shade. Kaal the Unmade (or a significant Path of Man force acting on his direction from the desert) has tracked the PCs to Shade and coordinates a moment of extraction that should be unambiguously life-threatening.*

*The specific staging is DM-flexible: a coordinated attack at the canyon gate as the PCs attempt to depart; an ambush in the Caravan Yard; a pursuit through the canyon stairways. The point is that the PCs should arrive at a moment where the odds are genuinely bad — outnumbered, cornered, or facing a force they cannot fight through.*

*Then the Lord intervenes.*

> *It is not an army. It is one figure — a man, in aspect, though the word feels insufficient — standing at the place where the Path of Man's force was a moment ago, and there is a quality to his stillness that makes the canyon very quiet. He looks at you. He does not smile, exactly, but there is something in his expression that is closer to amusement than to anything else. The Path of Man's nearest members have stepped back. Not fled — stepped back, involuntarily, as if the space around him does not accommodate them comfortably.*
>
> *"You are needed elsewhere," he says. To you. He does not look at the Path of Man at all. "Go."*
>
> *And then — and this is the part that will be hardest to explain later — the Path of Man forces are simply not a problem anymore. Some of them have gone to ground. Some have retreated. The figure is gone before you turn back to see him.*

*The ambiguity of whether this was the Lord himself or a champion of the Glimmering Court acting in his name should be maintained in play. If the PCs ask (to each other, to Dhara, to Mira): no one knows. The Glimmering Court does not confirm or deny. The intervention happened. The PCs are alive. That is what matters.*

*If Dhara is present:* She is the most shaken she has been in eleven years of council service. She says nothing for several minutes. Then: "You should go to Wheel."

---

### Chapter 9 Conclusion

The PCs depart Shade with whatever the council gave them and the knowledge that the Lord is watching. Award **Level 10** as they reach the coast road south. Act III is complete. The confrontation with Satvan has been set. Wheel is waiting.

# ACT IV — BROKEN WHEEL

## Chapter 10: The Free Port *(Levels 10–11)*

### Chapter Summary

The PCs arrive in Wheel to find the city on the edge of something. The Path of Man has been here for years — their money, their stability, their community services — and Arjan the Voice is days from acquiring access to Vira's records. The free-spirited resistance is disorganized and needs leadership. The PCs must find Vira's Heir, understand the ritual site, and begin to consolidate the resistance. Kaal the Unmade arrives in Wheel before the chapter ends, signaling that the final phase has begun.

### Scene 1: Arriving in Wheel

The coastal vessel enters the Wheel harbor through the narrow channel. The harbor's perfect calm and the low harmonic vibration of the Singing Sea floor greet the PCs simultaneously.

> *The channel is barely wide enough to make you hold your breath as the vessel threads through, and then you are inside, and the noise of the open sea is simply gone. The harbor is a circle of dark stone walls rising thirty feet above the waterline, the surface perfectly still, and in the silence — relative silence; the city above is not quiet — there is a sound that is not quite sound. A tone, deep and constant, coming from the water or the stone or somewhere beneath both. Several sailors on the vessel make a sign that means different things in different traditions but serves the same purpose.*
>
> *Wheel above the harbor walls is exactly what it was described to you as: chaotic, alive, and conducting every variety of human activity simultaneously at high volume. The path from the harbor to the city street requires navigating around four people arguing, two people repairing a net, and one person asleep in the middle of the walkway who no one is moving around because the walkway is wide enough.*

*The harbor vibration is immediately notable to anyone with Arcana training (DC 10): this is Void resonance from below, consistent and old. If the PCs have sufficient Research Points (7+), they recognize it from Priya's descriptions. If Priya is with them, she is visibly affected — she has read about this but never felt it.*

*The Wheel Resistance Tracker begins at 2. It will rise through PC actions across Chapters 10 and 11.*

---

### Scene 2: The Resistance

The free-spirited resistance to the Path of Man in Wheel is not organized. It is a collection of people who agree on the problem and disagree on the solution, meeting irregularly in the Saltings district with no permanent leadership and a significant internal conflict between those who want to fight and those who want to wait.

*The PCs can find their first contact through Sena of the Still Water (if they identify her quickly) or through the Saltings district's informal social network. Either route leads them to a meeting that is currently mid-argument.*

> *The room holds twelve people and at least four simultaneous disagreements. The largest argument is between a broad, scarred woman named Calla — who runs one of the Saltings' neighborhood associations and has been preparing for a physical confrontation with the Path of Man's enforcers for three months — and a quiet, precise man named Tomas, who believes that direct action will give Arjan the Voice a reason to request Path of Man military support from Window and turn Wheel from a contested city into an occupied one. Both arguments are correct. That is why the meeting has been going on for two hours.*

*The PCs' arrival is significant. The resistance needs someone who is not one of them — who hasn't been trapped in the same argument for months — to cut through the deadlock. DC 12 Persuasion (framed as unifying the two positions, not choosing one) to redirect the argument toward a common action. Success: both Calla and Tomas are willing to work with the PCs. A failed check means the PCs have aligned with one camp; they get one full ally but face passive resistance from the other.*

*Raising the Resistance Tracker:*
- Successfully unifying the resistance factions: +1
- Winning a public confrontation against Path of Man agents in the Saltings: +1

---

### Scene 3: Finding Sena of the Still Water

Finding Sena is a puzzle, not a door. The chandlery on the side street two blocks from the harbor has no visible sign and its goods are stacked to block visibility from outside. The person behind the counter, when the PCs enter, is Sena — who will complete their transaction efficiently, add the correct amount to their bill, and wait for them to leave.

*She will not reveal herself voluntarily. The PCs must give her a reason. The most direct approach: use Priya's name.*

> *You say the name. She does not react — she is very good at not reacting — but her hands stop moving over the counter for one second before they resume. Then she looks at you directly for the first time since you walked in. "You are going to tell me she is dead," she says. It is not a question. It is a preparation.*

*If Priya is alive and with the PCs:* Sena's prepared composure breaks for a moment — not dramatically, but visibly. Priya gets to this differently than explanation would. Let them have the scene.

*If Priya was not rescued:* The PCs must tell Sena that Priya is alive, in Path of Man captivity, and that finding what Priya would have sent here is part of why they came. Sena processes this in silence. Then: "I have her last letter. She said someone would come eventually. I did not expect you this quickly."

*Gaining Sena's trust:* Sena asks three questions, in whatever order she chooses, about the PCs' knowledge, purpose, and understanding of what the harbor holds. These are not test questions — she genuinely wants to know if they understand what they are dealing with. Research Points (6+) make the third question answerable confidently. If the PCs cannot answer the third question (what is the Void site and why did Vira build the harbor above it rather than destroying it), Sena will not open the Archive yet. She will tell them what they need to know first.

*What Sena provides (once trust is established):*
- Priya's cache (Sena's possession): +2 RP. This is Priya's encoded summary of the complete encoding structure and her theory about the disruption mechanism, sent to Sena as insurance.
- Vira's records (Archive): A detailed account of the harbor's construction, the ritual site below, and the passage down. Most importantly: the specific resonance frequency required to break a Void channel at the casting moment. This confirms Priya's disruption theory and adds a practical mechanism.
- Sena herself: She will assist the PCs if she is satisfied that they understand what they are doing. Her magic (12th-level sorceress, Feytouched) is significant.

*Council tracker note:* Sena publicly joining the resistance raises the Wheel Resistance Tracker by +2.

---

### Scene 4: Otha the Portmaster

Otha can be reached through the Portmaster's House or through the harbor's commercial routine. He is not hiding from the PCs; he is genuinely uncertain what the correct move is.

*The conversation with Otha is a negotiation, not a confrontation. He knows things are wrong. He has been watching Arjan the Voice's activities for months and has his own suspicions. What he lacks is a reason to act that outweighs the risk.*

*The argument that moves Otha:* The Path of Man does not want a free port. They want a controlled port. The same process they used in Window — philosophical society, then political faction, then administrative replacement — is already four years into its Wheel execution. Otha can either stay neutral until there is nothing left to be neutral about, or he can be part of the reason things go differently. DC 14 Persuasion; DC 12 if the PCs show him Varun's document from Window about the harbor route plan.

*If Otha commits:* He provides access to the harbor's official record system (the construction records that reference Vira's Archive), uses his office to create logistical delays for Arjan the Voice's supply chain, and informally authorizes the resistance to operate in the harbor district. +2 to the Wheel Resistance Tracker.

---

### Scene 5: Kaal the Unmade Arrives

Kaal arrives in Wheel by the chapter's end — a Path of Man vessel in the harbor, six days after the PCs. The city's inhabitants who encounter him describe the same thing to different people in different words: something that was once a man, walking toward the Pillar's House with the focus of something that knows exactly what it wants and is no longer distracted by what it is.

*The PCs will hear about Kaal's arrival before they see him. Mira (if present) feels his Void resonance before the ship docks. Sena recognizes the pattern in the harbor water.*

*This scene is informational. Kaal does not engage the PCs in Chapter 10 — there is no value in it for the Shadow Lords' plan until the ritual is underway. He is here to ensure Arjan completes the acquisition of Vira's records and begins the ritual sequence.*

*What the PCs should understand from Kaal's arrival:* The timeline is now. Arjan the Voice has been in Wheel for months. Kaal being here means the Path of Man is ready to begin.

### Chapter 10 Conclusion

The resistance is forming. Sena is an ally. The ritual site is understood. Award **Level 11** at the end of this chapter. The Path of Man is beginning to move. Chapter 11 starts immediately.

## Chapter 11: The Wheel Turns *(Levels 11–12)*

### Chapter Summary

The ritual sequence begins. Arjan the Voice, with Kaal the Unmade's authority behind him, initiates the process required to prepare the Void site beneath the harbor for the 33rd Rune's casting. The preparation happens in three stages, each requiring actions above the harbor before the descent. The PCs must fight through the stages while coordinating the resistance and managing Wheel's complicated faction geography. Satvan the First arrives by ship in the chapter's second half, increasingly certain and increasingly dangerous. Kaal the Unmade confronts the PCs directly in this chapter's climax.

### Scene 1: The Ritual Sequence Begins

The morning after Kaal's arrival, the Path of Man moves. A significant force — twenty Devoted and six Advocates, coordinated by Arjan the Voice — takes control of the harbor's public access points. They are not fighting anyone; they are positioning for something else. Arjan stands at the harbor's edge with Vira's records (he reached them last night, hours after Kaal's arrival) and begins reading from them quietly.

> *The harbor vibration changes. It has been constant since you arrived — a low, reliable tone that you have stopped noticing. Now it is different: a slow increase in frequency, very gradual, like something beginning to wake. The water in the harbor is still perfectly calm, but it has a different quality to the stillness. The sailors near the docks can feel it. None of them know what to call it.*

*Three ritual stages, each requiring actions above the harbor:*

**Stage 1 — The Harbor Alignment:** Arjan must place four resonance markers at specific points around the harbor's interior walls, corresponding to Void-proximity nodes in the site below. These markers are Void Magic artifacts brought from the desert ruins. If all four are placed without interruption, Stage 1 is complete in two hours.

*PC objective: Interrupt at least two of the four placements.* Each placement requires one Advocate to hold the marker at the wall for ten minutes. Disrupting a placement (by defeating the holder or destroying the marker) requires DC 12 Athletics or a combat encounter with one Advocate and two Devoted.

*Resistance support (Tracker 6+):* Calla's neighborhood association covers two of the four wall positions, handling their markers while the PCs handle the other two. *Tracker 4–5:* The resistance assists at one position. *Tracker 2–3:* The PCs are on their own.

**Stage 2 — The Channel Opening:** With the markers placed (even if only two are placed, the channel can be partially opened — it's weaker but functional), Kaal performs a more complex ritual at the harbor's southern arc: a Void Magic working that opens the channel between the site below and the Adi. This is a ten-minute ritual that Kaal conducts personally, generating visible effects (a darkness around him that is not shadow, a smell of nothing, the Void resonance increasing sharply).

*PC objective: Disrupt the channel opening.* This requires getting past the twelve Devoted guarding the southern arc and interrupting Kaal directly — either through combat (he is protected and will fight back), or through a specific Void Magic working (DC 16 Arcana, costs a 5th-level spell slot) that interferes with the ritual's spatial alignment. If the channel is disrupted, Stage 2 must restart from the beginning (another ten minutes). This can be done multiple times, but Kaal becomes more aggressive with each disruption.

**Stage 3 — The Descent:** The final stage is the descent below the harbor. The passage is through the submerged approach — forty feet below the waterline, through Vira's sealed passage. Only Satvan can cast the 33rd Rune; the entire preparatory sequence is for his benefit. Once Stage 2 is complete, Satvan arrives.

---

### Scene 2: Satvan at the Harbor

Satvan's ship enters the harbor approximately two hours after Stage 1 begins. He is not part of the fighting; his role is the casting, and he has left the preparation to Kaal and Arjan. But his presence in the harbor changes the character of the scene.

> *He walks through the middle of everything without appearing to notice the fighting at the harbor walls, which may be because he is not looking at it or may be because he has made peace with what the fighting means. He is calm in a way that takes effort. You can see the effort, if you are watching for it. He stops at the harbor's edge and looks down into the water for a long moment before looking up toward the harbor walls — toward you, or toward the fighting, or toward something he can only see from where he is standing.*

*The PCs can attempt to reach Satvan during Chapter 11.* If they reach him before Stage 2 is complete, he is reachable — the confrontation scene from Chapter 12 can begin early, with all its conditions. If they reach him after Stage 2 and he has begun to move toward the passage, he is harder to stop.

*What Satvan says if confronted during Chapter 11 (pre-ritual):*
- He is willing to hear the PCs. He remembers Shade. He has thought about what they said. His position has not changed — he cannot accept that everything the Path of Man has built is a corruption without evidence he can evaluate.
- If the PCs have RP 10+, they can present that evidence here. The Chapter 12 confrontation scene begins in Chapter 11 under these conditions — see Chapter 12, Scene 2.

---

### Scene 3: Kaal the Unmade's Final Form

Whether or not the PCs interrupted Stage 2, Kaal moves to clear the resistance so that Stage 3 can proceed. This is the definitive confrontation.

> *He is different from the desert encounter. Not dramatically — the same nondescript figure, the same absence of identifiable expression — but there is more of something in him now than there was then, and what that something is the word "him" no longer accurately describes. The rune on his palm is visible from across the harbor and it is not the same color as anything around it.*

*Kaal the Unmade (Final Form): CR 13, see Appendix B.* This is a serious combat encounter. He has the full range of Void Magic abilities the Shadow Lords have channeled through him, including:
- A darkness aura (20-foot radius, magical darkness that dim light cannot overcome)
- A void-touch ability that deals psychic damage on hit and reduces maximum HP until the target finishes a long rest
- The ability to negate a single spell targeting him once per round (reaction)

*The disruption option (RP 13+):* If the PCs understand the disruption mechanism, they can target the Shadow Lord bond during combat. This requires a PC to use an action to perform a Void Magic working (DC 17 Arcana, requires concentration, 3 rounds to complete) while others keep Kaal engaged. On completion, the bond fractures. Kaal takes 8d10 psychic damage and is incapacitated for 1 minute; the shadow-presence within him dissipates. What remains is the former Pillar — disoriented, amnesiac, and terrified — who collapses and needs stabilization rather than further violence.*

*If Kaal is defeated through conventional combat:* He is destroyed — the Shadow Lord's investment collapses catastrophically when the vessel is eliminated. Note: this is satisfying but closes the door on the disruption's more interesting outcome.

*If the resistance tracker is 8–10:* Several resistance members, coordinated by Calla, engage the Devoted guarding Stage 2 simultaneously with the PCs' confrontation with Kaal — creating genuine chaos across the harbor. This significantly aids the PCs tactically.

### Chapter 11 Conclusion

Kaal is defeated or disrupted. The harbor battle resolves. Award **Level 12** as the smoke clears. Satvan is in the harbor — at the passage, or waiting — and the ritual sequence is either completed, partially completed, or disrupted depending on PC actions. Chapter 12 begins.

## Chapter 12: The 33rd Rune *(Level 12)*

### Chapter Summary

The final confrontation. The 33rd Rune waits beneath the harbor. Satvan the First is at the threshold. Everything the adventure has built — the Research Points, the alliances, the understanding of what the Adi truly are — comes to bear in this chapter's central scene: the confrontation between the PCs and the man who is about to remake the world. The ending depends on what the PCs have done, what they know, and what they choose.

### Scene 1: The Ritual Site

*Whether the PCs are ahead of Satvan (preventing the descent) or behind him (descending in pursuit), the site below the harbor should be described the same way.*

> *The passage down is lit by nothing man-made — the stone itself carries a faint luminescence at this depth, a blue-gray quality that is not comfortable to look at directly. The floor of the passage is forty feet below the harbor surface; you descended through cool water that felt thicker than it should, past stone that was old when Wheel's harbor walls were new. And then the passage opens, and the site opens with it.*
>
> *It is large — larger than the harbor above would suggest possible. Circular, like the harbor, but much wider: a chamber cut or shaped by something that understood what it was building. The walls are carved with runic forms that are not the thirty-two and are not the fragments you have seen elsewhere — these are complete, intricate, and active in a low, continuous way that the Void resonance in the harbor was only a surface echo of. In the center of the chamber, a stone platform rises from the floor. The platform has a single feature: carved into its surface, in a form so precise it must have been made by the first Void Magic practitioners who understood what they were dealing with, is the 33rd Rune.*

*If Priya is present:* She stops at the chamber entrance and says nothing for a full minute. Then: "She was right about the scale. I underestimated the scale."

---

### Scene 2: Confronting Satvan

*This is the adventure's central scene. Give it space. Let it breathe. This is the culmination of twelve chapters of accumulated understanding.*

*Satvan is at the platform, or approaching it. He turns when he hears the PCs. He is not surprised.*

> *"You are here," he says. There is something in his voice that is hard to categorize — relief, possibly. Not that you will stop him; that you are here for this moment. "I want you to understand what I am about to do before I do it."*

*He explains his wish — openly, clearly, in the most cogent form of his philosophy:* The Lord and Lady's covenant constrains humanity. The Tuthá's divine patrons have been the ultimate authority over what humanity can and cannot be since the Settlement. The 33rd Rune gives him the power to end that constraint with a single act. He will wish for humanity's liberation — not supremacy, not conquest, simply the freedom to exist without divine subjugation. It is, he believes, the most just thing any human has ever attempted.

*The PCs' options in this scene:*

**Option A — Physical Confrontation:** The PCs attempt to stop Satvan by force. He has a stat block (CR 8, Appendix B) and will defend himself, but he does not want to fight. He is not a combatant; he is a philosopher who has practiced Void Magic for decades. A successful physical defeat prevents the casting. The cost: the most powerful version of the ending is foreclosed — Satvan cannot have his reckoning if he is unconscious.

**Option B — Confrontation With Evidence (RP 10+):** The PCs present the evidence that the Adi are the Shadow Lords. This is the scene the adventure has been building toward.

> *You lay it before him. Not just words — the documents, the cache contents, the physical evidence of what the channel is and what it does. He is silent for a long time. Then: "If this is true," he says carefully, "it means everything I built was built on their architecture." Another silence. "I need you to tell me why you have not simply forged this."*

*DC 16 Persuasion to make the case (DC 14 with advantage if RP 13+, DC 12 if Priya is speaking). On success: Satvan stops. He does not cast. He stands at the platform with the 33rd Rune in front of him and the evidence in his hands, and he does not move.* **Ending 1 becomes available.**

**Option C — Disruption During Casting (RP 13+):** If the PCs cannot stop the casting but understand the disruption mechanism (from Priya's notes and Sena's records), they can take specific action during the ritual. The casting of the 33rd Rune is a six-round process — the PCs can attempt the disruption on Round 4 (when the Shadow Lords' channel becomes visible as a dark column of Void resonance around Satvan). DC 18 Arcana (costs a 6th-level slot or equivalent), advantage if Sena assists. On success: **Ending 2 becomes available.** On failure: the channel holds. If there are still rounds left in the casting, the PCs may try again at higher DC.

**Option D — Failure to Intervene:** If the casting completes uninterrupted, **Ending 3 occurs.** The wish is spoken. The Adi answer.

---

### Scene 3: The Endings

#### Ending 1 — The Wish Is Never Made

> *He sets the notes down on the platform beside the carved rune and is quiet for a long time. When he speaks, his voice is steady, but only because he is making it so. "If this is true — and I cannot tell you that I am certain it is not — then I have spent forty years building exactly what they needed me to build." He looks at you. "Tell me what I should do now."*

*The casting never happens. The Void seal holds. The Shadow Lords' plan collapses as Satvan withdraws the intent that was to power it. What follows:*
- The Path of Man's divine power source is severed — over days and weeks, their divine magic begins to fail without explanation, then stops entirely.
- Satvan's authority in the Path of Man dissolves. He is no longer the founder of a movement; he is a man who stopped. What he becomes after that depends on the PCs — a prisoner, an exile, a broken man, or something stranger and more ambiguous. His arc ends not with drama but with a question: what does a man do after the defining purpose of his life is revealed to have been designed by something he would never have chosen to serve?
- The adventure ends. The world does not. The hard work of what comes after begins.

#### Ending 2 — The Wish Is Subverted

> *The casting is already in progress when the disruption lands. The dark column shudders — not a sound, exactly, but a sensation in the teeth and the chest — and then fractures sideways. Satvan speaks the wish. It goes somewhere. Not where it was supposed to go. The chamber shakes. A crack appears in the rune platform that was not there before. The resonance in the walls spikes and then diminishes, and the diminishment feels like something releasing a breath it had been holding for two centuries.*

*The Void seal holds, but it is damaged. The Shadow Lords seep through the fracture — not bodily, not yet, but their influence can now reach into Talumh directly. The world is changed.*

*Satvan, confronted with what just happened, with what he now knows about the Adi, is shattered. He does not fight. He does not flee. He sits down on the platform and looks at the cracked rune and says nothing for a long time. The PCs can speak to him. His response, whatever it is, will be the most honest thing he has said in forty years.*

*The adventure ends with a world that survived but needs to heal. That is a story for another day.*

#### Ending 3 — The Wish Is Granted

> *The chamber goes dark. Not the Void resonance dark — total dark, for one moment, in which the rune on the platform is the only light, and it is the wrong color. Then the darkness passes, and everything looks the same, and nothing is the same.*
>
> *Satvan is on his knees. His hands are shaking. "I said liberation," he says, very quietly. "I said freedom from subjugation. I said—" He stops.*
>
> *From a long way away — up through the water and the stone and the harbor walls and the Wheel harbor and the Singing Sea above — comes a sound that is not the familiar harmonic of the Void site. It is different. It is lower. It is beginning.*

*The seal is broken. The Lord and Lady retreat to Trí-na-nóg. The Tuthá courts splinter. The Shadow Lords can now act in Talumh directly. This age ends. Another begins.*

*The PCs survived. They reached the end of the trail Priya left. They understand what happened. They will carry that understanding into a world that is darker than the one they left Fountain to protect.*

*Satvan gets exactly what he wished for. He will spend the rest of his life with that knowledge.*

---

### Chapter 12 Conclusion

The adventure is complete. What the world looks like after depends on which ending the PCs reached — and what they choose to do with it. The hard work has always been what comes after.
