# IN LIMBO
## Player's Guide to the Pax

*A setting guide for players in the In Limbo campaign*

---

> **A note to players:** This guide contains everything your character would know as a native citizen of the Pax. It does not contain secrets. Some things described here are incomplete or partly wrong — that is intentional, because your character's knowledge is also incomplete and partly wrong. You will learn more as you play.

---

# WELCOME TO THE REALM

## A Thousand Years

The Pax has existed for a thousand years. This fact is the first thing children learn, the closing line of the civil pledge recited at every council meeting, and the centerpiece of the celebrations that are currently underway throughout Meridian.

In planar terms, the Pax is what scholars call a *Domain* — a region of Limbo stable enough to sustain permanent habitation, held together against the chaos by sufficient will, power, or circumstance to resist dissolution. Domains are not uncommon in Limbo; what is uncommon is what the Pax has made of one. The largest and most powerful Domains in the plane are embattled things, requiring continuous effort to maintain even basic cohesion. They shift at the edges, lose ground to the chaos, demand their inhabitants' collective attention to keep from collapsing inward. Maintaining a Domain of any meaningful size is a consuming, precarious undertaking — most such communities are perpetually one bad year from losing everything.

The Pax does not appear to struggle at all. This is, if you think about it, the most remarkable thing about it.

The plane of Limbo — the chaos — surrounds your home on all sides, above and below, always moving, always consuming. The Githzerai maintain their monasteries through individual will and ancient practice. The Xaositects ride the chaos rather than resisting it. Only the Pax — this island, this community, this particular experiment in ordered living — has persisted for a full millennium as a place where ordinary people can make ordinary lives.

Your character has grown up in this stability. You know the River Constant from childhood. You have looked up at the artificial sun and taken its steady light for granted. You have passed Foundation peacekeepers on your way to market without thinking twice. The Pax is not perfect — no place with a council is perfect — but it is *yours*.

The long success of the Pax is inextricably linked, in most citizens' understanding, to the Autarchs — the realm's mysterious founders and rulers, who have presided over a thousand years of unprecedented stability from their mountain seat. Whatever the Autarchs are doing, it appears to be working. Citizens who have spent time studying planar history tend to stop asking how the Pax holds together and start asking what holds the Autarchs together. It is a question that does not have a public answer.

It is also changing. The edges are fraying. There are earthquakes now, on the frontier. People are leaving settlements they've lived in for generations. The official explanation is "known Slaadi behavioral variance." Most citizens accept this. Some have started paying closer attention.

## Life in the Pax

The Pax is a multicultural society that has spent a thousand years accumulating people from across the planes. Tieflings, humans, Aasimar, Genasi, and dozens of other peoples live and work alongside each other without the novelty wearing off; everyone's neighbor is from somewhere, or is descended from someone who came from somewhere. This is not a cosmopolitan tolerance so much as a structural condition: the Pax was never a single people's homeland. It was always a refuge, a haven, a crossroads.

What it has become, over a thousand years, is something no other Domain in Limbo has managed: a society of nearly a quarter million people. One hundred thousand of them live in Meridian alone. The rest are distributed across the Interior farmland and the frontier settlements — a population that would be simply impossible anywhere else in the plane, sustained by infrastructure, ingenuity, and something that no planar scholar has fully accounted for.

Citizens of the Pax belong to several organizations simultaneously. The guild system encompasses not just trades but neighborhoods, philosophical circles, religious orders, hobby associations, athletic clubs, and mutual aid societies. Your character probably belongs to three or four such organizations; these memberships determine your social network, your civic representation, and much of your daily life.

**Daily life in Meridian** revolves around the river, the market, and the council's endless deliberations. The Constant is the city's artery; most commerce moves along it. The Market Quarter on the West Bank is the economic heart. The Council Hall on the East Bank is the political heart, and its proceedings are open to any citizen who wants to observe (many do; the debates are a form of public entertainment as much as civic participation). Meridian is also connected to the interplanar trade network through a number of stable portals — maintained passages to Sigil, to major trading cities in several of the Outer Planes, and to the Astral Sea. The Pax is a known destination to experienced planar traders, though it carries a reputation as something of a backwater: stable and reliable, but at the end of a long detour through Limbo. Interplanar visitors are uncommon enough to draw notice, and the city's Peacekeepers keep careful watch on them — politely, professionally, and without being asked.

**The Interior** — the region between Meridian and the frontier settlements — is its own distinct world: rolling farmland and orchards fed by the River Constant's extensive irrigation network, pastures supporting livestock drawn from a dozen planes, and the arcane machinery of the Geometer's Guild visible throughout as towers, channels, and humming conduits that help maintain the realm's cohesion against the chaos surrounding it. The animals, plants, and even the soil itself were imported from other realms when the Pax was established; the thin underlying substrate of the island would sustain nothing without intervention. That intervention has been so successful that most citizens have entirely forgotten it. The Pax's farmland is not naturally fertile — it is the result of a thousand years of continuous arcane and agricultural effort. It is a garden, built from nothing, in the middle of the chaos.

**Daily life on the frontier** is harder and more communal. Frontier settlements are smaller, more interdependent, and more self-reliant. They have had to be. The relationship between the frontier and the capital has always had friction — the settlers feel perpetually undertended, the capital perpetually underthanked — but the current crisis has sharpened that friction considerably.

# THE WORLD AROUND YOU

## Geography of the Island

The Pax occupies an island roughly 350 miles from east to west and 175 miles from north to south — large enough that most citizens will never see its full extent in their lifetimes, and large enough that "the frontier" means something different depending on which edge you're talking about. Meridian sits in the northeastern-central part of the island, near the mountain called the Sovereign. From the capital, the island extends south into its most productive lands, east into the great delta, west into open scrubland, and north into the hard country of the Highlands.

**The Highlands** — north of Meridian, and the smallest of the island's regions — are the Pax's least hospitable territory: badland flats and rough mountain country where the soil is thin and the chaos creature incursions out of Limbo are frequent enough that the Foundation maintains an unusually large Peacekeeper contingent there. The settlers who choose the Highlands tend to be the Pax's hardiest, most self-reliant inhabitants: miners, hermits, and exiles from other planes who have reasons to prefer distance from the capital's institutions. The region's chief settlement is **Ouster**, a rough and dusty township that grew up around an important mining site. It is not a comfortable place. Its residents tend to regard that as a selling point.

**The Heartlands** — south of Meridian and stretching across the island's broad middle — are the agricultural heart of the Pax. The majority of citizens who live outside Meridian live here, in farms, villages, and market towns fed by the River Constant's extensive southern tributaries. This is the land that feeds a quarter million people, and it shows: the Heartlands are the Pax at its most productive and most settled, a landscape of working farms, orchards, and livestock pastures that have been tended for generations. The region's chief settlement is **Garden** (population ~15,000), a modern market city where producers from across the Heartlands and the Eastern Delta bring their goods for transport to the capital. Garden is not ancient or architecturally interesting; it is efficient, well-organized, and bustling, the kind of city that exists because commerce required it to.

**The Eastern Delta** — the island's eastern region — is a different world: waterlogged, slow-moving, and intricate. The eastern branch of the River Constant fans out into dozens of tributaries and swampy wetlands before eventually reaching the island's southeastern edge, where thousands of waterfalls drop the region's rivers off the rim of the island and into the chaos below. The delta is home to a multitude of small settlements wherever dry land can be found between the rivers and the swamps; travel here is mostly by boat, and the communities of the delta have developed their own distinct culture over a thousand years of relative isolation. The region's largest city is **Diaspola** (population ~5,000) — nearly as old as Meridian, its buildings dressed in moss and ivy, home to some of the Pax's oldest temples and most respected universities. Diaspola is a city of scholars and priests, quieter than the capital and considerably more interested in the long view.

**The Selkawood** — a small, heavily forested region tucked between the Eastern Delta to the south and the Northern Highlands to the north and west — is sparsely populated frontier country: logging villages, small farms carved out of the treeline, and communities accustomed to managing their own affairs without much capital involvement. The largest settlement is **Tanis**, a logging town whose economy drives much of the Selkawood's modest timber trade.

**The Western Scrublands** — the island's western region, and at roughly a third of its total area its largest — are the driest and least settled lands in the Pax. The western branch of the River Constant does not travel far into this region before dropping off the island's southern edge, leaving the interior without reliable irrigation and supporting only scattered frontier communities. Most of the people who live here make their livings through livestock, mining, and subsistence farming, and the dozens of frontier settlements scattered across the Scrublands are as independent-minded as frontier communities tend to be, but more so. The region's largest settlement is **Window**, which serves as the primary administrative center for the western frontier — the place where tax records are kept, Foundation patrols are coordinated, and the Geometer's Guild maintains the infrastructure that keeps the western Scrublands from dissolving faster than they already are.

## Meridian — The Capital

Meridian is built against the north face of the Sovereign, the tall spindly mountain that serves as headquarters for the Autarchs and the Foundation. The River Constant cuts through its center, splitting the city into the East Bank and the West Bank. The East Bank is older and somewhat more established; the West Bank is more commercial, more mixed, and louder.

The city's architecture is eclectic to the point of apparent incoherence: Githzerai-influenced geometric structures stand next to buildings in styles from a dozen other planes, next to experimental local forms that don't reference anything prior. Neighborhoods grew where they grew, not where anyone planned them. The city that resulted from this process is impossible to describe systematically and easy to love.

**Key landmarks your character knows:**
- **The Sovereign Approach** — the broad, monument-lined avenue from the city's north edge to the mountain base. The site of all major public ceremonies.
- **The Constant Waterfront** — the commercial heart, both banks, dense with docks, markets, and river traffic.
- **The Foundation Monastery** — at the Sovereign's base; public precincts are open, inner sanctums are not. Citizens leave offerings at the public shrine.
- **The Temple District** — the informal West Bank quarter of Devoti shrines, small temples, and meeting houses.
- **The Council Hall** — large, architecturally chaotic, always busy.
- **The Market Quarter** — everything is available here, eventually.

## The Sovereign and the Autarchs

The Sovereign is the mountain. The Autarchs are in the mountain. Neither is accessible to ordinary citizens, except through the formal mechanisms described below.

Most citizens have seen the Autarchs at a public ceremony — the annual Foundation initiations, Founding Day, High Harvest Week. They are tall, robed, masked, and silent. Their masks show you your own face. Their constructs speak for them. Most citizens find the Autarchs' public appearances simultaneously impressive and deeply strange, and have long since processed this as "the way things are."

The **Right of Petition** exists in law: any citizen may petition the Autarchs directly. The Autarchs strongly discourage this, and their discouragements (immediate fines, imprisonment with no appeal) have made the right largely theoretical. The stories about it are cautionary more than inspiring.

## The Frontier and the Edge

**The frontier** is the outer belt of settlements nearest the island's edges. Your character may have family there, or have traveled there, or may have grown up there. Frontier life is harder; frontier people are often more independent.

**The Edge** is where the island ends — or rather, where it is currently ending. At the Edge, you can watch rock and earth dissolve slowly into the chaos of Limbo below. This has been happening faster in recent years. The official position is that it is manageable.

**What you know about the Fraying:** There are earthquakes now, at the frontier. There are storms. There is an effect your character may have heard described by travelers from the frontier — objects changing slightly, things vanishing from storage rooms when no one was watching — that is hard to explain and easy to dismiss if you don't live there. Scholars in Diaspola have given this a name — the *Unobserved Effect* — though naming it has not brought anyone closer to explaining it.

## Limbo — Beyond the Border

Limbo is the chaos. It surrounds the Pax on all sides. From the island's edge on a clear day, it is visible below: a gray-white, slow-moving expanse that is both featureless and somehow active, as if it is always in the process of becoming something it hasn't decided yet.

**Common knowledge about Limbo:**
- Matter exists in Limbo but is unstable; it shifts, dissolves, and reforms without obvious cause.
- Travel in Limbo is possible but requires will and skill to navigate. Unprepared travelers get lost.
- The Githzerai live in Limbo and maintain stable monasteries through their own willpower.
- The Slaadi — chaotic frog-like creatures of varying sizes and danger — are native to Limbo. They have always raided the Pax's borders. Their raids have recently intensified.
- Other beings live in or near Limbo: Xaositects (Planescape chaosophiles), Doomguard (who believe dissolution is natural and right), and scattered others.

**What your character does not know:** Most of what Limbo is like from the inside. What actually travels through it. What is organizing the Slaadi. What has been watching the Pax from deep within the chaos for a very long time.

# SOCIETY AND CULTURE

## The Foundation

The Foundation is the Pax's monastic order — its peacekeepers, scholars, and (most significantly) its Anarchs, the chaos-shapers who maintain the realm's stability within Limbo.

The Foundation is trusted and beloved in a way the Autarchs are not. Its peacekeepers are calm and fair-minded; its scholars maintain the realm's knowledge. The order's public face is warm, helpful, and reliable. Citizens with children who show inborn Anarch ability donate them to the order by age ten; this is considered a high honor and an act of genuine virtue.

Members of the Foundation who formally join the order take new names and sever contact with their families. Once initiated, they do not leave their monasteries except on official duties. The public explanation for this is the demands of ascetic practice; the actual structure of the order is not a matter of public knowledge.

The Foundation's **Shapers sub-order** — the Anarchs who actually maintain the Pax's stability — live in complete seclusion. They are never seen in public. Citizens are told this is necessary for the concentration required to sustain the realm. Most citizens have never questioned this.

## The Devoti

The Pax's primary philosophical and religious tradition. The majority of citizens belong to the Devoti in some sense; the organization is decentralized enough that membership can mean anything from daily temple attendance to a vague personal identification with the tradition.

The Devoti encompasses the worship of deities from across the planes — neutral, good, and chaotic alignments. There is no required pantheon and no hierarchy beyond individual temples. The primary focus is the sovereignty of all thinking beings and the value of personal understanding over imposed doctrine.

**Holidays:** *All Gods Day* — a parade of deity effigies through the streets. *Day of Contemplation* — a day of private fasting and reflection, ending with a communal feast.

## The Path

A small, public, largely tolerated sect of what most citizens call "well-meaning cranks." Followers are called Adherents. Their leader is **Brother Candor** — easily spotted by his plain black robe (inverting the Foundation's white), his spectacles, and his sonorous, unhurried voice haranguing the passing foot traffic from his corner of Weaver Street.

The Path preaches that the Autarchs are usurpers who divert the people's spiritual power, with the Foundation as an unwitting instrument. Most citizens dismiss this as philosophical overreach from people who do too much fasting. The Path's community good works — feeding the poor, tending the sick — are universally appreciated, which is why they are tolerated.

Brother Candor is a nuisance, but a hard one to dismiss entirely. He is clearly intelligent. He is clearly sincere. And lately, his preaching has started sounding a little less like nonsense to people who've been paying attention to the frontier situation.

## The Freelancer's Guild

A pragmatic organization of agents available for hire: investigators, couriers, arbitrators, negotiators, and various other skilled professionals. Run by **Caske Amanthri**, a female elf — rare in Meridian — who came up from the streets and built the Guild through competence and a reputation for keeping her word. No philosophical agenda; purely practical.

If you need something done and official channels are too slow, too visible, or too likely to inform the wrong people, the Freelancer's Guild is a reasonable first call.

## The Created

The Pax's population includes a small but notable number of constructed beings — made things that have achieved genuine selfhood through craft, circumstance, or something that has never been adequately explained. Citizens typically call them **the Created**, a category broad enough to cover beings of very different origins who share the quality of having been made rather than born.

The Created in the Pax are citizens in full standing: they own property, hold guild memberships, stand before the Council, and pursue whatever purposes they've arrived at through whatever history brought them here. Most were made here in the Pax — their creation a practice of the Geometer's Guild, whose knowledge does not travel beyond the realm's borders. A small number have arrived through the interplanar portals, but the Guild remains the source of nearly all Created living in the realm.

They are distinct in the public mind from the **administrative constructs** that handle significant portions of the Pax's institutional work: the Tender constructs who maintain the realm's agricultural and infrastructural systems, the Ledger constructs who manage the Foundation's records, and the Charter Minder who maintains the Council Hall's legal archive. These constructs serve, and their service is not generally understood to be voluntary. Most citizens do not find this troubling enough to discuss.

# FACTIONS OF THE PLANES

The following Planescape factions have a notable presence in the Pax. Your character is likely familiar with all of them; formal membership in one is possible as a background choice.

## Xaositects (Chaosmen)
The Xaositects embrace chaos as the fundamental truth of existence. Many of them live just outside the Pax's borders rather than within it — the realm's ordered nature is not entirely to their taste — but they have a significant presence in the Market Quarter and the Temple District. They are friendly, unpredictable, and occasionally useful. Their philosophy holds that the Pax's resistance to Limbo is an interesting experiment rather than a moral position; they bear the realm no ill will, but they do not believe in it the way its citizens do.

**Membership:** A Xaositect character embraces disorder as a guiding principle. This doesn't mean chaotic evil — it means a genuine philosophical commitment to the value of unpredictability, emergence, and the liberation of the possible.

## Free League (Indeps)
Merchants, independents, and people who refuse faction authority. A significant presence in the merchant quarter. The Free League values freedom and operates on individual principle; there is no central creed beyond the refusal to be told what to believe. Many of the Pax's most successful traders are Free League members.

**Membership:** A Free League character prizes independence and self-determination. They work with others readily enough but answer to no authority they haven't chosen.

## Doomguard (Sinkers)
A small but vocal presence. The Doomguard believe that dissolution is the natural fate of all things — that entropy is not the enemy but the destination, and that fighting it is both futile and philosophically misguided. Their presence in the Pax is paradoxical on its face; they are here because watching something dissolve slowly is more interesting than watching nothing.

**Membership:** A Doomguard character is not nihilistic in a self-destructive way — they value things, including people, exactly because nothing lasts. The Fraying is, from their perspective, the universe being honest for once.

## Transcendent Order (Ciphers)
A small monastery in Meridian, separate from and unaffiliated with the Foundation. The Ciphers believe in acting without thought — that the gap between impulse and action is where error enters, and that a perfected being acts in pure accord with the universe's movement. Their philosophy has considerable overlap with Githzerai thought, which makes them unusual bridge-builders.

**Membership:** A Cipher character acts on instinct, training, and genuine engagement with the moment. They are not impulsive — they are refined, in a way that looks like impulsiveness from outside.

## Revolutionary League (Anarchists)
Some League members are embedded in the more radical circles around the Path. Their goal is the dismantling of unjust power structures; their methods are considerably more direct than the Path's preaching. They are not officially present in the Pax; officially, they don't exist here. Unofficially, certain members of certain philosophical circles know how to reach them.

**Membership:** A Revolutionary League character is committed to structural change through direct action. They are not terrorists — they target systems, not people — but they are willing to do things that make others uncomfortable.

# CHARACTER CREATION

## Races

All officially published 5e races are available to characters native to the Pax. The Pax has been drawing people from across the planes for a thousand years; there is no "unusual" race here, only more and less common ones.

**Very common:** Human, Tiefling, Genasi (all types), Aasimar, Half-Elf, Half-Orc

**Common:** Githzerai (immigrants from outside or descendants of immigrants; their presence is notable but not rare), Dwarf, Gnome, Halfling

**Uncommon:** Elf (rare enough that a full-elf character draws occasional surprised notice, as Caske Amanthri demonstrates), Dragonborn, Tabaxi

**Rare but present:** The Created (see below for full racial traits), Kenku, Tortle, and other exotic races (work with your DM on flavor)

**Githzerai characters specifically:** You are a citizen of the Pax — descended from Githzerai who settled here generations ago, or recently emigrated. You probably have some contact with the nearby monastery, even if you chose not to join it. Zerith knows of your family; you know of her reputation.

## The Created

The Geometer's Guild has held, under the Autarchs' authority, the knowledge required to make the realm's administrative constructs since the early centuries of the Pax. It was not always content to make servants. Centuries ago, Guild artisans working at the edge of their mandate began experimenting with something different: beings built for purpose, but given genuine selfhood alongside it. The Autarchs were informed. The Guild was permitted to continue. The first Created had already been made.

Each Created is visually unique — there is no shared form. Some are built in geometric precision: angular planes, articulated joints, strict proportion. Others curve and flow, their construction suggesting organic life without quite replicating it. Some are stark and functional, their design communicating nothing beyond what they were made to do; others are painted, inlaid, or adorned with structural flourishes, whether by their makers or by themselves. They are made here, in the Pax — the knowledge of their construction belongs to the Guild and does not travel.

Every Created is built for a purpose. Many honor that purpose throughout their lives. Many others drift from it as they encounter more of the world than their construction anticipated, and the Guild has long supported this independence — a being with genuine selfhood that chooses its original purpose freely is more valuable, in the Guild's understanding, than one that simply enacts it. Each new Created is given a name at the time of their making. Some keep it. Others, in time, choose their own.

The personality of a Created is genuinely their own, though it develops differently than it does for biological beings. Young Created tend to imprint strongly on those they spend significant time with, absorbing traits and approaches they find useful or worthy of emulation; over time, this imprinting consolidates into something distinctively individual. Created also lack the gender identity most humanoids experience as a given. Some adopt a gender over time, shaping their appearance in line with that choice; others find the category simply inapplicable. Both are ordinary.

**Created Racial Traits**

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Constitution score increases by 2, and one other ability score of your choice increases by 1, reflecting the purpose for which you were constructed.

**Age.** Created do not age. They are functional adults from the moment of construction and, barring damage, can persist indefinitely.

**Alignment.** Created have no innate alignment tendency. They often default to neutrality in early development but can hold any alignment, shaped by experience.

**Size.** Created come in varied sizes reflecting their construction. You are Medium or Small (your choice).

**Speed.** Your base walking speed is 30 feet.

**Constructed Nature.** You don't need to eat, drink, or breathe. You are immune to disease, have advantage on saving throws against being poisoned, and have resistance to poison damage. You don't sleep. Instead, you spend 6 hours each day in an inactive resting state. During this state you are aware of your surroundings but cannot take actions or move.

**Living Construct.** Despite your constructed nature, you count as a humanoid for the purposes of spells, features, and effects. Healing magic affects you normally.

**Natural Armor.** Your constructed body provides inherent protection. When you are not wearing armor, your AC equals 13 + your Dexterity modifier. You can use a shield and still gain this benefit.

**Light Fortification.** When you are hit by a critical hit, you can use your reaction to treat it as a normal hit instead. Once you use this feature, you can't use it again until you finish a long rest.

**Built for Purpose.** During character creation, choose one of the following to reflect the design intent of your construction:
- *Combat Build:* You gain proficiency with one martial weapon of your choice and one set of armor (light or medium).
- *Scholar Build:* You gain proficiency in two skills of your choice from: Arcana, History, Insight, Investigation, Medicine, Nature, Religion.
- *Artisan Build:* You gain proficiency in two artisan's tool kits of your choice, and double your proficiency bonus on checks with one of them.
- *Courier Build:* Your base walking speed increases to 35 feet, and your long jump and high jump distances are doubled.

**Languages.** You can speak, read, and write Common and two additional languages of your choice.

## Barbarian

Barbarians in the Pax are almost exclusively creatures of the edges — settlers, wardens, and wanderers who have spent enough time where the Pax's ordered land meets Limbo's chaos that something in them has changed. They don't talk about this change much. It's visible enough in what they do under pressure.

In settled areas, barbarians are unusual but not unwelcome. Their skills — endurance, situational awareness, comfort with violence, comfort with uncertainty — are valued by frontier communities, mercenary outfits, and anyone who needs someone capable of operating where training breaks down and adaptability is the only relevant quality. The Foundation has no special relationship with barbarians; the Freelancer's Guild has many on its rolls.

The Fraying affects people who spend time near it in ways that aren't fully understood. Most frontier inhabitants develop a watchfulness, a heightened attention to environmental instability. Some develop something more: a resonance with the chaos that makes them more effective at channeling it than resisting it. These individuals make capable, unpredictable allies and deeply concerning enemies.

### Path of the Fraying
*For those who have looked into the chaos and let it look back*

Barbarians of the Path of the Fraying have been overexposed to Limbo's encroaching chaos and emerged changed rather than destroyed. Their rage doesn't channel primal fury or ancestral power — it channels the raw instability of the plane outside, the energy that makes matter temporary and physics negotiable. They have stopped fighting the Fraying and started using it.

**Chaos Channeling (Level 3)-** Once per turn while raging, when you hit a creature with an attack, you can cause it to deal additional damage of a random type. Roll a d10: 1—Acid, 2—Cold, 3—Fire, 4—Force, 5—Lightning, 6—Necrotic, 7—Poison, 8—Radiant, 9—Psychic, 10—Thunder. The attack deals an additional 1d6 damage of the rolled type.

**Mutable Form (Level 6) -** At the start of each rage, choose one damage type. You gain resistance to that damage type for the duration of the rage.

**Etheric Dodge (Level 10) -** While raging, you can use your reaction when hit by an attack to briefly phase into the chaos. Make a Constitution saving throw (DC 10 + the triggering attack roll's damage dice total, before modifiers). On a success, you take no damage from the attack. You can use this feature once per rage.

**Discorporation (Level 14) -** While raging, you can use a bonus action to become incorporeal until the start of your next turn. While incorporeal, you can move through solid objects and creatures (ending your movement outside them), you are immune to nonmagical physical damage, and you cannot make physical attacks. You can use this feature once per rage.

## Bard

Bards in the Pax fill a cultural role that goes beyond entertainment: they are the realm's institutional memory. In a domain where things at the frontier change when no one is watching — where roads that were there yesterday aren't today, where settlements at the edge report records and objects that seem subtly different from what anyone remembers — a bard's commitment to preserving accurate, witnessed accounts is not merely artistic. It is a civic service.

The Pax has a strong tradition of oral history, musical chronicle, and ceremonial remembrance. The Millennium itself — the thousand-year anniversary of the realm's founding — is sustained largely by bardic tradition, by songs and stories that carry information the written record doesn't always preserve. Bards are welcome in every stratum of Pax society, from the merchant quarter to the frontier settlements, and they are trusted in a way that scholars are not, because they speak to people rather than at them.

Most bards in the Pax are simply good at what they do: performers, historians, social lubricants who make every gathering better. Their relationship to the Autarchs is the same live-and-let-live arrangement most citizens maintain. The fact that a bard's memory of events sometimes diverges from official accounts is, in most cases, a conversation that never quite happens.

### College of a Thousand Years
*The Pax's living memory — oral historians who remember what time erases*

Bards of the College of a Thousand Years have committed to something more than performance: they are the Pax's deliberate archive, trained to retain and transmit accurate accounts of what they personally witness. In a realm where the Unobserved Effect means that unattended things change, the College's members represent a counterforce — not through magic, but through the disciplined habit of attention and recall that their training instills.

**Memory Palace (Level 3) -** Your training gives you exceptional recall of events you have personally witnessed. You can accurately reproduce any conversation, scene, or document you have directly observed. Once per short rest, you can spend 1 minute in contact with an object, location, or person to receive a 1-minute sensory impression of the most significant event involving that subject within the past year (DM determines what qualifies as "significant"). You gain proficiency in History and Religion. The DM may require an Intelligence (History) check for precise details of events you witnessed more than one year ago.

**Psychic Archive (Level 6) -** Once per long rest, you can cast *legend lore* without expending a spell slot, drawing on the unconscious collective memory of the Pax's inhabitants — a thousand years of people who lived here and remember it — rather than any magical or divine source. When cast this way, the information arrives as fragmented impressions from many witnesses. Additionally, when a creature uses one of your Bardic Inspiration dice, it gains advantage on the first Intelligence-based ability check it makes before the die is used.

**Thousand Voices (Level 14) -** Once per long rest, you can ask one question about any person, place, or event within the Pax's history. The DM provides a true answer drawn from the collective memory of the realm's inhabitants — even information that has been officially suppressed or forgotten. The answer arrives as fragmented impressions from multiple perspectives, not as a clean statement, but it is accurate.

## Cleric

The Devoti are the Pax's primary religious and philosophical tradition, and clerics are their formal clergy — practitioners who have moved beyond philosophical commitment into the active channeling of divine principle. In the Pax's understanding, the divine is not housed in a single deity but in the underlying sanctity of sovereign will: the belief that every thinking being possesses an inviolable self that cannot rightfully be surrendered or taken. Devoti clerics draw power from this principle directly.

Clerics serve in temple communities throughout the realm, providing spiritual guidance, officiating ceremonies, and performing the practical healing and social work that religious communities sustain. They are embedded in the Pax's civic life in a way that most other spellcasters are not — connected to neighborhood networks, trusted by people who would be suspicious of a wizard or a monk, and called upon in circumstances that require both power and discretion. Their relationship with the Autarchs is respectful and distant; the Autarchs fund Devoti temples as part of their general support for civic institutions.

The Domain of Sovereignty represents a particular strain of Devoti theology: those who take the principle of inviolable will not merely as a personal belief but as a channeled divine mandate. Their magic centers specifically on protecting the self against compulsion and freeing those whose sovereignty has been violated. These clerics are not fringe figures — sovereignty is central Devoti doctrine — but their powers make them unusually aware of when something has gone wrong with another being's will.

### Domain of Sovereignty
*Devoti clerics channel the divine principle that every will is sacred*

Sovereignty clerics have moved past philosophical commitment into active defense of personal will. Their magic is oriented around a single question: is this being acting freely? If not, what can be done about it? In the Pax's generally ordered society, this vocation most often manifests as healing, counseling, and the removal of mundane enchantments that afflict people in any realm. It becomes something more pointed in circumstances where the answer to that first question is more complicated.

## Druid

The Pax's ecosystem is entirely artificial. Its soil was built, its river was channeled, its farms were established by generations of settlers who coaxed growth from conditions that had no business sustaining it. Druids are the descendants of that effort — practitioners whose connection to the natural world is not about wilderness untouched by civilization, but about life stubbornly persisting in conditions it was never designed for. In the Pax, that is the only kind of nature there is.

In the capital and the farmland that surrounds it, druids tend gardens, manage the Constant's riverside ecology, consult on crop yields, and maintain the balance of an agricultural system with no margin for error. There is no wild here; every living thing that grows in the Pax does so because someone is paying attention to it. Druids are that attention given professional form, and they are valued accordingly — by the Merchant Guild, by farming communities, and by anyone who understands that the Pax feeds itself only through continuous effort.

In the frontier, the role shifts. Here, the Fraying is not a philosophical concern but an operational one — chaos is consuming the farmland, and the communities that depend on it are struggling. Frontier druids are emergency responders as much as cultivators, holding productive land together as the edges dissolve. Some travel between the capital and the frontier, carrying techniques developed in one environment to address crises in the other.

### Circle of the Constant
*Named for the river — cultivators of fragile life in impossible conditions*

Druids of the Circle of the Constant take their name from the Pax's great river and its central truth: in a plane of chaos, the only permanence is what you tend. Their magic is oriented toward growth in impossible conditions, toward the sustaining of life that has no business surviving where it finds itself. They are the Pax's agricultural backbone, its frontier lifeline, and the practitioners most likely to understand — in concrete, practical terms — what happens when the chaos gets closer.

**Tended Growth (Level 2) -** You can cause plants to take root and grow in any soil, including chaos-touched ground where normal growth is impossible. Once per long rest, you can cast *plant growth* (the 8-hour enriching version) without expending a spell slot.

**Constant's Blessing (Level 2) -** While within 30 feet of flowing water, you have advantage on Constitution saving throws. When you take a short rest within 30 feet of flowing water, you recover one expended spell slot of the lowest level you have available.

**Impossible Garden (Level 6) -** You can cast *create food and water* at will without expending a spell slot. Plants you tend require only half the normal soil, water, and light to grow and can establish themselves in rock, sand, or magically barren ground.

**Unbroken Current (Level 10) -** Once per long rest, you can sustain one concentration spell for up to 1 hour without making concentration checks. Taking damage does not break your concentration on this spell during that time.

**Ancient Roots (Level 14) -** Once per long rest, you can cast *commune with nature* without expending a spell slot. When cast this way, instead of the spell's normal questions, you may ask up to five yes/no questions about the history of your current location — what grew here, who lived here, what changed, what was taken, what is hidden. The answers span the full history of the Pax.

## Fighter

Fighters in the Pax come from everywhere and train in everything. The frontier militia, the merchant caravan guard, the veteran of some forgotten conflict at the realm's edge — all find a natural home in the fighter's toolkit of adaptable combat skill. The Pax is not a militarized society; it has no standing army and no organized martial tradition beyond the Foundation's peacekeeping corps. Fighters are, accordingly, people who came to their skill through varied and personal paths.

The Foundation trains a cadre of fighters in its Peacekeeper tradition — practitioners of calibrated intervention, de-escalation, and proportional force who serve as the realm's peacekeeping presence in the capital's streets. These are not soldiers; they are trained in soft power first and hard power second, in the philosophy that the goal of intervention is the minimum disruption necessary to restore order. It's a demanding discipline, and not everyone who trains with the Foundation stays. Some complete their training and take independent work. Some find the Foundation's approach too constrained and apply its techniques in less institutional contexts.

Outside the Peacekeeper tradition, fighters in the Pax pursue their craft as circumstances direct: guild enforcers, frontier wardens, private escorts, independent operators who don't fit neatly into any category. In a realm where the interior's stability is maintained by institutions rather than force, a capable fighter occupies a particular niche — the person you call when something has gone wrong that institutions weren't designed to handle.

### Peacekeeper
*Foundation-trained; soft power before hard, but capable of both*

Peacekeepers are trained by the Foundation in the tradition of calibrated intervention: the art of resolving conflict with the minimum necessary force, applied with precision. They have studied the philosophy of proportional response, trained in the techniques of restraint and de-escalation, and developed capabilities oriented around controlling situations rather than winning them. The Peacekeeper who stands before you may or may not currently hold a Foundation commission. Either way, they were trained by people who take this work seriously, and it shows.

**Measured Response (Level 3) -** When you hit a creature with an attack, you can choose to deal no damage and instead impose one of the following conditions until the start of your next turn: grappled, restrained, or frightened. The creature makes a Strength or Dexterity saving throw (your choice, DC 8 + your proficiency bonus + your Strength modifier) to resist the condition.

**Foundation Training (Level 3) -** You gain proficiency in the Insight and Persuasion skills. Once per short rest, you can cast *calm emotions* without expending a spell slot.

**Situational Awareness (Level 7) -** When you have been able to observe an area for at least one full round before initiative is rolled, you have advantage on Initiative rolls. You cannot be surprised in urban environments.

**Precision Intervention (Level 10) -** When you use Action Surge, you can choose to make the additional attacks non-lethal. All damage dealt by these attacks becomes 0, but each target hit takes the Restrained condition until the end of your next turn.

**Guardian's Resolve (Level 15) -** When an ally within 5 feet of you takes damage, you can use your reaction to take half of that damage yourself (rounded down). The ally takes the other half.

**Pacific Aura (Level 18) -** Once per long rest, you can declare a 30-foot radius zone of enforced calm centered on yourself. All creatures of your choice in the zone must succeed on a DC 17 Wisdom saving throw or be unable to make attacks or cast spells that deal damage for 1 minute (this requires your concentration). The effect ends immediately for a particular creature if you or any of your allies deal damage to it.

## Monk

The Foundation is the Pax's most respected institution, and its monks are its most visible representatives — practitioners of a discipline that combines martial art, will-based reality shaping, and the philosophical tradition of the Devoti into a single integrated practice. Foundation monks serve as peacekeepers, mediators, scholars, and — in the frontier — the closest thing the realm has to a rapid-response stabilization force. They are, by reputation and by genuine practice, the people who keep things working.

Most monk characters in the Pax have some relationship to the Foundation, even if they aren't formally affiliated. The Foundation runs training programs open to the public; it accepts dedicated students from promising practitioners; it places junior monks in roles throughout the capital. The Order Acolyte background specifically represents a character who trained with the Foundation and chose not to join — a decision that is respected, if sometimes questioned. Monks who developed their abilities independently are rarer and tend to draw more curiosity.

The Anarch ability — the innate capacity to impose will directly on Limbo's matter — is what the Foundation most specifically cultivates. Characters who have this ability and are not Foundation-affiliated occupy an interesting position: they possess something the Foundation values highly, and the Foundation is very aware of them. Monks of the Way of the Unformed Hand have taken the Anarch ability in a direction the Foundation doesn't formally teach, developing it as an independent practice rather than a disciplined institutional one.

### Way of the Unformed Hand
*For those who impose their will without asking the world's permission*

Monks of the Way of the Unformed Hand have taken the Anarch ability that the Foundation trains so carefully and let it develop without institutional constraints. The result is less precise than Foundation work, less controlled — and in certain key respects, more powerful. These monks don't shape reality within approved parameters. They shape it the way Limbo itself does: completely, immediately, and with no particular regard for what was there before.

**Touch of Chaos (Level 3) -** You can spend 1 ki point when you hit with an unarmed strike to channel chaos energy through the blow. The strike deals an additional 1d8 damage of a random type. Roll a d10: 1—Acid, 2—Cold, 3—Fire, 4—Force, 5—Lightning, 6—Necrotic, 7—Poison, 8—Radiant, 9—Psychic, 10—Thunder.

**Will Shaping (Level 3) -** You can spend 2 ki points as an action to reshape nonmagical, non-living matter within 30 feet of you. The affected area is one 5-foot cube, plus one additional 5-foot cube for every 4 monk levels you have. You cannot create structural changes that would cause a collapse.

**Translocation (Level 6) -** You can spend 2 ki points to cast *misty step*. Wisdom is your spellcasting ability for this spell.

**Unraveling Strike (Level 11) -** You can spend 3 ki points when you hit a creature with an unarmed strike. The creature must succeed on a Constitution saving throw (DC 8 + your proficiency bonus + your Wisdom modifier) or gain vulnerability to the next damage type it takes before the end of your next turn. On a failed save, the creature also loses its reaction until the end of its next turn.

**The Shape of Things (Level 17) -** Once per long rest, you can spend 4 ki points as an action to enter a heightened state of will imposition that lasts for 1 minute (requires concentration). While active: Will Shaping costs a bonus action and its area increases by 2 additional cubes; your unarmed strikes deal an additional 1d10 force damage; creatures that start their turn within 60 feet of you must succeed on a Strength saving throw (DC 8 + your proficiency bonus + your Wisdom modifier) or have their speed halved until the start of their next turn.

## Paladin

Paladins in the Pax draw their power from the same source as the Devoti's clerics: the divine principle of sovereign will, the inviolable selfhood of every thinking being. What distinguishes paladins is the form that commitment takes — not mediation and counsel, but oath. A paladin has not merely adopted the Devoti's philosophical framework; they have staked something on it, made a formal vow before whatever principle they hold sacred, and accepted the transformation that follows.

In the Pax, this vow most commonly centers on personal sovereignty — the belief that no thinking being may rightfully be coerced, enslaved, or stripped of their capacity for self-determination. It's a well-established Devoti position, and paladins who hold it are respected within the tradition. They work alongside clerics in temple communities, serve as advocates for citizens caught in legal disputes, and occasionally appear in frontier communities as traveling protectors whose presence is welcome regardless of formal affiliation.

What separates paladins from other Devoti practitioners is not the content of their belief but its intensity. Most Devoti follow their philosophy as a framework for living; paladins have internalized it as an operational mandate. The Pax's institutions have limited formal space for this kind of conviction — the Autarchs prefer civic order to moral absolutism, and the Foundation's philosophy is measured where a paladin's tends toward the categorical. This creates a certain friction that most paladins navigate without much difficulty, until the day something specific makes it difficult.

### Oath of Deliverance
*Sworn to the principle that no will may be enslaved*

Paladins of the Oath of Deliverance have made a specific commitment: not merely to believe in personal sovereignty, but to actively defend it and, where it has been violated, to restore it. Their powers center on detecting and ending compulsion, protecting the will of those around them, and — when necessary — confronting those who use coercion as a tool. In a realm as generally ordered as the Pax, most of their work is quiet. They wait, they watch, and they help where they can. The oath does not wait with them.

## Ranger

Rangers are the Pax's eyes at the edge of the world. While the Foundation holds the center and peacekeepers keep the streets, rangers watch the boundary where the realm's ordered land gives way to Limbo's raw chaos — and they report back what they find. In the frontier settlements and border hamlets, they serve as wardens, scouts, and guides: people whose knowledge of the terrain between civilization and dissolution is the difference between a community that survives and one that doesn't.

Their skills are intensely practical. A ranger knows which routes through the frontier are stable and which shifted overnight. They know how to read the subtle signs that the Fraying is advancing — a tree that wasn't there yesterday, a road that ends in open air, a farmstead where everything is inexplicably fine, which is somehow more alarming than the alternative. They maintain the warning networks that let outlying communities evacuate in time, and they carry urgent dispatches from the outer settlements inward when conditions deteriorate faster than anyone expected.

In the Pax's interior, rangers find other roles: game wardens along the Constant's riverbanks, escorts for merchant caravans moving between the capital and the outer settlements, independent guides for anyone willing to pay for expertise in territory that doesn't appear on standard maps. They tend to be pragmatic, self-sufficient, and quietly distrustful of anyone who hasn't personally stood at the edge and looked out. That's not arrogance — it's a particular kind of knowledge that doesn't transfer secondhand.

### Conclave of the Vanishing Shore
*The frontier is their home — even as it ceases to exist*

Rangers of the Vanishing Shore have stopped treating the Fraying as a hazard to navigate around and started treating it as terrain to master. They know the behavior of chaos matter — how it shifts, what it responds to, which manifestations are dangerous and which are merely strange. Some came to this knowledge through years of frontier work; others have a temperamental affinity for instability that made them naturals from the start. Either way, they move through dissolving terrain, use chaos itself as cover, and fire through the spaces that ordinary physics pretends don't exist.

**Frontier Expertise (Level 3).** You can always choose Fraying frontier and Limbo environment as favored terrain options, regardless of which terrain types your ranger archetype normally offers. You gain advantage on Wisdom (Survival) checks to navigate, track, or predict conditions in these environments.

**Entropic Camouflage (Level 3).** While in Fraying frontier or Limbo environments, you can use a bonus action to briefly dissolve your outline into the surrounding chaos. You become invisible until the start of your next turn or until you make an attack roll, whichever comes first. Once you use this feature, you can't use it again until you finish a short or long rest.

**Unsteady Ground (Level 7).** You've learned to use the terrain as a weapon. When a creature within 30 feet of you that you can see moves while in Fraying frontier or Limbo terrain, you can use your reaction to destabilize the ground beneath it: the creature must succeed on a Strength saving throw (DC 8 + your proficiency bonus + your Wisdom modifier) or fall prone. You can use this feature a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus, and you regain all uses when you finish a long rest.

**Chaos Arrow (Level 11).** When you make a ranged weapon attack, you can choose to route the projectile through a brief fold in chaos. The attack ignores all cover and can target invisible creatures without disadvantage. On a hit, roll a d10 to determine the type of additional damage dealt (1—Acid, 2—Cold, 3—Fire, 4—Force, 5—Lightning, 6—Necrotic, 7—Poison, 8—Radiant, 9—Psychic, 10—Thunder); the attack deals an additional 1d10 damage of that type. You can use this feature twice, and you regain all uses when you finish a short or long rest.

**Eyes of the Unraveling (Level 15).** Your time at the edge of existence has sharpened your perception beyond what normal sight allows. You can see normally in magical darkness and through illusions as if they were not present (you do not need to interact with them to notice them). Once per long rest, you can cast *true seeing* without expending a spell slot.

## Rogue

The Pax's economy is too complex to run entirely on visible transactions. Rogues are the people who understand this — operators who work in the spaces between institutions, handling business that formal channels can't or won't touch. In the merchant quarter, they're fixers and freelancers. In the frontier, they're procurement specialists and information brokers. In every corner of the realm, they're the people you find when you need something done and don't want to explain what or why.

The Freelancer's Guild gives this profession its closest thing to legitimacy: a loose association of independent contractors who share resources, safe houses, and referrals, but answer to no one's agenda but their own. Membership is informal and largely self-declared, but the name carries weight in the right circles. It signals a particular kind of professional — one who completes the job, keeps their mouth shut, and doesn't ask questions they're not being paid to answer. Not everyone who operates in this space claims the Guild, and not everyone who claims it operates cleanly. The term covers enough ground that it stops meaning much beyond *resourceful and available.*

Rogues in the Pax are defined by adaptability more than any particular skillset. Some are consummate social operators who haven't held a weapon since training. Others are precision fighters who happen to know which pocket to pick and which door to leave open. What they share is a preference for working at the margins: of society, of institutions, of the law as written versus the law as enforced. In a realm governed by masked figures who communicate through intermediaries, that skill set carries a particular value — though most who develop it aren't thinking that far ahead when they start.

### Freelancer
*Resourceful, adaptable, and impossible to surprise*

A Freelancer's edge isn't any single skill — it's the habit of preparation that makes every other skill more effective. These rogues invest in networks of contacts, favors owed, and contingencies laid down before the job began. When something goes wrong (and something always goes wrong), they already have a plan for it. The Freelancer's Guild fosters this approach: not rules, but a culture of professional competence, mutual aid, and the understanding that your reputation is the only thing worth protecting long-term.

**Guild Network (Level 3).** You have contacts. Once per session, you can invoke your network to acquire one of the following: a piece of local information (recent events, specific rumors, a person's last known whereabouts), a safe house for one night, or a piece of non-magical equipment worth up to 50 gp (increasing to 100 gp at 6th level, 200 gp at 9th level, 400 gp at 12th level, 800 gp at 15th level, and 1,600 gp at 18th level), arriving within the hour. The DM may determine that a specific request is beyond your network's reach in a given location.

**Discerning Eye (Level 3).** You can use a bonus action to study a creature you can see and identify its most exploitable vulnerability. Learn one of the following (your choice): one damage type the creature is vulnerable to, the creature's current hit point range (healthy, bloodied at roughly half HP, or critical below one-quarter HP), or one condition that the creature is not immune to. You can use this feature once per short rest.

**Adaptive Strike (Level 9).** Once per turn when you deal Sneak Attack damage, you can choose the damage type: piercing, slashing, bludgeoning, or psychic. If you used Discerning Eye to identify a damage vulnerability, you can instead deal damage of that vulnerability type.

**Contingency Plan (Level 13).** When you would fail a saving throw, you can use your reaction to succeed instead. Once you use this feature, you can't use it again until you finish a short rest.

**Inscrutability (Level 17).** You cannot be tracked by magical means unless you choose to be. You are immune to the *zone of truth* spell and to any magical effect that would compel you to speak truthfully. Once per long rest, you can take the Hide action as a free action immediately after making an attack, regardless of whether you have cover or concealment.

## Sorcerer

In the Pax, the ability to reshape reality through raw will has a name and an institutional home. The Foundation has cultivated Anarch ability for a thousand years — identifying those who carry it, training them in its disciplined use, and channeling their gifts toward the work of maintaining the realm. When this power manifests outside that system, in someone who didn't seek it and wasn't prepared for it, it tends to be noticed. The Foundation maintains records. They follow up.

Sorcerers in the Pax are, almost without exception, people with an institutional file somewhere. The relationship varies considerably: some were formally approached and chose another path; some were identified late and never properly contacted; some trained briefly before finding that the Foundation's discipline wasn't compatible with the way their power actually worked. A few are genuinely unknown, their ability quiet or late enough to have slipped through the tracking systems. What these individuals share is Anarch ability that bypasses years of practiced development — something simply present in them, that they did not earn and cannot fully account for, and that tends to behave less predictably than Foundation-trained work.

Playing a sorcerer in the Pax means knowing what your relationship to the Foundation is. Were you formally approached and declined? Did your power manifest late enough that their records are still catching up? Did you train with them for a time and leave on terms that were more or less amicable? The Foundation doesn't pursue unaffiliated Anarchs — the Pax is not that kind of society — but they do keep track, and they do follow up eventually. Most Anarch sorcerers navigate this without much friction. The Foundation is not an enemy. It just has opinions.

### Anarch Blood
*Not learned, not granted — simply present, since birth, and impossible to suppress*

Anarch Blood sorcerers carry the same fundamental ability that the Foundation trains and channels — the capacity to impose will directly on the fabric of Limbo. The difference is that their power arrived untrained, unstructured, and without institutional guidance, and it has developed accordingly. Where Foundation Anarchs learn precision, Anarch Blood sorcerers develop range and instinct. Their magic is less predictable and more responsive; the kind of power that doesn't ask for directions.

**Transmutation of Force (Level 1).** You can spend 1 sorcery point when you cast a spell that deals damage to change its damage type to any damage type of your choice. The change applies to all damage dealt by the spell on that casting. Additionally, when you cast a spell that creates a persistent physical effect — a wall, a structure, a terrain feature, a conjured object — the effect lasts twice as long as normal and has twice the normal hit points (if applicable).

**Anarch Surge (Level 6).** Once per short rest, you can cast a spell of 1st level or higher without expending a spell slot. When you do, roll a d6. On a 1 or 2, a wild magic surge occurs (roll on the Wild Magic Surge table as per the Wild Magic Sorcerous Origin). On a 3 or higher, the surge is suppressed and the spell is cast normally.

**Reality Shaping (Level 14).** Once per long rest, you can use your action to instantly reshape terrain within a cube you can see with dimensions of 10 feet, increasing by 10 feet for each sorcerer level beyond 14th — stone to water, solid ground to open air, earth to dense vegetation, and similar transformations. The change is permanent and requires no concentration. The DM determines the precise limits of what can be transformed in a given casting.

**Relativistic Anchor (Level 18).** While you are in the Pax or in Limbo, all environmental chaos effects — including random terrain transformation, Fraying encounter effects, and chaotic wild magic that would affect you — are automatically negated. Additionally, allies within 30 feet of you are immune to chaos-based environmental hazards.

## Warlock

Warlocks draw power from a relationship — a compact with an entity greater than themselves, in exchange for capabilities that study or training alone cannot produce. In a realm like the Pax, where arcane learning is the domain of the Foundation and divine intervention is distant, this creates an unusual position. Warlocks occupy a category that institutions don't quite have forms for: not monks, not sorcerers, not scholars, but people who found a door that wasn't supposed to be there and decided to walk through it.

The patrons accessible in and around the Pax range widely — old powers drifting in from the chaos outside, philosophical principles given enough coherent will to grant gifts, the occasional planar entity with its own interests in the realm's stability or its dissolution. For a rare few, the compact is with something closer to home. Warlocks who have entered pact with the Autarchs describe the experience differently depending on when they're asked. Early on, it tends to feel like recognition — as if something significant has noticed your existence and decided you are worth an investment. The power is real. The full shape of the exchange tends to become apparent over time.

Playing a warlock in the Pax means knowing which door you walked through and how much you've thought about what was waiting on the other side. The nature of your compact shapes everything: who approaches you in public, what information you can access, and what is quietly expected of you in return. Whether you find the arrangement clarifying or unsettling says something about your character. Whether you intend to understand it fully before it understands you is a question worth sitting with.

### The Autarch Pact
*Power offered freely — the price revealed slowly*

A warlock of the Autarch Pact received their compact as something that felt, at the time, like an obvious yes: real power, genuine access, a sense of institutional backing from the most significant authority in the realm. What they perhaps didn't fully consider was that the authority in question has collective interests — and has been quietly shaping the warlock's opportunities in directions that serve those interests. The warlock is aware of this, to a degree. What they do about it is their own affair.

**Expanded Spell List.** The Autarch Pact lets you choose from an expanded list of spells when you learn a warlock spell. The following spells are added to the warlock spell list for you.

| Spell Level | Spells |
|---|---|
| 1st | *command, comprehend languages* |
| 2nd | *detect thoughts, enthrall* |
| 3rd | *glyph of warding, sending* |
| 4th | *arcane eye, confusion* |
| 5th | *dominate person, modify memory* |

**Mask of Authority (Level 1).** Once per long rest, you can invoke your patrons' authority. For 1 minute, you project an aura of absolute institutional legitimacy. Creatures of a challenge rating equal to or less than your proficiency bonus that fail a Charisma saving throw (DC equal to your spell save DC) treat your words as legitimate official commands — not magical compulsion, simply authority they have no framework for questioning. This feature has no effect on creatures already engaged in combat with you or your allies, and affected creatures must be able to hear and understand you. The effect ends for a particular creature if you or any of your allies deal damage to it, or if you act in a way that obviously contradicts the legitimacy you are projecting.

**I Was Watching (Level 6).** Once per short rest, you can declare that your patrons observed something on your behalf. You retroactively gain one piece of specific information about a person, place, or recent event within the Pax: a location, a recent conversation, a face connected to a name. The information is accurate. The DM determines its precise form and scope.

**Protected Asset (Level 10).** When you would be reduced to 0 hit points, you instead drop to 1 hit point and become invisible until the end of your next turn, your patrons briefly redirecting their attention to keep you operational. Once used, this feature cannot recharge until you complete a long rest and have reported recent events to your patrons. The method of reporting is established between you and your patrons. The requirement is not.

**Telepathic Intervention (Level 14).** Once per long rest, when a creature you can see makes an attack roll, ability check, or saving throw, you can use your reaction to impose subtle influence: the creature must roll again and take the lower result. The effect manifests as a moment of doubt or hesitation and does not appear as magic unless specifically detected. The creature can resist with a DC 18 Wisdom saving throw; on a success, the creature also recognizes it was being manipulated. The Autarchs are aware each time you use this feature.

## Wizard

Wizards are the Pax's academic mages — practitioners whose power comes not from innate Anarch ability or divine compact, but from accumulated study, precise notation, and the disciplined application of arcane theory developed over centuries. They fill the realm's scholarly institutions, its engineering offices, its private consultancies, and its independent research concerns. In a realm whose stability depends on a thousand years of carefully maintained magical infrastructure, there is no shortage of work for people who understand how it functions.

The Foundation trains Anarchs to shape reality through will, but the structural formulas underlying the Pax — the planar anchors, the chaos suppression wards, the boundary maintenance protocols — were developed through a different tradition entirely: rigorous arcane scholarship that predates any living practitioner's memory. Wizards who specialize in this material occupy a particular niche. Their expertise is urgently needed as the Fraying advances and the infrastructure it stresses requires more frequent attention, and their knowledge of the realm's fundamental architecture gives them a perspective that most citizens, including Foundation monks, simply do not have.

Arcane Geometry wizards tend to be meticulous, systematic, and comfortable with long problems — the kind of scholars who keep their own copies of official formulas because they prefer to verify calculations themselves. This habit of verification is, eventually, where things get interesting. The structural formulas are comprehensive, internally consistent, and clearly effective. They also require a power input that no official record accounts for. Most wizards who notice this file it as an anomaly and move on. Some find that particular anomaly difficult to leave alone.

### Tradition of Arcane Geometry
*The math is precise. The math also doesn't add up.*

Wizards of the Arcane Geometry tradition specialize in the structural mathematics underlying the Pax's magical infrastructure — planar anchors, chaos suppression wards, boundary maintenance, stabilization protocols. These are not abstract academics. They are called in when something is failing and needed before anyone else knows it's failing, and their work requires a combination of theoretical depth and practical urgency that distinguishes them from scholars who work only in archives. The formulas they study are extraordinarily sophisticated, clearly the product of considerable accumulated knowledge, and contain at least one variable whose source no official text has ever named.

**Structural Assessment (Level 2).** Once per short rest, you can spend 1 minute studying a physical structure, magical field, or planar boundary within reach. You learn its current structural integrity, what is maintaining it, whether it has been recently altered or damaged, and one significant anomaly or point of vulnerability (DM's choice). This feature works on any magical infrastructure within the Pax, including planar anchors, chaos suppression wards, and the Pax-Limbo boundary.

**Arcane Annealing (Level 2).** You can cast *mending* as a bonus action rather than an action, and it repairs magical damage as well as physical damage. Once per short rest, you can use this enhanced casting to restore an object weighing up to 10 pounds to complete functionality regardless of the extent of its damage.

**Planar Anchor (Level 6).** Once per long rest, you can cast *private sanctum* without expending a spell slot. While concentrating on this spell, the warded area gains additional stability: creatures within it cannot be involuntarily teleported or planeshifted, and all environmental chaos effects — Fraying encounters, terrain transformation, wild magic — are suppressed within the boundary.

**Perfected Formulae (Level 10).** Your internalization of structural formulas allows you to optimize the geometric expression of your own spells. When you cast a wizard spell of 1st level or higher, you can apply one of the following enhancements without expending an additional resource: the spell ignores resistance (but not immunity) to its damage type; the spell's range is doubled; or the spell's duration is doubled (maximum 24 hours). Once you use this feature, you can't use it again until you finish a short rest.

**Geometric Snare (Level 14).** Once per long rest, when an arcane spell whose level is equal to or less than the highest-level spell slot you currently have is cast within 120 feet of you, you can use your reaction to deconstruct its geometric structure before it completes. This feature affects only arcane spells — those from the wizard, sorcerer, warlock, or bard spell lists. It has no effect on divine spells (cleric, druid, paladin, or ranger) or on spell-like abilities. This functions as *counterspell* but requires no spell slot and requires no ability check. After using this feature, you may identify the precise formula of the countered spell; if it was a wizard spell, you may copy it into your spellbook as if you had found a written copy.

## Custom Backgrounds

The following backgrounds are specific to the Pax. Use them in addition to the standard 5e backgrounds.

---

### Realm Citizen
**Skill Proficiencies:** History, Persuasion
**Tool Proficiencies:** One type of artisan's tools or one gaming set
**Languages:** One of your choice
**Equipment:** A set of fine clothes, a guild membership token, a small Devoti shrine token, a pouch containing 15 gp

**Feature: Civic Ties.** You belong to several civic organizations, as all Pax citizens do. Once per session, you can invoke your guild connections to obtain information, a favor, or an introduction. The DM determines the scope of what's available, but citizens of the Pax look out for each other.

*Suggested personality:* You believe in the Pax — its institutions, its traditions, its people. Discovering its contradictions will affect you specifically because you had specific faith to lose.

---

### Frontier Survivor
**Skill Proficiencies:** Nature, Survival
**Tool Proficiencies:** Herbalism kit
**Languages:** One of your choice
**Equipment:** A traveler's pack, a set of frontier clothes (practical, worn), a map of your home region (possibly partly dissolved), a pouch containing 10 gp

**Feature: Frontier Knowledge.** You know the Pax's border regions better than any city-dweller. You have advantage on Survival and Nature checks in frontier environments. You recognize signs of chaos incursion that others might miss, and frontier settlers trust you more readily than they trust people from the capital.

*Suggested personality:* You are practical, self-reliant, and quietly angry at the capital's inadequate response to the frontier crisis. You have seen the dissolution up close. You know it is not being handled.

---

### Order Acolyte
**Skill Proficiencies:** Arcana, Insight
**Tool Proficiencies:** Calligrapher's supplies
**Languages:** One of your choice
**Equipment:** A Foundation training text (old, personal, well-worn), a white robe (kept, though no longer worn), a small arcane focus, a pouch containing 12 gp

**Feature: Foundation Training.** You underwent eight years of Foundation training and chose not to formally join. You know the order's practices, its publicly-facing culture, and its behavioral patterns. Foundation members recognize you as a former trainee and treat you with a certain ambivalence — respect for the training, uncertainty about the choice. You have advantage on Insight checks when interacting with Foundation members, and you can recognize conditioning-related behavioral signs (with a DC 14 Wisdom check) that others might miss.

*Suggested personality:* You left the Foundation at the hardest moment — when leaving was most difficult and staying most expected. Something in the training didn't sit right. You don't talk about it easily.

## Character Hooks

Answer as many of the following as are useful for defining your character. Share answers with the DM; they may become plot-relevant.

1. **Who did you donate to the Foundation?** A sibling? A childhood friend? A teacher who recognized your ability and encouraged you to develop it? Do you still think about them? When did you last hear from them?

2. **Where were you when the first earthquake hit the frontier you knew?** What did you think? Did you believe the official explanation?

3. **What organization in Meridian do you belong to?** (Not the Freelancer's Guild — that's Caske's domain. A guild, a neighborhood association, a Devoti temple, a hobby circle.) Who do you know through it?

4. **Have you ever seen an Autarch in person?** At a ceremony, or in a more unusual context? What did you think?

5. **What is your relationship to the Anarch ability?** Do you have it? Did you choose not to develop it formally? Do you know someone who joined the Shapers sub-order?

6. **What is the lie you most want to remain a lie?** Everyone in the Pax believes something comfortable that is wrong. What is yours?

# WHAT YOUR CHARACTER KNOWS

## Common Knowledge

- The Pax has existed for 1,000 years, founded by the Autarchs.
- The Foundation maintains the Pax's stability through the Shapers sub-order, who live in seclusion and whose concentration must not be disturbed.
- The Autarchs are the rulers; they are distant, strange, and present at major ceremonies. The Right of Petition exists but is dangerous to use.
- The frontier is under increasing pressure from Slaadi raids and chaos dissolution. The official position is that this is manageable.
- Limbo is navigable but dangerous; travel outside the Pax requires skill and will.
- The Xaositects, Githzerai, Doomguard, and other planar factions maintain a presence near the Pax.
