# THE 33RD RUNE
## Player's Guide to the World of Talumh

*A setting guide for players in The 33rd Rune campaign*

---

> **A note to players:** This guide contains everything your character would know as a native of Talumh. It does not contain secrets. Some things described here are incomplete or partly wrong — that is intentional, because your character's knowledge is also incomplete and partly wrong. You will learn more as you play.

---

# WELCOME TO TALUMH

## What This World Is

Talumh is a world apart. Not in the way travelers use that phrase — meaning a place with its own character — but literally. The philosophers of the Shining Path describe it as a pocket within the deeper fabric of the spirit world: a dream sustained by a sleeping god, bounded on all sides by the Mist that marks the edge of what is real. Sail far enough in any direction and the sea becomes fog, and the fog becomes nothing. Come back before you fade entirely. This is not metaphor. Everyone knows this. Children learn it before they learn to write.

The people of Talumh have made their lives inside this boundary without much difficulty, because the boundary is large. There are six regions, twelve communities, forests and mountains and deserts and jungles, rivers enough to sustain everything that lives here. The Mist at the edge of the world has never felt like a cage. It has always felt, more simply, like the edge of the map.

What it means — why Talumh is bounded, what the Mist is keeping out or keeping in — is a question that different peoples answer differently. The Path of Light says the Creator made the boundary to protect his children. The Shining Path says the boundary is a natural consequence of what the world is and worrying about it is a category error. The Tuthá — the fey peoples who share the world with humans, dwarfs, gnomes, and smallfolk — do not discuss the Mist with outsiders. They have known its nature for longer than the other peoples have been here, and they have decided that some knowledge is better held close.

You have grown up knowing that the world has edges and that something exists beyond them. You have learned to live with that knowledge the way you live with any true thing that cannot be changed. It is not what concerns you. What concerns you is what happens in the interior — in the communities, on the roads, in the quiet accumulation of ordinary days that constitutes a life. That is what is at stake now. That is what you are being asked to protect.

## The Current Moment

Something has changed. You can feel it the way you feel weather coming in — not the storm itself but the pressure drop, the quality of the silence, the way the air sits differently against your skin.

Priya the Wanderer is gone. The scholar who has spent twenty years mapping the 32 runes of Void Magic, building a circle of researchers, field agents, and students into something that functions like a family — she is simply absent from the places she should be. Her rooms in Fountain are undisturbed. Her notes are in their usual disorder. She left no word.

The timing is wrong. In the weeks before she disappeared, Priya had been unusually excited, the way she gets when a research thread is pulling tight. She had been asking questions about old records. She had been re-reading the oldest accounts of the runes with an attention that felt different from her usual thoroughness. She had mentioned, once, a document she had found — something that suggested the histories everyone knows are missing something.

The Path of Man has agents in Fountain. This, too, has changed in the last year. The Path's presence in Fountain used to be negligible — the community has been Path of Light territory for as long as anyone has tracked these things. Now there are chapter houses, private meetings, people who were not here before. The Path of Light's temple is quieter than it was. The old families who used to run the festivals have become reluctant to discuss their devotion in public. Nobody has named what is happening. Naming it would require saying aloud what everyone is beginning to suspect.

These two things — Priya's disappearance and the Path of Man's spreading presence — are connected. You are certain of this the way you are certain of things you cannot yet prove. What you do not yet know is how.

# THE WORLD AROUND YOU

## How Talumh Came to Be

The oldest stories — told by the Tuthá in their courts, preserved in the libraries of the Shining Path, passed down in fragments through the oral histories of every people who lives here — describe an entity called the One. Not a god in the way most people use the word. Something older and larger. Something that does not act but simply *is*, the way existence itself simply is. The One, according to these stories, entered a sleep long before anything else existed — and within that sleep, a dream began to take shape.

Talumh is the dream. Everything within the Mist exists because the One dreams it. The Mist is the boundary where dreaming becomes not-dreaming, where the interior of the dream gives way to whatever lies beyond it.

Within the dream, two things arose that were not dreamed into existence but arose from it: the spirits, and the peoples.

The spirits came first. Entities of pure purpose — guardians, challengers, nurturers, destroyers — each embodying some aspect of what the dream required. They built things in the dream. Two great civilizations, now long collapsed, whose names are preserved only in the oldest Tuthá songs. They disputed. They made war on each other. The war grew so vast that it began to tear the fabric of the dream itself — to pull Talumh toward dissolution into whatever surrounds it. The histories call this the War of the Spirits. It ended only when the most powerful entities on both sides were banished — cast into what the Tuthá call the Void, the nothingness outside the Mist — and when the remaining spirits sacrificed much of their own power to seal the dream closed.

That sealing is why the Mist exists as it does. The boundary was made stronger when the Void nearly broke through. The sacrifice that held it cost the spirits enormously. The great civilizations collapsed. The world was, for a time, nearly empty.

The Lord and Lady remained: two guardian spirits who had led the defense of the dream, now diminished but alive, keeping the covenant that holds the Tuthá peoples in their care. Their two courts — the Radiant Court under Queen Glaréad, and the Glimmering Court under King Dwynnan — still exist in the places between places, accessible to those with fey blood or fey blessing. They are patient. They have been waiting a long time.

## How Humanity Arrived

Humans did not arise in Talumh the way the Tuthá did. This is one of the things that separates human history from the history of every other people in the world, and it is not a comfortable distinction.

Human beings were brought here from somewhere else. Where they came from, the histories say, was a world already dying — some other place beyond the Mist, collapsing toward its end. Who brought them and why are questions the different traditions answer differently, and none of the answers are simple.

What most humans believe: a spirit or spirits of the old world recognized that humanity deserved a chance at life, reached through the Mist to a dying place, and carried the people of that place into the safety of the dream. This is broadly accurate as far as it goes.

What some humans believe: the Creator — the divine figure at the center of Path of Light worship, and the one that Path of Man theology also references — is the entity responsible for this rescue. That this act was an expression of love for a newly made people, and that human history in Talumh is a story of children finding their way in a world that was not made for them but was given to them as a gift.

What the Tuthá believe about the arrival of humans, they do not generally share.

What is certain: the arrival of humanity changed everything. The people who came through the Void were fractured, frightened, and extraordinarily numerous compared to the Tuthá peoples who already inhabited the dream. They settled. They expanded. They learned Void Magic — the manipulation of the rune-patterns that express the deep structure of the dream — from sources that the histories are vague about but that Void scholars associate with spirits who had reason to want human power to grow. Within a few generations, humanity was building an empire.

## The Great War and the Settlement

Roughly two hundred years ago, that empire reached a crisis point. A human empire — unified under leaders who commanded Void Magic at a scale no one had attempted since the War of the Spirits — made war on the Tuthá peoples and their patron spirits. The details of this war are present in the histories but contested: who struck first, what the empire actually intended, whether the leaders knew what they were doing to the seal between Talumh and the Void.

The war ended with the empire's defeat. The Lord and Lady, roused from their long guardianship, led the response. The human military was broken. The leaders who had wielded Void Magic at the highest levels died or disappeared. The empire dissolved.

What followed is called the Settlement. The Lord and Lady, weighing what to do with the people who had caused so much damage, made a decision that strikes most humans — when they learn of it in its full form — as somewhere between wise and cold: they separated humanity. The twelve communities exist because a unified human civilization was judged to be too dangerous to allow. Each community was given a territory and the knowledge it needed to sustain itself. Contact between communities was not forbidden, but the geography of Talumh makes maintaining close connection difficult, and the Settlement's design seems to have intended that difficulty.

Two hundred years of relative peace followed. The communities have developed distinct cultures, built distinct institutions, and gradually come to think of their separation as normal rather than as the consequence of a judgment handed down by the guardians of the dream. This is what most humans in Talumh believe. It is substantially correct.

What the current generation is beginning to discover is that the peace was never as complete as it appeared.

## The Twelve Communities

Talumh has twelve permanent human settlements, one in each of the six geographic regions plus six smaller Wildermen communities distributed between them. Their names are:

**Major communities:** Fountain, Vault, Vista, Window, Shade, Wheel.

**Wildermen communities:** Council, Meeting, Longhome, Valor, Tower, Token.

Each major community was established by the Settlement as a self-contained civilization. Each developed its own governance, its own relationship to the paths, its own economy and culture. They trade with each other when geography permits. They share a loose sense of common origin — most communities hold some version of the history above — but they do not share a government, and there has been no serious attempt to create one since the empire fell.

The Wildermen communities are different in character. The peoples who settled them are humans who never fully accepted the Settlement's arrangement — who continued to move through the wilds between the major communities, maintaining relationships with the Tuthá and the land spirits, resisting the consolidation that the other communities embraced. They are not nomads, exactly, but they are not settlers in the way that Fountain or Window is settled. They have their own deep knowledge of the land and the things that live in it.

# THE COMMUNITIES

## Fountain

Fountain sits at the base of the great escarpment — the geological fault line where the western Expanse drops away to the lower lands to the east. The waterfall from which the community takes its name is not a single cascade but a series of them, the Sona River descending over three hundred feet in a sequence of falls and rapids that has given the city its central visual fact for as long as anyone has kept records. The sound of the water is constant. You don't notice it until you leave, and then its absence is one of the first things that tells you you're far from home.

Fountain is a city of color. The tile roofs — deep reds, blues, ochres, and occasional exuberant greens — catch whatever light comes over the escarpment and reflect it back in ways that make the city look, from the approach road, like something that should not be real. The market districts around the base of the falls are loud and crowded for most of the day. The upper residential neighborhoods that cling to the escarpment face are quieter, higher, and occupied by families with money enough to pay for the view and the climb. The wealth gap in Fountain is not subtle. It does not try to be.

The community is traditionally Path of Light territory. The great temples here were built by the first families who settled the escarpment, and the Path of Light's festivals — particularly the great spring water-blessing and the autumn harvest ceremony — are the most elaborate civic events in the calendar. This has been changing in the last year. It is changing in ways that long-established families are noticing and discussing in private.

Fountain has two client towns: **Grove**, two hours north on the road that runs along the escarpment base, and **Portal**, six hours south where the road passes through a natural stone arch that the first settlers took as a good omen. Both towns are smaller, simpler communities that look to Fountain for trade and governance. Both have been touched by what is touching Fountain.

## Window

Window is the largest settlement in Talumh — the center of long-distance trade, built on a series of islands in the delta of the Asura River where the river fans out to meet the Long Coast sea. The islands are connected by bridges so numerous that the city's layout has to be learned rather than intuited; visitors routinely get turned around in the delta district, taking the wrong bridge and finding themselves in the fish market instead of the banking quarter. The locals find this charming. The locals are wrong.

The buildings here are dark stone — the particular volcanic basalt that the Asura carries down from the highlands and deposits in the delta in enormous quantities. In the morning mist, which is the consistent characteristic of Window's climate, the dark buildings against the grey water give the city a serious, considered quality that its people have inherited or at least learned to perform. Window is the most orderly of the twelve communities in terms of governance: it has a council of Harbor Lords, it has established merchant law, it has an inspectorate. It functions. It has always functioned. This is why what has happened here over the last two years is so disturbing to those who know Fountain well enough to compare.

The Path of Man now controls Window. This is not a fact that is announced; it is a fact that is observed. The Harbor Lords who resisted the Path's expansion have retired, been discredited, or in two cases simply ceased to be visible in the way that prominent people in a city should be visible. The Path's chapter house is the largest new construction in Window in a decade. The council meetings, by the accounts of those who attended them before things changed, are different now.

## Shade

Shade is carved. Not built the way Fountain or Window is built — not assembled from materials brought to a site — but cut from the stone of the canyon that the Lakhsa River made over thousands of years as it worked its way through the rock of the Dune Sea's western edge. The faces of both canyon walls are worked stone, windows and doorways and terraces cut directly into the cliff. The canyon floor, forty feet below the canyon rim in the narrowest sections, is the city's main street. The great canvas shades — tens of thousands of square feet of fabric strung on a framework that took three generations to build — are stretched across the canyon's upper width, filtering the Sward's brutal sun to something survivable.

Shade's Elder council is its governance: seven Elders chosen by the heads of the major families, serving until they choose to step down or until they die. The council has historically been conservative, suspicious of outside influence, and focused on the twin challenges of maintaining the caravan trade with Window and managing the ongoing conflict with the desert peoples to the east — the fey and humanoid communities that dispute Shade's access to certain water sources. The council is not conservative about this conflict; it is simply persistent, the way all things in Shade are persistent. The canyon teaches endurance.

The Path of Man has been courting Shade's council. This is, from the council's perspective, a complex situation. They do not want to be under anyone's thumb — Shade's history is a history of surviving without external help — but the Path of Man offers resources, trade connections, and military support against the desert peoples. Some Elders find the offer reasonable. Others find it suspicious. The council is currently split in ways it has not been in living memory.

## Wheel

Wheel is exactly what it sounds like: a port built in the shape of a wheel, with the harbor at the hub and the residential, commercial, and administrative districts arranged in a ring around it. The magician who designed it was working with a specific engineering problem — how to give every vessel in the harbor an equal and convenient path to open water — and solved it so elegantly that the result is also beautiful. The circular outer wall has twelve gates, one for each of the twelve communities, though only six of them are regularly used. The spokes that divide the wheel into districts are wide enough for two wagons to pass and lined with buildings all the way to the hub.

Wheel is the most chaotic of the major communities in governance. The Portmaster is the nominal authority, but the Portmaster is elected by the harbor merchants, which means the Portmaster is whoever the harbor merchants currently trust, which changes. The Portmaster's authority over the residential districts is real in theory and contested in practice. The community has always settled disputes through negotiation, bribery, and the occasional dramatic confrontation in the harbor district that everyone agrees never to speak of again. This is not disorder; it is a system adapted to a population that includes too many people with too many independent interests to submit to centralized authority without deciding, independently, that it serves their interests.

The Path of Man has significant inroads here. The free-spirited culture of Wheel is, it turns out, not immune to an organization that offers structure, community, and a coherent account of why things have gone wrong. There is a resistance — genuine and growing — but the Path of Man is organized in ways the resistance is not yet. This situation is developing.

## Vault

Vault sits in the northern reaches of the Pale — not in the Twilight Vale itself, where the Feytouched communities have their deepest settlements, but at the edge of the Darkwood, where the old-growth forest meets the White Mountains. It is a fortified settlement in a way that the other major communities are not, because the Pale is a different kind of dangerous than anywhere else: not dangerous in the way the Dune Sea is dangerous — open hostility, clear enemies — but dangerous in the way of a place where the boundary between the human world and the fey world is thin and old agreements can fray.

The people of Vault are accustomed to complexity in their relationships with their neighbors. They trade with the Tuthá. They host the occasional Feytouched family that has come down from the Vale for reasons of their own. They maintain the ward-stones along the Darkwood's eastern edge that the Greensingers placed there three generations ago, because no one in Vault is sure what would happen if those stones were allowed to fail. They are practical in the way of people who live at an intersection of things larger than themselves.

## Vista

Vista sits at the crossroads of the Wilderland — the geographic center of Talumh's interior, where the rivers from the Golden Mountains meet the lowland routes from Fountain and the northern tracks from Vault. It is the community most accustomed to travelers and trade. The market here runs six days out of seven. The Smallfolk population is the largest of any major settlement — Goodlund, the traditional Halfling homeland, is two days' walk north — and Vista has adapted accordingly: negotiable over reliable, hospitable over formal, pragmatic over ideological.

The Greensingers have a strong presence in Vista, which is consistent with the community's general orientation toward the natural world of the Wilderland. There is a Path of Light temple that is reasonably well-attended. There is no Path of Man chapter house yet, though there is a rumor of one being planned.

## The Wildermen Communities

The six Wildermen settlements are different in kind from the major communities. They are not cities or towns in the conventional sense — they range from Council (a permanent governance seat for the dispersed Wildermen groups, large enough to be a small town) to Meeting (a seasonal gathering place with no permanent population, just maintained infrastructure). They exist in the spaces between the major communities, and the people who live in or move through them are shaped by that in-between quality.

**Council** — The closest thing the Wildermen have to a capital: a permanent settlement where the heads of the major Wildermen bands meet several times a year to coordinate, resolve disputes, and present a unified face to the major communities on matters that affect them all.

**Meeting** — No permanent residents. Maintained structures, cleared ground, reliable water. Exists to host the great seasonal gatherings where Wildermen bands from across the continent meet, trade, exchange news, and conduct the marriages, apprenticeships, and adoptions that keep the bands connected to each other.

**Longhome** — The largest semi-permanent Wildermen settlement, home to several bands that have chosen a more settled existence without fully joining the major communities. Functions as a waystation and supply point on the routes between the Expanse and the Wilderland.

**Valor** — A military community, disciplined in a way that most Wildermen settlements are not. Founded to watch the borderlands between the settled regions and the wilder territories where the Tuthá's influence is strongest and the rules of human society apply inconsistently.

**Tower** — Named for the ancient ruin at its center — a structure from before the Settlement whose origins no one can confidently identify. Home to the community's small but serious scholarly tradition; the Tower's library is the best source outside the major settlements for pre-Settlement documents.

**Token** — A trade waypoint, positioned deliberately at the junction of four major overland routes. Everyone passes through Token at some point. Nobody stays long.

# THE PEOPLES OF TALUMH

## Choosing Your People

The peoples described below represent the full range of ancestry available to player characters in this campaign. Most are human or human-adjacent — a reflection of the Settlement, which made Talumh a world shaped primarily by human history. The non-human peoples (Smallfolk, Gnomes, Dwarfs, and those with Tuthá blood) each bring their own history and their own relationship to the human communities and the world's deeper powers.

Within the human category, you have a choice: **Men** (settled human communities, the common majority) or **Wildermen** (humans who never fully accepted the Settlement's arrangement and live in the spaces between). This choice says something about where you come from and what relationship you have to the established order.

When selecting your race, use the stat blocks below. For races that use standard D&D 5e subraces (Smallfolk, Gnomes, Dwarfs), you may choose any standard subrace available in the 5e Player's Handbook.

---

## Men

*The common people of the twelve communities. Olive-skinned and dark-haired as a rule, though generations of mixing have produced significant variation. Adaptable. Found everywhere. The dominant culture in Talumh.*

Men arrived in Talumh through the Void, from a world no living person remembers, and have been adapting to their circumstances ever since. Two hundred years of separation since the Settlement have given each of the twelve communities its own customs, accent, and disposition — Window's orderly pragmatism, Fountain's festive religiosity, Shade's canyon-bred endurance, Wheel's chaotic independence — but the thread that runs through all of them is a talent for fitting in. Men settle. Men build institutions. Men make themselves at home.

This talent for settlement is also the thing that the Path of Man calls a weakness. The Path of Man argues that the Settlement itself was an act of subordination — that the Lord and Lady scattered humanity to control it, and that two centuries of comfortable separation have trained humanity to accept confinement as normalcy. Most Men do not accept this reading of their history. Some, lately, have begun to find it difficult to dismiss.

**Men Traits**

*Ability Score Increase.* Your Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma scores each increase by 1.

*Age.* Humans reach adulthood in their late teens and live less than a century.

*Alignment.* Humans tend toward no particular alignment. The best and worst of Talumh's history has been made by Men.

*Size.* Medium.

*Speed.* Your base walking speed is 30 feet.

*Skills.* You gain proficiency in one skill of your choice.

*Languages.* You can speak, read, and write Common and one additional language of your choice.

---

## Wildermen

*Humans who never accepted the Settlement's arrangement. Roam the wilds between communities. More deeply touched by the natural world — and by the Tuthá who inhabit it — than their settled kin.*

When the Lord and Lady scattered humanity into the twelve communities, some humans refused to stay put. Not in open defiance — there was no organized resistance — but in the simple persistent fact of people who kept moving, kept living between the places they were supposed to stay. The Wildermen communities — Council, Meeting, Longhome, Valor, Tower, Token — grew from these people over generations.

Wildermen are recognizable to anyone who knows what to look for. They move differently than settled people. They read landscapes that settled people see as empty. Their relationship to magic is more immediate — the Sorcerer cantrip that most Wildermen carry is not unusual among them, not a sign of exceptional gift, just the way that proximity to the Tuthá and the land spirits has slowly worked its way into bloodlines that have lived in those presences for generations.

The Path of Man has complicated feelings about Wildermen. On one hand, Wildermen are human and therefore fall under the Path's declared concern for human self-determination. On the other hand, Wildermen's comfort with the Tuthá and the spirit world is exactly the kind of accommodation the Path of Man argues humanity should have outgrown.

**Wildermen Traits**

*Ability Score Increase.* Increase three ability scores of your choice by 1 each.

*Age.* As Men.

*Alignment.* Wildermen favor no particular alignment. Their culture values adaptability and self-reliance over doctrine.

*Size.* Medium.

*Speed.* Your base walking speed is 30 feet.

*Touch of the Wild.* You know one cantrip of your choice from the Sorcerer spell list. Charisma is your spellcasting ability for it.

*Languages.* You can speak, read, and write Common. You also know Sylvan at a conversational level — enough to be understood by Tuthá who choose to communicate in it, though Wildermen acknowledge they speak it imperfectly.

## Smallfolk (Halflings)

*Called "halflings" in most D&D references but known as Smallfolk throughout Talumh. Nomadic by tradition, social by nature, welcome almost everywhere.*

The Smallfolk arrived in Talumh alongside humans — the histories treat the two peoples as having come through the Void together, though whether they came from the same dying world or simply arrived at the same moment is disputed. What is not disputed is that the Smallfolk made different choices after arrival. Where humans built the twelve communities and settled, most Smallfolk kept moving. Where the Settlement is a constraint for humans, it is largely irrelevant to the Smallfolk, whose homeland — the Goodlund region of the Wilderland — was not a settlement arranged by the Lord and Lady but a territory they claimed themselves, and which they treat less as a fixed home and more as a base of operations.

The Tinkers — the traveling bands of Smallfolk artisans, traders, and entertainers who move between communities — are the most familiar face of Smallfolk culture to most of Talumh. A Tinker caravan is universally welcomed. They bring goods that can't be found locally, news from communities weeks' travel away, and an entertainment tradition that has been refined over two centuries of practice. Smallfolk have a gift for making people comfortable enough to tell them things they might not tell others. This gift has commercial applications.

**Smallfolk Traits**

Use the standard Halfling stat block from the D&D 5e Player's Handbook. You may choose the Lightfoot or Stout subrace.

*Setting Notes:* Smallfolk in Talumh tend to be associated with the Tinker tradition — traveling merchants, entertainers, and information brokers. The Goodlund homeland in the Wilderland is a place of rolling hills, gentle rivers, and communities sized more to Smallfolk comfort than to human scale.

## Gnomes

*Live alongside the Tuthá rather than in human communities. Natural affinity for illusion and subtle magic. Technically Mannish — human-adjacent in ancestry — but culturally closer to the fey world than to the settlements.*

Gnomes occupy an unusual place in Talumh's social order. They are Mannish — meaning their ancestry is, in the very deep history, human-adjacent rather than purely fey — but they arrived in Talumh as guests of the Tuthá rather than through the same channels as humans and Smallfolk. They live in Tuthá settlements and at Tuthá edges, sharing the fey world's relationship to illusion and subtle magic. They trade and interact with human communities freely, but they do not settle in them. The distinction is cultural more than biological — nothing prevents a Gnome from living in Fountain or Window — but the culture has not changed much in two hundred years.

The Gnome talent for illusion is the quality that most commonly draws them into contact with human communities: as illusionists, as disguise artists, as the kind of intelligence operative whose work requires them to seem like they aren't there. Human communities that need this kind of work done know who to call.

**Gnome Traits**

Use the standard Gnome stat block from the D&D 5e Player's Handbook. You may choose the Forest Gnome or Rock Gnome subrace.

*Setting Notes:* Gnomes in Talumh live alongside the Tuthá peoples and are more comfortable in fey spaces than most non-fey beings. A Gnome entering play should have a reason for being involved with a human circle — trade, curiosity, research, or a specific relationship with one of the NPCs in Priya's circle.

## Dwarfs

*Six clans, one holdfast. The Yashim Mountains are home and the Lord is patron. Finest craftspeople in Talumh. Slow to extend trust; reliable once extended.*

The Yashim Holdfast is the oldest continuously occupied constructed site in Talumh — older than the oldest human community, older than the Settlement, old enough that the Dwarfs themselves are uncertain when it was built, only that it predates anything they would need to compare it to. The six clans of the holdfast — whose names and divisions are complex matters of internal Dwarf politics that outsiders rarely bother to learn correctly — have held this territory through the War of the Spirits, through the human empire's expansion, through the Settlement, and through two centuries of subsequent history with the consistent stoic endurance that the mountains apparently teach to those who live in them long enough.

Dwarfs venerate the Lord — not as a god in the Path of Light sense, but as a patron spirit whose covenant with the holdfast predates his covenant with the Tuthá. This gives Dwarf spiritual practice a specific character: less mystical than Tuthá relationship to the Lord, more contractual. The Lord has kept his side of the covenant. The Dwarfs keep theirs. This is not sentiment; it is the oldest binding agreement in Talumh.

Dwarf Void Practitioners exist — the rune system came to humanity through sources the histories associate with the Void, and Dwarfs have human-adjacent Mannish ancestry — but they are uncommon and regarded with specific wariness within the holdfast, because the history of Void Magic in the Great War is not something any people who lived through it approach lightly.

**Dwarf Traits**

Use the standard Dwarf stat block from the D&D 5e Player's Handbook. You may choose the Hill Dwarf or Mountain Dwarf subrace.

*Setting Notes:* Dwarfs from the Yashim Holdfast are not, as a rule, people who leave. A Dwarf player character should have a specific reason for being in the wider world — debt of honor, exile, a research obligation, or a family connection to someone outside the mountains.

## Half-Elf

*Children of a human parent and a Tuthá parent (specifically of the Elf-tribe Tuthá). Rare. Caught between two worlds that are not at war but are not entirely at peace.*

The Elfs are one of the twelve tribes of the Tuthá — one of the peoples who live under the covenant of the Lord and Lady — and they are the Tuthá most likely to form lasting relationships with humans. Their children, when those relationships produce them, are what the rest of Talumh calls half-elves: people who belong fully to neither world and must choose how to inhabit both.

There are two paths for a half-elf in Talumh. The **standard path** is the common case: a half-elf who has grown up mostly in human communities, carrying their Tuthá heritage as a gift of grace and adaptability, but shaped primarily by the human culture around them. The **Feytouched path** describes half-elves who were raised in the Twilight Vale or at the edges of the Pale — people whose formative years were spent in fey spaces, whose Tuthá inheritance runs deeper, and who sometimes receive the call from the Lord or Lady that marks the beginning of a Witch's path.

### Half-Elf (Standard)

Use the standard Half-Elf stat block from the D&D 5e Player's Handbook.

*Setting Notes:* A standard-path half-elf has grown up navigating the gap between human and Tuthá cultures. They know things about the fey world that most humans don't, and they know things about human society that most Tuthá don't. This makes them valuable in situations where both worlds are in play — which describes the current situation rather precisely.

### Half-Elf (Feytouched)

*A half-elf raised in the Pale, deeply touched by fey magic. The Lord and Lady sometimes call them to become Witches.*

**Feytouched Traits**

*Ability Score Increase.* Your Charisma score increases by 2, and your Dexterity score increases by 1.

*Age.* Feytouched age more slowly than standard half-elves. They reach adulthood around age 20 and can live to 150.

*Alignment.* Feytouched tend toward the fluid alignments of the fey world — chaotic good is common, but the range is wide.

*Size.* Medium.

*Speed.* Your base walking speed is 30 feet.

*Darkvision.* You can see in dim light within 60 feet as if it were bright light, and in darkness as if it were dim light. You can't discern color in darkness, only shades of grey.

*Fey Ancestry.* You have advantage on saving throws against being charmed, and magic can't put you to sleep.

*Tuthá Blood.* You know one cantrip from the Druid spell list. Charisma is your spellcasting ability for it.

*Wilderness Ease.* You have proficiency in the Stealth skill. When you are in natural terrain — forest, grassland, hills, or mountains — you can attempt to hide even when you are only lightly obscured by foliage, heavy rain, falling snow, mist, or other natural phenomena.

*Animal Kinship.* You have proficiency in the Animal Handling skill. Beasts of Small size or smaller do not attack you unless provoked, charmed, or directly threatened.

*Languages.* You can speak, read, and write Common, and either Sylvan or Elvish (your choice).

*The Call.* The Lord and Lady have been known to choose Feytouched as Witches. If your character is a Sorcerer who selects the Witch subclass, the Calling feature's flavor applies with full weight: you were genuinely chosen, in dreams, before you chose.

# CHARACTER CLASSES

## Classes in Talumh

All standard D&D 5e classes are available. What follows are the setting-specific notes for each class, including the custom subclasses developed for this world. Standard subclasses from the Player's Handbook are available unless noted otherwise.

A note on magic in Talumh: the world has four distinct magical traditions, and your class choice implies which tradition your character practices.

- **Sorcery** — Natural-world magic, flowing through bloodlines with fey or spirit proximity. Sorcerers, Bards, and the Witch subclass.
- **Elemental Magic** — Wild nature magic, rooted in pacts with the land itself. Druids, Rangers, and the Summoner subclass.
- **Void Magic** — The magic of the 32 Runes, gifted to humanity from outside the dream. Wizards who practice the Void Practitioner tradition.
- **Divine Magic** — Power granted by great spirits. Clerics, Paladins, and (in a different form) Witches.

---

## Barbarian

Barbarians in Talumh are found most commonly among the Wildermen and among peoples living close to Tuthá territory, where the raw energy of the wild world runs closer to the surface of daily life. The rage of a Barbarian in this setting carries a spiritual weight — not a berserker's thoughtlessness, but the kind of focused ferocity that comes from being completely present in the land.

The **Wild Magic Barbarian** subclass (Player's Handbook supplement) fits naturally for Wildermen or characters from the Tuthá borderlands. Void Magic and Barbarian culture do not mix; the 32 Runes require concentration and controlled ritual, which is the opposite of what a Barbarian brings to a fight.

All standard Barbarian subclasses are available.

---

## Bard

Bards in Talumh are the keepers of oral history and the travelers who carry information across the long distances between communities. Their magic has a divine flavor — most Bards understand their gifts as music taught by spirits, songs learned in dreams — but the mechanics are unchanged. A Bard's knowledge of Talumh's deep history is frequently more reliable than what the community temples officially teach.

All standard Bard subclasses are available.

## Cleric

Clerics in Talumh serve one of five patrons, each representing a genuine source of divine power in the world.

### Domain of the Perfected Self
*Cleric subclass — Shining Path*

The Shining Path holds no god and seeks no patron. Its clerics draw power not from an external source but from the accumulated spiritual weight of their own practice — years of discipline made manifest, the body and mind trained to a point where the boundary between the practitioner and the divine becomes permeable. This is not humility. It is a very specific kind of pride: the claim that perfection is possible and that the work of achieving it is its own source of power.

Shining Path clerics are rare. The Path is monastic and demanding, and most of its practitioners are not clerics — they are monks, scholars, and teachers. The clerics who emerge from the Shining Path's most intensive training are something else: people who have reached a state where their practice has begun to generate effects that the world recognizes as miraculous, even though no god has been asked to intervene.

The Path of Man regards Shining Path clerics with a complicated hostility. They are human, which the Path of Man approves of. They serve no spirit patron, which the Path of Man also approves of. But they are conspicuously unwilling to accept the Path of Man's account of humanity's purpose, and their discipline produces people who are extremely difficult to manipulate or intimidate.

## Druid

Druids in Talumh practice Elemental Magic — the wild nature magic of the land spirits, the rivers, and the places where the world's deep power runs close to the surface. They are not a religious organization in the way the Paths are religious organizations; they are practitioners of a craft that requires years of apprenticeship and a willingness to enter into relationships with entities that do not think the way humans think.

The Greensingers are the primary druidic tradition in Talumh — an organized sect with its own culture and history, responsible for maintaining the elemental pacts established after the War of the Spirits.

All standard Druid circles are available. The Circle of the Greensingers is the custom circle for this setting.

---

### Circle of the Greensingers
*Druid subclass*

The Greensingers are Talumh's oldest organized druidic tradition — a sect that predates the human settlements, whose founding myths describe them as having been taught directly by land spirits in the chaos following the War of the Spirits. Their purpose is specific: they maintain the elemental pacts. Not nature worship in a general sense, not ecological protection in the way a Guardian-path Druid might understand it, but the specific, ongoing, contractual relationships between particular spirit entities and the living world that keep Talumh stable.

The Greensingers know things that no written history records, because the records they keep are the pacts themselves — held in memory, renewed through ritual, carried in an oral tradition that has been passed from teacher to student without break for two hundred years. A Greensinger who reaches the full expression of their training is not just a powerful druid. They are an institutional memory and a diplomatic channel in a world that desperately needs both.

The Path of Man has no formal policy on the Greensingers, but the Path's theology — which holds that humanity should not be in debt to or in relationship with spirits — implies a hostility to everything the Greensingers do. This has not yet produced organized conflict. Most people who follow the situation expect it eventually will.

**Bonus Proficiencies (2nd level)**

You gain proficiency in the Persuasion skill. You also learn to speak, read, and write Sylvan if you do not already know it.

**Spirit Kinship (2nd level)**

You can cast *speak with animals* at will without expending a spell slot. Additionally, when you cast a spell that summons or conjures a beast or elemental, the summoned creature has advantage on its first attack roll or ability check made on your behalf.

**Greensinger Spells (2nd level)**

| 9th | *commune with nature*, *conjure elemental* |

**Land's Voice (6th level)**

You can cast *commune with nature* as a ritual without expending a spell slot by spending 10 minutes in physical contact with natural ground. When you do, you may ask three questions instead of the standard information gain, and the land's answers are specific — the DM provides concrete details rather than vague impressions. This ritual does not require the ritual casting feature.

**Elemental Intermediary (10th level)**

When you are within 60 feet of a spirit or elemental creature that is not hostile toward you, you can use your action to attempt to negotiate with it. ## Monk

The Shining Path's monastic tradition is the primary source of Monks in Talumh. ## Ranger

Rangers in Talumh are the people who know the spaces between the communities — the roads, the wilds, the territory that is nobody's jurisdiction. Some are community scouts or border wardens. The Greensingers maintain a loose relationship with certain Rangers, particularly those who have developed knowledge of the elemental pacts and the spirit territories they protect.

## Rogue

Talumh's major communities all produce Rogues, because major communities always produce people who find the official channels insufficient for their purposes. All standard Rogue archetypes are available.

## Paladin

Paladins in Talumh serve one of four divine patrons. The Shining Path does not produce Paladins — its power is entirely internal to the practitioner — but the other three major paths (Path of Light, Lord/Lady covenant, Path of Man) each produce champions who take oaths in service of something larger than themselves.

| Patron | Suggested Oath |
|---|---|
| **The Creator** (Path of Light) | Oath of Devotion |
| **The Lord** | Oath of the Ancients |
| **The Lady** | Oath of Glory |
| **Path of Man** | **Oath of Man** (custom — see below) |

Paladins of the Path of Man are the organization's militant vanguard. They are genuinely devoted to human self-determination in the way that the most dangerous believers are devoted to anything — not as propaganda, but as the organizing principle of their lives.

### Oath of Man
*Paladin subclass — Path of Man*

The Oath of Man is sworn by Paladins who have fully committed themselves to the Path of Man's vision of human ascendancy. They are not the political operators who manage the Path's community infiltrations, and they are not the scholars who write the Path's theology. They are the ones who fight. The ones who go first through doors. The ones who enforce what the Path's leaders have decided.

Most Oath of Man Paladins believe their power comes from their own conviction — from the righteous force of human will expressing itself through dedicated practice. Some have noticed that the source of their divine power behaves in ways that do not quite match this account. Very few have pursued that observation to its conclusion.

**Tenets of the Oath of Man**

*Humanity First.* The interests of humankind take precedence over the interests of all other peoples.

*No Masters.* No spirit, god, or fey has the right to bind humanity's fate. Reject all pacts made without humanity's consent.

*The Strong Lead.* Weakness invites domination. Protect the community and punish those who would undermine it.

*The Work Completes Itself.* The ends justify the means when the cause is humanity's freedom.

**Oath Spells**

You always have these spells prepared. They do not count against the number of spells you can prepare each day.

| Paladin Level | Spells |
|---|---|
| 3rd | *command*, *heroism* |
| 5th | *hold person*, *zone of truth* |
| 9th | *fear*, *remove curse* |
| 13th | *compulsion*, *locate creature* |
| 17th | *dominate person*, *geas* |

**Channel Divinity: Rally the Faithful (3rd level)**

As an action, you present your holy symbol and invoke the strength of human will. Each humanoid creature of your choice within 30 feet that can hear you gains temporary hit points equal to your Charisma modifier + your paladin level. Non-humanoid creatures within the same area must succeed on a Wisdom saving throw (DC = 8 + your proficiency bonus + your Charisma modifier) or be frightened of you until the end of your next turn.

**Channel Divinity: Break the Pact (3rd level)**

As an action, you target one creature within 30 feet that is under a magical compulsion, charm, or supernatural obligation — including warlock pacts, *geas*, or similar effects. - You have advantage on all attack rolls against non-humanoid creatures.

## Sorcerer

Sorcerers in Talumh practice Sorcery — the natural-world magic that flows through bloodlines with deep proximity to the fey world or the land spirits. It is less structured than Void Magic and less contractual than the Summoner's spirit pacts. It is simply *there*, in certain bloodlines, sometimes skipping generations, expressing itself when the conditions are right.

Wild Magic is particularly common among Wildermen and in communities where Tuthá proximity has been sustained over many generations. Draconic Bloodline is uncommon but not unheard of — the deep spirits of Talumh take forms that sometimes influence bloodlines in ways that produce what elsewhere would be called draconic traits.

The Witch subclass is specific to those called by the Lord or Lady.

All standard Sorcerer origins are available. The Witch is the custom subclass for this setting.

---

### Witch
*Sorcerer subclass — Lord/Lady Pact*

A Witch is not trained. A Witch is chosen.

The Lord and Lady select people for reasons that are not fully explained and cannot be reliably predicted — individuals touched by fey blood or fey circumstance, at some moment when the courts have need of a presence in the mortal world. The choosing happens in dreams. You know it is happening because the dreams have a quality that ordinary dreams lack: they are coherent, they build on each other, and they are asking you something.

The Path of Man persecutes Witches. This is official policy. A known Witch in Path of Man territory is in danger. Most Witches in the current period have learned to be private about what they are.

There are two courts whose choosing matters: the **Radiant Court**, under Queen Glaréad, associated with the Lord; and the **Glimmering Court**, under King Dwynnan, associated with the Lady. The court that chooses you shapes the flavor of your practice — Radiant Court Witches tend toward protection, healing, and the deep magic of earth and fixed things; Glimmering Court Witches tend toward change, movement, and the wild edge of fey power. These are flavor distinctions, not mechanical ones. The DM will tell you which court has called your character. If you have not yet begun play, discuss it during character creation.

**The Calling (1st level)**

You were chosen in a dream. You know the *druidcraft* cantrip, and it does not count against your cantrips known. Additionally, you can communicate telepathically with any Tuthá creature that is willing to receive your thoughts, at a range of 60 feet.

**Dream Walk (1st level)**

During a long rest, you can enter a Dream Quest — a waking vision sent by the Lord or Lady. When you do, you can ask one question. The DM answers honestly but in the oblique language of the courts: not lies, but rarely direct. You must complete the long rest to regain spell slots; the Dream Quest does not extend the rest.

You can use this feature once per long rest.

**Invoke Power (6th level)**

You gain a pool of Invoke Power uses equal to half your Sorcerer level (rounded down, minimum 3). As a bonus action, you can spend one use to activate one of the following effects:

- *Spirit Shield:* You or one ally within 30 feet gains resistance to one damage type of your choice until the start of your next turn.
- *Fey Step:* You teleport up to 30 feet to an unoccupied space you can see.
- *Dream Touch:* One creature you can see within 60 feet must succeed on a Wisdom saving throw against your spell save DC or be incapacitated until the start of your next turn.

You regain all expended uses when you finish a long rest.

**Ritual Magic (6th level)**

You learn two Witch rituals from the list below. Rituals require 10 minutes to perform and specific material components (DM provides details) but do not expend spell slots. You learn one additional ritual at 14th level.

- *Warding Circle* — As *protection from evil and good*, but affecting a 10-foot-radius area around the casting point for 1 hour.
- *Second Sight* — As *augury*, but the answer is more specific: a brief vision rather than a one-word omen.
- *Call to Nature* — As *animal messenger*, but the messenger can be any Small or smaller beast in the immediate area.
- *Dream Binding* — A willing or restrained creature falls into a light trance. While in the trance (up to 1 hour), the creature cannot speak falsehood, as *zone of truth*. The creature is aware of the effect.

**Fey Resilience (14th level)**

You have advantage on saving throws against spells and magical effects created by non-fey sources. Additionally, when a creature targets you with a charm or fear effect, you can use your reaction to redirect that effect: the creature that cast it must make the same saving throw against its own effect.

**Walking Between (18th level)**

Once per long rest, you can cast *plane shift* targeting the Feywild or the Ethereal Plane without expending a spell slot. You may bring up to six willing creatures with you.

While you are in the Feywild, you have advantage on all Charisma-based checks when interacting with Tuthá creatures.

## Warlock

Warlocks in Talumh draw power from pacts with entities larger than themselves. The standard Warlock patrons — Archfey, Fiend, Great Old One — are technically available, but they are uncommon in Talumh and carry their own complications in a world where fey relationships and spirit relationships are politically charged. Most Warlocks in Talumh are Summoners: practitioners who have entered into pacts with the great spirits of the land itself.

The Summoner subclass is the custom subclass for this setting. All standard Warlock patron options are also available for players who prefer them, with DM guidance on how those patrons exist in Talumh's cosmology.

---

### Summoner
*Warlock subclass — Great Spirit Pacts*

Summoners do not serve a single patron. They accumulate relationships.

The great spirits of Talumh — ancient entities bound to specific places, watersheds, forests, and crossroads — are not gods in the Path of Light sense. They do not want worship. They want relationships: ongoing, negotiated, maintained through ritual and personal presence. The Summoner is the practitioner who has entered into these relationships formally, who carries the pacts in their memory and their body, and who can call on what they have agreed to in exchange for what they offer.

The spirit pact system is different from the standard Warlock relationship in one important way: you do not serve the spirit. You are in covenant with it. The spirit has obligations to you as surely as you have obligations to it. This distinction matters to the spirits, and Summoners who treat their pacts as unilateral service relationships find their agreements deteriorate.

The spirits available for pacts are listed in the Summoner Spirit appendix. Each spirit has a manifestation site — a specific location where the pact must be negotiated in person — and each spirit grants a combination of spell access, passive power, and active power.

**Pact Magic**

Summoners use standard Warlock Pact Magic: spell slots recovered on a short rest, limited slots upcast to maximum available level, spell slots shared across all active spirit pacts.

**First Pact (1st level)**

You form your initial spirit pact. Choose one spirit from the Summoner Spirit List (Appendix). Working with your DM, establish the in-fiction context of this pact — when you negotiated it, what you offered, what the spirit requires of you ongoing.

You gain:
- The spirit's **Passive Power**, which is always active as long as the pact holds.
- The spirit's **Pact Spells**, which are added to your spells known and do not count against your spells known limit.
- The ability to cast the spirit's **Active Power** once per short rest without expending a spell slot. At higher levels, you may also activate it by expending a Warlock spell slot.

**Spirit Sense (1st level)**

You always know the direction and approximate distance to each of your pacted spirits' manifestation sites, even across great distances or from within structures. You can cast *detect magic* at will without expending a spell slot, but only to detect magic associated with spirits and fey entities.

**Second Pact (6th level)**

You are ready to negotiate a second spirit pact. When you reach 6th level, work with your DM to arrange an in-fiction approach to a second spirit's manifestation site. When the negotiation occurs, make a Charisma (Persuasion) check:

- **DC 15:** You gain the spirit's Passive Power, Pact Spells, and Active Power.
- **Failure:** You may try again after one week. The DM may modify the DC based on your actions, reputation, and what you offer.

**Third Pact (10th level)**

As Second Pact, with a third spirit. The base DC is 18.

**Spirit Mastery (14th level)**

Your connection to all pacted spirits has deepened past the terms of the original agreements. Each spirit's Active Power can now be used twice per short rest without expending a spell slot.

Once per long rest, you can call upon all of your pacted spirits simultaneously. For 1 minute, each spirit's Passive Power extends to all allies within 30 feet of you.

*The full Summoner Spirit appendix — including all spirits available for pacts, their manifestation sites, their Passive Powers, Pact Spells, and Active Powers — will be provided as a supplement.*

## Wizard

Wizards in Talumh are Void Practitioners. The tradition of academic magic that produces standard D&D Wizards does not meaningfully exist here — what exists instead is the 32 Runes, a system of magical patterns that express the deep structure of the dream, gifted to humanity from outside the Mist and practiced with rigid care ever since.

There are two exceptions. Gnome illusionists sometimes develop a magical tradition that draws on Sorcery rather than the Void — the School of Illusion is available to Gnome characters who want to play this way. And scholars who have studied magical theory without practicing it sometimes develop capabilities that look like standard wizard traditions. These cases are unusual, and players who want to pursue them should discuss it with their DM.

For everyone else: if you are a Wizard in Talumh, you are a Void Practitioner.

---

### Void Practitioner (Arcane Tradition)
*Wizard subclass — Void Magic / The 32 Runes*

The 32 Runes are the foundation of Void Magic. They are not spells in themselves — they are patterns, each one expressing a fundamental principle of how reality can be negated, reshaped, or commanded. A Void Practitioner learns to work with these patterns, to channel raw Void energy through them, to produce effects that feel different from other kinds of magic to anyone who knows what to look for. Void Magic has a quality: a slight darkening at the edges, a sense of something being drawn away rather than generated. Experienced practitioners learn not to notice it. Everyone else eventually does.

The Runes are divided into the **15 Greater Runes**, which govern broad domains, and the **17 Lesser Runes**, which express more specific applications within those domains. A Void Practitioner's training centers on one Greater Rune — their Focus — and branches outward from there.

**The Risk** is not a myth. The Void is outside the dream. Every time a Void Practitioner draws power from beyond the Mist, there is a chance the Void draws something back. Experienced Practitioners minimize this chance. They do not eliminate it.

**The Void Scar** is the permanent mark of practice. It appears when a Practitioner first channels Void Magic — a dark marking, the shape of their Focus Rune, somewhere on their skin. It glows faintly when they cast. It cannot be removed by mundane means, though magical disguise covers it. It is recognized by anyone who knows what Void Magic looks like. This currently includes the inner circle of the Path of Man, several Shining Path scholars, and most experienced Tuthá.

**The 32 Runes**

*Greater Runes (15) — each governs a domain and maps to a cluster of spells:*

| Greater Rune | Domain | Associated Spell Cluster |
|---|---|---|
| Rune of Air | Wind, sky, breath | *gust of wind*, *fly*, *wind wall*, *control weather* |
| Rune of Arcana | Raw magic, dispelling | *detect magic*, *dispel magic*, *antimagic field*, *Nystul's magic aura* |
| Rune of Calling | Summoning, binding | *find familiar*, *conjure elemental*, *planar binding*, *gate* |
| Rune of Compulsion | Domination, charm | *charm person*, *hold person*, *dominate person*, *geas* |
| Rune of Correspondence | Communication, divination | *message*, *sending*, *clairvoyance*, *telepathy* |
| Rune of Delusion | Illusion, unreality | *silent image*, *mirror image*, *major image*, *programmed illusion* |
| Rune of Enervation | Drain, weakening | *ray of enfeeblement*, *bestow curse*, *blight*, *enervation* |
| Rune of Fear | Terror, despair | *cause fear*, *fear*, *phantasmal killer*, *weird* |
| Rune of Flame | Fire, destruction | *burning hands*, *scorching ray*, *fireball*, *wall of fire* |
| Rune of Making | Creation, alteration | *mending*, *fabricate*, *creation*, *wish* (limited application) |
| Rune of Radiance | Light, truth | *faerie fire*, *daylight*, *sunbeam*, *sunburst* |
| Rune of Resistance | Protection, warding | *mage armor*, *shield*, *wall of force*, *globe of invulnerability* |
| Rune of Transformation | Polymorph, change | *alter self*, *polymorph*, *true polymorph*, *shapechange* |
| Rune of Veiling | Invisibility, concealment | *invisibility*, *pass without trace*, *nondetection*, *mind blank* |
| Rune of Warding | Barriers, locks | *alarm*, *arcane lock*, *Leomund's tiny hut*, *forcecage* |

*Lesser Runes (17) — more specific applications, listed in the full Void Practitioner appendix.*

**Void Attunement (2nd level)**

You gain access to Void Magic. Choose one Greater Rune as your **Rune Focus**. The cluster of spells associated with your Focus Rune are always in your spellbook. When you cast a spell from your Focus Rune's cluster using a spell slot, you may use a slot one level lower than the spell's normal minimum (minimum 1st level).

**The Risk (2nd level)**

When you cast any wizard spell, roll a d20 before the spell resolves:

- **Natural 1 — The Void Speaks:** The spell still resolves, but roll on the Void Mishap Table (provided by your DM). Something comes back through the channel you opened.
- **2–4 — The Cost:** The spell resolves normally, but you gain one level of exhaustion. This exhaustion is removed when you finish a short or long rest.
- **5 or higher — Clear Passage:** The spell resolves normally. No cost.

**The Void Scar (2nd level)**

A dark marking appears on your skin — the shape of your Focus Rune. It glows faintly when you cast. It cannot be concealed by mundane means, though spells like *disguise self* cover it for their duration.

**Rune Mastery (6th level)**

Your Focus Rune's spells have become deeply familiar. When rolling the Risk die for a spell from your Focus Rune's cluster, the thresholds change: **1 = The Void Speaks, 2 = The Cost, 3 or higher = Clear Passage.** This does not affect other spells.

**Void Resilience (10th level)**

You no longer gain exhaustion from the Cost result on any Risk roll. When you roll a 2–4, you lose your reaction until the start of your next turn instead.

**Mastery of Negation (14th level)**

Once per long rest, you can cast any spell from your Focus Rune's cluster without triggering the Risk roll — the result is automatically Clear Passage.

Additionally, when you roll a Natural 1 on the Risk die, you can choose to reroll. If the reroll is also a Natural 1, the Void Mishap is doubled in severity (DM determines the specific escalation).

## Scholar of the Runes

You have spent time studying the 32 Runes of Void Magic. You may or may not be able to cast them — many Rune scholars are historians, not practitioners — but you know what they are, what they do, and what it looks like when someone has been using them. This knowledge puts you at the intersection of two worlds that are currently in conflict: the academic tradition that has preserved Rune knowledge, and the forces that have decided Rune research is dangerous.

**Skill Proficiencies:** Arcana, History

**Tool Proficiencies:** Calligrapher's supplies

**Languages:** One of your choice

**Equipment:** A set of calligrapher's supplies, a journal filled with annotated copies of Rune transcriptions (mundane, not magical), a reference book on the history of Void Magic, a set of common clothes, and a pouch containing 10 gp.

**Feature — Rune Lore**

You can identify Rune-based magical effects by sight, recognize the Void Scar on a Void Practitioner and identify which Focus Rune it indicates, and recall historical information about specific Runes with a DC 10 Intelligence (Arcana) check. You are known in scholarly circles and can find temporary lodging and access to reference materials at libraries, archives, and scholars' guildhalls. People who study Runes — a small, often secretive community — are more likely to speak with you openly than with an unknown stranger.

**Personality Traits (d6)**

1. I cannot resist a gap in the historical record. I will find the missing piece.
2. I speak carefully, because imprecision in language leads to imprecision in thought.
3. I find the Void fascinating in a way I cannot fully explain and that I recognize is not entirely comfortable.
4. I treat everyone as a potential source of information until proven otherwise.
5. I have a specific thesis about the 33rd Rune that I am not prepared to discuss in detail yet.
6. I have spent so much time alone with documents that I sometimes forget other people are not primarily sources of data.

**Ideals (d6)**

1. *Knowledge:* The truth about the Runes exists and should be known. (Neutral)
2. *Caution:* Some knowledge is dangerous. The question is who gets to decide. (Lawful)
3. *Preservation:* Everything that has been learned should be protected from those who would destroy it. (Good)
4. *Power:* Understanding the Runes is understanding power. I intend to understand it completely. (Any)
5. *Memory:* The history of what the Runes cost is as important as the history of what they can do. (Any)
6. *Freedom:* Knowledge belongs to no faction. I will not allow it to be controlled. (Chaotic)

**Bonds (d6)**

1. My mentor was the most thorough Rune scholar of their generation. They have disappeared. I intend to find them.
2. I made a promise to a colleague who died that their research would be published. I intend to keep it.
3. A particular Rune appears in my dreams in ways that feel like it is trying to tell me something.
4. I owe my education to a patron who I am no longer certain I trust.
5. There is one Rune I have never been able to find adequate documentation for. I believe it has been deliberately erased from the record.
6. I witnessed what happened when a Void Mishap escalated. I have been trying to understand why ever since.

**Flaws (d6)**

1. I become obsessive about incomplete data to the point of ignoring everything else.
2. I have a tendency to share what I know with people who probably shouldn't have it.
3. I underestimate physical danger because I have spent most of my life among books.
4. I am deeply competitive with other scholars in ways I do not always acknowledge.
5. I am convinced I am close to a breakthrough. I may be wrong, and I am not willing to consider that seriously.
6. I find people who don't care about intellectual rigor almost physically uncomfortable to be around.

## Path Disciple

You are a committed member of one of Talumh's religious paths. Not a casual worshipper — a disciple: someone who has formally joined the organization, completed the entry rites, and made the path's principles part of how you understand yourself. The path gave you community, purpose, and a framework for understanding the world. What it is asking of you now — and what you are willing to give — is something you may still be working out.

Choose one path when you take this background: **Path of Light**, **Path of Man**, or **Shining Path**.

**Skill Proficiencies:** Religion, Persuasion

**Languages:** Two of your choice

**Equipment:** A holy symbol or path emblem, a copy of the core texts of your path, a set of traveler's clothes, and a pouch containing 15 gp.

**Feature — The Path's Network**

You are a recognized member of your chosen path. You can find shelter, information, and basic supplies at any temple, shrine, chapter house, or formal gathering of your path in any of the twelve communities. Fellow disciples will assist you in small ways without expecting payment. *Note: Path of Man disciples may face hostility in communities where that path is actively resisted.*

**A note on the Path of Man:** Player characters who are Path of Man Disciples are playing people who genuinely believe in the Path's public principles — human self-determination, freedom from spirit dominance, the rights of humanity to govern its own fate. These are not inherently evil beliefs, and the Path of Man contains people who hold them in good faith. What those people do not yet know, when they enter the adventure, is everything else.

**Personality Traits, Ideals, Bonds, and Flaws:** Work with your DM to develop characteristics appropriate to your specific path. A Shining Path disciple and a Path of Man disciple will approach the world from very different angles.

## Community Born

You are from one of the twelve communities, and that community is the most fundamental fact about your identity. Not the community's current politics — not the Path of Man's influence in Window, or the Elder council's debates in Shade — but the community itself: the streets you grew up on, the festivals you attended, the people who taught you things without meaning to. Home is home.

Choose one of the twelve communities as your home when you take this background.

**Skill Proficiencies:** Insight, and one of your choice from the following: Athletics, Acrobatics, Survival, or Stealth

**Tool Proficiencies:** One type of artisan's tools (appropriate to your community's primary industry)

**Equipment:** One set of artisan's tools, a set of common clothes, a memento from your community (a coin, a small carving, a dried flower from the last festival — whatever your character would have kept), and a pouch containing 10 gp.

**Feature — Of This Place**

You know your home community the way you know your own name: the layout, the back ways, the faces, the who-knows-who. When you return to your home community or interact with its citizens elsewhere, you have advantage on Charisma (Persuasion) checks. Once per adventure arc, you can call on a contact from your home community for help — temporary shelter, information, a rumor, or the name of someone worth talking to. The DM determines who this contact is and what they can provide.

**Personality Traits (d6)**

1. I have strong opinions about how things are done, most of which are based on how they're done at home.
2. I measure new places by how they compare to home. Nothing is quite right.
3. I am genuinely curious about how other communities manage things differently.
4. I have a loyalty to the people I grew up with that does not require them to deserve it.
5. I have been waiting my whole life for an excuse to see more of the world.
6. I am very good at pretending I belong somewhere I don't.

**Ideals, Bonds, and Flaws:** Work with your DM based on your specific home community. A Wheel native and a Shade native have different relationships to authority, outsiders, and change.

# THE PATHS

## Religion in Talumh

Talumh's religious life is organized around four paths — each with its own doctrine, organization, and relationship to the actual sources of divine power in the world. Characters in this adventure will encounter all four paths in significant ways.

What follows is what your character knows. It is not everything there is to know.

---

### The Path of Light (Spiritualism)

The Path of Light is the oldest human religious tradition in Talumh. It centers on the **Creator** — a divine figure believed to have brought humanity to Talumh from a dying world, out of love for a people who deserved a chance at life. Path of Light theology teaches that Talumh is a gift: the world is good, the Creator watches over it, and the proper response to this is gratitude expressed through community, festival, and generous living.

Path of Light practice is organized around a calendar of festivals — most communities with Path of Light presence celebrate a dozen or more formal occasions annually, from the water-blessings that mark the beginning of the growing season to the remembrance ceremonies for those who died in the year just ended. The festivals are communal in a way that reflects the Path's core theology: nobody celebrates alone. The Path of Light's temples are the social centers of the communities that host them.

Fountain is the most notable Path of Light stronghold among the adventure communities. The great spring festivals there draw visitors from client towns Grove and Portal and from communities as far as Vista.

*What your character knows:* The Path of Light is a warm, community-oriented tradition. Its clergy are generally respected. It has been present in Talumh for as long as human memory goes back.

*What your character may have noticed:* The Path of Light's influence in some communities has been declining. People who used to attend festivals publicly no longer do. Nobody explains why.

---

### The Path of Man

The Path of Man is the most politically significant religious development in Talumh in the last generation. It did not exist two hundred years ago. It emerged within the last fifty years from a philosophical tradition centered on a principle: that humanity has the right to govern itself, without obligation to spirits, fey patrons, or the Lord and Lady's old arrangements.

The Path of Man argues that the Settlement was not a gift or a protection — it was a containment. That the Lord and Lady's covenant with the Tuthá has always prioritized fey interests over human ones. That the twelve communities' separation is not a product of wisdom but of the same subordination that cost humanity its freedom before, and will cost it again if humans do not choose differently.

The Path of Man is currently the fastest-growing religious organization in Talumh. It controls Window. It has significant influence in Wheel and is actively courting Shade's Elder council. It maintains chapter houses in communities where it does not yet hold power, including Fountain.

Path of Man clergy have a martial character. Their Paladins (who take the Oath of Man) are visibly organized in ways that the more pastoral clergy of the Path of Light are not.

*What your character knows:* The Path of Man is a human-first religious movement with a coherent political program. Its followers include people who genuinely believe in human self-determination and people who have found in the Path a useful framework for personal or political ambition. It is organized, growing, and making enemies.

*What your character may have noticed:* The Path of Man's expansion has been unusually rapid. It has resources that do not obviously correspond to the donations of its membership. People who resist its expansion sometimes disappear.

---

### The Shining Path

The Shining Path is the oldest indigenous institution in Talumh — predating the Settlement, predating the Great War, maintained by an unbroken line of practitioners who have passed its disciplines from teacher to student for as long as anyone has been keeping track. It holds no god, claims no patron, and makes no theological assertions about the nature of the divine. What it does claim is that perfection is achievable through discipline, and that the work of achieving it is both the purpose and the reward.

Shining Path monasteries are found in most of the six regions. They are small, self-contained, and tend to be located in places that are geographically inconvenient to reach — on ridgelines, in canyon alcoves, at the far end of passes that require effort to cross. This is not accidental. The Path believes that people who want its teaching badly enough to make the journey are the people worth teaching.

The Path accepts students from any background, any race, any community. It does not distinguish between its peoples. This makes it unusual in Talumh and, in the current political climate, increasingly valuable as a neutral ground.

*What your character knows:* The Shining Path is an ascetic monastic tradition with a very long history, genuine martial capability, and a principled refusal to take sides in political conflicts. Its practitioners are respected across communities and paths.

*What your character may have noticed:* The Shining Path has been very quiet about the Path of Man's expansion. This may be neutrality. It may be something else.

---

### The Greensingers

The Greensingers are not, technically, a religious path in the way the others are — they do not organize around doctrine, they do not proselytize, and they do not recruit in the way that the Path of Light or Path of Man recruit. They are a druidic sect: practitioners of a specific tradition of Elemental Magic who have organized themselves around the maintenance of the elemental pacts.

They are included here because their presence is felt throughout Talumh and because their practice has religious weight even if they would not use that word. The Greensingers engage with the spirit world as directly as any religious organization in Talumh, and their understanding of that world's nature is, in many respects, more accurate than the theologies the paths have built.

The Path of Man regards the Greensingers with the particular hostility it reserves for any human institution that maintains ongoing relationships with the spirit world. This has not yet become a direct confrontation. The Greensingers are aware of the Path of Man's position and have made no public statement about it.

*What your character knows:* The Greensingers maintain the old pacts with the land spirits. They are distributed through all six regions. They are not aligned with any of the formal paths.

# CHARACTER HOOKS

The adventure begins in Fountain, in the circle of people who know Priya the Wanderer. The hooks below suggest how your character might fit into that circle. Work with your DM to place yourself.

**1. The Adopted Child**
Priya took you in when you had nowhere else to be — as a child, as a young adult, during a crisis, at a moment when her door was open and you walked through it. You are not her research assistant. You are closer than that: a member of the family she made over twenty years of collected people. Her disappearance is not an abstract problem. It is a wound.

**2. The Field Agent**
Priya's research does not happen only in libraries. She has a network of people who go to places, find things, bring information back. You are one of these people. You have traveled roads that most of her scholars will never see, and you have a particular kind of knowledge — practical, geographic, relational — that her academic circle values but cannot replicate. You were due to report back to her when you arrived to find her gone.

**3. The Fellow Scholar**
You work in the same tradition as Priya — Rune scholarship, history of the Void, the mapping of what was known and lost after the Great War. You may have disagreed with her on specifics (scholars always do), but the work was shared. When she went missing, what she had been researching recently was the first thing you tried to reconstruct. What you found in her notes was not what you expected.

**4. The Skeptic**
You do not fully trust Priya's circle, or you did not when you arrived. You came for your own reasons — a debt, a contract, a piece of information that someone in the circle has and you need. What happened when you arrived has complicated your reasons. You are now involved in something larger than what you came for.

**5. The Path Crosser**
You are a member of one of the paths — Path of Light, Path of Man, or Shining Path — and your path has brought you into contact with Priya's circle. How that contact developed, and what your current relationship with your path is, will depend on which path you belong to and what you have learned since being involved with the circle.

**6. The Witness**
You saw something. Not the whole picture — you don't know what you saw yet — but you were in Fountain during the days before Priya disappeared, and something you observed has been sitting wrong in your memory. You came to tell her about it and found she was already gone. Now you are the person who knows something that nobody else knows, and you are not yet sure who to tell.
