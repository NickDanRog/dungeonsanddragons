# HUMAN ETHNITICES IN ARTAXARTA

## The Artaxartoi

Perhaps the most changed of cultures since their genesis, the Artaxartoi nonetheless retain a powerful sense of cultural identity, deeply rooted in their unique heritage. Ancient tribes which dominated the mountainous lands of what now makes up modern Artaxarta were considered savage and culturally backward. They were feared and shunned by many of their neighbors, and strongly resisted any attempts at conquest or trade in their lands. An invasion of Delians during the Xoshan imperial period brought waves of soldiers and colonists from the lake states to Artaxarta however, and under the famous Xoshan general and statesman Alcias, these peoples began to mix into a new culture that took on the best aspects of both its forbearers. Bandorian Imperial conquest added further to this mix of traditions, and has made modern Artaxarta into one of the few successful fusion cultures in history. This culture is prevalent in the country that bears its name, though some of its neighbors have been strongly influenced by it as well, especially nearby Morna, whose people are closely related to the ancient Artaxartoi racially. 
Artaxartoi are of slightly larger than average size and height, and exhibit a mix of hair and eye colors and skin tones. People living in the great cities situated in the many valleys of the country tend to have lighter coloration, whereas those living in the more remote regions tend to be darker as their bloodlines have been less mixed since ancient times. Whatever their stripe, Artaxartoi are very conscious of their shared culture and practice their traditions in both cities palaces and mountain huts. Artaxartoi put a particular emphasis on their extended clans and on their tribes, to which each person is a member. 
Equality and merit are the two main driving principles of Artaxartoi culture. Every person, no matter their station, is considered an important member of society. This only holds as long as a person does their share however – those who refuse to do what is required of them will quickly find themselves shut out of society. The Artaxartoi make no differentiations between men and women, or between class, and people. As long as a person can prove their ability, they will be venerated by their peers, regardless of their gender or station. Artaxartoi do believe in nobility, typically the descendants of famous ancestors, but if anything these people need to work twice as hard to prove their worth to their higher station. 
Artaxartoi art is a mix of the many traditions from which this culture was fused, but also retains a unique style of its own with an emphasis on strong, powerful imagery, featuring chiefly animal forms. Their artistic sense is strongly influenced by their ancient religious practices which feature a dualist pantheon locked in a struggle of good versus evil. These traditions retain a strong impact on modern Artaxartoi culture, though it has been well folded into the official state religion of the Empire over the years.

## Regional Peoples
These are people native to the region near Artaxarta. People of these ethnicities are either native to the region or are recent immigrants - most people who spend a more than a few generations here tend to assimilate into Artaxartoi culture, something it excels at. 


### Astani
Descended from the native horse riders of the great Astani plain, the Astani are a proud and fierce people. In the current age they are prinarly found in the Kingdom of Asta and the Principate of Morna which border Artaxarta, and the much larger Grand Duchy of Asta further to the west, which encompasses great swaths of farms and grasslands ruled over by squabbling regional nobles. Astani are fiercely competitive in all aspects of their lives, finding joy in every victory and looking to revenge every slight or defeat. This has led to a strong tradition of dueling in Astani cultures. Physically they are smaller and slighter than average with pale skin and dark hair and eyes.

### Keshites
Peoples of the great planes and hill country found in the lowlands near the eastern ocean, the Keshites are a distinct ethnicity from the other peoples of the old Empire's east. They practice a varied religious tradition that worships dozens of distinct deities which the Empire attempted to syncretize with the traditional deities of the Twelve Divines with limited success. Keshite cultural practices go back over 2,000 years to the time of the prophet Re'inam Mulistinabi. Keshite culture centers around duty to family, clan, and kingdom in that order. They practice ritual bonding of their children to cement alliances which evolve into marriages when the children reach their majority. Unmarried people and those without a clan to support them are consdiered an underclass in Keshite society. Keshites are smaller and slimmer than average, with dark brown skin and light or dark eyes.

### Albani
The great "middle people" who settled the lands between the Delians and the pre-Imperial Bandorians, the Albani are descendants of of Delian settlers who migrated to the western and central Eutora River valley region. Here they formed a unique culture all their own. The great Albani city-states of the region warred extensively with other groups in the region including the Bandrorians, the Ashtari, and most especially themselves. Several of their most powerful cities rose to control the region through a combination of arms and political and economic influence. Far into the Imperial era and afterward these cities held a strong grip on power in the central regions of the Empire. Similar to Delians physically, Albani have a lighter skin tone with dark to light hair and light eyes. Red hair is considered lucky among them. Albani culture is meritocratic and centered around civic participation.

### Bandorians
Modern Bandorians are descended from Albani peoples who conquered and mixed with the tribes of the central and western Eutoras River valley, from whom they take their name. The Durasi-Bandorians would spread their culture to much of the known world at the height of their great Empire. Driven by ambition, tradition, and inventiveness, the Durasi-Bandorians are at once both a curious  and a stubborn people who have a deep-seated love of knowledge for its own sake. Conditioned toward  perseverance, they are celebrated for their ability to snatch victory from the jaws of defeat. Bandorians are similar in appearance to their Albani forbearers - they are of medium height and build with light skin and dark hair and eyes.

### Delians
One of the most populous ethnic groups in the old Empire, the Delians migrated from the legendary destruction of their homeland in the eastern seas over 2 millenia ago. Settling heavily in the Lake Uriel region in the east-central region of the continent, and their ancient culture has influenced many that have come after it, particularly the continent spanning Durasi-Bandorian Empire. A primary example is their traditional religous practices, which stablized over time into the Temple of the Twelve Divines, the offical Imperial state religion. The Delians have a particular love for invention, artistry, and philosiphy which underlies their culture practices. The Delians underwent a major cultural split soon after their settlement in the lake region between the eastern and western branches of their culture. Eastern Delians tend to embrace change and innovation, and have more democratic centered forms of governemnt. Artaxarta was founded in its modern form by the Eastern Delian general Alcias, who hailed from Xosha, one of their most prominent city-states. Western Delians tend to be more traditional, and to favor more autocratic or aristocratice governments, though most retain at least some form of democracy. Arataxarta's northweestern neighbor Kyrexos is a Western Delian state, led by a prince elected by the people from among a group of noble houses. Delians tend to be of average height and build with an olive complexion, light to dark hair, and light eyes.
