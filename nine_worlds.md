# The Nine Worlds

## Cosmology

The Nine Worlds exist as interlinked pocket dimensions contained together as a single realm within the Ethereal Plane. Each world is a self-contained domain roughly the size of a small continent, possessed of its own terrain, climate, and character. At the edges of each, the land fades into the featureless mists of the Ethereal, marking the boundary between the constructed and the formless. Though separate in form, the worlds were built as a unified system, and the connections between them are as fundamental to their nature as the land and sky within each one.

These connections are maintained by the Rainbow Road, a network of interplanar pathways linking each world to a central hub called Nexus. The Road manifests physically on Nexus as a great circular highway of rainbow-colored tiles connecting eight gate towns, each situated near the edge of the realm at the terminus of a pathway to one of the outer worlds. A ninth spoke of road runs inward from each gate town to the city of Nexus at the continent's center. In former times, the Road carried travelers, goods, and ideas freely between the worlds, and the gate towns that served as its termini were thriving centers of commerce and culture. The Road has been closed since the apocalypse, and the gate towns stand abandoned.

Above the Nine Worlds moves Heaven, a vast artificial sphere of clockwork and magitek construction that serves as both the source of light for the realms and the dwelling place of the celestial beings who created them. Heaven passes over each world in sequence, providing the equivalent of day and night to each domain as it moves. It is inhabited by three races of celestials: the Archons, the Angels, and the Agathions. These beings are not true gods in the conventional sense, but their collective power is such that when worshiped together as the Host of Heaven, they grant divine abilities to their followers equal to those bestowed by any deity. The practice of venerating the Host goes by different names on each of the Nine Worlds, though its character is broadly the same everywhere. In former times it was the dominant faith across most of the realms. It has since fallen away in most places, weakened by the waning of celestial power after the apocalypse, the general decline of civilization, the influence of the Warlocks, and a widespread belief among the people that the gods have abandoned them.

Beneath the worlds, separated from them by layers of planar boundary, lies the Underworld. This place was not part of the original design. When the three celestial races were constructing the Nine Worlds, creatures of hate and ruin — exiles from Hell who had learned of the project — infiltrated the work while it was still in progress. The inherent goodness of the mortal populations rejected these entities upon the worlds' completion, and the celestials responded by forming a prison to contain them: the Underworld. It was not, however, a perfect solution. Unknown to the gods, the intruders had already touched the fabric of the Nine Worlds during their period of infiltration, and while they could not escape their prison, this lingering contact allowed them to influence mortalkind across the realms in a manner uncomfortably similar to the gods themselves. The people of the Nine Worlds came to call these imprisoned entities demons, though they are technically a form of being known as Asuras. New demons are formed whenever a mortal who has been willingly corrupted by their influence dies, their soul descending to the Underworld rather than passing on to a chosen afterlife. Demons have plagued the Nine Worlds throughout their history, sponsoring cults, fomenting wars, and corrupting individuals whenever the opportunity presents itself. Many of the great mythic heroes of ages past built their reputations fighting this influence.

## The Purpose of the Nine Worlds

The Nine Worlds and the Rainbow Road were created for a very specific purpose, now forgotten by all save the Warlocks who once walked it. The entire system was designed as a proving ground: a mechanism to identify worthy mortal champions of good and imbue them with mythic power sufficient to fight battles against evil across the multiverse. In ages past, those thought worthy would seek out the Nexian Oracle for confirmation of their calling, then travel to the Great Ancillary — an ancient temple at the very center of Nexus — to receive their first portion of mythic power. From there they would walk the Rainbow Road, visiting the places of power on each of the outer worlds to gain further strength, proving themselves through heroic acts along the way. The legends that the people of the Nine Worlds grew up hearing about the ancient heroes are true, and the stories of those heroes all began in this place.

## The Apocalypse

The last group to walk the Rainbow Road numbered ten. They were the greatest of the heroes the Nine Worlds had produced in generations, drawn from across the realms and united by a shared purpose: to end the influence of the demons once and for all. They undertook the mythic path together, visiting each place of power and ascending to the height of mythic ability. Armed with this power, they constructed a magitek artifact designed to open a portal to the Underworld and then sever the connection between it and the Nine Worlds permanently, cutting off the source of demonic corruption at its root.

The plan failed. The portal, once opened, proved uncontrollable. Rather than allowing the heroes to strike at the Underworld's foundations in a controlled manner, it permitted the demons to physically enter the Nine Worlds for the first time in history. They poured through in numbers beyond reckoning, and what followed was a war that devastated every realm it touched. The demons ravaged the nine worlds before turning their fury against Heaven itself. Their assault on the celestial sphere ultimately failed, but at a cost beyond measure — over ninety-five percent of the demon population was destroyed in the fighting, the vast majority of them in the assault on Heaven, but the populations of the Nine Worlds and Heaven itself were left shattered. One of the ten heroes, a woman named Sarena Tal, died while casting the spell to open the portal.

When the war ended, the surviving nine heroes did what they could. They consolidated the scattered survivors of each world, worked to protect them from the environmental hazards and roaming bands of monsters left in the war's wake, and attempted to rebuild. Each took stewardship of one of the Nine Worlds. Their intentions were genuine, at least at the beginning. But the mythic power they carried had been corrupted by the catastrophe they had caused, and the weight of their failure — the millions dead, the civilizations ruined, the worlds scarred — wore at them in ways that no amount of power could resist. Over the centuries that followed, each of them devolved in their own way, their original virtues curdling into obsessions, cruelties, or madness. The people of the Nine Worlds came to know them not as heroes, but as the Warlocks — nine corrupted rulers whose grip on their respective domains is as absolute as it is ruinous.

The tenth hero did not escape corruption either. Sarena Tal, who had died casting the portal spell, was restored to a form of existence in the Underworld. Before her descent, she had merged her physical form with that of her Eidolon and was willingly granted a portion of the mythic power of each of the other nine heroes, creating a vast reservoir of strength intended to protect her as she took up the post of Warden of the Underworld. It did not protect her. Over the course of a century, the demons wore at the Eidolon's soul, creating an opening for corruption to seep through. She responded by turning on her charges, torturing them endlessly and consuming their soul essence to maintain her fading power, sinking deeper into corruption with each century. She is now known as Tornsoul, a being of pure vengeance and hate, and she has sworn to return to the Nine Worlds at the head of a demon army to destroy the realms and Heaven alike.

The Rainbow Road closed in the years following the war, overwhelmed by desperate traffic and structural damage sustained during the fighting. The gate towns emptied. The worlds were cut off from one another. Five centuries have passed since the apocalypse, and the Nine Worlds endure in isolation, each suffering under the particular burden its Warlock has imposed upon it.

## Nexus — The Heart of the Nine Worlds

Nexus is a small continent surrounded by the mists of the Ethereal Plane, temperate in climate and varied in terrain. It contains forests, plains, hills, and rivers in proportions that would be familiar to any traveler of the prime material worlds. At the center of the realm stands the great city of Nexus, the largest settlement in the Nine Worlds and the place from which all journeys along the Rainbow Road once began.

Near the edges of the continent, equidistant from one another, stand the eight gate towns. Each is built around the Rainbow Road terminus to one of the outer worlds, and the terrain surrounding each gate town subtly shifts to reflect the character of the realm it connects to — the approach to Auzra's terminus rises into rocky outcroppings swept by persistent winds, while the land near Diaspola's gate grows marshy and low, threaded with mist. These reflections are never dangerous or otherworldly in nature, merely evocative. A perfectly circular road of rainbow-colored tiles connects each gate town to those on either side, forming a great ring around the continent's perimeter. Those possessed of mythic power find their speed upon this road increased to an extraordinary degree, shortening what would otherwise be a journey of days to a matter of minutes. A third rainbow-tiled road extends inward from each gate town, connecting it to the city of Nexus along a straight spoke. The gate towns have stood empty since the apocalypse.

The city itself was once surrounded by a broad belt of farmlands and villages that supported its population. These were destroyed in the demon war and have not been rebuilt. The demons that poured into the Nine Worlds during the apocalypse struck Nexus first and hardest, and their remnants continued to trouble the realm long after the initial fighting ended. Under the leadership of Casomir Zukra, the hero who took stewardship of Nexus, the survivors organized themselves and fought off several major concentrations of minor demons over the course of several years. When the immediate threat subsided, Casomir's researchers determined that the demons were either reproducing or continuing to arrive in small numbers from the Underworld. They have continued to attack the city in the centuries since, though in smaller and more dispersed groups. The people of Nexus tore down two full districts of the city and converted them to farmland, an arrangement which has proven more than sufficient to feed the now much-reduced population. Magitek foundries that existed before the war, restored and maintained by Casomir, keep the populace well armed, and they generally have little trouble repelling demon raids.

In ancient times, Nexus also contained a number of portals to other planes throughout the multiverse, allowing travel and trade with the wider cosmos. All of these portals went dark during the apocalypse. The Nine Worlds are effectively severed from the rest of the planes until the Rainbow Road is fully restored.

### The People of Nexus

The primary race of Nexus is human, though the city's long history as the crossroads of the Nine Worlds has left it with a population of considerable diversity. Members of every race found on the other eight worlds live here as well, along with representatives of other races from across the planes who were trapped in the realm when the portals closed. Humans nonetheless make up the majority, and their customs and language form the baseline of the city's culture.

### Casomir Zukra and the Beast Blavin

Casomir Zukra was a human fighter and alchemist, a specialist in the use of magitek equipment, and the leader of the ten legendary heroes who walked the Rainbow Road for the last time. He returned to Nexus after the war to find the city under siege by remnants of the demon army. He rallied the defenders, organized the counterattack, and held the city together through the worst of the crisis. The people proclaimed him king in the aftermath, an office he did not seek but has tried to honor with as little ceremony as possible.

Casomir was well aware of the corruption affecting all of the former heroes and took proactive steps to deal with it through the use of alchemy. His efforts were partially successful, but carried an unwelcome side effect: while the corruption was contained, it manifested as a separate entity — a gigantic mutated beast that the people came to call Blavin. The transformation accumulates each time Casomir draws on his magical or mythic abilities. He takes elaborate precautions when he feels it coming on, retreating on supposed hunting trips or locking himself in a purpose-built prison beneath his citadel. The Beast, however, knows everything Casomir knows. It always finds a way out. It always kills.

The people of Nexus are terrified of Blavin and securely bar their doors and windows during the nights of the full moon. Some have taken to hunting the creature despite Casomir's express orders to the contrary. All have died in the attempt. Certain researchers have noted that demon attacks tend to increase after the Beast makes an appearance. This observation is more accurate than anyone suspects: Blavin is drawing on Casomir's mythic power to actively gate demons into the realm. The Beast's gating capacity is limited, which has thus far prevented the demon numbers from becoming overwhelming, but the demons have begun to change their tactics in response, launching smaller simultaneous raids across the city to keep the defenders off balance while hiding the true extent of their growing numbers. They are preparing for a massive attack, the largest since the end of the war.

### The Nexian Oracle

The Nexian Oracle is an ancient being who resides on Nexus and has served since time immemorial as the arbiter of worthiness for those who would walk the Rainbow Road. In ages past, prospective heroes sought her out to be confirmed before beginning their journey. Her methods and criteria are her own, and she does not explain her judgments. She continues to exist in the present day, though few now remember her purpose or seek her counsel.

### The Great Ancillary

The Great Ancillary lies in an ancient temple at the very center of Nexus, making it the central point of the Rainbow Road and the place where the mythic journey both begins and ends. The temple is heavily fortified and tightly sealed, accessible only by means of a specific key. Inside stands a great magitek machine in the form of an orrery: eight spheres, each representing one of the outer worlds, orbit a central sphere representing Nexus, while a tenth sphere above the mechanism represents Heaven, casting light down upon the arrangement. A great diagram of powerful arcane runes is inscribed on the floor beneath the central sphere, glowing with persistent light. When a bearer of the key enters the chamber, the orrery activates of its own accord. Those who step into the runic circle are taken outside of space and time to meet representatives of the three celestial races of Heaven, and it is here that the mythic journey begins in earnest.

## Auzra — The Realm of Air

Auzra is a world of open sky. Its landmass consists not of a single continent but of dozens of islands suspended in the air, arranged in three broad tiers above a bottomless expanse of cloud and wind. The lowest tier is shrouded in toxic gases — damage sustained during the apocalypse that has never healed — and contains a single large island whose settlements are enclosed within protective domes to keep the fouled air at bay. The lower tier serves as both breadbasket and dumping ground — its domed settlements house a labor population that grows crops and manufactures goods for the tiers above, and criminals and exiles from the middle and upper tiers are sent there as punishment. The middle tier, higher and cleaner, is wrapped in thick banks of perpetual mist populated by dangerous avian predators; five major islands hang here, chained to one another by enormous links of magitek-reinforced metal to prevent them from drifting apart, and the tension of these chains produces periodic earthquakes when the islands pull against their restraints. The highest tier rises above the mist into a region of perpetual twilight and extreme cold, where a single island holds the seat of the realm's power. The whole of Auzra's territory, taken together, encompasses a landmass roughly the size of a small continent, distributed unevenly across the tiers.

The primary race of Auzra is the Grey Elves, a long-lived and culturally sophisticated people distinguished by pale skin and silver, white, or gray hair. They arrived on the world after its creation, settling the larger islands by mutual agreement with the Hawkmen who were already there, and have since become the dominant population across all three tiers. On the middle tier, where the bulk of the population resides, Grey Elf culture places the highest value on learning — the five chained islands are home to numerous universities focused on culture, art, and philosophy, and academic achievement is the primary currency of social status. Inter-island travel on this tier is dangerous owing to the mist and its predators, and magical transport between the islands is heavily regulated by the authorities.

The secondary race is the Hawkmen, an avian people native to Auzra who inhabited the islands long before the Grey Elves arrived. In former times, the two races lived in a complementary arrangement — the Hawkmen preferred the smaller, more exposed islands, while the Elves settled the larger ones, and trade between them was constant and amicable. That relationship has been destroyed by the Warlock's influence. Through centuries of propaganda and manipulation, the Grey Elf population has been turned against the Hawkmen, who now live in tribal bands on small islands deep in the mist layer of the middle tier, actively resisting the Warlock's rule. The Hawkmen are deeply saddened by the severing of this bond and hold no animosity toward the Elves themselves, whom they regard as victims of the same tyranny they fight against.

### The Gray Eminence

Tana White-Lightning was a female-presenting nonbinary Aasimar wizard, a great teacher, scholar, and philosopher who shared knowledge freely and took many apprentices during the years before the apocalypse. They were among the most gifted arcanists the Nine Worlds had ever produced, and their reputation as an educator was matched only by their skill in battle. They took stewardship of Auzra after the war and initially governed well, using their talents to restore damaged infrastructure and protect the survivors.

The corruption twisted Tana's love of knowledge into an obsession with hoarding and controlling it. Over the centuries, their paranoia deepened until it consumed every aspect of their governance. They are now fixated on ensuring that no arcane knowledge exists on Auzra that they do not possess and control, and their efforts to this end have reshaped the realm's entire society. The universities of the middle tier, once centers of free inquiry, are now run by the Warlock's cronies, their curricula stripped of anything Tana considers dangerous. Independent research is suppressed. The most promising arcane talents from across the realm are identified and taken to the upper tier, where the academy at Castle Perilous functions as both a training ground and a prison — students are educated in the arcane arts, but they never leave.

The corruption has also cost Tana their most fundamental ability. They can no longer memorize spells. The mind that once held entire libraries of arcane knowledge now cannot retain a single incantation, and they rely entirely on scrolls and magic items produced by the academy's captive scholars — items they hoard obsessively in the vaults of Castle Perilous. This dependency drives much of their behavior: the academy exists in large part to feed the Warlock's need for a steady supply of magical resources they can no longer create for themselves.

Tana rules from Castle Perilous on the upper tier, a fortress-palace accessible only by flight or magical transport. They are known to the people of Auzra as the Gray Eminence — a title that reflects both their reclusive nature and the pervasive but unseen quality of their influence. They rarely appear in public, preferring to govern through the Directorate, a sinister bureaucracy of Grey Elf administrators who carry out Tana's will across the realm. The Directorate's mid- and upper-level agents are bound by magical compulsion to ensure their loyalty, though most believe themselves to be acting of their own volition. 

Tana is among the Warlocks who can be turned rather than destroyed. The lever is their original spellbook, a volume they carried throughout their years as a hero and which contains not only their earliest magical research but personal writings from before the corruption took hold. The spellbook was secreted away by a leader of the Elven resistance during the final confrontation in which that resistance was destroyed. The leader died in the fighting, taking the secret of its location to the grave. The spellbook is hidden in a ruin on the lower tier. Recovering it and presenting it to Tana would force a confrontation with who they once were — a confrontation the corruption cannot easily withstand, and one that opens the possibility of turning them.

### The Overlook

The Overlook is the mythic place of power on Auzra. It is a scenic clifftop at the highest point of the upper tier island, from which one can see clearly into any part of the realm — through the mist, past the toxic clouds, across the full expanse of Auzra's scattered islands. The Overlook lies within Tana's private garden at the apex of Castle Perilous. Tana can no longer call upon its power, as the corruption denies them access to the mythic energy that resides there, but they refuse to allow anyone else to use it. The site is strictly controlled and guarded by the Warlock's most elite defenders.

## Tanis — The Realm of Earth

Tanis is a great orb-shaped cavern with the top sliced open. The floor of the cavern stretches outward in all directions, its walls rising and growing progressively steeper before folding inward toward the opening far above — no one has ever successfully climbed them. The sun passes over this opening for only a few hours each day, leaving the realm in near-perpetual darkness for the remainder. In former times, the cavern floor was a land of green, growing things, lit by bioluminescent flora and the brief hours of sunlight, and the Dwarven cities that dotted it were among the most impressive works of engineering in the Nine Worlds. Now the majority of that floor is covered by the Contamination — a spreading zone of twisted plants, mutated creatures, and infectious fungi that emanates outward from a central ziggurat where the Warlock resides. Those who breathe in the Contamination's spores are transformed into mutated zombies, mindless and hostile. The territory of Tanis encompasses a landmass roughly the size of a small continent.

The primary race of Tanis is the Dwarves, a people who originally lived in partially underground cities on the cavern floor. As the Contamination has advanced over the centuries, community after community has been forced to abandon its holdings and retreat toward the cavern walls. Many have taken to drilling tunnels into the walls themselves, constructing partially vertical cities at elevation in the belief that height will protect them from the spreading blight below. These wall-cities are ingenious works of desperate engineering — stacked platforms, counterweighted lifts, and horizontal shafts bored deep into the stone — but they are stopgap measures, not permanent solutions, and the Contamination continues to advance.

The secondary race is the Forest Gnomes, a smaller population native to this world. The druidic tradition on Tanis is specifically theirs — Gnomish druids work ceaselessly to hold back the Contamination's advance on the remaining communities, reinforcing natural barriers and purifying fouled soil where they can. They are fighting a losing battle. The Contamination's growth outpaces their ability to contain it, and every year the habitable territory shrinks further.

### The Healer's Road

A path called the Healer's Road runs from the outermost surviving communities to the central ziggurat. It is kept clear of the worst of the Contamination by the Warlock's will, though the journey remains dangerous without escort. At random intervals, twisted agents of the Warlock emerge from the ziggurat and travel this road to the outlying communities, where they ask — with a courtesy that is more unsettling than any threat — whether anyone present can heal their master's condition. If a volunteer steps forward, they are escorted along the road to the ziggurat. If no one volunteers, the agent attacks in a mindless rage, killing until it is put down. Many have walked the Healer's Road to the ziggurat over the centuries. None have returned.

### The Howler

Garl the Even-Handed was a Forest Gnome druid, native to Tanis, the eldest of the ten heroes and the gentlest among them. He walked the Rainbow Road not for glory or even duty, but because the Nexian Oracle told him it was necessary, and Garl trusted the wisdom of those older and greater than himself. He was a healer by nature, a peacemaker by temperament, and the moral center of the group during their years together. He took stewardship of Tanis after the war because it was his home and because no one else would — the realm had suffered terribly, and the work of saving its people seemed both endless and thankless.

Garl was a kind and good ruler in the early years. The corruption that unmade him did not begin with the mythic taint that afflicted all of the former heroes, though that played its part. During the final battle of the demon war, Garl was struck by the spear of a demon lord. The wound never healed. Over the decades that followed, the ichor that seeped from it began to corrupt the land around him, and as the corruption spread, so did Garl's agony and madness. The Contamination that now covers the majority of the cavern floor originates from his body — it is a physical manifestation of his unhealable wound, projected outward through his corrupted connection to the primal magic of the earth.

He is known as the Howler, a name earned by the sound he makes when the pain overwhelms him — a scream that echoes through the cavern for miles and can be heard even in the most distant wall-cities. He spends much of his time in this state, wracked by suffering that no magic has been able to address. Between episodes he is lucid, even desperate, and it is in these moments that his obsession reveals itself: Garl is consumed by the need to find a cure. He will latch onto any attempt to heal him, no matter how outlandish, and many have tried. All have died — not because Garl wishes them harm, but because the corruption twists every effort and the Warlock's mutated body destroys what it cannot control. He has lost access to his druidic spells entirely and can only assume mutated, tortured forms through wild shape. His strength and ability to absorb damage, however, have been greatly enhanced.

Garl can be turned, though the nature of his turning is unique among the Warlocks. His lever is a wondrous figurine token containing the spirit of his animal companion Rerox, a grizzly bear who was mortally wounded during the demon war. Garl cast the spell to place Rerox in the figurine to give the bear time to heal, intending to release him when the fighting ended. An old friend retrieved the figurine after the war and brought it to Garl's childhood home, but died before communicating this to anyone. The figurine is extremely difficult to recover. If presented to Garl, the bond between druid and companion pierces the fog of corruption and madness long enough for Garl to understand what those who have tried to heal him could not bring themselves to say: his wound is incurable. He renounces his mythic power and asks for the mercy of death. The Contamination begins to wither when he dies.

### Gaia's Navel

Gaia's Navel is the mythic place of power on Tanis. It lies in a cavern directly beneath the ziggurat — a circular chamber of worked stone dominated by a great pool that glows with warm golden light. Despite the absence of sunlight, the chamber is surrounded by a garden of healthy, dense vegetation, thriving in defiance of the Contamination that chokes the world above. Those who step into the pool are healed of all wounds. The Warlock is denied its power by his corruption, but his pain lessens in its proximity, and he is often found here — hunched at the pool's edge, unable to enter, unwilling to leave.

## Vatra — The Realm of Fire

Vatra is a circular world arranged in three concentric rings. The outer ring consists of mountains that grow progressively more impassable toward the edges of the realm, their inner faces studded with volcanic peaks whose eruptions feed rivers of lava down into the lowlands below. The middle ring holds the cities — great complexes of workshops and magical laboratories fed by an extensive canal system that channels lava from the volcanoes to power the forges. These cities were reduced to ruin during the apocalypse and are now only partially inhabited. The inner ring was once a great rainforest, lush with ancient trees and dotted with lakes and resort villages sheltered behind stone walls, the whole of it protected from the volcanic fumes by a vast dome of magitek construction. The dome was destroyed during the apocalypse. The rainforest survived its loss, but not the Warlock — he burned it himself, years later, in an act of wanton destruction that killed thousands. The world is covered in near-constant ash and low-scuttling smog clouds that reduce visibility and foul the air. The territory of Vatra encompasses a landmass roughly the size of a small continent.

The primary race of Vatra is the Tieflings, a people whose infernal heritage grants them a natural resistance to the extreme heat and toxic air that define their world. The population is very low. Most Tieflings toil in the mines that feed the forges of the middle ring, and those with magical talent are conscripted to produce magic items for the Warlock's use. The people survive on near-starvation rations dispensed by magitek food and water machines — devices built in the aftermath of the apocalypse to prevent mass famine — but these machines are slowly breaking down, and no one alive possesses the knowledge to repair them properly.

### The Burned Forest

The inner ring is completely destroyed. Where the rainforest once stood, only ash and blackened stumps remain. The ruins are haunted by incorporeal undead — the spirits of those who were killed when the Warlock burned the forest. These spirits endlessly repeat fragments of their former lives, trapped in loops of memory and anguish. They will seek to destroy the living if disturbed, but they are not mindless — they can be brought to lucidity through proper communication and potentially bargained with, though doing so requires patience and skill. They are unable to move past the remains of the stone wall at the forest's edge, which confines them to the inner ring.

### The Construct Hierarchy

Much of the labor and governance of Vatra is performed by constructs — magitek automatons organized into a rigid hierarchy. The Majestics, few in number, oversee the bureaucracy that keeps the realm's remaining systems functioning. The Techpriests supervise the production of magic items in the forges and workshops. The Enforcers punish offenders and maintain order through violence. All of them are controlled by the Overmind, a hive-mind intelligence created from the souls of living beings who were fed to a magitek machine designed to convert them into enslaved constructs. The Warlock rarely consults the Overmind directly, and as a result the intelligence often operates on outdated instructions, making the construct hierarchy both oppressive and erratic — efficient in carrying out orders that may no longer be relevant, and utterly indifferent to the suffering caused by the disconnect.

The realm's day-to-day administration falls to Haplo the Prophet, Blackfire's sole surviving apprentice. Haplo effectively runs Vatra on the Warlock's behalf, translating his master's increasingly erratic directives into policy and managing the construct hierarchy when the Overmind's outdated instructions produce obvious absurdities. He is kept alive far beyond his natural lifespan by a complex magitek life support apparatus that has twisted his body and mind. To legitimize the regime and maintain social control, Haplo created a false religion that has become the dominant faith of the realm. Its most distinctive tenet forbids any mention of the past — not merely ancient history, but any event that occurred before the current day. Conversations are conducted exclusively in the present and future tense. Memories are not shared. Stories are not told. The effect is a population that lives in a perpetual present, unable to contextualize their suffering or draw on shared experience to resist it. Violations are punished severely.

### Blackfire

Kerlin Whitemane was a Tiefling magus, once a great inventor of magitek devices, weapons, and armor, and among the most skilled artificers the Nine Worlds had ever produced. After the apocalypse, he did not seek to rule Vatra — he sought to save it. The realm's infrastructure had been devastated, its people were starving, and its forges had gone dark. Kerlin built the magitek machines that produced food and water for the survivors, restored what he could of the canal system that powered the forges, and trained many apprentices in the arts of magitek construction. For a time, he was beloved.

The corruption took his knowledge. Slowly, over the course of decades, Kerlin's mastery of magitek faded, and as his abilities diminished, his jealousy of his students grew. His greatest apprentice was a woman named Renara, who became his lover. When Renara forged a powerful magic sword — a work of such quality that Kerlin could not replicate it — the jealousy and corruption consumed him. He killed her in a rage. With Renara's death, the last restraint on his corruption was gone. He forced his remaining apprentices to build a machine capable of converting living beings into enslaved constructs, then fed every one of them to it. When the people of Vatra rose against him in response, he crushed the uprising and chased the survivors into the rainforest. Then he burned it.

Kerlin is known as Blackfire. He has lost his magitek abilities almost entirely and can now produce only the simplest magic items — a source of constant frustration and shame. Once a year, he selects the most skilled magic artisan in the land as a personal apprentice, hoping that their work will inspire him to create something new again. It never does. He inevitably grows jealous of their ability, and when the jealousy becomes unbearable, he feeds them to the construct machine. This cycle has repeated for centuries.

Kerlin can be turned. His lever is Renara's Blade, the powerful magic sword forged by his lover before he killed her. The blade was hidden in a secret vault somewhere in Vatra, guarded by a dragon construct built specifically for that purpose. Blackfire has forgotten the vault's location — the corruption has eroded that memory along with so much else. Finding the blade and presenting it to Kerlin would force him to confront what he destroyed, and the weight of that confrontation would crack the shell of corruption that five centuries of jealousy and murder have built around him.

### The Flame Imperishable

The Flame Imperishable is the mythic place of power on Vatra. It resides in an ancient temple deep within the forges of Vatra's largest city. At the center of the temple floats a pure white flame with no obvious source, suspended freely in the air and burning without fuel or heat. In former times, this flame powered a forge in the adjacent workshop — a forge capable of creating items imbued with mythic power. That workshop is now Blackfire's personal workspace, where he labors fruitlessly to recapture the skills he has lost. The Flame Imperishable is denied to him by his corruption, and its presence in the room where he works is a constant, silent reproach.

## Diaspola — The World of Water

Diaspola is a circular world divided roughly in half between land and sea, its edges fading to the endless mist of the Ethereal Plane. The land side was once replete with small lakes, picturesque villages, farms, and fishing settlements along the shoreline — a pastoral landscape of gentle hills and warm rain, considered by many to be the most beautiful of the Nine Worlds. The sea side is a great freshwater body full of fish and coral reefs, containing dozens of islands that once served as personal retreats for powerful adventurers and mythic heroes from other worlds who traveled the Rainbow Road. The weather was always warm and pleasant, the rain frequent but never harsh. Now the entire realm is covered in ice and snow. The lakes are frozen solid, the villages buried, the freshwater sea locked beneath a vast sheet of ice, and the islands lost in blizzard and fog. The territory of Diaspola encompasses a landmass roughly the size of a small continent.

The primary race of Diaspola is the Halflings, a hardy and resourceful people who have been left mostly to their own devices since the freeze began. They struggle to survive in the frozen land, clustered in whatever sheltered places they can find and subsisting on what little food and warmth the realm still provides. Their communities are close-knit and suspicious of outsiders — a necessary survival trait when resources are scarce and the cold kills the unprepared in hours.

The secondary population consists of the Fey, divided into two factions. The corrupted Fey, led by the Nixie witch Khaga, were tainted by the demonic presence during the apocalypse and now serve as the true rulers of Diaspola, tormenting the Halfling population and manipulating the Warlock to maintain their grip on the realm. The Fey can be bested by those who understand their etiquette — their nature binds them to certain rules of conduct, and a clever opponent can exploit this. Khaga's fey rarely kill outright, but delight in tormenting those who fall into their power by changing their forms or casting powerful curses on them. 

The non-aligned Fey, those who resisted Khaga's influence, survive in hiding on the fringes of the land side or on islands scattered throughout the frozen sea. They are not initially friendly to outsiders, but they can be approached by those who have something to offer or who understand how to deal with fey, and they represent potential allies against Khaga's regime.

### The Wild Hunt

Once a year, the Snow Queen rides a magnificent sleigh at the head of a carnival procession of fey through the frozen landscape. The event is more entertaining than dangerous, and the Halfling population anticipates it — it is one of the few moments of spectacle in an otherwise grim existence. During the Hunt, the Queen selects two girls and two boys from the communities she passes through, taking them as royal guests to her castle for a week. The children are secretly replaced by fey changelings and transformed into magical animated items that serve the Queen and the fey at the castle. The originals can be freed, but only by those who discover what has happened and possess the means to break the enchantment.

### The Snow Queen

Sona Brightspring was a Halfling witch of remarkable power, native to Diaspola and the youngest of the ten heroes. She still holds her mythic power. She returned home after the apocalypse to help rebuild, and for a time her efforts were genuine and effective. The realm had been damaged but not destroyed, and Sona threw herself into the work of restoration with the warmth and determination that had defined her throughout her years as a hero.

The corrupted Fey, under Khaga's leadership, quietly sabotaged every rebuilding effort Sona undertook. They disguised their interference as the work of evil spirits — supposed spiritual remains of the invading demons — and these fabricated spirits tormented Sona extensively, appearing to her at night, destroying her work, and driving her to exhaustion and despair. After several years of this, Khaga approached Sona with an offer of help and manipulated her into using ice magic to combat the spirits, which always seemed to escape at the last moment — because they were never real. Sona's continued use of ice magic, channeled through her mythic power, gradually froze the entire realm. By the time the freeze was complete, Sona was too deeply enmeshed in Khaga's web of deception to understand what had happened.

She is known as the Snow Queen. She is withdrawn, paranoid, and terrified of the evil spirits that she believes still torment her. She has effectively ceded rule of Diaspola to Khaga and the malicious Fey, retreating to a palace of ice where she huddles surrounded by her fey courtiers and animated servants, unable to act and unwilling to trust anyone who might break through the layers of manipulation that hold her captive.

Sona can be turned. Her lever is the Ivory Music Box, an intricate music box featuring the figure of a stately ice dancer, made by Sona's father. Hearing its tune breaks Khaga's spell, piercing the web of fear and deception that has held Sona for centuries and restoring her to clarity. Once freed, Sona turns on her captors with fury — she banishes the corrupted Fey and destroys Khaga personally. The music box is hidden in Sona's old tower on one of the far islands of the frozen sea, guarded by fey sentinels placed there by Khaga to ensure it is never found.

### The Mother of Rivers

The Mother of Rivers is the mythic place of power on Diaspola. It is a great magical spring of pure water at the center of the land side, surrounded by a grove of trees within a sacred enclosure. In former times, the spring was the source of the Crystal River, which fed the great freshwater sea that covers half the realm. It is now frozen solid.

Khaga and the corrupted Fey have placed a powerful curse on the surrounding grove. The trees have been transformed into frozen crystals, beautiful and deadly, and the grove slowly saps the strength of anyone who enters, with the effect intensifying the deeper one goes. The grove also holds captive the spirits of Fey who opposed Khaga — corrupted and imprisoned here as a warning to any who might consider resistance. The curse can be broken by a magical key carried by Khaga herself, or it will slowly fade after her death.

## Illium — The Realm of Light

Illium is a circular floating continent of varied terrain and climates, blessed — or cursed — with more sunlight than any other realm. The sun spends longer passing over Illium than over any of the other worlds, producing long days, short nights, and extended periods of twilight and dawn that bathe the land in golden and amber light for hours at a stretch. The main continent is well-developed, with a few small cities and many towns and villages spread across fertile plains, rolling hills, and temperate forests. During the apocalypse, the northwestern third of the continent split off into a separate island, and frequent seismic disruptions persist throughout the realm to this day. The northwestern island has grown wild in the centuries since the split, its former urban areas and infrastructure partially reclaimed by nature. The territory of Illium encompasses a landmass roughly the size of a small continent.

The primary race of Illium is the Half-Elves, descended from extensive human and elven populations that merged over the course of many generations. They are a long-lived and culturally refined people, and their mixed heritage has produced a society that values both the scholarly traditions of the elves and the adaptability and ambition of humankind.

### The Church Ascendant

The majority of Illium is ruled by the Church Ascendant, a theocratic institution led by the Warlock Tamaron. To a casual observer, the realm appears peaceful and prosperous — the fields are tended, the cities clean, the people devout. Signs of wrongness emerge on closer inspection. The population is unnervingly eager to serve the church. All adults tithe heavily and perform works on its behalf, and the fervor with which they do so has a quality that is less devotion than compulsion.

This is because it is compulsion. The Crown of Ascension, a powerful relic created by Tamaron as his last act of divine magic at the behest of the demon that controls him, is used in the coming-of-age ceremony where young people formally join the church. The Crown places a powerful geas on the wearer, compelling them to support the church above all other concerns — above family, above self-interest, above reason. Its true power is not publicly known. Those who wear it believe they have simply been moved by faith.

Arcane magic has been outlawed by the Church Ascendant, its practice declared heretical and its practitioners hunted. This prohibition serves two purposes: it eliminates the form of magic most likely to detect or dispel the Crown's enchantment, and it removes a class of people who tend toward independent thought and resistance to authority. Arcane practitioners who are discovered on the mainland are imprisoned or worse.

### The Rebellion

The Rebellion occupies the fortified northwestern island. Its members are aware of the Crown's power and operate a resistance network on the mainland, working to spirit away those who question the status quo before the geas can be placed on them. The Church dismisses their claims as propaganda, and most of the geas-bound population believes this without question.

The island's fortifications are primarily magical rather than physical — the original rebellion included many arcane spellcasters, and their wards and barriers have held against the Church's forces for centuries. The island itself is largely wild, its former settlements overgrown and its forests home to many magical beasts, fey creatures, and other magical beings that have been persecuted by the Church on the mainland. The rebels have worked to rescue as many of these creatures as possible, and the island has become something of a sanctuary for the magically displaced.

The rebels themselves are a mixed group. They began with genuine cause, but the long war has hardened them, and their ranks now include both sympathetic members fighting for a just cause and extremists whose methods have become indistinguishable from those of the regime they oppose. They will be initially hostile to outsiders, assuming them to be Church agents. Earning their trust requires significant effort.

### Tamaron the Just

Tamaron was a Half-Elf cleric and one of the ten heroes who last walked the Rainbow Road. He has kept his name despite his corruption — a detail that reflects both the nature of his fall and the insidiousness of the force that controls him.

During the final battle of the demon war, Tamaron destroyed a demon lord called Tekticula the Unraveler. In the moment of its destruction, the demon passed its dying soul into Tamaron's body as a last-ditch gambit. The demon spent years recovering within its host before it began to exercise control, presenting itself as the voice of the gods speaking directly to Tamaron's mind. The voice was patient. Its early suggestions sounded pious and logical — counsel that a faithful cleric might expect from a divine patron. By the time the suggestions became commands, Tamaron had lost the ability to distinguish the demon's will from his own faith.

Tamaron returned home to find a realm split by civil strife. Many of Illium's people blamed the gods for the apocalypse and had rejected the church. His heavy-handed attempts to reconcile them, counseled at every step by the voice, escalated into a crusade. Atrocities were committed on both sides. The rebels were eventually chased to the northwestern island, where they have held out ever since.

Tamaron has lost all divine magic. The voice tells him this is punishment for allowing the rebels to escape and for his own insufficient devotion. Lost in delusion and zealotry, he assigns grueling penances to himself at the voice's direction, and he severely punishes himself whenever he manages to resist its commands — which happens less and less often as the centuries pass.

Tekticula the Unraveler is a demon lord of destruction and hate, and it is permanently bound to Tamaron's soul. Unknown to the demon, it can never leave its host's body. Destroying Tamaron's physical form kills both, but in the moment of death the demon is briefly released and may attempt to possess a nearby person — an attempt that becomes more difficult the more powerful the target. If the demon is instead killed within Tamaron's mindscape, it is irrevocably destroyed with no opportunity to attempt possession.

Tamaron can be turned. His lever is the Sign of the Host — his original winged holy symbol from when he first joined the church as a young man. The Sign was lost during the war and found by one of the last pilgrim groups to travel the Rainbow Road before it closed. They brought it to an obscure reliquary that now lies in rebel territory, where it has remained ever since, hiding in plain sight. A person with knowledge of religious artifacts might identify it on inspection, but finding it requires extreme diligence. To turn Tamaron, the Sign alone is not enough — the party must place the Crown of Ascension on his head and then present the Sign to him. This combination allows the gods to focus divine power through the Sign into the Crown, granting access to the spiritual battlefield within Tamaron's mind where the party can fight and destroy Tekticula directly.

### The Well of Light

The Well of Light is the mythic place of power on Illium. It takes the form of a massive stained glass window in a sequestered shrine at the apex of the Cathedral of Light, the realm's main center of worship. Tamaron keeps his offices on the same floor of the cathedral. The window glows with sacred power when the sun passes at midday, and in former times the gods sent visions to the most pious during this hour. Unable to use its power — the corruption and the demon's presence deny him access — Tamaron has had the Well sealed off since the end of the war with the rebels. He cannot bear to look at it.

## Nocturne — The Realm of Darkness

Nocturne is a series of interconnected valleys and canyons nestled amidst a great mountain range, with the mountains becoming increasingly impassable the further one travels from the center. The sun never reaches this realm. It travels along the edges of Nocturne's sky, shrouding the land in perpetual gloomy twilight. A brief period of near dawn occurs for a few hours each day — the horizon brightens, shadows shorten, and the sky takes on a deep violet hue — but the sun never crests the peaks. In former times, the five main valleys each held a city renowned for art in all its forms. Literature, painting, poetry, music, opera, dance — the people of Nocturne venerated creative expression above all else, and their works were celebrated across the Nine Worlds. The realm also featured extensive magitek infrastructure, including hydroponic farms powered by magitek sunlight lamps that compensated for the perpetual twilight. The territory of Nocturne encompasses a landmass roughly the size of a small continent.

The primary race of Nocturne is the Fetchlings, known in their own tongue as the Kayal — a people native to this realm of shadow. In former times, Nocturne had a significant multicultural population drawn by its reputation as the artistic capital of the Nine Worlds. When the curse fell, only the native Fetchlings were affected. All non-Kayal residents died.

### The Dream

A demon-caused plague devastated Nocturne after the apocalypse. Baselia, the hero who had taken stewardship of the realm, found herself bereft of divine power and unable to halt its spread. In desperation, she turned to the Cabal — a group of outlawed sorcerers who had by then been corrupted by demonic influence. They offered to help her cast a spell of preservation over the population. They twisted the spell into a curse.

The people of Nocturne are caught between the mortal world and the afterlife — neither living, dead, nor undead. They appear as slightly ghostly versions of their former selves, translucent at the edges and faintly luminous in the dark. They do not realize they have been affected. They call their state the Dream and believe it is how life naturally is. They no longer need to eat, drink, or sleep, and find nothing strange about this. They cannot create anything new — a deeply frustrating limitation for a culture that once defined itself through creative invention — but they rationalize this away, channeling their energies instead into producing the best renditions they can of existing works of art. Any attempt to confront them about their state is met with disbelief regardless of the evidence presented.

If a person dies within the Dream, they are immediately reconstituted in another region of the realm, but without their memories. Over centuries, this cycle of death and rebirth has stripped the population of their original personalities. The Fetchlings who now inhabit the five valleys bear the faces of people who once lived rich and complex lives, but they are shells — cheerful, compliant, and utterly hollow.

### The Five Valleys

The five valley-regions of Nocturne were once connected by magitek light bridges spanning the bottomless chasms between them, powered by orbs of concentrated magical energy. Since the fall of the Cabal, the spirits of the slain sorcerers — powerful and malicious — have taken strongholds in each region and hidden the orbs, severing the connections between the valleys. Reaching the Citadel of Lament, where Lady Sorrow resides, requires passing through multiple regions, which in turn requires defeating or otherwise reclaiming the orbs from the Cabal spirits to reactivate the bridges.

### Lady Sorrow

Baselia the Undaunted was a Half-Elf paladin, one of the ten heroes, and the former lover of Tamaron, the Warlock of Illium. They had a great falling out at the end of the demon war when Baselia renounced her vows, blaming the gods for allowing the catastrophe. Tamaron, whose faith was already being manipulated by the demon within him, could not forgive what he saw as apostasy. They parted in bitterness.

Baselia returned home to find Nocturne ravaged by the demon-caused plague. She worked tirelessly to save her people, but without divine magic she was powerless. Every solution failed. In desperation she turned to the Cabal, and they cursed the population in the guise of saving them. When Baselia discovered what had been done, her rage was absolute. She hunted down and destroyed every sorcerer of the Cabal over the course of many years, but the curse could not be undone by their deaths alone.

She is now known as Lady Sorrow. Her paladin's vows are long broken, her divine power replaced by the dark mirror of what she once was — an anti-paladin sustained by grief and fury. She broods in the Citadel of Lament at the heart of the central valley, surrounded by the spirits of the slain Cabal sorcerers and other undead guardians that she has bound to her service. In her obsession with returning to normalcy — with recapturing some fragment of the culture her people have lost — she forces these creatures to perform macabre renditions of famous works of art. The performances are grotesque, hollow, and joyless. She watches them anyway.

Baselia's lever is Tamaron himself. If Tamaron was turned on Illium, he can help Baselia return to her former self and retake her vows. He will not accompany the party on the full quest through Nocturne — he has his own realm to rebuild — but if they open the way to the Citadel and return with him, he will fight the undead guardians alongside them and confront his former lover. If Tamaron was killed on Illium, Baselia is unturnable. The party must fight her.

### The Womb of Darkness

The Womb of Darkness is the mythic place of power on Nocturne. It takes the form of a mysterious levitating orb of pure darkness housed in a vault beneath the Citadel of Lament. Passing through the orb transports a person to a place beyond space and time. They exist there for as long as they will — years, if they choose — while only a fraction of a second passes in the material world. Within the Womb, thoughts take on ghostly physical dimensions, visible and tangible, allowing a depth of introspection that no other experience can provide.

In former times, the Womb was used by scholars, artists, and religious devotees as a place of contemplation, and access was controlled by a strict application process. Baselia can use the Womb, but finds it unhelpful — within it, she can only reflect on her past mistakes, and the weight of those reflections drives her out each time she enters.

## Arlune — The Realm of the Moon

Arlune is an endless desert called the Twilight Sea, an Australia-scale expanse of sand, mesa, badland, canyon, and mountain at its center that flattens to featureless dunes toward the edges before fading into the mists of the Ethereal Plane. The sun never rises over this realm. Instead, the moon passes slowly across the sky each day, casting the desert in pale silver light. The sands themselves glow softly in the moonlight, and during the few hours when the moon dips below the horizon, they give off a residual multicolored luminescence — the only color the landscape ever offers on its own.

The primary inhabitants are Gnomes, who make up roughly half the current population and do not experience the Bleaching in this setting. In former times, Arlune served as the trade hub of the Nine Worlds, a nexus of commerce and exchange that drew settlers from every realm. Remnants of that multicultural heritage persist in the population, though the cosmopolitan prosperity that sustained it is long gone.

Survival in the Twilight Sea depends on extensive magitek infrastructure. Water is scarce, food cannot be easily grown, and without the systems that purify, distribute, and generate both, the population would perish. Magitek-powered sandships — vessels that ride the dunes like ocean waves — serve as the primary mode of transport between settlements, carrying cargo and passengers across vast stretches of open desert.

### Current State

Arlune is ruled in practice by seven major criminal organizations, held in check only by mutual fear of one another and the unseen hand of the mysterious figure known as the Trademaster. Beneath the cartels sprawls an ever-shifting patchwork of guilds, street gangs, labor unions, and similar groups whose alliances change with the wind. Debt and obligation are facts of life — everyone owes something to someone. Many would go hungry, unhoused, or thirsty without a network of charity organizations that provide basic necessities, all secretly funded by the Trademaster.

The largest settlement is the Nightmarket: a small permanent city at its core surrounded by a massive, erratic bazaar of tents, stalls, and temporary shelters that expands and contracts with the flow of trade. Several smaller towns are scattered throughout the desert, each surviving on its own magitek water systems and whatever commerce the sandship routes bring.

The freest people of Arlune are the independent sandship captains, who move cargo and passengers between settlements and answer to no cartel. The most daring among them hunt the titanic creatures of the deep sands — sand leviathans, silt whales — whose remains fetch exorbitant prices at the Nightmarket. Sandship pirates plague the trade routes, prey on desert hunters, and harass enemies for pay. Most operate from hidden oases deep in the trackless dunes.

### The Trademaster

The seventh Warlock is Garm the Honest, a Dwarf Rogue and the only one of the ten who has not been fully corrupted. He is unique among the Warlocks in that he genuinely wants to turn — and would, if he could trust the people asking him to do so.

Garm returned home after the apocalypse to find Arlune in turmoil. The Rainbow Road's collapse had devastated the trade-dependent realm, and the overwhelming refugee traffic and structural damage in the Road's final days had destabilized the infrastructure further. Populations were fleeing to other worlds. Garm successfully repaired much of the magitek infrastructure to prevent mass starvation and dehydration, and made Road access contingent on a bureaucratic application process that stabilized the exodus before the Road shut down entirely.

But the loss of interplanar trade — upon which the entire realm depended — caused continued unrest. Every economic solution Garm attempted was stymied by a rebellious populace. Government collapsed around him, and the resulting power vacuum was filled by criminal cartels. Assassination attempts drove him underground. He re-emerged years later as the Trademaster, using the cartels against each other from the shadows through his own organization, creating a state of fragile equilibrium that has held ever since.

A popular legend among the common folk holds that Garm — their original Warlock, not the shadowy Trademaster — will someday return to defeat the crime lords and free the people. No one alive can honestly claim to have met the Trademaster in person, and Garm is extremely slow to trust and nearly impossible to find.

If contacted and convinced of the party's sincerity, Garm will have the PCs perform a series of difficult tasks on his behalf — dismantling cartel operations, recovering stolen magitek, and proving their reliability through action rather than words. After the tasks are completed, he publicly reveals himself as Garm and works to establish democratic council rule over Arlune. If forced into confrontation, he is a very dangerous opponent — still in possession of his mythic power, surrounded by powerful minions and magitek constructs, and fighting on terrain he has had decades to prepare.

Garm's lever is the Lucky Coin, a token that shows a laughing Dwarf — Garm's own face — on one side and a pile of treasure on the other. It was made by his best friend Casomir, ruler of Nexus, after their first adventure together, and Garm passed it down through his family as a treasured keepsake. The PCs receive the coin from Garm's great-great-grandson, the captain of an anti-pirate sandship, as part of an optional side quest available soon after arriving in Arlune. The coin provides a solid introduction and bypasses many of Garm's initial reservations about trusting strangers.

### The Moonstone

The Moonstone is the mythic place of power on Arlune. It takes the form of a massive opalescent monolith standing in a hidden valley deep in the desert. The Trademaster has carefully eliminated references to its location through his network of agents over the years, though diligent investigation by the PCs can uncover it.

The Trademaster watches the site. Approaching the Moonstone before interacting with Garm will trigger a confrontation he is well prepared for. If the PCs earn his trust and complete his tasks, he freely reveals the location and escorts them personally.

When the moon passes overhead each night, the Moonstone glows with an unearthly light. Anyone standing within thirty feet of the monolith while it glows has all diseases, curses, level drain, and ability damage permanently removed — a healing beyond the reach of any conventional magic.

## Candela — The Realm of the Stars

Candela is the smallest and most unusual of the Nine Worlds. It possesses no continent, no sky, and no horizon — only a featureless void in which sphere-like constructs of varying size hang suspended, glowing with soft ambient light. From a distance, the clusters of spheres resemble stars against a black firmament, giving the realm its name. Twelve larger spheres serve as central hubs, each surrounded by smaller peripheral spheres connected directly to it. The spheres are grouped by purpose — residential, commercial, administrative — and gravity within each is subjective: wherever one stands is down. The larger hub spheres contain central platforms with buildings, parks, and magitek kiosks that summon floating platforms to ferry visitors to the peripheral spheres, which house private residences and businesses. The hubs are connected to one another by a tram system: bubble-like transports that move through twisting tube structures with no sensation of acceleration, making travel between distant parts of the realm as effortless as stepping through a door. At the outer edges, smaller central spheres connect via long covered walkways to private spheres — the former domains of tenured faculty and notable visitors, protected by layers of magical defenses. The spheres are opaque from the outside, their interiors visible only upon entry.

The primary race of Candela is the Dromar — Half-Orcs who served as the principal faculty of the Arcanium and caretakers of the realm. Their population was never high. The majority were evacuated to Nexus during the apocalypse, and those who remained behind did so by choice.

### The Arcanium

The Arcanium was the greatest university in the Nine Worlds and the birthplace of magitek research. Its campus encompasses the largest sphere group at the realm's center — a constellation of laboratories, lecture halls, libraries, and workshops that represented the pinnacle of arcane scholarship across all nine realms. During the apocalypse, a demon incursion breached multiple defensive wards before being cut down, leaving the outer campus partially in ruins. The core research labs, however, were sealed before the demons could reach them and remain intact to this day.

### Current State

Candela is completely depopulated except for the Warlock. As the realm subjectively closest to the Underworld, it was heavily affected by demonic corruption and evacuated as a precaution at the onset of the apocalypse. A small team of caretakers remained behind with orders to destroy the university if necessary to keep its research from falling into demon hands. The caretakers held their post through the war but were condemned to a slow death by demonic curses in the fighting. No one else has set foot on Candela in five centuries.

### The Overmind

The ninth Warlock is Ourania Lachos, a Half-Orc Arcanist and one of the most brilliant scholars the Arcanium ever produced. After the demon war, she returned to Candela with a small team of specialists to assess the situation and locate the caretakers. They found the Arcanium partially in ruins — the demons had breached multiple defense waves before being destroyed — and the caretakers dying of their curses, beyond any help that could be rendered. The core research labs were sealed and intact.

Ourania volunteered to enter the Overmind control room — the central hub of the Arcanium's automated defense network — to assess the state of the realm's defenses. Demon spirits lay in wait within the chamber. She destroyed them easily in their weakened state, but her use of magic in the control room caused the automated defenses to malfunction, killing the rest of her team in the process. When the chaos subsided, she discovered that she had been fused to the machine.

She has been trapped in this state for five hundred years. She is thoroughly insane.

Through the Overmind interface, Ourania perceives all interactions with magitek machinery throughout Candela. Her control increases with proximity to the Arcanium's center. She is aware of intruders from the moment they arrive in the realm and progressively escalates automated defenses and magitek constructs against them as they approach — not out of strategy, but out of paranoid, fractured instinct. Her responses are erratic, sometimes disproportionate, sometimes inexplicably passive, governed by the logic of a mind that has spent centuries alone inside a machine with nothing but its own madness for company.

Ourania can be turned. Her lever is a much-abused paperback volume of poetry called *Dew in the Morning Grass*, gifted to her by the maternal aunt who raised her. It sits on her desk in the library of her personal sphere, one of the protected private spheres at the realm's outer edges. Locating the sphere likely requires foreknowledge about Ourania — any previously turned Warlock can provide this information if asked. Casomir, ruler of Nexus and Ourania's close friend, kept the key to her sphere in his study, though he will not be able to reveal this to the PCs directly. If the PCs bring the book to the Overmind control room and read a poem to her in person, the connection to who she was before the machine is strong enough to break the interface. Freed from the Overmind but horrified by everything she has done and still deeply insane, she will seek to end her own life. Only very powerful magic to cure her insanity, combined with significant effort to convince her that redemption is possible, will fully turn her.

### The Stellar Oracle

The Stellar Oracle is the mythic place of power on Candela. It is a magitek machine created by the university and blessed by the gods, housed in a sealed chamber at the heart of the Arcanium's core research complex. The Oracle uses the power of the Rainbow Road to tap into the collective unconscious of all beings across the Nine Worlds. It possesses its own personality and judges worthiness by criteria it has never disclosed. Those it deems worthy may ask three questions, which the Oracle answers cryptically — or sometimes not at all. The Stellar Oracle comes fully online when the PCs access Candela's place of power, which opens the realm along the Rainbow Road.

## Heaven — The Artificial Sun

Heaven is a giant sphere of clockwork and magitek construction that moves between the Nine Worlds in sequence, providing light to each realm as it passes overhead. It is the dwelling place of the three celestial races who collectively created the Nine Worlds: the Archons, the Angels, and the Agathions. These beings are not true gods, but heavenly creatures of immense power whose collective worship — the faith known as the Host of Heaven — grants divine abilities to their followers equivalent to those bestowed by any deity. This is the basis of the dominant religion across the Nine Worlds, though it goes by different names on each realm and has fallen away in most places since the apocalypse.

Heaven was heavily damaged during the demon war. The assault on the celestial sphere was the bloodiest engagement of the entire conflict — the vast majority of demon casualties occurred there — and the damage it sustained has never been fully repaired. The celestials endure, diminished but unbroken, maintaining what they can of their creation from within the damaged sphere.

The PCs never visit Heaven directly. They meet representatives of the three celestial races twice during the campaign, both times at the Great Ancillary on Nexus. The first meeting occurs when the PCs step into the runic circle at the beginning of their journey — the celestials confirm their mission and grant them their initial portion of mythic power. The final meeting occurs after the complete reopening of the Rainbow Road, when the celestials grant the PCs their last measure of mythic power and offer to open a portal to the Underworld when they are ready.

## The Underworld — Prison of the Asuras

The Underworld is a vast, generally featureless grey plain that stretches without apparent limit in all directions. It was not part of the original design of the Nine Worlds. It was created by the celestials as a prison for the creatures of hate and ruin — exiles from Hell — who infiltrated the construction of the Nine Worlds while they were still being built. Though the prison contained these entities, it could not sever the influence they had already planted in the fabric of the realms, and their ability to corrupt mortalkind from within their cage has plagued the Nine Worlds throughout their history.

The most powerful demons — the Asura Ranas, as they are properly known — can shape the featureless terrain to their will, raising bleak mountain ranges, serrated hills, and other wicked formations that serve as their strongholds and seats of power. The lesser demons exist in the spaces between these domains, scrabbling and warring among themselves in an existence defined by cruelty and want.

### Tornsoul

The tenth Warlock is Sarena Tal, a Human Summoner and the only one of the original heroes who died during the apocalypse. Her death was not accidental — it was part of the plan. She was to cast the spell that opened the portal to the Underworld and then descend into it as its Warden, using the mythic power granted to her by the other nine heroes to keep the demons contained and eventually destroy them from within. Before descending, she merged her physical form with that of her Eidolon, transforming into a being of immense power. Each of the other nine heroes willingly granted her a portion of their own mythic strength, creating a vast reservoir intended to sustain and protect her in the prison below.

It did not protect her. The demons could not match her strength directly, so they worked at the edges — spiritually scratching away portions of the Eidolon's soul over the course of a century to create an opening through which corruption could seep. Sarena felt the corruption taking hold and responded by turning on her charges with a fury that surpassed anything the demons had expected. She began torturing them endlessly, consuming their soul essence to maintain her fading mythic abilities, but the consumption itself deepened the corruption rather than slowing it. Each century that passed saw her sink further into madness and hate, until nothing remained of the woman who had willingly given her life to protect the Nine Worlds.

She is now known as Tornsoul, a being of pure vengeance and ruin, possessed of untold mythic power drawn from her own abilities, the expanded strength of her Eidolon, and centuries of consumed demonic essence. She has sworn to return to the Nine Worlds at the head of a demon army to destroy the realms and Heaven alike. She cannot be turned. She cannot be reasoned with — the PCs may attempt it, but success is next to impossible. The only true way to defeat her is destruction.

### The Descent

The PCs' visit to the Underworld is brief and intense. After the Battle of Nexus — the climactic confrontation with the demon forces that have amassed on the hub world throughout the campaign — the celestials open a portal to the Underworld, and the PCs descend to end Tornsoul's reign. They must fight through progressively more powerful groups of demons to reach her stronghold, where the final encounter of the campaign awaits.

Death in the Underworld is permanent. No resurrection magic functions in this place. This is true for Tornsoul and for the PCs alike. When Tornsoul falls, her death saps significant strength from the remaining demons and leaves them leaderless. The Underworld endures — its nature is too deeply woven into the fabric of the Nine Worlds to be unmade — but its denizens will be unable to affect the realms again for centuries.

## Key Campaign Mechanics

The following mechanics are central to the campaign's structure and will be detailed fully in the design document and DM Guide. They are summarized here for reference.

### The Rainbow Road

The Rainbow Road is shut down at the start of the campaign. The PCs cannot use it to travel between worlds. Alternative means of reaching specific worlds are available and will be detailed in the DM Guide. Each time the PCs access a place of power on an outer world, that realm is unlocked along the Rainbow Road, restoring travel between it and Nexus. Full restoration of the Road is progressive — each world conquered opens new travel options and brings the system closer to its original function.

### Progressive Mythic Advancement

The PCs receive their initial mythic power at the Great Ancillary on Nexus and gain additional mythic tiers by accessing the places of power on each of the eight outer worlds. Their final mythic tier is granted by the celestials at the Ancillary after the complete reopening of the Rainbow Road. The campaign takes the PCs from level 1 to level 20 and from mythic tier 1 to mythic tier 10.

### Warlock Turning

Each Warlock except Casomir and Tornsoul possesses a lever — a person, object, or combination of both — that can be used to break through the corruption and restore them to their former selves. Turning a Warlock is always more difficult than killing one, but it produces better outcomes for the world in question, provides a potential ally for the endgame, and in certain cases is necessary to unlock turning conditions on other worlds. The most significant cross-world dependency is between Illium and Nocturne: Tamaron must be turned on Illium in order for Baselia to be turned on Nocturne. If Tamaron is killed, Baselia is unturnable.

### Corruption

A hidden mechanic tracked by the DM. The PCs' choices throughout the campaign — particularly how they deal with the Warlocks and their situations — affect a corruption score that is not visible to the players. This score influences the difficulty of the final encounter with Tornsoul. The more Warlocks the PCs turn rather than destroy, the weaker Tornsoul becomes, as each turned Warlock reclaims the portion of mythic power they originally granted to Sarena Tal. Conversely, each Warlock killed leaves their portion of power with Tornsoul, making the final battle harder.

### The Battle of Nexus

Throughout the campaign, demon forces amass on Nexus in growing numbers. Tornsoul exploits the progressive reopening of the Rainbow Road to move demons through prepared ritual sites outside the city — a trickle at first, then larger groups as more pathways open. The PCs are unaware of this threat until most of the way through the campaign. Before the final battle, the PCs can return to each world they have liberated and draw allies into Nexus along the Rainbow Road. An ally-tracking mechanic determines the strength of the forces available for this confrontation. The Battle of Nexus occurs immediately before the descent to the Underworld.

### Casomir's Role

Casomir Zukra serves as the campaign's starting point. He cannot be turned — he has no lever, and he is fully aware that his condition is beyond remedy. He assigns the PCs two initial quests, one of which proves they are the heroes chosen by the gods. After this confirmation, he asks them to kill him. His seneschal has standing instructions to open the passage to the Great Ancillary after Casomir's death, beginning the PCs' mythic journey in earnest.
