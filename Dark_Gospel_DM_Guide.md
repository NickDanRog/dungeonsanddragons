# DARK GOSPEL
## Dungeon Master's Guide

*A D&D 5th Edition adventure for characters of levels 3–12*

---

# INTRODUCTION

## About This Adventure

**Dark Gospel** is a full-length campaign adventure set in the Kingdom of Artaxarta — an ancient mountain realm, one of the great successor states of the fallen Bandorian Empire, whose rugged terrain and warrior culture have defeated every conqueror for a thousand years. The adventure begins with the player characters drawn into royal service, charged with protecting a young prince and executing missions on behalf of a crown that is about to cease to exist. By the end, they will have fought their way back into a burning capital to face the man who burned it, knowing that even their victory will not be peace.

The dark secret at the heart of this adventure is not the cult. The cult is well understood — the Kingdom of Artaxarta has spent generations suppressing the worshipers of Umgul, whom they rightly regard as servants of an evil god. What no one understands is that the cult has nothing to do with Umgul, or with any recognized deity. The entity driving the incursion is the **No-God**: something older than the Twelve Divines, something that predates every theology in the known world, a nihilistic force with a single imperative — the destruction of all existence. It does not want power. It does not want worship. It forms pacts with mortals through the seductive coherence of its philosophy, and those pacts escalate until there is no exit. The kingdom's religious establishment has been persecuting the wrong threat for three hundred years, and the right one has been hiding in plain sight behind the misidentification ever since.

The primary instrument of the No-God's incursion is **Orestes Sarkhazi**, Lord of the Magisterium, a member of the royal council who has spent thirty years quietly building the cult's network throughout the kingdom's power structure. His story is not one of ambition or hatred — it began with intellectual curiosity, proceeded through a killing he believed to be righteous, and arrived at corruption through the slow escalation of a pact he did not fully understand when he accepted it. He is the adventure's most dangerous figure and its most tragic one. Whether he dies a monster or breaks the pact at the cost of everything he has left is one of several questions whose answer depends entirely on the choices your players make.

Player agency is the structural foundation of this adventure. What the party completes, what they neglect, what they say to a grieving child in a mountain fortress, and what they choose when there is no good choice — all of it accumulates into the shape of the ending they reach. Three distinct outcomes are possible. None of them is easy.

---

## How to Use This Guide

This book is organized as follows:

- **The Realm** covers everything you need to understand and portray Artaxarta, its people, its institutions, its history, and its secrets. Read this section thoroughly before running the adventure — the setting is the adventure's emotional foundation, and players must come to love it before it can hurt them.
- **Running the Adventure** covers all mechanical and narrative systems: milestone leveling, the Faction Relationship Tracker, the Corruption Mechanic, the Act II Mission System, and the Multiple Endings Framework. Read this section before Chapter 1 and keep it accessible throughout.
- **Chapters 1–10** present the adventure act by act. Each chapter begins with a summary and DM guidance, followed by scene-by-scene descriptions with read-aloud text, DM notes, encounter information, and skill check DCs.
- **Appendices A–D** contain all custom stat blocks, magic items, and random tables.

**Read-aloud text** is presented in blockquote format:

> *This text is meant to be read aloud or paraphrased to your players. It describes what characters see, hear, and experience — not what they conclude.*

**DM notes** appear in italics and address pacing, likely player divergences, and conditional outcomes. Where the text says *"if the party does X,"* treat that as conditional logic, not prescription.

## Adventure Synopsis

**Act I: The Black Flame (Chapters 1–3, Levels 3–6)**
The characters are called into royal service and dispatched south to investigate a force of unknown identity that has laid siege to the Brotherhood of Light's mountain fortress. The road provides the first evidence that something organized is moving through the uplands. They arrive to find the siege active and help the Brotherhood drive it back — and in the aftermath, discover that the cult they just fought bears no connection to Altair, the god it is supposed to serve. They have no time to make sense of this before a message arrives from the capital. They return to Alcidea on the night of the Jinsoona, the kingdom's most sacred festival, to find it already falling. King Telamon Aristedes Alcimidias II is dead. The city cannot be held. They extract through fire and chaos, find the prince on the road to Mazrok, and fight off pursuit through the mountain dark. Act I ends when they reach the Citadel.

**Act II: The Remnant Kingdom (Turns 1–12, Levels 6–9)**
Operating from the Citadel of Mazrok — a mountain fortress that has never fallen to siege — the characters become the instrument of a crown that exists on paper and in the loyalty of a grieving general. Act II is structured as a mission pool: twelve operations across four difficulty waves, revealed through an intelligence-gathering system that the party's own choices drive. Some missions will expire before they can be reached. The enemy has been inside the kingdom's institutions for thirty years, and every piece of intelligence that surfaces makes the situation look worse. Across these months, a name eventually emerges — Orestes Sarkhazi — and the investigation becomes personal. Simultaneously, the young prince they are protecting is showing signs that attentive players will recognize and inattentive ones will miss. Act II ends when all missions are resolved. The Sadars, who have been trickling toward Mazrok across the campaign, are finally present in sufficient numbers to call the Jurga.

**Act III: The Dark Gospel (Chapters 8–10, Levels 9–12)**
The Jurga convenes. The Sadars vote on the prince's coronation, their starting dispositions shaped entirely by what the party accomplished in Act II. The night after the vote, the cult attacks the encampment. The military outcome of that attack scales with the party's Act II record, but the result is the same regardless: the prince is taken to Alcidea, and the majority of his retainers are taken to a nearby cultist stronghold scheduled for execution by nightfall. The party faces an impossible choice — save their comrades or race to the prince — and that choice determines the corruption level they bring into the climax. The adventure ends in a multi-stage assault on Alcidea and a final confrontation with Orestes Sarkhazi, whose arc concludes based on the relationship the party built or failed to build across the entire campaign.

## Themes and Tone

**Dark Gospel** is a dark fantasy investigation with political and philosophical dimensions. The adventure's central threat is not a monster or an army — it is an idea. The No-God does not manifest physically in this campaign; it operates through the coherence of its argument. Its tenets are genuinely seductive to people in sufficient pain, and it finds those people systematically. This is the design: the cult expands not through fear or force but through philosophy, reaching individuals at their most vulnerable and offering them a framework that, for a time, genuinely helps them make sense of their suffering. The horror is the escalation that follows, and the discovery that by the time the escalation becomes visible, it is already too late to exit.

Aim for weight and moral complexity throughout. The Brotherhood of Light has spent generations fighting the wrong enemy with sincere dedication — they are not corrupt, and their error is understandable. Orestes Sarkhazi was a man before the pact made him what he is, and echoes of that man remain. The prince is a child carrying grief he cannot articulate, and his vulnerability is not weakness — it is the entirely predictable result of what has happened to him. The Sadars are not obstacles; they are people with legitimate concerns about committing their followers to a cause led by an eight-year-old boy in a mountain fortress.

The setting should feel like a real place that people love before it is taken from them. The Artaxartoi are proud, competent, and fiercely loyal — qualities the cult has learned to weaponize. Every institution the party encounters should feel like something worth saving before it is threatened.

## Adjusting for Your Party

**Dark Gospel** is designed for a party of four to five characters. For other party sizes:

- **Three characters:** Reduce enemy hit points by 25% and lower encounter DCs by 1–2. The night attack in Chapter 9 should be run with more emphasis on escape and rescue than on holding the perimeter. The Act II mission system does not require adjustment, but consider alerting the party more visibly when missions are at high expiration risk.
- **Six characters:** Add hit points to named antagonist encounters throughout. The cultist assault in Chapter 9 should include a second assault wave after the first is repelled. The final confrontation with Orestes Sarkhazi should include at least two elite cult guards as persistent combatants alongside him.

**If the party rushes Act I:** The road to the Brotherhood's fortress contains encounter hooks that can be skipped without breaking the story, but Chapter 2 (The Siege) should not be shortened — the evidence discovered in the siege's aftermath is the party's first window into the No-God's true nature, and it needs space to register. If the party clears the siege unusually fast, give the Brotherhood's surviving adepts time to speak.

**If the party stalls in Act II:** The mission expiration system creates organic pressure. If a party is indecisive about mission selection, allow the expiration rolls to run normally — missions will begin to close, which is the intended consequence of delay. Do not rescue parties from their own strategic choices. The system is designed to produce failure; failure is part of what this adventure is about.

**If players engage deeply with the prince:** This is the intended experience. Eurimedos rewards attention. His conversations with the party, his questions about the deaths they have witnessed, his slowly developing understanding of the situation he is in — all of these have mechanical consequences through the Corruption Tracker, but they are also simply good drama. Give him space.

**If players disengage from the prince:** Do not force the interaction. The Corruption Tracker will reflect the neglect, and the available endings will narrow accordingly. Ending 3 is not a punishment — it is a legitimate outcome that follows naturally from a particular kind of play.

# THE REALM

## The Kingdom of Artaxarta

Artaxarta is one of the oldest and most formidable states in the known world — a mountain kingdom whose network of high river valleys has fed civilizations for millennia, defeated conquerors for just as long, and produced a people who regard competence as a moral virtue and failure as a wound that heals slowly. The Bandorian Empire took twenty years and a catastrophic portion of its military strength to conquer it, and even then the conquest required treachery as much as force. No one else has managed even that.

The kingdom occupies a complex of mountain ranges, river valleys, and high upland plateaus that sprawl across the former Empire's eastern borderlands. To the east, the Yashim Mountains form a wall so severe that they constitute less a border than an endpoint — the world simply stops there, and the narrow passes through the peaks lead to near-uninhabited terrain before eventually reaching the Kelish tribal territories, with whom Artaxarta has essentially no contact. The kingdom's other borders are managed affairs with real diplomatic weight: Kyrexos to the northwest is the most significant military concern; Morna to the west is a simmering nuisance with territorial ambitions the Artaxartoi regard as delusional; Kesh to the north is a trading partner the Artaxartoi find culturally baffling but economically useful. Kentana to the south is secretive and wealthy from its gem trade. Asta to the southwest is a religious irritant — its people worship Altair, whom the Artaxartoi equate with their ancient enemy Umgul, and the near-impassable mountain border between them has never done anything to warm the relationship.

The culture that emerged from millennia of isolation and then centuries of Xoshan and Bandorian influence prizes three things above sentiment: merit, martial excellence, and loyalty. These are not abstract virtues — they are enforced by a social system that rewards demonstrated competence and punishes failure publicly. The aristocracy exists, but its members are expected to outperform commoners at their own roles or face the social consequences of falling short. The Artaxartoi find most foreign cultures bewilderingly slack about this.

### Physical Features

**The Yashim Mountains** form the eastern border of the kingdom and define its character. These are not hills or even ordinary mountains — they are an extreme alpine environment, with passes that close for months each year and peaks that have never been climbed by recorded history. The kingdom's most famous defensive advantage flows from the Yashim: every invasion force that has ever attempted to enter from the east has either turned back or died in the attempt. The Bandorian Empire, which came from the west through Durasi-controlled corridors, remains the only power that has successfully invaded, and even they needed twenty years.

**The Twelve River Valleys** are the kingdom's economic and agricultural heart. Flowing outward from the high Yashim peaks, these river systems carve through the mountain terrain in descending networks that create hundreds of subsidiary valleys at various elevations. The Artaxartoi have developed a sophisticated system of terraced agriculture over centuries of Xoshan and Imperial engineering, and the result is a kingdom that produces significantly more food than its apparent terrain should support. The valleys vary considerably: the lower, northern valleys are warm, wide, and densely settled; the higher, more eastern valleys are narrow, colder, and predominantly given over to herding.

**The Five Cities** are Artaxarta's urban centers, all situated in the more accessible northern region where the river valleys open into broader terrain. Originally established as Xoshan military colonies — rigid, planned, laid out in the Xoshan grid style — they have long since accumulated centuries of organic growth atop their original bones. Alcidea is the largest and the capital; the other four range from significant regional centers to cities that exist primarily because of their position along major trade routes. All five have a characteristic quality: the old Xoshan skeleton is still visible in the straightness of the central avenues and the regularity of the civic buildings near the original forum, while everything built since sprawls in the irregular, human-scale pattern of a city that grew by need rather than plan.

**The High Uplands** are the pastoral highlands above the valley floors — a world apart from the cities and farming villages below. The people who live here are descended from the original pre-Xoshan tribes, and while they have nominally adopted the religion and political structures of the larger kingdom, their day-to-day lives look more like their ancient ancestors than like their urban countrymen. They herd the large warhorses for which Artaxarta is famous, practice a form of Ilura's worship that includes animal sacrifice and ancient rites absent from urban temples, and regard lowlanders with a combination of polite contempt and genuine cultural pride. The Zavra-Prag draw primarily from these communities; the Sadars are their leaders.

**The Citadel of Mazrok** rises from a cliff face directly above Alcidea, 600 feet overhead, accessible only via a single massive staircase cut into the rock and sealed at the top by a gate that can only be opened from within. It contains its own aquifer, years of stored provisions, and defensive positions that have defeated every assault in its history — the single exception being a famous mountaineering raid during the Bandorian siege, which succeeded only because a gifted group of climbers scaled the cliff face at night and forced the gate from the inside. The Citadel is where Act II of this adventure takes place and where the remnant crown makes its base.

### Regions

**The Northern Valleys and the Five Cities** form the kingdom's political and commercial core. The terrain here is the most accessible in the kingdom — wide valley floors, reliable roads, dense settlement. The culture here is the most cosmopolitan in the kingdom, shaped by centuries of Xoshan colonial influence and subsequent Bandorian administration. In the adventure's present, the fall of Alcidea has severed the administrative web that connected this region; the other four cities are either under cult control or scrambling to establish independent authority in the power vacuum.

**The Central Valley Network** is the agricultural heartland — hundreds of farming villages spread across the middle elevation valleys, supported by the terraced farming system that makes Artaxarta a major food exporter. The cult has penetrated this region primarily through civic infiltration rather than military force — corrupting local magistrates, replacing community leaders with sympathizers, quietly strangling the loyalist networks that might otherwise organize resistance. Civic missions in Act II draw heavily from this region.

**The Southern Highlands** are wilder and less settled than the central region — higher elevation, thinner soil, communities that exist primarily to support the herding economy and the Brotherhood of Light's operations. The Brotherhood's fortress is built into the southern mountain terrain, isolated enough to operate outside ordinary royal oversight but close enough to the populated valleys to deploy effectively. This region is the setting for Act I's first two chapters.

**The High Uplands** stretch across the elevated plateaus above the valley networks — a vast, cold landscape of seasonal pastures, small fortified clan settlements, and ancient sacred sites that predate the Xoshan colonization. The Sadars govern here through the authority of tradition and personality. The Zavra-Prag are called from these communities when the king requires military force. The cult has been slower to penetrate the uplands than the cities — the tight-knit clan structure resists outside infiltration more effectively than urban institutions — but several Sadars have been targeted, and at least one has been compromised.

**The Western Marches** are the borderlands between Artaxarta and its western neighbors — Kyrexos to the north, Morna to the south. The Mornai territorial claim on portions of this region makes it a recurring diplomatic irritant; the Kyrexan threat makes it a military concern. Whether Kyrexos sees the fall of Alcidea as an opportunity to press its claims is one of the background diplomatic pressures that Act II's military missions are partly managing.

### The Crisis (DM Only)

The cult's infiltration of Artaxartoi institutions was not a rapid operation. Orestes Sarkhazi spent thirty years building it, and the result is a penetration that is deep, patient, and precisely targeted at the systems that would otherwise detect and respond to it. The Kryptea — the kingdom's intelligence service — was the most dangerous obstacle, and it was eliminated first: over the decade before the Jinsoona, Orestes identified and either turned or marked every Kryptea operative he could find. By the time the strikes happened on the festival night, the service was compromised to its core. Delana Geminia Sabinus survived only because her cover as a lady's maid kept her off the operational rolls that Orestes had accessed.

The cult's outer layer — the rank and file who light the fires, guard the ritual sites, and make up the siege force in Act I — genuinely believe they are serving Umgul, the dark god of ancient Artaxartoi tradition. Most of them found their way to the cult through genuine suffering and the genuine comfort of the No-God's philosophy in its early, seductive form. They do not know what they are actually serving. The inner circle — those who have made direct pacts or been elevated to Orestes's confidence — know exactly what they serve and what the pact requires. These are the most dangerous, and the most tragic.

## Alcidea — The Capital

Alcidea is built at the foot of the cliff that holds Mazrok, spread across the broad mouth of the central valley in the grid pattern of a Xoshan military colony that has been accumulating centuries of organic growth. The straight central avenues and the rectangular civic forum near the original Xoshan heart are still visible from any elevated vantage point; the neighborhoods that grew up around them follow the natural logic of a city that built outward by habit and necessity. The effect is a city that is simultaneously ordered and alive — legible from a distance, labyrinthine on the ground.

The population before the Jinsoona disaster was perhaps eighty thousand, a number that included a significant Durasi-descended community whose ancestors were the Imperial garrison absorbed into the kingdom three generations ago. They constitute their own tribal designation and still favor Durasi-style names while being otherwise culturally indistinguishable from their neighbors. The city is also home to a substantial merchant class, the professional legionary families stationed at the barracks, the Magisterium's scholarly community, and the artisan guilds that service all of them.

Since the fall, Alcidea is under cult control. The streets are patrolled by cultist forces. The institutions have been decapitated, occupied, or co-opted. The royal palace is the cult's operational headquarters. The library — and the vault beneath it — is sealed and guarded. What was a living city is now a demonstration: this is what the No-God's incursion looks like when it wins a round.

### Key Locations

**The Royal Palace** occupies the northern quarter of the city, built into the base of the cliff immediately below the Mazrok staircase. In its original form it was the Xoshan military governor's residence; additions over centuries of Artaxartoi kingship have wrapped it in wings and courtyards that sprawl east and west from the original structure. The great hall where King Telamon received petitions is the largest interior space in the city. The palace is now Orestes Sarkhazi's headquarters and the seat of the cult's administration of the occupied capital.

**The Royal Library** stands adjacent to the palace — connected by a covered colonnade — and is the largest repository of knowledge in the kingdom. Orestes worked here as a young man. The vault his mentor showed him still exists, accessible through a passage in the restricted archives. The vault contains the original Umgul texts that began the entire chain of events, along with additional materials accumulated over the decades of Orestes's tenure as Lord of the Magisterium. It is now guarded. It is also one of the most important locations in the adventure's climax.

*DM note: The vault contains at minimum: the original Umgul texts; a thirty-year record of the cult's internal communications in Orestes's hand; and a set of ritual materials for No-God pact ceremonies. If the party reaches it, this is the most damning evidence available — and potentially the thing Orestes would most want to prevent them from reaching. Destroying it is one of the conditions for Ending 1.*

**The Temple of Ilura** is the city's largest religious structure and the most emotionally significant building in the capital to most of its citizens. Built in the Artaxartoi style rather than the Xoshan — rougher stone, native iconography, mounted statues of the goddess in her warrior aspect — it occupies a full city block near the original forum. The head priest and most of the temple's senior clergy were killed on the Jinsoona night. A skeleton staff remains, some of whom are cult sympathizers and some of whom are simply terrified. During Act III, the temple is a potential rally point for civilian resistance and a location with significant symbolic weight for the final assault.

**The Magisterium** is housed in a converted Xoshan administrative building three blocks from the library — a long, colonnaded structure whose scholars and scientists constituted Artaxarta's intellectual elite. Most were not told what their leader was. Several have been killed; others have been pressed into service by the occupation. A few may be reachable as sources of intelligence or assistance during Act III.

**The Legionary Barracks** occupy the eastern quarter of the city, a walled compound large enough to house two full legions with their auxiliary support. The barracks fell quickly on the festival night. The compound is now a prison and a cult military staging area.

**The Agora** is the city's primary market, built along both banks of the stream that runs through the center of Alcidea. Before the fall it was the loudest and most cosmopolitan space in the kingdom. It is quieter now — the foreign merchants are gone, many of the local vendors have been replaced by cult operatives using commercial cover, and the remaining citizens shop quickly and speak quietly.

**The Cliff Stairs** rise from a fortified gate at the palace's northern edge, cut directly into the cliff face, ascending 600 feet to the Citadel of Mazrok. The staircase is the only route between the city and the Citadel; it is defensible at multiple points, and the gate at the top can only be opened from inside the Citadel. The stairs are where the party's extraction occurs at the end of Chapter 3, and they are one of the primary tactical considerations in the Act III assault on the capital.

## The Power Structure

### The Crown

The kingdom is an absolute monarchy. The king rules through a council of fifteen ministers who manage the practical business of government, each heading a ministry with its own bureaucratic organization. Ministers serve at the king's pleasure. The Artaxartoi model of government is notably lean — form follows function, organizations are smaller than their counterparts elsewhere, and performance is evaluated against results rather than ceremony.

**King Telamon Aristedes Alcimidias II** was the reigning monarch when the adventure begins. He was killed on the Jinsoona night. He was considered a capable administrator rather than a great military figure, which made his close friendship with General Heskalo — who is the latter emphatically — one of the court's defining partnerships. He appointed Orestes Sarkhazi as Lord of the Magisterium based on the genuine quality of Orestes's scholarship, which was, by any objective measure, extraordinary. His son Eurimedos was the center of his hopes for the kingdom's future; the boy's intensity and intelligence clearly delighted him, and he believed the child would become a great king.

### The Dark Secret (DM Only)

The Kryptea was not just penetrated — it was rebuilt around Orestes. Over the course of a decade, he identified operative after operative through careful cultivation of court relationships and access to records his ministerial position gave him. Those he identified were either turned or marked for elimination on the Jinsoona night. The list the cult operated from on that night was Orestes's list. Delana Geminia Sabinus is the only known survivor because she was deep-cover: her operational file showed her as a lady's maid, and her actual status as an active Kryptea officer was known only to her handler, who is dead.

The extent of cult penetration across the other ministries is not fully known even to Orestes. He built a network of networks, each isolated from the others. This means the party will not be able to fully map the infiltration even with complete access to Orestes's records — he does not know everything his network has done, by design. The question of what pieces of the Artaxartoi government are salvageable is one that will outlast this campaign.

## The Brotherhood of Light

### Public Face

The Brotherhood of Light is one of the most respected institutions in the kingdom — a holy order of fighting monks devoted to Ilura whose reputation for martial excellence and personal integrity has endured for generations. Their 250 active adepts are recruited exclusively from male orphans donated to the temple as infants; they survive a series of trials of extraordinary difficulty to earn full membership, and even then must wait for a slot to open before joining the active rolls. The survivors of this process are among the most formidable individual combatants in the known world.

The Brotherhood operates independently of the crown's chain of command. They answer to their own hierarchy and ultimately to the High Priest of the Temple, not to the king. Their five Knight Commanders govern the order by seniority, and all must consent before an aspirant joins the active rolls. The Brotherhood's fortress in the southern highlands is a significant military installation — designed to be defended by a small number of highly capable fighters, provisioned for long sieges, and positioned to deploy quickly into the surrounding region.

The Brotherhood has suppressed Umgul cult activity in the kingdom for centuries and considers itself the institutional guardian of the faith against its oldest enemy.

### The Dark Truth (DM Only)

The Brotherhood's account of the Umgul cult is wrong in important ways, and they do not know it. Their historical records document centuries of suppressing what they believe to be worshipers of a dark aspect of the native Artaxartoi religion — the entity called Umgul, identified by Xoshan missionaries with the Twelve Divines' dark aspect Altair. This identification was made three hundred years ago by scholars who had good reason to equate what they observed with what they already understood, and it was never seriously revisited.

The No-God is not Umgul. It is not Altair. It is not any deity the Brotherhood has a theology for. Marcus Ivor, the Brotherhood paladin the party can recruit in Act I, is the member of the order most likely to eventually understand this — his prior history in the city of Vista exposed him to enough unusual theology to recognize when a theological category is simply wrong. But even he will need evidence before he will say so clearly.

The Brotherhood's incorrect account of what they are fighting is one of the adventure's central ironies: the institution most dedicated to stopping the No-God's incursion is also the one most completely wrong about what the incursion is.

## Key Factions

### The Crown (Prince Eurimedos / General Heskalo)

The nominal authority the party serves. In practice: a grieving eight-year-old with a gift for military theory and no political capital, and a combat veteran in his late fifties who is trying to hold himself together by force of professional habit. Their legitimacy is genuine — the prince is the rightful heir, Heskalo is the ranking military officer, and the legal apparatus of the kingdom recognizes them. Their practical power is precisely what the party can build for them.

**What they want:** Survival, first. Legitimacy, second. The army they need to retake the capital, third. All three require the party.

### The Brotherhood of Light

Badly damaged by the Act I siege but not destroyed. Perhaps 180 of the order's 250 adepts survived; the fortress held, but the assault took a toll. Their knowledge of Umgul cult history is the party's best early window into the No-God's nature — flawed knowledge, but better than nothing. Their five Knight Commanders are divided on the question of committing fully to the prince's cause versus maintaining the order's traditional independence.

**What they want:** To understand what they actually fought in Act I, and to respond appropriately. Marcus Ivor's presence with the party is the most direct link between the Brotherhood's leadership and the truth.

### The Kryptea

Functionally destroyed. Delana Geminia Sabinus is the thread from which any rebuilt intelligence capacity must grow. She has some contact with former Kryptea assets who are either in hiding or operating autonomously, but the network's coherence is gone. Rebuilding it is the narrative function of the Act II Intelligence bonus.

**What they want:** Delana wants two things: to rebuild the Kryptea, and to understand the full scope of how thoroughly it was penetrated. The second may be more painful than the first.

### The Legions

Seven professional legions, descended from the Bandorian Imperial forces absorbed into the kingdom three generations ago. Their loyalty flows to whoever can credibly claim legitimate authority. The fall of Alcidea scattered the legionary command structure; individual commanders are either dead, captured, under cult coercion, or independently trying to hold their formations together. Consolidating surviving legionary officers around the prince's authority is a background task of Act II with significant payoff in Act III.

**What they want:** Clear legitimate authority to follow and a credible plan for retaking the kingdom.

### The Zavra-Prag and the Sadars

The tribal militia and their leaders. The Zavra-Prag constitute the bulk of Artaxarta's fighting force by numbers; the Sadars hold their loyalty. Neither is automatically available to the prince's cause — the Sadars require real reasons to commit their clans to a fight led by an eight-year-old boy operating from a mountain fortress. The Jurga in Act III is the formal mechanism for winning or losing their support; Act II's missions build the foundation.

**What they want:** Protection for their people, honest information about what is happening, and leadership they can respect.

### Arslan Zedan's Warband

A young tribal chieftain of considerable cavalry reputation and a border-on-villainous personal history, depending on who is telling the story. He brings approximately 300 highly capable mounted warriors if recruited, along with tactical creativity that has repeatedly surprised commanders who thought they knew what cavalry could do. His motivation is personal — the cult killed people he loved — and his commitment would be genuine.

**What they want:** Access to the cult. Everything else is negotiable.

### The No-God's Cult

The primary adversary. Not a religious movement in any traditional sense — a three-decade infiltration operation that now controls the capital and significant portions of the kingdom's institutional infrastructure. The cult is organized in isolated cells; the outer layer believes it is serving Umgul; the inner circle knows it is serving the No-God and understands what that means.

**What they want:** The destruction of the royal line, the elimination of the Brotherhood of Light as an organized institution, and the use of Artaxarta as a staging ground for the No-God's broader agenda. The prince is not a target for assassination — he is a target for conversion.

## Key NPCs

### Prince Eurimedos Toxamones Alicimidia
*8 years old. The crown's heir. The party's primary charge.*

**Appearance:** Small even for his age, with his father's dark coloring and a quality of focused attention in his face that makes him look older than he is until something catches him off-guard and he looks exactly eight. He dresses in whatever he is given; he has no strong feelings about clothes. He almost always has a book nearby.

**Personality:** Eurimedos is the product of a childhood spent more in libraries than in training yards, and it shows. He is intellectually voracious — specifically obsessed with military history and tactical theory, a fixation his tutors found embarrassing and his father found promising — and he applies this knowledge in conversation with an accuracy that startles adults who have forgotten they are talking to a child. He is not socially confident; genuine warmth from adults is something he approaches with caution, as though unsure whether it is real. With people who prove consistent — Marcus Geminius especially, the party if they put in the time — he opens fully and the intelligence underneath becomes even more apparent.

**Role in the adventure:** He is the party's responsibility and their connection to legitimate royal authority. He is also — unknown to him, and ideally for some time unknown to the party — the No-God's identified candidate for the next pact. His grief at his father's death, his intellectual hunger, and his isolation from peers make him susceptible in ways the cult is working to exploit. Whether he arrives at the campaign's end as an unbroken boy who will be a great king, or as a child who has already been reached, depends on choices the party makes across the entire campaign.

**Secret:** The cult is not trying to kill him. They are trying to reach him. Orestes Sarkhazi has been assigned his conversion personally, and the prince's time in custody during Act III is spent in careful philosophical conversation rather than in a cell.

**Corruption Tracker:** Eurimedos begins the adventure at Corruption 0. See the Corruption Mechanic in Running the Adventure for the full system.

### General Tanos Mitradanes Heskalo — *The Laughing Lion*
*Late 50s. Commanding General of the Royal Guard.*

**Appearance:** Enormous frame — genuinely large, in the way of men who were built that way and then spent forty years making it worse through military exercise. A great blonde beard shot through with gray, kept neat by habit rather than vanity. Battlefield scars on both hands and the left side of his neck, covered by his collar in formal settings. The name came from his battlefield habit of laughing in situations that make other men pale — it still happens, but since the king's death the laugh doesn't always reach his eyes.

**Personality:** The Laughing Lion is the most straightforward person in the party's orbit: competent, honest, loyal, and operating on a combination of professional discipline and personal grief that is becoming harder to maintain as the campaign progresses. He has been the king's closest friend for thirty years. He has buried that friend without a proper ceremony, in the chaos of an extraction, and he has not had time to stop since. The grief surfaces in erratic moments — excessive force in a training session, an unusual silence in a council meeting, occasionally something that was almost an order to do something tactically inadvisable. The party may need to protect him from his own worst impulses as much as from external threats.

**Role in the adventure:** The party's commanding officer and the senior military authority in the prince's circle. Fully trustworthy and experienced, but increasingly erratic.

**Secret:** He suspects, at a level he has not yet consciously acknowledged, that someone on the royal council was responsible for the Jinsoona attacks. He does not yet have a name. When he gets one, his response will be significant.

### Delana Geminia Sabinus
*Junior Kryptea officer. Sole known survivor of the service's destruction.*

**Appearance:** Deliberately unremarkable — medium height, features that are pleasant without being memorable, the kind of face that does not stick in other people's recollection. This is partly natural and partly practiced; she has spent years cultivating the specific quality of not being noticed. She carries herself with the controlled ease of someone who is always reading the room.

**Personality:** Meticulous, patient, and intensely loyal to the people she has decided to trust — which is a short list developed slowly and defended fiercely. She is not warm on first meeting and does not pretend to be. She is also carrying the full weight of watching an institution she dedicated her career to be dismantled overnight by an enemy who was inside it for years, and she has not had a moment to process that because the work is continuous and her replacement is not coming. The party will learn this gradually, if they pay attention.

**Role in the adventure:** The party's intelligence resource. Everything they learn about the cult's infiltration of the kingdom passes through or is organized by Delana. Rebuilding the Kryptea is her project; the party's missions generate the intelligence that makes it possible.

**Secret:** She knows the Kryptea was penetrated far more deeply than anyone has officially acknowledged, and she has a working theory about how it was done. She has not shared this theory because it implicates someone the General trusts. She is waiting until she has evidence rather than inference.

### Kaske Heskalo
*Cavalry officer. Daughter of the General.*

**Appearance:** Built like her father but leaner, with the particular physical confidence of someone who spends most of her time on horseback. Her mother's coloring — darker than the General — and an expression that defaults to assessment. She is almost never still.

**Personality:** Decisive and capable, with the specific self-possession of someone who has had to prove herself in systems that were not inclined to give her credit. She earned her Kataphractoi slot after being relegated to the auxiliary corps; she then won the tournament to qualify for the active rolls. She has done everything twice over and stopped being surprised by it. Her relationship with her father is close and complicated by military hierarchy — she loves him and respects his capability, and she sometimes finds herself acting as his de facto second-in-command at moments when he is not quite himself.

**Role in the adventure:** Capable cavalry officer and one of the party's most reliable tactical assets. One of two NPCs who escape the Act III night attack and remains with the party through the climax.

### Marcus Geminius Sabinus
*Former legionary officer. Delana's twin brother.*

**Appearance:** Quiet where his sister is watchful — they share the same features, but his carry a different quality, more inward. Built for a legionary: solid, economical in movement. He has the manner of someone who has learned to take up less space than he is actually occupying.

**Personality:** Contemplative and deeply loyal to the people who have earned his trust. His bond with the prince is genuine on both sides — they have spent long hours together discussing military history and tactical theory — and it has given Marcus a quiet influence over Eurimedos that no one else quite matches. He does not speak about why he left the legions. Whatever it was, it cost him something he has not gotten back.

**Role in the adventure:** Companion to the prince; bridge between the prince's world and the party's. His capture in Act III is the loss the prince will feel most directly, and his execution — in the Option B scenario where the party races to Alcidea first — is the permanent cost of that choice.

### Fleur Dontillion
*Former mercenary. One-eyed. The only foreigner in the royal guard.*

**Appearance:** From the Kingdom of Murundus, far to the west — her features mark her as distinctly foreign in Artaxarta. The missing eye is her left; she wears nothing over it. The scar that caused the loss extends from her temple to her cheekbone. She is not small, and she carries herself as though she expects to need the space.

**Personality:** Fleur does not explain herself and does not invite inquiry. Her skills are well beyond what they need to be for a royal guard position, which is the question nobody asks directly because nobody wants to hear the answer without context. She is not unfriendly — she is simply economical about what she shares. The party is likely to spend some time around her before they learn anything about her past, and when they do learn something, it will raise more questions than it answers.

**Role in the adventure:** Combat capability and the particular value of an outside perspective — she sees Artaxartoi institutions without the assumptions of someone raised inside them. Her capture in Act III adds a personal dimension to the rescue decision for any party that has spent time with her.

### Abala Kadar
*Son of a tribal chieftain. The prince's personal bodyguard.*

**Appearance:** Young — early twenties — and built for his single purpose: large, physically capable, trained from childhood in close protection. Upland coloring: darker skin than the city-born Artaxartoi, with the characteristic mountain tribesman's broad build. He carries himself with the alert economy of someone who is always tracking the room.

**Personality:** Abala has no ambiguity about his purpose and no interest in acquiring any. He was raised for this role; he is good at it; he considers the protection of the prince to be the correct use of his existence. This is his greatest strength and, in the right circumstances, a vulnerability — someone with sufficient leverage over the prince could potentially use that leverage on Abala, because his devotion is to the person rather than to any abstract principle of service.

**Role in the adventure:** The prince's shadow. His capture in Act III removes the prince's most dedicated close-range protection at exactly the moment the prince needs it most.

### Marcus Ivor
*Paladin of the Order of Light. Former Knight of Vista.*

**Appearance:** A foreigner — Vista is a city-state far to the west with a distinctive cultural heritage, and Marcus carries it in his features, his accent (present but controlled), and the particular way he holds himself in religious spaces, which is slightly different from the Artaxartoi norm. He is in his forties, with the physical conditioning of a man who has maintained a demanding regimen for decades. His armor, when he wears it, is the Brotherhood's white-and-gold.

**Personality:** Marcus Ivor is the most theologically flexible person in the party's orbit, and the most knowledgeable about what the party is actually facing — though it takes time for his knowledge to reach its own accurate conclusions. His faith is genuine. His ability to update his theological understanding in response to new evidence is the quality that makes him uniquely valuable in this adventure.

**Role in the adventure:** The party's window into the Brotherhood's knowledge of the Umgul cult's history, and eventually the person most capable of articulating what the No-God actually is when the evidence accumulates.

**Secret:** His transition from Vista's Knights to the Brotherhood followed from something he witnessed in Vista that his previous faith could not account for. He has never found an adequate theological category for what he saw. The No-God, when it finally becomes visible, fits that category.

---

### Arslan Zedan
*Young tribal chieftain. Cavalry tactician.*

**Appearance:** Mid-twenties. The upland build — broad, physically capable — combined with the manner of someone who is used to being underestimated and has stopped minding because it keeps being useful. He is not physically remarkable in any single dimension; his reputation precedes him, which means the first meeting is usually a surprise in some direction.

**Personality:** Intelligent, tactically creative, and operating on a genuine desire for revenge that he makes no effort to disguise. He is not reckless — his reputation for unconventional tactics reflects genuine strategic creativity rather than mere aggression — but his motivation is personal in a way that makes him a complicated ally. He will be useful, committed, and occasionally difficult to manage when his personal objectives and the campaign's operational objectives diverge.

**Role in the adventure:** Available for recruitment via a Military mission in Act II. He brings approximately 300 cavalry and tactical creativity that is a genuine asset in the kind of mobile warfare Act II and Act III require.

**Secret:** His "villainous" reputation among several Sadars is based on real events; what those Sadars omit from their account is the context that makes his actions understandable, if not unambiguous. Whether the party investigates and what they do with what they find has diplomatic consequences at the Jurga.

### Orestes Sarkhazi
*Lord of the Magisterium. Priest of the No-God. Primary antagonist.*

**Appearance:** Late sixties, with the appearance of a man who has spent forty years at a desk — not frail, but not physically imposing either. His hands are notable: long-fingered, ink-stained even now, the hands of someone who has written constantly for his entire adult life. His eyes carry a quality of full attention that students and colleagues always described as flattering and that, at close range, reads as something else entirely. He dresses as a scholar even in the occupied palace.

**Personality:** Orestes is the product of a mind that was genuinely exceptional before the pact and has become something more and worse since. He is patient, precise, and intellectually honest in a way that is disturbing — when he tells you something is true, it usually is, even when that truth is being used as a weapon. He does not hate. He does not feel contempt for the people he has destroyed. He has made his peace with what he is, and that peace is perhaps the most unsettling thing about him. He can still feel the shape of the person he used to be — the young man who killed his mentor in an act of genuine piety, who spent years in the library reaching for something he could not name. Whether that person can be reached depends on whether the party gives it a chance.

**Role in the adventure:** The primary antagonist and the final confrontation. He is the architect of everything that has happened to the kingdom, and how that confrontation resolves determines which ending is available.

**Secret:** He knows that breaking the pact will kill him. He has known this for years. The question the party has to answer is not whether he knows this, but whether they can make him choose it anyway.

# RUNNING THE ADVENTURE

## Milestone Leveling

Characters begin at Level 3 and advance to Level 12 across the three acts. Advancement is tied to act completion and specific dramatic beats, not to experience points.

| Level | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 3 | Start of adventure |
| 4 | Chapter 1 complete — the PCs depart Alcidea and confirm the siege is real |
| 5 | Chapter 2 complete — the Brotherhood's siege is broken; first evidence of the No-God surfaces |
| 6 | Chapter 3 complete — the PCs reach the Citadel of Mazrok with the prince; Act I ends |
| 7 | Act II: 3 missions completed |
| 8 | Act II: 6 missions completed |
| 9 | Act II complete — all 12 turns resolved; the Jurga is called; Act III begins |
| 10 | Chapter 8 complete — the Jurga vote is taken; the night attack concludes |
| 11 | Chapter 9 complete — the pivotal decision is made; the march to Alcidea begins |
| 12 | Chapter 10 — before the final confrontation with Orestes Sarkhazi |

*Note on Act II milestones:* Levels 7 and 8 are triggered by missions completed, not turns elapsed. A party that completes six missions by turn 5 reaches Level 8 at turn 5. A party that completes only four missions across all twelve turns reaches Level 8 at the start of Act III. Adjust encounter difficulty accordingly — know the party's current level before each session begins.

*Note on Level 12:* Award this milestone when the PCs enter the final wing of the palace, before they face Orestes Sarkhazi. The last level should be felt during the confrontation, not granted afterward.

## The Faction Relationship Tracker

Dark Gospel uses faction relationships as a resource system. Standing with each of four factions determines what support is available in Act III and which endings remain accessible. Track each faction independently on a four-point scale.

### Standing Levels

| Standing | Description |
|---|---|
| **Estranged** | The faction actively mistrusts the PCs or has been alienated; their support is unavailable |
| **Neutral** | The default starting position; willing to cooperate but uncommitted |
| **Allied** | Actively committed to the prince's cause; their resources are available |
| **Committed** | The faction's full weight is behind the PCs; they will take significant risk on the party's behalf |

### Starting Standings

| Faction | Starting Standing | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Brotherhood of Light | Neutral | Grateful for Act I assistance; not yet trusting |
| Sadar Council | Neutral | Open to the prince's claim; nothing proven yet |
| The Legions (loyalist) | Neutral | Loyalty follows legitimate authority; needs evidence of it |
| Arslan Zedan's Warband | — | Not yet contacted; becomes Neutral after his recruitment mission |

### How Standing Changes

**Brotherhood of Light**
- +1 (→ Allied): PCs successfully break the Act I siege; Marcus Ivor is recruited
- +1 (→ Committed): 2 or more Spiritual missions completed; the No-God's true nature confirmed through Brotherhood scholarship
- -1: PCs publicly contradict or undermine the Brotherhood's religious authority without diplomatic recovery
- -1: 3 or more Spiritual missions declined or lost

**Sadar Council**
- +1 (→ Allied): 2 or more Civic missions completed; Arslan Zedan's reputation managed diplomatically with skeptical Sadars
- +1 (→ Committed): 3 or more Civic missions completed; the Jurga lobbying period produces 8 or more votes
- -1: Arslan recruited without any diplomatic groundwork with skeptical Sadars
- -1: A Sadar is killed in the night attack due to low military completion (caps standing at Allied; does not prevent it)

**The Legions (loyalist)**
- +1 (→ Allied): 1 or more Military missions directly supporting legion consolidation; General Heskalo maintains command authority
- +1 (→ Committed): 3 or more Military missions completed; a senior legion officer formally pledges to the prince's banner
- -1: Arslan Zedan given operational authority over legion units without prior coordination
- -1: General Heskalo dies or is incapacitated before the Jurga

**Arslan Zedan's Warband** *(after recruitment)*
- Begins at Neutral
- +1 (→ Allied): Arslan's reputation is investigated and the true context is shared with skeptical Sadars
- +1 (→ Committed): His warband is given meaningful operational authority in a Military mission; his personal vengeance is partially satisfied
- -1: Arslan is given no operational role after recruitment — treated as a resource, not a partner

### Standing and Ending Availability

| Faction Standing | Act III Effect |
|---|---|
| Brotherhood Allied or Committed | Ending 1 available; the spiritual dimension of the incursion can be fully closed |
| Sadar Council Allied or Committed | 8+ votes available at the Jurga; prince gains legitimate authority for Act III |
| The Legions Allied or Committed | Full assault force available for the Alcidea operation |
| Arslan Zedan Committed | His cavalry screens the assault and cuts off Orestes's escape route |

*Ending 2 (The Last Convert) is not gated by faction standing. It requires a relationship with Orestes Sarkhazi built through dialogue across Acts II and III — see Managing Multiple Endings.*

FACTION STANDINGS — Session [N]

The Legions:           Neutral / Allied / Committed

## The Corruption Mechanic

The **Corruption Mechanic** tracks Prince Eurimedos's exposure to the No-God's influence across Acts II and III. It is the mechanical engine behind Ending 3. It operates invisibly to the players — they see the narrative signs without knowing they are tracking a number.

### The Scale

Corruption is measured from 0 to 10.

| Range | Status | What the DM Sees |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | **Vigilant** | The prince is engaged, present, and asking healthy questions about what he has been through |
| 3–5 | **Susceptible** | His questions are deepening; his isolation is noticeable; he reads more and talks less |
| 6–7 | **Compromised** | He attributes meaning to things in ways that echo No-God tenets, though he does not recognize the source |
| 8–9 | **Claimed** | He has found the tenets, understands them, and finds them compelling. He will not say so |
| 10 | **Lost** | The pact is possible. Ending 3 is locked in |

### What Raises Corruption

| Event | Increase |
|---|---|
| Prince goes a full turn without meaningful PC interaction (isolation) | +1 |
| Prince accesses cult materials without PC intervention | +2 |
| PCs choose Option A in Chapter 9 (save comrades before the prince) | +2 |
| Spiritual mission lost that would have destroyed a ritual site | +1 per mission lost, max +2 total from this source |
| PCs notice signs of vulnerability and dismiss or ignore them | +1 |
| General Heskalo's erratic behavior goes unaddressed and Eurimedos witnesses it | +1 |

### What Lowers or Prevents Corruption

| Action | Effect |
|---|---|
| PC spends meaningful in-character time with the prince (history, conversation, honest talk about loss) | Prevents isolation increase that turn; -1 if Corruption ≥ 4 |
| Marcus Geminius Sabinus is present and active with the prince | Prevents isolation increase automatically each turn he is present |
| Spiritual missions completed | -1 per two Spiritual missions completed, max -2 total from this source |
| PCs recognize and directly address signs of vulnerability in conversation | -1, once per sign noticed |
| PCs choose Option B (rescue prince first) | Prevents the +2 from Option A; no additional benefit |

### Visible Signs by Corruption Level

These are the narrative signals delivered through Eurimedos's dialogue and behavior. Players never see the number.

- **3–4:** Eurimedos asks pointed questions about death — whether the people who died would have wanted the PCs to grieve them, whether grief serves any purpose. He is not morbid. He is curious in the way he is always curious. The questions sit differently than they used to.
- **5–6:** He quotes something during a conversation without attribution. If pressed on the source, he says he read it somewhere and does not remember exactly where. He seems slightly evasive in a way he has not been before.
- **7–8:** He tells a PC that the No-God's tenets, as he understands them, "make a kind of sense — not the violence, but the rest of it." He does not appear distressed by this. He appears thoughtful.
- **9+:** He stops volunteering information about what he reads. Abala has noticed. Marcus has noticed, if he is still alive. If asked directly, Eurimedos deflects with the smooth ease of someone who has been practicing.

### Corruption and Ending Availability

| Corruption at Start of Chapter 10 | Available Endings |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Endings 1 and 2 |
| 6–7 | Ending 1 still available with active PC intervention during Chapter 10; Ending 2 always available |
| 8+ | Ending 3 locked in regardless of other conditions; Ending 2 available if Orestes conditions are met |

*If Marcus Geminius Sabinus is killed in the Chapter 9 pivotal decision (Option B), remove his protective benefit from all future turns. This can accelerate corruption faster than any other single factor in the campaign. The party will not understand why until it is too late — or until they recognize what they traded.*

## The Act II Mission System

Act II replaces the linear chapter structure of Acts I and III with a **mission pool**: twelve available operations across four waves, played across a maximum of twelve turns. This section is the complete mechanical reference for running it.

### The Turn Structure

Each turn in Act II proceeds in this fixed order:

**Step 1 — Intelligence Reveal Roll**
The DM rolls `d20 + the party's current Intelligence bonus` against the reveal DC of every unrevealed mission in any unlocked wave. Every mission whose DC is met or beaten is revealed to the party. Missions in locked waves cannot be rolled for. Roll openly or secretly, but be consistent across the campaign.

**Step 2 — Mission Selection**
The party chooses which available mission to attempt. They may confer with their allies. One mission per turn, no exceptions.

**Step 3 — Mission Execution**
The selected mission is played out as a full scene or sequence. Mission entries in the Mission Roster include scene structure, encounters, and outcomes.

**Step 4 — Intelligence Gain**
On completing a mission, the party gains +1 to their Intelligence bonus (maximum +8). This bonus is permanent and cumulative. Record it.

**Step 5 — Investigation Roll (Optional)**
After completing a mission, any PC may attempt an Investigation check against a DC equal to the higher of 15 or the completed mission's original reveal DC. On a success, the DM indicates — in narrative terms, not mechanical ones — which currently-available mission faces the highest expiration risk. *("Delana's contacts are describing increased activity near Mission X. Whatever window existed may be closing.")*

**Step 6 — Expiration Roll**
At the end of the turn, the DM rolls d100 for every **revealed but uncompleted** mission. Any mission whose roll meets or falls under its current expiration percentage is lost. Record losses — they matter in Act III.

### Intelligence and Wave Unlocking

| Wave | Missions | Reveal DC | Unlocks When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wave 1 | Missions 1–3 (1 per category) | Pre-revealed | Available from Turn 1 |
| Wave 2 | Missions 4–6 | DC 14 | Turn 1 (always unlocked) |
| Wave 3 | Missions 7–9 | DC 17 | Intelligence reaches +3 |
| Wave 4 | Missions 10–12 | DC 20 | Intelligence reaches +6 |

Wave 1 missions begin already revealed — no roll required. Wave 2 is always unlocked but must be rolled for. Waves 3 and 4 unlock when the Intelligence bonus reaches the listed threshold; they cannot be rolled for, revealed, or expire until unlocked.

### Mission Expiration Tracks

Expiration percentages begin accruing from the turn the mission's wave unlocks, not from Turn 1.

| Track | Starting % | Increase Per Turn | Effective Window | Typical Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fast | 25% | +25% per turn | ~3 turns | Time-critical rescues; imminent executions |
| Standard | 15% | +15% per turn | ~6 turns | Default for Wave 2–3 missions |
| Slow | 10% | +10% per turn | ~10 turns | Default for Wave 4 missions |

*Example: A Standard mission in Wave 2 unlocks on Turn 1. By Turn 5, its expiration percentage is 15% + (4 × 15%) = 75%. A d100 roll of 75 or lower on Turn 5 means the mission is lost.*

*Fast missions in Waves 1 and 2 demand early attention. A Fast mission revealed on Turn 1 will almost certainly expire before Turn 4. The Investigation roll system is the party's warning mechanism — use it.*

### Act II Outcomes and Act III Resources

The DM should record category completion at the end of Act II. These results determine what is available for the assault on Alcidea.

| Spiritual Missions Completed | Act III Effect |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | Cult retains full supernatural advantage; no Brotherhood combat support; ritual sites active during the assault |
| 2 | Brotherhood can advise and provide intelligence but cannot field units; some ritual sites remain |
| 3–4 | Brotherhood fields a combat unit in the assault; all major ritual sites destroyed; Orestes is weakened at the start of the final confrontation |

| Civic Missions Completed | Act III Effect |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | No civilian network in Alcidea; streets are hostile; no advance intelligence on cult positions |
| 2 | A small loyalist cell exists; limited positional intelligence available |
| 3–4 | An active loyalist network provides advance intelligence and safe passage; civilian resistance complicates cult troop movements during the assault |

| Military Missions Completed | Act III Effect |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | Night attack is overwhelming; multiple Sadar deaths; assault force is minimal |
| 2–3 | Night attack is costly but survivable; moderate assault force available |
| 4 | Night attack repelled; full assault force intact; Arslan Zedan's cavalry confirmed for Alcidea |

### Narrative Milestones Within Act II

These events occur within the mission framework regardless of turn order. Place them at dramatically appropriate moments rather than fixing them to specific turn numbers.

**Arslan Zedan Recruitment** — Available via a Military mission early in Act II. Completing it makes Arslan recruitable, adds his warband to loyalist forces, and opens his faction track.

**Orestes Sarkhazi Identified** — The antagonist's identity emerges through accumulated Spiritual and Military missions, typically mid-act after five or six missions are complete. Prior to this, the PCs know there is a figure inside the power structure directing the cult. When the milestone is reached, Delana's network produces his name. This changes the nature of everything that follows.

**The Prince's Vulnerability** — Subtle signs of Eurimedos's susceptibility begin to appear across Act II through his dialogue and behavior in scenes between missions. Whether the PCs notice and respond determines Corruption's trajectory into Act III.

## The Kryptea Intelligence Network

**Delana Geminia Sabinus** is the surviving thread of the Kryptea and the in-world mechanism through which missions are revealed. She is not a behind-the-scenes administrator — she is an active intelligence officer doing real work at real risk, and she should be played that way.

At the start of Act II, Delana has access to a handful of surviving contacts: individuals who were not connected to the main Kryptea network and thus escaped the cult's systematic purge. Her information is fragmentary and her capacity is severely limited. The Intelligence bonus represents the growth of this network as she rebuilds it — each completed mission produces recoverable assets, freed informants, captured documents, and reestablished contact chains that expand what she can see.

**Playing Delana in Act II**

Delana is the voice through which the DM delivers intelligence to the party. After each mission, she should appear with updated information — framed as genuine intelligence work, not convenient exposition. She will sometimes be wrong. She will sometimes know less than the party wants. She will never pretend to know what she does not.

Core behaviors:
- She always distinguishes between confirmed intelligence and inference. *"I know he was at the compound three days ago. I don't know where he is now."*
- She never lies to the PCs, but she withholds what she does not yet trust them with — especially early in Act II.
- She is carrying the full weight of watching an institution she built her career inside be dismantled overnight by an enemy that had been there for years. The PCs may occasionally see through the professionalism.

**The Network's Dark Secret (DM Only)**

The Kryptea was not merely defeated in the fall of Alcidea. It was penetrated. Orestes Sarkhazi had assets inside the service for at least fifteen years. Some of those assets are still active, operating among the surviving fragments Delana is rebuilding around. Whether and how the PCs and Delana discover this is one of Act II's investigative threads — and the answer determines whether the Kryptea can be rebuilt into something trustworthy or whether it remains irreparably compromised going into the reconstruction that follows the campaign.

## Managing the Revelation of Secrets

Dark Gospel is built around layered secrets whose revelation must be paced carefully. The central truth — that the cult serves the No-God, not Altair — should feel earned, not telegraphed. Each revelation has a natural trigger point and guidance on what to do if the party arrives at it early or misses it entirely.

**Secret 1: The cult is not what the kingdom believes**

*Natural trigger:* The aftermath of the Act I siege. Marcus Ivor can identify the rituals used in the assault as inconsistent with any recorded Altair practice. This is the party's first firm indication, delivered with appropriate uncertainty — Marcus knows what he saw; he does not know what it means.

*If the party doesn't press:* This revelation cannot be skipped — the entire campaign depends on the PCs understanding they are facing something new. If they do not engage with Marcus Ivor or press the Brotherhood for analysis, Delana's network surfaces the same conclusion through a captured cult document in Act II, no later than Wave 2.

*How to deliver it:* As a genuine scholarly puzzle, not a dramatic announcement. The most effective moment is a quiet one — Marcus or Delana placing a document in front of the party and saying: *"This is not Altair. I don't know what this is."* Let the players sit with what that means.

**Secret 2: Orestes Sarkhazi is the architect**

*Natural trigger:* Mid-Act II, after sufficient Spiritual and Military missions have produced the intelligence Delana needs. The precise trigger is the DM's judgment — it should feel like an investigative payoff, not a scheduled reveal.

*If the party suspects early:* Do not confirm prematurely. Delana will not name a member of the King's council without evidentiary basis — she knows what happens to intelligence services that accuse without proof. If the party names Sarkhazi, she says she does not have enough to bring to the General. This maintains tension without lying to the players.

*How to deliver it:* Through Delana, in a scene that acknowledges the weight of what she is saying. The man who built this network sat on the King's council for thirty years. The implications for everyone the King trusted are unavoidable. Let her feel that.

**Secret 3: The prince is the No-God's next target**

*Natural trigger:* Tied to Corruption level and Spiritual mission progress. The party can piece this together from two directions — the pattern of the No-God's operations (it escalates toward future vessels, not just immediate power) and the observable signs of Eurimedos's vulnerability. A Spiritual mission in Wave 3 or 4 will produce documents that make the targeting explicit.

*If the party never connects the dots:* They proceed into Act III without knowing the prince is at specific risk. This is not a failure state — it is the condition that produces Ending 3. Do not correct the oversight. Let it unfold.

**Secret 4: The Kryptea was penetrated from within**

*Natural trigger:* Available through active investigation during Act II. Not required for any ending, but parties who find it have a materially different understanding of what they have been playing — and that understanding reshapes how they approach state reconstruction in the epilogue.

*How to deliver it:* Through a document or a surviving Kryptea agent who was not compromised — someone who noticed discrepancies and stayed quiet because they did not know who to trust. The revelation should not implicate any NPC currently traveling with the party. Leave Delana's potential exposure as a question the party must answer themselves, based on evidence they find, not confirmation the DM provides.

## Managing Multiple Endings

The three possible endings are determined by a small set of tracked conditions. Maintain this tracker across Acts II and III and check it before the final confrontation.

### Conditions Tracker

| Condition | How to Track | Key Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Eurimedos Corruption | Record after each turn; update at Chapter 9 | ≤5 for Endings 1/2; ≥8 locks Ending 3 |
| Spiritual missions completed | Record at Act II end | 2+ for Ending 1's spiritual closure |
| Relationship with Orestes | Count meaningful dialogue scenes: 0 / 1 / 2+ | 2+ for Ending 2 |
| Option A or B chosen (Ch. 9) | Record when the decision is made | Option A adds +2 Corruption |

### Ending Availability at Chapter 10

**Ending 1 — The Long Vigil**
*Available when:* Corruption ≤ 5 at the start of Chapter 10; at least 2 Spiritual missions completed; Orestes is defeated by any means.
*Outcome:* The incursion ends. The kingdom survives, damaged. Eurimedos, unbroken, will eventually take the throne. The Brotherhood and whatever remains of the Kryptea are charged with a permanent vigil. The PCs know the silence is not peace.

**Ending 2 — The Last Convert**
*Available when:* The PCs have had 2 or more meaningful scenes of dialogue with Orestes Sarkhazi across the campaign — confrontations that left room for something other than destruction, in which his humanity was addressed directly. This condition cannot be satisfied by a single conversation immediately before the climax; it must be tracked across Acts II and III.
*Outcome:* Confronted with the full weight of what he has become and what his compliance means for the kingdom — Orestes defects. The act of will against the No-God provides the opening the PCs need to collapse the incursion. He does not survive it. What the party does with the truth of who he was, and what he chose at the end, is their own decision.
*Note:* Endings 1 and 2 are not mutually exclusive. If Corruption ≤ 5 and Orestes's dialogue conditions are met, both resolutions are available simultaneously.

**Ending 3 — The Seeds of Ruin**
*Triggered when:* Corruption ≥ 8 at the start of Chapter 10, or the PCs chose Option A and Corruption reaches 6+ before the final confrontation.
*Outcome:* The cult is broken. Orestes is defeated. The No-God's incursion collapses. Eurimedos, apparently safe, eventually takes the throne. Decades later, the cult reappears — not from a remnant cell, but from within the palace. The prince, in his grief and his hunger for understanding, found the No-God's tenets during the long nights in the Citadel. He never told anyone. The campaign ends with this revelation in epilogue, after the apparent victory, in the quiet voice this adventure has always used for its most unsettling truths.

### The Redemption Scene (Ending 2)

If the Ending 2 condition is met, the final confrontation should build toward a moment in which Orestes is given one last choice. He will not take it unless it is offered.

The two prior dialogue scenes must have included:
- At least one in which a PC acknowledged the person he used to be — not just the destruction he caused, but the scholar in the library, the man who made a choice he believed was righteous
- At least one in which the No-God's logic was challenged on its own terms, not merely opposed by force or disgust

If both are present and the party creates the moment in the final confrontation, he will break the pact. Play it quietly. This is not a dramatic conversion — it is an exhausted man making a decision he has been slowly approaching for thirty years. The cost is immediate and absolute. Let the room be still.

# CHAPTERS

## Chapter 1: Dispatches from the Front *(Levels 3–4)*

### Chapter Summary

The characters are called before King Telamon Aristedes Alcimidias II and assigned to a newly formed special unit under General Heskalo, charged with investigating the siege of the Brotherhood of Light's southern fortress. The briefing is direct: an organized force of unknown composition has sealed off the Brotherhood's mountain bastion for eleven days, no couriers have come through, and the Jinsoona is eight days away. The crown wants to know who these people are.

The road south makes it a larger problem immediately. Farming communities in the foothills are empty in ways that are not the empty of a market between uses, but the empty of people who have decided not to be there. A bridge has been deliberately demolished. A patrol sent ahead from a garrison post is found dead at the roadside, their papers missing. By the time the party reaches the high ground above the Bastion of the Bright Watch, the siege encampment spread across the valley below is organized, well-provisioned, and stripped of every identifying mark. This was planned. The party does not yet know by whom.

### Scene 1: The King's Commission

The party is summoned to the Royal Palace. King Telamon receives them in a working session — maps spread across the side table, ministers observing, General Heskalo present and thinking harder than he is saying.

> *The throne room is smaller than its reputation. Built in the Xoshan military style — high ceilings, plain dressed stone, sight lines unobstructed from every angle — it is a room designed for communication, not ceremony. The King stands at the map table rather than the throne, one hand resting on the edge of the spread paper. His attention as you enter is precise and unhurried, the attention of a man who has already formed a working model of what he needs from you.*
>
> *"The Brotherhood of Light's southern fortress has been under siege for eleven days," he says, without preamble. "No declarations. No identifying marks. Three assault attempts, all repelled. No prisoners. The road south has been closed since the fourth day — no couriers, no supply traffic." He pauses. "I need to know who these people are before the Jinsoona. That is why you are here."*

*General Heskalo provides operational specifics: a cavalry escort will accompany the party to the foothills and then return; the party continues south on foot or horseback through terrain the Brotherhood knows better than any army. The signal system — three fires on the eastern tower of the fortress — means the Brotherhood is prepared to coordinate a breakout. If the party creates an opening, that signal authorizes combined action.*

*Let the party ask questions. The King and the General answer honestly — they know less than they would like. If the party asks about the Umgul cult: it is a working theory, but the Brotherhood's last message described the attackers as "not what we anticipated" without further clarification, and that message is five days old. That gap is the party's job to fill.*

*If the party asks to see the Brotherhood's original message (DC 13 Persuasion): the king's aide produces it. The relevant passage reads: "The attackers are not what we anticipated. Advise the crown that our theological classification may require revision. We hold, but inquire urgently into the matter of identification." Signed by Knight Commander Velas.*

**Information available in this scene:**
- DC 12 Insight (observing the General): He is more worried than his posture suggests — not professional concern, but something older. He and the king have been close friends for thirty years; the General knows what "theological classification may require revision" means coming from the Brotherhood's most experienced commander.
- DC 14 History: The Bastion of the Bright Watch has never fallen to direct assault. An eleven-day siege represents a force large enough and disciplined enough that the crown should have heard about its assembly weeks ago. It did not.

### Scene 2: The Road South

The party travels south with a cavalry escort that turns back at the foothills. The road provides progressive evidence of a situation more serious than the briefing suggested.

> *The Jinsoona is seven days away, and the road south should be moving — grain coming north from the harvest towns, festival goods and travelers heading toward the city. It is not. The road is empty in the way that a market square is empty when something has happened: not the comfortable emptiness of a place between uses, but the deliberate absence of people who have decided not to be there.*
>
> *The first village you pass through is intact — doors closed, shutters latched, livestock in their enclosures. Someone has been keeping the animals fed. But no one answers a knock. The curtains move.*

*Three distinct pieces of evidence accumulate as the party travels south:*

*First: an abandoned courier's saddlebag, still attached to a tethered horse that has been grazing for several days. The bag contains routine administrative documents from a border garrison and a cheerful personal letter from a soldier to their family in Alcidea — nothing sensitive, nothing to explain why the horse was simply left here.*

*Second: a bridge across the Serras tributary, its central section deliberately dropped into the river below. Passable carefully on foot or with a light horse; impassable to any supply wagon. The demolition is recent, no more than two weeks old, and professionally done.*

*Third: a patrol of six soldiers from the Tamaro garrison, found at the side of the road with their documents gone. They were killed cleanly — no defensive wounds, no signs of a struggle. Someone they trusted, or someone they did not see.*

*The cavalry escort turns back at the foothills as ordered. The escort commander is uneasy; if the party engages him, he will admit he thinks the order is wrong. He follows it anyway.*

**Investigation and skill checks:**
- DC 11 Survival (the courier's horse): Tethered for at least three days. Someone passed by and did not take it — the road has not been entirely unmonitored.
- DC 13 Investigation (the bridge): Deliberate, professional demolition. An organized force with engineering knowledge, not a raiding party.
- DC 14 Investigation (the dead patrol): No defensive wounds. Either a concealed ambush or an approach by someone appearing nonthreatening. DC 16 Medicine: wounds consistent with military weapons, not improvised ones. These were trained fighters who didn't expect a fight.
- DC 13 Perception (the foothills, as the escort turns back): Smoke on the southern horizon. Not festival bonfires — wrong color, wrong direction, wrong amount. Coming from the valley where the Bastion of the Bright Watch should be.

**Encounter: Cult Advance Scouts**

Before the party reaches the high ground above the Bastion, they encounter a four-person cult scouting patrol — positioned on the road to prevent any contact between Alcidea and the Brotherhood's fortress.

*Creature types:* 4 **Cult Scouts** (use Spy stat block, Appendix A). They are not in formation; they are positioned to cover sight lines from natural terrain features, watching the road.

*Tactics:* The scouts will attempt to stop the party quietly — ideally killing them without raising an alarm, failing that retreating to warn the siege commander. One scout has a signal horn. If the horn sounds, note it: the siege commander will know someone approached from the north, which affects the next scene.

*If the party attempts stealth:* DC 13 group Stealth check to approach without triggering the scouts' detection. On success, the party may act with surprise.

*If the party captures a scout alive:* DC 14 Persuasion or Intimidation extracts: the scout was told they serve "the old truth" and was assigned to watch the road. They believe they are servants of Umgul. There are "hundreds" at the Bastion. A commander they have never met personally directs operations from inside the encampment; the scouts call this figure "the Voice."

### Scene 3: The Siege Perimeter

The party reaches the high ground above the valley and observes the siege for the first time.

> *From the ridge, the Bastion of the Bright Watch is immediately recognizable as the Brotherhood's home: white stone built into the cliff face, towers designed to give archers maximum elevation over every approach. From here you can see why it has not fallen — the approaches are narrow, the walls are thick, and the three fires on the eastern tower are burning steadily, which means they've been watching this ridge.*
>
> *The encampment below is not what you expected. It is organized. Guard fires at regular intervals around the perimeter. Rotation patterns visible at the entry points. No banners, no sigils on the pavilions — nothing that would tell you whose army this is. But it is an army. Several hundred people down there, and they are not going anywhere.*

*The three fires on the eastern tower are the Brotherhood's signal — lit and maintained in hope of exactly this contact.*

*If the scout's horn sounded in Scene 2:* A patrol from the siege perimeter has moved toward the high ground. The party has approximately 20 minutes before it arrives, which limits their reconnaissance time considerably.

**Investigation and skill checks:**
- DC 13 Perception (observing the encampment): The perimeter rotations are professional — not tribal militia, not a raiding party. Someone has drilled this force. DC 16: small ritual sites are visible at several points around the perimeter. Stones arranged in patterns, objects placed within them. Not the upturned sun symbol of Altair.
- DC 15 Religion (if the ritual sites are spotted): The iconography is unfamiliar. Not Umgul as the kingdom has recorded it. Not any recognized branch of Altair worship. DC 18: the patterns are void-referent — they orient to absence rather than to any positive symbol. Genuinely unfamiliar.
- DC 12 Survival (reading the encampment's supply arrangement): Provisions for at least two more weeks. This was planned well in advance.

### Chapter 1 Conclusion

The party has confirmed the siege, assessed the force, and established that the Brotherhood is ready to coordinate. Award **Level 4** when the party completes their reconnaissance and commits to a plan for Chapter 2. The Jinsoona is now six days away.

## Chapter 2: The Siege of the Order *(Levels 4–5)*

### Chapter Summary

The party breaks the siege of the Bastion of the Bright Watch through a combined operation with the Brotherhood's surviving adepts — fighting through the outer ring of the encampment, coordinating a Brotherhood sortie from the fortress gates, and routing a force that has numbers, discipline, and organizational coherence on its side. The battle is won not by overwhelming force but by creating chaos in the right place at the right moment.

In the aftermath — the bodies, the ritual sites, the captured documents — the Brotherhood's scholar-fighters begin piecing together something that does not fit the kingdom's understanding of what they have spent generations suppressing. Marcus Ivor reaches the conclusion first, because of something he saw years ago in Vista that he has never been able to explain. The evidence is not dramatic. It is a collection of small wrong things that accumulate into an impossible conclusion: what attacked the Brotherhood of Light was not the Umgul cult. It was not Altair's servants. It was something the Brotherhood has no theological category for.

### Scene 1: Breaking the Cordon

The party must reach the Bastion — either by punching through the siege perimeter directly or by creating enough chaos to trigger the Brotherhood's sortie. The approach from the high ground passes through terrain that provides cover for a careful advance but puts the party inside the cultist perimeter before they reach the gates.

> *The approach follows a dry streambed that cuts through the encampment's western edge — the weakest point in the perimeter from what you could read above. In the dark, with the distant hilltop bonfires of early Jinsoona preparations visible on the northern horizon, the encampment resolves into heat and noise. They are singing something. The words don't carry this far, but it does not sound like any festival song you know.*

*The party has three approaches:*

*Option A — Stealth approach through the streambed:* A group Stealth check (DC 14) gets the party within striking distance of the western guard post without raising a general alarm. On success, they can neutralize the post quietly and signal the Brotherhood from close range, or push through to the gate before the alarm sounds.

*Option B — Distraction and rush:* Create a diversion on the eastern perimeter (fire, noise, the captured signal horn from Chapter 1) while the party moves through the west. Requires a Deception or Performance check (DC 13) for a convincing diversion, then an Athletics or Acrobatics check (DC 12) to clear the perimeter gap before guards investigate.

*Option C — Direct assault:* Attack the western guard post and fight through. This raises a general alarm within 1 round. The Brotherhood will see the chaos from the eastern tower and begin their sortie — but the party will be fighting their way to the gate with pursuit closing.*

*The Brotherhood sortie:* If the party signals from inside the perimeter (three torchlight flashes in any direction from within the outer ring), or if combat erupts and is sustained for 2 or more rounds, the Brotherhood's gates open and an adept team of 8 fighters sallies out — experienced, aggressive, and very glad to see someone who is not the enemy.

**Encounter: The Perimeter Guards**

*Creature types:* 5 **Cultist Soldiers** (use Veteran stat block, 36 HP each, Appendix A) and 1 **Cult Sergeant** (use Bandit Captain stat block, Appendix A). The sergeant has a signal horn.

*Tactics:* Soldiers fight in pairs, covering each other's flanks. The sergeant attempts to reach the horn if the fight turns against them. If the horn sounds before the second round, treat as general alarm: two additional groups of six soldiers begin converging from adjacent posts (arriving in 3 rounds each).

*If the party neutralizes the horn threat first:* They have 2–3 rounds to act before the relief rotation discovers the silent post manually.

### Scene 2: The Battle of the Bright Watch

The combined operation: the party fighting through the interior of the encampment while the Brotherhood sortie engages the eastern perimeter. The goal is to collapse the encampment's organizational coherence, not to defeat every combatant.

> *The Brotherhood comes out fighting — eight of them in white-and-gold moving through the eastern chaos at a pace that makes the cultists scramble. Someone on the western wall has spotted you and is sending shouts of encouragement in your direction. The encampment, which was organized twenty minutes ago, is becoming something else. Not a rout yet. But the edges are fraying.*

*The battle is structured as a sequence of challenges rather than a single mass engagement. The party needs to accomplish two things:*

*1. Neutralize the command post.* The siege's tactical leadership is coordinating the response from a central pavilion. The cult officer commanding the siege is not Orestes — this is a mid-level inner-circle member, a genuine believer with real tactical training. Disrupting the command post ends coordinated resistance within 4 rounds. The cult officer is attempting to burn documents in a brazier when the party arrives.

*2. Reach the Brotherhood sortie.* Once the command post falls, the siege force begins fragmenting. The Brotherhood's adepts push from the east; if the party can fight toward them, the two forces form a combined line that completes the rout.

**Encounter: The Cult Officer**

*Creature type:* 1 **Cult Officer** (use Knight stat block, 60 HP, Appendix A), with 4 **Cultist Soldiers** as guards.

*Tactics:* The Cult Officer fights as a rearguard, taking one action per round to feed documents into the brazier, while the soldiers try to hold the party back. If the party does not interrupt the burning within 2 rounds of engaging him, the most sensitive documents are lost. If they save the documents: see Scene 3.

*Terrain:* The canvas command pavilion is flammable and collapses dramatically if fire spreads from the brazier (4 rounds from ignition, providing difficult terrain and heavy obscurement as it falls). DC 12 Perception checks required to identify targets clearly within the pavilion interior.

*If the party is incapacitated here:* The Brotherhood sortie reaches them within 1 round and stabilizes the situation. The cult officer escapes. The documents are burned. This is a costly but survivable outcome.

### Scene 3: The Aftermath

The siege is broken. The Brotherhood tends their wounded and their dead. In the daylight, the party and the Brotherhood's scholar-fighters begin examining what the encampment left behind.

> *The Bastion's great hall has the quality of a space that has been under siege: spare, organized, deeply tired. The Brotherhood's surviving adepts move with the controlled efficiency of people who have been maintaining high alertness for eleven days and haven't entirely accepted that the pressure is off. Knight Commander Velas — a compact, scarred woman in her late forties — receives you at the center table with the exact measure of formality appropriate to the situation, which is very little. "We owe you a debt," she says. "Tell me what you found out there."*

*The investigation of the encampment's remnants produces the following findings:*

*The ritual sites around the perimeter are the first thing the Brotherhood examines. Marcus Ivor is the one who stops and stares longest. He doesn't say anything immediately. He photographs the patterns in his memory and says he needs to look at the captured documents.*

*The captured documents (if the party saved them from the brazier) include: internal orders from the Cult Officer to the guard posts; a fragment of a prayer text; several pages of what appears to be a personal religious journal.*

*The prayer text is the key discovery:*
- DC 12 Investigation (reading the prayer text): The language is theologically inconsistent with Altair worship. It does not invoke Altair at all.
- DC 15 Religion: The text references an entity defined by absence rather than by any positive attributes. *"The truth before all truths. The end that has always been. The silence that does not wait."*
- DC 18 Religion: This language pattern is consistent with pre-Divine nihilistic theology — very old, poorly documented, associated with traditions the Xoshan missionaries largely suppressed. This is not Umgul. It is not Altair. It is something that predates both.

*Marcus Ivor reaches this conclusion independently, faster, because of what he saw once in Vista. He will share it in Scene 4.*

*Additional findings:*
- Knight Commander Velas confirms that the three assault attempts used tactics the Brotherhood had never seen from Umgul cultists — precise, adaptive, informed about the Bastion's specific defensive geometry. Someone planned this with real knowledge of the order's capabilities.
- DC 16 Investigation (the personal journal, if recovered): This belonged to the Cult Officer. It records his journey from grief and loss toward the cult's tenets. The No-God's philosophy is legible here, clearly and compellingly articulated: the world as a lie, suffering as the consequence of attachment to false permanence, oblivion as the only true release. It is well-reasoned. It is entirely without comfort.

### Scene 4: Marcus Ivor

Marcus Ivor seeks the party out — or can be found if the party looks. He wants to share what he has concluded, and he is being careful about how he says it.

> *He finds you in the evening, when the Bastion is quieter. He has the prayer text in his hand, folded, but he sets it on the table between you rather than keeping it. "I have been trying to find a category for this for six hours," he says. "I don't have one. Neither does our library, and we have the best archive on Umgul cult practice in the kingdom." He pauses. "I need to tell you what I think this is. I need to be clear that I don't know — I have a theory, and I have seen something adjacent to it before, and I am not certain they are the same thing. But I think they might be."*

*Marcus tells the party — carefully, in the manner of a man who has learned not to make claims he cannot support — that what the Brotherhood fought at the Bright Watch was not the Umgul cult. Not in any meaningful sense. The outer layer of believers may have thought they were serving Umgul, but the texts and the ritual structure point somewhere older and more specific: toward a pre-Divine entity that predates the Twelve Divines, whose theological character is purely nihilistic. Not destruction as a means. Destruction as an end. Existence itself as the target.*

*He saw something like this once, years ago, in Vista. He has never been able to classify it.*

*On recruitment:* Marcus is willing to accompany the party. Knight Commander Velas will release him — she considers it an appropriate way to repay the debt, and she believes Marcus is the member of the order most useful to an investigation that has crossed into theological territory the Brotherhood is not equipped for. His condition is simple: he wants to understand what this is. Whatever the party is pursuing, the answer to that question is at the end of it. He will go.

*If the party doesn't think to ask:* Commander Velas suggests it before they leave.

**Skill checks:**
- DC 13 Insight (watching Marcus): He is frightened. Not of the party, not of consequences — of being right.
- DC 14 Persuasion (pressing him on Vista): He describes it briefly. A ritual he interrupted in a lower district. An entity invoked that had no name he recognized. Three people present, all dead before he could question them. He joined the Brotherhood six months later.

### Chapter 2 Conclusion

The siege is broken. Marcus Ivor travels with the party the following morning. Knight Commander Velas sends her own message to Alcidea with a separate rider. Award **Level 5** as the party prepares to depart. The Jinsoona is six days away.

## Chapter 3: The City That Was *(Levels 5–6)*

### Chapter Summary

The party rides north. The Jinsoona is approaching — six days when they leave the Bastion, then fewer with each morning. The signs that something is wrong accumulate as they near the capital: wrong smoke on the horizon, roads empty in the wrong direction, a steady stream of refugees moving south through the hills when everyone should be moving toward the city. By the time the party reaches Alcidea's southern gate, the cult's coordinated strike has already begun.

King Telamon Aristedes Alcimidias II is dead. The capital is falling. The party fights through burning streets, finds the prince's extraction already in motion, and protects the flight to the Citadel against organized pursuit — all in a single night that ends, if they survive it, with the Mazrok gate closing behind them and the kingdom they knew burning in the valley below.

### Scene 1: Something Is Wrong on the Road

The party is approximately a half-day's ride from Alcidea when the evidence becomes undeniable.

> *The road has been getting quieter since morning — not the deliberate empty-quiet of the foothills, but the wrong direction of movement. Everyone you pass is going south. Families with carts loaded with what they grabbed. Two legionary soldiers in full armor who give you a single hard look and keep moving. A woman carrying a child who will not stop and who will not tell you what she saw. The festival bonfires that should be marking the Jinsoona across every hilltop are lit, but the smoke is wrong — too black, too much, coming from too many places. Alcidea is still over the southern ridge. The glow above the ridge is orange.*

*A DC 11 Perception check reveals the smell of smoke before the city comes into view. DC 13: the fires are concentrated in the northern quarter of the city. Where the palace is.*

*Refugees encountered on the road (DC 12 Persuasion):* The city gates were open for the festival procession. Something came through them. It has been happening since sundown. The palace is surrounded. The barracks are on fire. The streets between the gate and the forum are not safe. No one knows about the king. No one has seen the king.

**Encounter: Cult Forward Unit**

A group of cultists is positioned on the road specifically to intercept soldiers or officials returning to the city who might organize a response.

*Creature types:* 2 **Cult Veterans** (use Veteran stat block, Appendix A) and 6 **Cultist Soldiers** (use Bandit stat block, Appendix A), waiting at a road chokepoint.

*Tactics:* Their orders are to prevent organized military response from reaching the city. They will attempt to take the party prisoner if possible — dead soldiers are noise at a stage when the operation benefits from confusion, not clarity. If the party bypasses the chokepoint through terrain (DC 15 Survival or Stealth), the encounter is avoided.

*If a prisoner is taken* (DC 12 Intimidation): The city was hit at sundown across six points simultaneously — the palace gate, the barracks, the temple of Ilura, the Kryptea offices, the Magisterium, and the treasury. The cultist does not know if the king is alive. They were not sent to the palace; they were sent to the road.

### Scene 2: The Southern Gate

The party enters Alcidea during the height of the chaos. The gate is open and unguarded.

> *The gate stands open. The guard post beside it is empty and dark. The festival lanterns are still burning along the processional street, throwing orange light onto stone that is also lit from somewhere deeper in by something that is not a lantern. The sounds are wrong: shouting that doesn't have the shape of celebration, a bell being rung in the wrong rhythm, no music. The street ahead is empty except for a single cart abandoned sideways across the road. The horse is still in the traces, completely still, watching the city with the calm of an animal that has decided not to understand what it is seeing.*

*The cult is not yet in full control of the streets — they are still managing pockets of armed resistance and a population that is not cooperating. The party has options other than direct combat; the city is chaotic and the cult does not have enough eyes to watch every alley.*

*Movement through the city toward the palace district requires navigating occupied streets, fire, and panicking crowds. Use the following framework per city block:*

| Situation | Check | Failure Result |
|---|---|---|
| Slipping past a cult patrol | DC 14 group Stealth | Patrol spots the party; 4 **Cultist Soldiers** engage |
| Moving through smoke-filled streets | DC 12 Constitution saving throw | Disadvantage on attack rolls until 10 minutes of rest |
| Navigating around a collapsed building or barricade | DC 13 Athletics or Survival | 10-minute delay |
| Getting information from a panicked civilian | DC 13 Persuasion | Nothing useful without showing obvious authority |

*Scenes en route:* The DM should include 1–2 street encounters that give texture to the fall of Alcidea without stopping the party's momentum — a city guard holding a doorway against three cult soldiers, a temple of Ilura where the junior clergy have barred the doors and the sick and elderly inside, a retreating legion squad fighting a rearguard action down an alleyway. These are not required encounters; they are the city dying around the party, and the party has to decide what they can afford to stop for.

### Scene 3: The Palace District

The palace district is more heavily occupied than the streets leading to it. The cult has prioritized the palace. What the party finds depends on how quickly they've moved.

> *The palace gate is open. The bodies in the forecourt are royal guards — you can tell by the armor, the formal kind worn for a festival procession. There are too many of them. The light inside the palace is coming from the wrong direction, which means fire in the interior somewhere.*

*King Telamon Aristedes Alcimidias II is dead. He was killed in the throne room by a cult agent who had served in the palace staff for three years under a false identity. General Heskalo reached him within minutes of it happening. There was nothing to be done. The General has not said anything since then that did not involve operational orders.*

*Heskalo is in the palace's eastern courtyard with Prince Eurimedos, Abala Kadar, and approximately twelve royal guard survivors, preparing to extract through a servants' passage that connects below the wall to the Mazrok staircase approach. He is operating on professional discipline alone.*

*How the party reaches the General:*
- DC 13 Investigation or Perception (searching the palace approaches): Signs of organized movement toward the eastern wing — fresh boot prints, a dropped document in the General's handwriting, a surviving guard who saw which direction the survivors went.
- Asking any palace staff (DC 11 Persuasion, since most are terrified and grateful for anyone with authority): They point toward the east wing.

*When the party reaches the General:* He does not stop moving when they arrive. He incorporates them. *"Good. We leave in two minutes. The eastern passage goes under the wall — it comes out sixty yards from the staircase base. We move fast and we don't stop until we're through the gate at the top."*

*The prince is here.* Eurimedos is standing next to Abala with his jaw set in a way that a child does not normally set it. He is holding a small book against his chest. He has not asked a single question since his father's room. The General keeps glancing at him with an expression that suggests this silence is more worrying than crying would have been.

**Skill check:**
- DC 12 Insight (observing the prince): He is in shock, not composure. The stillness is not self-control — it is the stillness of someone who has not yet fully processed what they are being still about. He is eight years old and his father is dead and no one has had time to tell him this directly.

### Scene 4: The Road to Mazrok

The extraction moves through the underground servants' passage and emerges into the chaos outside. The staircase base is visible. It is also watched.

> *The passage smells of smoke — the ventilation shafts have been pulling it in from the palace interior. When you emerge into the night air, it is immediately clear that the chaos is closer here than it was at the front of the palace. The staircase base is ahead — the massive gatehouse at the foot of the cliff, with the long stone stairs visible above through the smoke, switchbacking upward toward a gate that is closed, as it must be. There are figures between you and it. A dozen, at least, with more hearing the sounds and moving toward the noise.*

*The cult does not know about the servants' passage; they are positioned on the main road to the gate, not covering the servants' exit. This gives the party a round or two before the nearest group spots them.*

*Option A — Fight through:* A running engagement against the first group (4 **Cultist Soldiers**) while the General's party moves to the gatehouse. Additional cultists begin converging each round (2–3 per round from adjacent streets). The goal is to reach the gatehouse, not to defeat everyone present.

*Option B — Stealth sprint:* DC 14 group Stealth check. Disadvantage unless the party splits, with the General taking the slower civilian group separately. On success, they reach the gatehouse before being spotted. On failure, they're spotted at close range — collapse into Option A.

**Encounter: The Gate Fight**

*Creature types:* 4 **Cultist Soldiers** (Bandit stat block) guarding the gatehouse entrance, with reinforcements arriving in waves.

*Tactics:* The gatehouse guards are covering it in case of a palace breakout attempt. They fight defensively, trying to hold the door until reinforcements arrive rather than pushing the attack. Once the party is through the gatehouse and the door is forced, the reinforcements will not follow up the stairs — the staircase is extraordinarily defensible, and they know it.

*If any PC is incapacitated here:* General Heskalo picks them up if they are within reach. He is that kind of soldier.

### Scene 5: The Cliff Stairs

Six hundred feet of carved stone stairs in the dark, with the city burning below and the Citadel gate sealed above.

> *The stairs rise in the dark above you, switchbacking across the cliff face in a geometry that makes the body want to lean into the rock rather than away from it. Below, Alcidea is orange and loud with things that are not festival sounds. The staircase is wide enough for two people abreast; handrails are cut into the stone on the cliff side, and nothing on the other. The top is not visible. The gate is up there somewhere. It is closed, as it must be — it can only be opened from within.*

*The climb takes approximately twenty minutes for a group in fair condition. The party and the General's survivors make it most of the way before a group of cult soldiers, having decided the staircase is worth attempting, begins ascending behind them.*

**Encounter: The Pursuit**

*Creature types:* 8 **Cultist Soldiers** (Bandit stat block) ascending the stairs. They are at the base when the party is approximately two-thirds of the way up.

*Tactics:* The staircase heavily favors defenders — fighting downhill on narrow stone against enemies below. The cultists push if they believe they can close the distance; they halt and hold position if the party turns and engages, driving them back even briefly. A DC 14 group Athletics check keeps the party ahead of the pursuit without combat; failure means the cultists close to melee range at a switchback.

*If the party turns to fight:* Two characters can hold a switchback effectively against four pursuing cultists. This delays the party's arrival by one round per round of combat, which matters only if the Citadel gate is not yet opening — which it will be.

*The Citadel gate:* The gate watch has been observing the approach through the smoke by signal lamp since the party was spotted at the base. The gate is already opening when the party arrives at the top. The gate guard commander is a veteran corporal who has been watching Alcidea burn for hours. He has decided that whatever comes through that gate is going to answer for it if it is not the right people. It is the right people.

> *The gate closes with a sound like a judgment. Below, the city is still burning. Through the arrow-slot windows of the gatehouse, the bonfires of the Jinsoona are visible across the valley — every hilltop, every village, every place where people have gathered to celebrate the goddess's night. And in among them, the other fires. The ones that don't belong to the festival.*
>
> *General Heskalo puts one hand flat against the closed gate. Holds it there for a moment. Then removes it and turns around.*
>
> *"Find the prince quarters," he says to no one in particular. "Find him something to eat. And someone find me a map."*

### Chapter 3 Conclusion

The party has reached the Citadel of Mazrok. Act I is complete. Award **Level 6** when the gate closes. Act II begins the following morning, when Delana Geminia Sabinus sits down with the party at the map table and begins telling them what she has managed to learn about the scope of what happened — and how much of it she suspects Alcidea already knew was coming, before the fires were lit.

#### Mission 1: The Rite at Darova
*Spiritual | Wave 1 | Expiration: Fast (25% → 50% → 75% → lost)*

**Overview:** An ancient sacred site in the southern highlands — a hilltop ring of standing stones predating the Xoshan colonization — has been occupied by a cult cell conducting an ongoing ritual. Delana's contact in the region reports the ritual is days from completion. After that, the site becomes a permanent anchor for cult supernatural activity in the region.

**Background (DM Only):** The site, called the **Darova Ring**, was an Artaxartoi sacred place to Ilura long before Xoshan missionaries arrived. The cult has deliberately chosen sites with pre-existing spiritual significance. The ritual being conducted is a "binding" — the No-God's version of consecration, anchoring the location to a void-adjacent resonance that weakens divine magic performed nearby and accelerates the No-God's philosophical influence in the surrounding area. The cult cell here consists of a ritual leader (inner-circle) and approximately 20 outer-layer believers who think they are serving Umgul.

**Scene 1: The Road to Darova**

A Kryptea contact of Delana's — a shepherd named **Oryn** — meets the party in the valley below the Darova Ring.

> *The shepherd is waiting at the sheepfold where Delana said he would be — a slight, weathered man who watches you approach without moving. When you're close enough, he speaks without greeting. "They have maybe three days. Maybe two. I can't get close enough to tell anymore — the air near the ring makes my animals sick. They won't go near it."*

*Oryn provides: approximately 20–25 figures at the ring, guard posts on the two approach paths, the ritual leader performing at the central stone at dawn and dusk. He has not seen weapons other than knives, but he notes: "The violent kind I'm not worried about. These are the devoted kind. I consider that worse."*

*DC 12 Persuasion (asking Oryn to guide them):* He will take them to a vantage point above the ring and no further.

**Scene 2: The Darova Ring**

> *From the ridge above the ring, the ritual site is visible in the early morning light. The standing stones — eight of them, perhaps four feet high, arranged in a rough circle — have been marked with chalk or ash in patterns that make the eye want to slide away from them. At the center, a figure in dark robes kneels at the largest stone. Around the ring's outer edge, at intervals, you can make out the shapes of people sitting or standing. They are very still.*

*The challenge is the open terrain — no cover within 40 yards on any approach. The party's options:*

*Option A — Dawn strike:* The ritual leader is stationary at the central stone. A coordinated rush from two directions while guards are focused on the kneeling figure can neutralize the posts before an alarm is raised.

*Option B — Nocturnal approach:* DC 15 group Stealth in total darkness. No guard posts visible at night, but the ritual leader is also absent — they cannot be confronted or the ritual disrupted without finding them first.

*Option C — Disguise:* DC 14 Deception to pass as outer-layer cultists. Gets the party close to the ritual leader without alerting the site. Breaks on any direct combat or if the ritual leader succeeds on a DC 14 Insight check.

*If the ritual leader survives more than 3 rounds after an alarm is raised:* They complete a partial binding. The full anchor does not take effect, but divine magic cast within the ring has a 25% chance of failing for one month.*

**Encounter: The Ritual Leader and the Ring**

*Creature type:* 1 **Cult Ritual Leader** (use Priest stat block with *Charm Person*, *Command*, and *Inflict Wounds* prepared, 27 HP, Appendix A). 20 **Devoted Cultists** (Commoner stat block) — believers, not fighters. They will not attack PCs unless directly threatened, but they will physically shield the ritual leader. They are, in many cases, people who joined through genuine suffering and have committed no violence.

**Scene 3: The Aftermath**

The ritual leader carries a satchel containing: a ritual manual in cipher (Delana can partially decode it); a correspondence fragment referencing orders from "the Architect" directing site priorities; and a list of 14 individuals in the five cities marked as conversion candidates, with notes on the specific grief that makes each susceptible.

- DC 13 Investigation (the satchel): The correspondence fragment is recovered.
- DC 16: Two names on the candidates list match people General Heskalo mentioned by name in his first briefing. People he trusts. *Delana will want to see this immediately.*

| Result | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Success** | Ritual disrupted; site cleansed; satchel recovered (first Orestes puzzle piece); cult loses a binding site in the southern region |
| **Failure (expired or partial)** | Ritual completes; +1 Corruption (the anchor actively seeks susceptible minds); no documents recovered |

#### Mission 2: The Captives at Vennara
*Civic | Wave 1 | Expiration: Fast (25% → 50% → 75% → lost)*

**Overview:** The cult has rounded up the adult male population of the farming village of Vennara — roughly 80 men and older boys — and is using them as forced labor on cult fortifications in the eastern valley. Delana's contact says they will be transferred to a more secured location within two weeks. After the transfer, recovery becomes effectively impossible.

**Background (DM Only):** The captives are held in a converted granary at the edge of the fortification site. The fortifications are a significant permanent installation the cult is building to control valley access. Among the captives is a former legion quartermaster named **Phaedros**, who has been mentally mapping the construction in detail and whose knowledge of cult supply routes is genuinely valuable. Approximately 60 soldiers guard both the fortification site and the captives.

**Scene 1: The Fortification Site**

> *The fortification is not what you expected from a group that hit the capital in one night and vanished. This is deliberate construction — earthworks, palisades, what looks like the foundation of a permanent gatehouse in stone. It is perhaps a third complete. The granary complex at the eastern end is enclosed on two sides by the new works. The captives are in there; you can hear them from the approach road.*

- DC 13 Perception (from the ridge): Guard rotation at the main gate — 4 soldiers, changing every 2 hours. A patrol of 3 circles the perimeter on a roughly 20-minute interval.
- DC 14 Survival (reading the construction): The fortification is built to hold a choke point, not to attack outward. The cult is planning a long occupation.

**Scene 2: The Granary**

> *The granary's interior smells of grain and fear. The men inside are intact — they have been fed, kept in workable condition; the cult wants workers, not corpses — but they have been here for three weeks, and the faces that turn toward you when the door opens carry the particular look of people who have stopped expecting the door to mean rescue.*

*A successful rescue requires: neutralizing the granary guards (6 **Cultist Soldiers**, Bandit stat block), getting 80 people out before the perimeter patrol returns (20 minutes), and extracting through terrain without a clean road.*

*Phaedros identifies himself immediately. His intelligence: ~60 soldiers total at the site, supply depot location, and construction materials coming from the north — which tells him where the cult supply route runs (cross-references with M3).*

*DC 12 Persuasion (organizing the captives):* Getting 80 frightened people to move quietly requires calm authority. Failure means the perimeter patrol arrives 5 minutes early.

| Result | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Success** | 80 captives freed; Phaedros provides supply route intelligence; the regional Sadar begins the Jurga Neutral rather than Skeptical; Delana's eastern valley network strengthened |
| **Failure (expired)** | Captives transferred; the eastern Sadar begins the Jurga Skeptical; supply route intelligence not recovered |

#### Mission 3: The Broken Spear
*Military | Wave 1 | Expiration: Standard (15% → 30% → 45% → ...)*

**Overview:** A Zavra-Prag warband from the Zedan clan — approximately 150 riders — was ambushed by cult forces three weeks ago in the northern uplands. The survivors, roughly 60 riders, have retreated to a defensible position in the high terrain and are surrounded by a cult blocking force. Their chieftain, Arslan Zedan, has sent a message asking whether anyone is coming. Delana believes the message may have been compromised.

**Background (DM Only):** The ambush was deliberate — the cult targeted Arslan specifically because his cavalry tactics are dangerous to their operational security. The blocking force is 80 soldiers waiting for a relief-in-force to finish the job. Arslan has been holding through tactical creativity and stubbornness. He is running out of both.

**Scene 1: The High Pass**

> *The pass narrows here, and it has been watched — you can tell by the boot prints going the wrong direction and the cold fire ring that someone used one night and then carefully covered. The cult has a screen on this approach. Arslan's warband is maybe three miles above you, and between you and them, there are people who do not want you to arrive.*

- DC 13 Survival (tracking the blocking force's patrol pattern): Three patrol groups rotating on roughly 4-hour intervals. The current patrol cleared this area approximately 90 minutes ago.
- DC 14 Perception (scanning the high terrain): An observation post above the tree line — two figures, motionless.

*A group Stealth check (DC 14) bypasses the patrol cleanly. Failure means the blocking force receives a signal within 1 hour, tightening the cordon before the party reaches Arslan.*

**Scene 2: Arslan's Position**

> *Arslan Zedan is not what the Sadars' accounts of him suggested. He is young — mid-twenties at most — and what his reputation did not prepare you for is visible immediately: the quality of attention in his face, the way his riders clearly watch him rather than the threat, the precision with which he assesses you in the first three seconds of your arrival. "You're late," he says. "I stopped counting the days a week ago. It was getting depressing."*

*Arslan's situation: 58 functional riders, 8 wounded, 4 days of food remaining. The blocking force is 80 soldiers in three groups covering the viable descent routes. His assessment: a direct breakout through the western route at night is possible at high cost; with outside assistance creating a diversion at the eastern route, the cost drops considerably.*

*Arslan engages on equal terms. He is a chieftain, not a subordinate, and he establishes this in the first five minutes.*

**Scene 3: The Breakout**

> *The eastern blocking post erupts exactly when Arslan said it would. Below you, on the western slope, you can hear the riders — no shouting, just hooves on rock, moving fast. The cult soldiers at the eastern post are choosing between the threat in front of them and the sound behind them. That indecision is the window.*

*Encounter:* 1 **Cult Lieutenant** (Veteran stat block, 58 HP) and 10 **Cultist Soldiers** (Bandit stat block) at the eastern post. Distracted (disadvantage on Perception) for the first round when the breakout begins.

*The party holds the eastern post occupied for 3 rounds. After 3 rounds, signal fires on the western ridgeline confirm the warband is through. If the party goes down before 3 rounds: Arslan loses 15 additional riders but the breakout still succeeds.*

**The Recruitment Conversation**

Once the warband is clear, Arslan finds the party.

*"I want the people who did this. All of them, eventually, but specifically the ones who planned the ambush." He pauses. "I understand you work for the prince. I'm prepared to work for the prince too, if the prince is the kind of authority that lets me aim in the direction I need to aim." If the party engages honestly — including the complications, not just the mission — he commits. He will bring his remaining riders and take orders from General Heskalo, with the understanding that his warband is his.*

*DC 13 Persuasion is not required if the party has been honest with him. It is required if they have been managing or evading him.*

| Result | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Success** | Arslan and ~58 riders extracted; Arslan recruitable; his faction track opens at Neutral; blocking force neutralized or scattered |
| **Failure (expired)** | Arslan breaks out alone with ~20 riders before the window closes; still alive but embittered; warband reduced to 80 riders even if eventually recruited; DC 17 Persuasion for recruitment |

#### Mission 4: The Converted Brothers
*Spiritual | Wave 2 | Expiration: Standard (15% → 30% → 45% → ...)*

**Overview:** Two Brotherhood of Light adepts captured during the Act I siege have been located — not dead. They are being held at a cult facility in the central valley and subjected to an extended psychological conditioning process. Delana believes a window exists before the process becomes irreversible.

**Background (DM Only):** The cult's conversion methodology does not break people through torture — it finds what they already believe is wrong with the world and provides a framework that validates that belief, escalates it, and makes oblivion feel like a reasonable conclusion. The two adepts, **Brothers Calix and Demetros**, have been isolated and engaged in continuous philosophical dialogue for two months. Calix is close to breaking. Demetros has already broken — he now genuinely believes in what he serves. Recovering Calix is fully possible. Recovering Demetros in the time available is not.

**Scene 1: The Conversion Facility**

The facility is a converted manor house that appears, from the outside, to be operating normally.

> *The manor sits in the middle of its own fields, which look normal — recently worked, fences intact, smoke from the kitchen chimney. Two figures are visible at the gate, dressed as farmhands. Without knowing what to look for, you would not see the symbols cut into the gatepost. With knowing: they are there.*

- DC 12 Investigation (the grounds): The farmhands carry themselves wrong — not farmhands.
- DC 14: A structure behind the manor that does not match agricultural use — low, windowless, reinforced door.

*The manor has 12 cultist guards total: 4 visible, 8 rotating through the interior and the rear structure. The conversion rooms are underground.*

**Scene 2: The Cells**

> *The cells are not what you expected. No chains. Each is a small room — pallet, lamp, books. The books are cult texts. The door is locked from outside.*
>
> *In the first cell: a man who sits up when he hears you, and his face has the quality of someone who has been offered hope so many times and had it withdrawn that he no longer fully processes its arrival. "Calix," he says, when you ask. "My name is Brother Calix. Is Marcus Ivor with you?" He looks at you very carefully. "Is it actually you?"*
>
> *In the second cell: Demetros does not stand. He looks at the party with the calm of someone who has reached a conclusion and is no longer troubled by the world. "You've come to take me back," he says. "I won't go."*

*DC 14 Persuasion (reaching Demetros):* Possible but insufficient for reversal — the conditioning is too complete. On success, he is confused and silent rather than compliant; he will need months of care this campaign does not have. The party can recover him physically; they cannot recover him theologically on this timeline.*

**Encounter: The Practitioner**

The conversion practitioner will initially attempt to prevent the party from leaving through philosophical engagement rather than combat — arguing, calmly, that what they have done to the Brothers is an act of compassion.

*Creature type:* 1 **Cult Inner-Circle Priest** (Priest stat block, 27 HP). Fights only if cornered. 8 **Cultist Soldiers** (Bandit stat block) respond to combat noise within 2 rounds.

*DC 15 Insight (watching the Practitioner):* They genuinely believe what they are saying. They have been through the same process they are administering. This is the No-God's methodology in its most unsettling form: it works.*

| Result | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Success (both recovered)** | Calix rescued; recovers within weeks; becomes an intelligence asset for Marcus Ivor regarding conversion methodology; Demetros recovered but unreliable |
| **Partial success (Calix only)** | Calix rescued; Demetros remains; Marcus Ivor finds out and the scene is worth running |
| **Failure (expired)** | Both fully converted; Calix and Demetros appear in Act III as cult operatives; Marcus Ivor learns what happened |

#### Mission 5: The Voice of Seras
*Civic | Wave 2 | Expiration: Standard (15% → 30% → 45% → ...)*

**Overview:** The city of Seras has an active loyalist resistance cell of approximately 40 people, organized by a former city magistrate named **Iloria Caspius**. They have been sending messages to Mazrok through a channel Delana believes is now compromised. The cell needs to be contacted, the channel replaced, and their operation put on more secure footing before the cult locates them.

**Background (DM Only):** Caspius is competent and has organized the cell well given her resources — former city guards, non-collaborating traders, two junior Kryptea assets she happened to know personally. The compromised channel is a message runner who was caught and doubled by the cult. The runner knows the cell's general location but not its specific members. The cult has perhaps two weeks before their investigation closes in.

**Scene 1: The Contact**

> *Seras is quieter than it should be for a city of its size. The market is open but thin — stalls that require supply lines are empty, and the vendors who remain watch strangers with the specific quality of attention that has learned to be unobtrusive about itself. Something in the way conversations end when a patrol passes. No music.*

*Entry requires a plausible cover (DC 13 Deception at the gate). Finding Caspius requires following a contact protocol — three locations in sequence, each with a sign confirming the chain is intact (DC 11 Investigation at each location).*

**Scene 2: Caspius**

> *Iloria Caspius is in her late forties and was clearly, before everything changed, someone who dressed better than this. She wastes no time. "The channel is burned," she says, before you have finished explaining who you are. "I figured it out three days ago. I've been waiting to see who would come through it — you, or them." She studies you. "It's you. Good. Now tell me what you actually need from us, because I don't have people to spare and I need to know whether what you're asking is worth the risk."*

*Caspius needs: a new secure channel to Mazrok; confirmation the crown still exists in a form worth maintaining contact with; and an honest assessment of the timeline. DC 13 Persuasion — she responds to directness and notices if she is being managed. If the party is honest about the difficulty, she commits more fully.*

**Scene 3: The Burned Runner**

Before the party leaves, Caspius tells them about the compromised runner — a young man named **Bevos** who she knows was doubled. She has not moved on him because doing so would confirm to the cult that she knows.

*The party's options: leave him in place (the cult's information is stale enough to be viable for a few more weeks); extract him covertly (he is frightened and acted under coercion — DC 15 Persuasion to flip him back; his intelligence on the cult investigation is useful); or neutralize him (Caspius will do it if asked, and will feel it, and the party should understand what they are asking her to do).*

| Result | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Success** | Caspius cell secured with new channel; Seras resistance activated; Bevos situation resolved; active intelligence asset in the five cities |
| **Failure (expired)** | Cell arrested; Caspius publicly executed; loyalist morale in Seras collapses; civilian network cannot advance beyond tier 1 |

#### Mission 6: The Legion's Banner
*Military | Wave 2 | Expiration: Standard (15% → 30% → 45% → ...)*

**Overview:** Tribune **Maro Vennicus**, commanding approximately 400 surviving legionaries in the eastern valleys, has been operating independently since the fall of Alcidea. His last lawful orders came from King Telamon. He has sent a message to Mazrok requesting authentication of command authority before he will transfer loyalty to the prince's banner.

**Background (DM Only):** Vennicus is doing exactly the right thing — holding his formation together, avoiding engagements he cannot win, waiting for a legitimate authority. He is skeptical not because he doubts the prince's bloodline but because an eight-year-old king in a mountain fortress is a difficult military proposition to commit 400 soldiers to. What he needs is to meet the General, hear the plan, and have his professional judgment engaged rather than just his obedience commanded.

**Scene 1: Finding the Fourth Legion**

The contact point is a village with loyalist sympathies, where a guide — a former legion supply contractor — can take the party to Vennicus's current position.

> *"He's careful," the guide says, which in context means he is still alive. She takes you east at a pace that suggests she is used to getting people to difficult places quickly.*

*DC 12 Survival (maintaining pace):* A failed check means a 2-hour delay and a brush with a cult patrol (6 Cultist Soldiers, avoidable with DC 13 group Stealth).*

**Scene 2: Tribune Vennicus**

> *The Fourth Legion's camp looks like a Fourth Legion camp. Rows properly maintained, sentries posted with correct spacing, the smell of a field kitchen producing something actual. Tribune Maro Vennicus is a compact man in his late forties who has been sleeping in his armor for two months and has made his peace with it. "General Heskalo sent you," he says. It is not a question. "Tell me about the Citadel."*

*Vennicus engages with specifics. He needs to understand how his 400 fit into a coherent operational picture — not "the prince is the rightful heir" (he knows that), but what it means tactically.*

*DC 14 Persuasion (the case for consolidation):* Military reasoning, not sentiment. If the party articulates how the Fourth Legion fits into the Act III operational plan, he commits. If General Heskalo is present: no check required — Vennicus stands up when the General enters the tent.*

**Scene 3: The Oath (Optional)**

> *Vennicus speaks the oath quietly, looking at the General rather than at any of you, and you realize that what this is, for both of them, is a professional conversation between people who understand what they are promising. The senior centurions speak a line in response. It is not a dramatic scene. It is what the legions do when they mean something.*

#### Mission 7: The Pale Crossing
*Spiritual | Wave 3 | Expiration: Slow (10% → 20% → 30% → ...)*

**Overview:** Delana's network has identified a significant cult ritual site — not a local cell operation like Darova, but a permanent installation the cult has been maintaining for years, predating the Jinsoona strikes. Located at the confluence of two rivers in the central uplands, it is the anchor for the cult's supernatural activities throughout the kingdom's core. Destroying it will materially weaken the cult's ritual capacity and recover correspondence connecting it to a figure inside the royal council.

**Background (DM Only):** The **Pale Crossing** is the cult's oldest established site in Artaxarta, maintained by a predecessor to Orestes and operational for decades. The inner-circle official overseeing it — **Sister Verath**, stationed here for fifteen years — is the most theologically sophisticated cult figure the party has encountered outside of Orestes himself. Destroying the site requires dismantling the ritual superstructure (physical task) and a counter-rite taught to Marcus Ivor by the Brotherhood, which takes 10 minutes and requires concentration — a Concentration check each round against DC 10, +2 for each wound taken. If concentration breaks, the ritual resets. It can be attempted up to 3 times.*

**Scene 1: The Confluence**

> *The confluence is visible from the ridge above the valley — two rivers meeting at a flat stone shelf that nature made and someone improved over decades: dressed stone, carved channels that direct the water around the structure, and at the apex a formation of black glass rods that catch the light wrong, bending it into angles that don't correspond to the sun's position. The sound from here is wrong too — the water sounds like it is moving through a place that does not want it.*

- DC 13 Perception: 20 guards visible around the platform. DC 15: a large permanent building on the far bank serving as quarters for the site's staff.

**Scene 2: The Platform**

> *Sister Verath is at the apex when you arrive — she turns to face you with the manner of someone who has been told this day would come eventually and has spent fifteen years deciding how she would be when it did. She is not young. She does not look afraid. "You understand," she says, "that what you destroy today has no permanence. Nothing does. That is the point."*

*Encounter:*
*Creature types:* 1 **Sister Verath** (use Archmage stat block with *Inflict Wounds*, *Blight*, and *Finger of Death* replacing standard offensive spells, 99 HP, Appendix A). 6 **Cult Veterans** (Veteran stat block). 8 **Devoted Cultists** (Commoner stat block) who physically interpose themselves to protect the ritual structure — each one that reaches the counter-rite performer forces a DC 12 Concentration save.

*Verath's priority is interrupting the counter-rite, not eliminating the party. She will use Finger of Death on the performer if she can reach them.*

**Scene 3: The Collapse**

> *The black glass rods crack — not shatter, crack, as though something inside them has been removed rather than broken. The sound of the water changes back to what water sounds like. Marcus Ivor sits down on the stone platform and puts his face in his hands for a moment. When he raises it, he says: "I need to tell you something about what I felt in there. Not today. But soon."*

*Among Verath's correspondence files: twelve years of communications with a handler identified only by cipher name — but the content references operational decisions Delana can identify as coming from inside the royal council. This does not yet name Orestes, but it points directly at him.*

| Result | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Success** | Major ritual anchor destroyed; cult's supernatural capacity in the central valleys severely degraded; correspondence files recovered (key Orestes identification piece); Orestes weakened at the final confrontation |
| **Failure (expired or counter-rite fails 3 times)** | Anchor remains active; cult retains full supernatural advantage through Act III; no correspondence recovered |

#### Mission 8: The Imprisoned Sadar
*Civic | Wave 3 | Expiration: Slow (10% → 20% → 30% → ...)*

**Overview:** **Sadar Breskan**, leader of the Mila upland clans and controller of approximately 1,200 Zavra-Prag riders, has not responded to any Mazrok communications. Intelligence has established he is alive but effectively held in his own hall — cult operatives positioned themselves as his household guard following the death of his actual guard captain in the Jinsoona attacks. He is under constant surveillance and deeply skeptical of outside intervention.

**Background (DM Only):** Breskan is in his mid-sixties, proud, and historically resentful of Alcidea's tendency to treat the Sadars as instruments rather than partners. He allowed the cult operatives in because their cover story — loyalist soldiers seeking refuge — was plausible in the chaos of the Jinsoona aftermath. He figured out what they were. He has been waiting. What he is waiting for is not entirely clear, even to him. His 1,200 riders are in the valleys below and will follow his direction; whether that direction points toward the Jurga depends on this mission.

**Scene 1: The Mila Uplands**

> *The uplands here are vast and cold and empty in the way of places that are not actually empty — you are being watched from the moment you pass the first clan marker, and the watching is not subtle. It is the watching of people who have decided to make a point about seeing you.*

*DC 14 Persuasion (the clan members watching):* They will not interfere with the approach but will not help — they are waiting to see what happens. *If Arslan Zedan is with the party:* his presence changes the scene; DC 10 Persuasion, and two clan members escort the party to the hall.*

**Scene 2: The Hall**

> *The man at the gate is well-spoken and entirely wrong — not in anything you can point to immediately, but in the combination: the way he holds the gate frame, the position of the two flankers, the fact that Sadar Breskan's clan totem is not displayed anywhere on a hall that should display it on a feast day. "The Sadar is not receiving guests," he says, pleasantly. "These are unsettled times."*

- DC 13 Insight: The gate guards are not clan members — none carry the Mila upland physical type.
- DC 15: Concealed weapons in addition to visible ones.

*The party can: demand entry (the operatives stall and alert companions inside); find another way in (DC 14 Stealth, servant entrances exist); or create a public scene that forces Breskan to acknowledge the situation. Inside, 6 **Cult Operatives** (Spy stat block) are stationed throughout the hall.*

**Scene 3: Breskan**

> *Breskan looks at the bodies of the people who were in his hall for three months and says nothing for a long time. Then he looks at you. "The prince is eight years old. And in a fortress he cannot leave without an army he does not have. Tell me why this is better than negotiating a position with whoever controls Alcidea." He is not being hostile. He is asking the question he has been asking himself every day for two months.*

*DC 14 Persuasion (the case for the Jurga):* Breskan responds to specific arguments, not appeals to loyalty. The best arguments are concrete: the cult is expanding control of upland resources; its conversion practices will eventually reach the clans; the Jurga is a constitutional mechanism Breskan himself is part of — his absence is not neutrality, it is a vote.*

*If Arslan Zedan makes the argument directly (if present):* Arslan and Breskan have history involving grazing disputes, but Breskan respects Arslan's tactical judgment. Arslan speaking to specific threats to the uplands carries more weight than outside arguments; DC 11 Persuasion instead of 14.*

| Result | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Success** | Breskan agrees to attend the Jurga; his 1,200 riders are neutral but available; Sadar Council standing can advance; 1 additional Jurga vote |
| **Failure (expired)** | Breskan remains under cult supervision; 2 fewer Jurga votes available; 1,200 riders unavailable for Act III |

#### Mission 9: The Road of the Araxes
*Military | Wave 3 | Expiration: Standard (15% → 30% → 45% → ...)*

**Overview:** The cult has established a major supply route through the Araxes Valley connecting their occupied territories. A supply convoy moves through every eight days. The next convoy will carry a sealed dispatch case from the occupied palace to cult regional commanders — a case Delana's network believes contains operational planning documents and correspondence. Intercepting it will sever the supply route and may produce the intelligence that finally identifies the operation's director by name.

**Background (DM Only):** The dispatch case contains a sealed inner envelope addressed in the cipher seen before in Mission 1 (Darova satchel) and Mission 7 (Verath's files). Unlike the previous communications, this one is signed with a real name — Orestes Sarkhazi, Lord of the Magisterium. Delana will cross-reference it immediately if the party also recovered documents from Missions 1 and/or 7. If not, the identification still occurs but requires additional analysis (one more turn). This is the most direct path to the Orestes identification milestone.*

**Scene 1: The Araxes Valley**

> *The road through the Araxes Valley was a trade route before it was anything else, and it shows — well-maintained, well-signed, the kind of road that has been used seriously for generations. The cult has been using it seriously too. The ruts from the recent supply wagons are fresh and deep, and there are guard posts at the valley narrows every four miles.*

- DC 13 Survival (reading the road): Convoy runs on a predictable schedule. DC 15: the best ambush point is at the third narrows, where the road bends around a rockfall limiting sight lines to 40 yards.

**Scene 2: The Ambush**

> *The convoy is moving at speed for a supply operation. The escort outriders are watching the ridgeline. You have about thirty seconds before the lead element passes the bend.*

*The ambush requires: initiating with surprise (DC 13 group Stealth from above the rockfall); neutralizing the escort before they organize; and reaching the sealed carriage before the driver destroys the dispatch case (they attempt this in round 3 if not stopped).*

*Encounter:* 16 **Cultist Soldiers** (Bandit stat block) in escort formation; 1 **Convoy Commander** (Bandit Captain stat block) in the sealed carriage. The escort splits immediately on ambush — 8 covering the carriage, 8 dealing with the threat. Noncombatant drivers flee.*

**Scene 3: The Documents**

> *Delana opens the sealed inner envelope in front of you, reads it, and is silent for a long time.*
>
> *"It's signed," she says. "Not the cipher name. The actual name. He signed it." She sets the document on the table. "It's Orestes Sarkhazi. Lord of the Magisterium. He's been running this from the council for thirty years." She does not look surprised. She looks like someone who has confirmed the worst version of the thing they already suspected. "I need to brief the General. Tonight."*

*The Orestes identification milestone triggers here — or earlier, if the party has already recovered documents from both M1 and M7, in which case Delana has already been building toward this conclusion and the dispatch case only confirms it.*

| Result | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Success** | Supply route severed; dispatch case recovered; Orestes identified (milestone triggers); cult logistics degraded for Act III |
| **Failure (expired)** | Supply route intact; dispatch case not recovered; Orestes identification comes through other means (M1+M7 cross-reference) or is delayed to Wave 4 |

#### Mission 10: The Prophet
*Spiritual | Wave 4 | Expiration: Slow (10% → 20% → 30% → ...)*

**Overview:** A senior inner-circle figure has been identified operating in the central valley — not a combatant or site keeper, but the cult's primary theological propagandist. This person designs and oversees the conversion methodology used on the Brothers in Mission 4, identifies candidates for inner-circle elevation, and has been personally briefed by Orestes Sarkhazi on the operation's ideological objectives — including the specific vulnerabilities the cult has identified in Prince Eurimedos.

**Background (DM Only):** The Prophet is a former court philosopher named **Adamas**, recruited through grief following the deaths of his family in a plague. He travels constantly and maintains no fixed base — he cannot be struck at a location, only intercepted in transit. Adamas is not dangerous in combat but is extraordinarily dangerous in conversation — he is the most persuasive voice in the cult outside of Orestes. He knows in detail: which texts have been placed within Eurimedos's reach, how the prince's isolation has been managed, and the threshold Orestes is waiting for before making direct contact.*

**Scene 1: Tracking the Prophet**

Adamas moves between three locations in the central valley on a loose, predictable schedule.

- DC 13 Investigation (tracking the pattern): Adamas can be predicted; the next stop is a sympathizer's townhouse in a market district, in two days.

*The interception point: a road between valley towns. Adamas travels with two guards and one or two conversion candidates he is currently cultivating.*

**Scene 2: Adamas**

> *He stops when you step into the road. His guards react; he touches one of them on the arm and they stop. He looks at you with genuine attentiveness. "You've come to take me in," he says. "Or kill me. One or the other." He tilts his head. "Before you do — would you be willing to tell me why?"*

*If the party engages:* Adamas conducts a philosophical conversation with the genuine curiosity of someone who considers this a professional interest. He does not argue that the party should join the cult. He asks questions about what they believe and listens to the answers with apparent respect. DC 15 Insight: he is not performing the respect — he genuinely finds it interesting. He has made his peace with dying, if it comes to that.*

*What Adamas knows about the prince, and will share if:*
- Taken prisoner and interrogated seriously (DC 16 Persuasion or DC 14 Intimidation)
- The party creates conditions where sharing the information serves an argument he wants to make about the No-God's inevitability

*What he reveals:* The cult has been managing the prince's environment since before the Jinsoona — specific texts placed within his reach, deliberate cultivation of his isolation, a plan for Orestes to make direct contact when the corruption threshold is reached. *"He is not being forced into anything. He is being given what he already wants: a framework that makes sense of his pain. You want to protect him? Give him a better one."*

*This revelation, if obtained, allows the party to understand the Corruption Mechanic's mechanism and respond to it. The party going directly to Eurimedos and having the conversation Adamas has described provides a -1 Corruption reduction.*

*Encounter:* 2 **Cult Veterans** (Veteran stat block). They fight professionally if the party turns hostile; they stand down if Adamas instructs them to, which he will do if a conversation is possible.

| Result | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Success (Adamas captured)** | Full intelligence on prince's targeting; -1 Corruption if party acts on it; intelligence on conversion methodology; Adamas available as ongoing information source |
| **Success (Adamas killed)** | Conversion methodology disrupted; DC 15 Investigation for notes (partial prince-specific intelligence); no conversation possible |
| **Failure (expired)** | Adamas completes his circuit; +1 Corruption; cult maintains ideological infrastructure through Act III |

#### Mission 11: The Hidden City
*Civic | Wave 4 | Expiration: Slow (10% → 20% → 30% → ...)*

**Overview:** Delana has identified a fragmented network of loyalist civilians inside occupied Alcidea — former city guards, non-collaborating merchants, two Magisterium scholars, Kryptea assets who went to ground. If the party can reach Alcidea, make contact, organize the network, and establish a secure channel, they become the civilian intelligence asset that supports the Act III assault from within.

**Background (DM Only):** The network's most important member is a Magisterium scholar named **Yara Kessian**, who has remained in her office under cult supervision — her assigned role as a physical sciences consultant gives her supervised access to the Magisterium building. She has been quietly documenting the cult's operations and has a specific piece of intelligence she cannot get out: the location and access schedule of Orestes's vault beneath the Royal Library. This is critical for the Act III assault — the vault contains the original No-God texts that began everything and is one of the conditions for Ending 1.*

**Scene 1: Into Alcidea**

> *Alcidea under occupation looks almost normal from the south road, which is the point. The gates are open and staffed. The market is operating. The cult's administrative presence is visible but not theatrical. What's missing is harder to name: something in the way people walk, the way conversations end when a patrol passes, the specific quality of silence that replaces what would otherwise be the noise of a city that belongs to itself.*

*Entry requires false identities as merchants, craftspeople, or travelers (DC 13 Deception at the gate, DC 11 Persuasion at the administrative check-in). Movement through the city: DC 12 group Stealth per district; failure draws a patrol (DC 14 Deception can still extract the party without combat).*

**Scene 2: Kessian**

> *The contact method is meticulous: a specific table at a specific tea house, a specific order, a coin placed in a specific position. You have been sitting here for twenty minutes when a woman in scholar's robes sits down across from you and orders the same thing. "I was told someone would come," she says, to the table. "I was told to tell them about the Library."*

*Yara Kessian is calm, precise, and very tired. Her intelligence:*
- *The vault beneath the Royal Library: Orestes visits it alone, typically in the hour before midnight. Access is through a locked room in the restricted archives; one guard posted, rotating every 6 hours.*
- *Vault contents: she has heard it described as "the origin of everything." She does not know what is in it, but she knows Orestes treats it as irreplaceable.*
- *The Magisterium: 4 of her colleagues are cooperating with the cult. Two are genuine believers; two are terrified. She has files.*

**Scene 3: The Organization**

Getting 40+ loyalists onto a functional, secure footing requires a full day in the occupied city — 4 contact locations, each requiring transit (DC 12 Stealth) and an initial trust conversation (DC 11 Persuasion), then establishing the dead-drop channel.

*Complication:* A cult security officer has noticed patterns in the city's behavior — not enough to identify the network, but watching. DC 14 Investigation to notice the tail on the third transit; DC 15 Stealth or DC 14 Deception to lose them without alerting them that they were noticed.

| Result | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Success** | Loyalist network organized and secured; Kessian's vault intelligence recovered (critical for Act III Library assault); advance intelligence on cult positions available; civilian network at tier 3–4 |
| **Failure (expired)** | Network remains fragmented; two members arrested; no vault intelligence; Act III civilian support minimal |

#### Mission 12: Into the Capital
*Military | Wave 4 | Expiration: Slow (10% → 20% → 30% → ...)*

**Overview:** Before the Act III assault, the loyalist command needs to know the cult's defensive positions, troop concentrations, command infrastructure, and — critically — where within the palace Orestes Sarkhazi has established his operational base. This is a covert reconnaissance mission with five specific intelligence objectives.

**Background (DM Only):** This mission and Mission 11 (The Hidden City) share the same city-entry logistics and can be run concurrently if both are available simultaneously. If Mission 11 has already been completed, Kessian's intelligence automatically satisfies Objective 4 (Orestes's palace location), reducing the independent workload.*

**Scene 1: The Intelligence Brief**

> *Heskalo stands over the map of Alcidea for a long time after everyone has spoken. "We know the outer positions from the approach roads," he says. "We don't know the palace interior. We don't know where he is in there. We don't know how many guards he keeps close." He looks at the map. "I won't send people into that building without knowing."*

*The five intelligence objectives:*

1. **Outer wall defensive positions** — guard posts, weapon placements, choke points (DC 13 Perception from appropriate vantage points)
2. **Barracks compound troop count** — current strength and readiness (DC 14 Investigation — counting rotations across a 4-hour observation window)
3. **Palace interior command structure** — where operational decisions are made (DC 15 Stealth inside the palace district, or DC 14 Persuasion with a Magisterium scholar under cult supervision)
4. **Orestes Sarkhazi's location within the palace** (DC 16 Investigation, or automatic if Kessian's intelligence was recovered in Mission 11)
5. **Cliff staircase gate status** — can it be forced from the city side, and if so how (DC 13 Athletics to assess the mechanism from below)

**Scene 2: The Reconnaissance**

> *The palace forecourt is visible through the main gate — you can see the guard rotation from the street without attracting attention if you time it with the market. The original royal guard post has been replaced by something that makes tactical sense and looks entirely wrong in its context: sandbag emplacements in classic defensive positions, the kind of thing you build when you intend to stay.*

*Each objective location requires transit (DC 12 Stealth) and the specific check listed above. Patrol encounters (4 **Cultist Soldiers**, Bandit stat block) can be bypassed (DC 13 Stealth), talked through (DC 13 Deception), or fought — combat ends the reconnaissance and forces extraction. If Mission 11 was completed, the loyalist network's safe house is available as an emergency fallback.*

**Scene 3: The Debrief**

> *Heskalo listens to the full report without interrupting. Then he stands over the map, marking positions. When he finishes, he looks up. "We can do this. Not easily. But we can do this." He looks at each of you in turn. "Rest. Because the next time you walk into that city, it will not be quietly."*

| Objectives Completed | Act III Effect |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Assault begins without knowledge of interior positions; opening stage is more costly |
| 3–4 | Full outer position intelligence; interior command position unknown; significant tactical advantage |
| 5 | Complete intelligence picture; Orestes's location known from the outset of the assault |

| Result | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Success (3+ objectives)** | Act III assault begins with tactical advantage commensurate with objectives completed; Heskalo's plan is fully formed |
| **Failure (expired or 0–2 objectives)** | Assault proceeds with limited intelligence; additional complications in Act III opening |

## Chapter 8: The Jurga *(Levels 9–10)*

### Chapter Summary

The Sadars have been trickling toward Mazrok across the twelve turns of Act II — ones and twos, arriving through the mountain passes as the security situation allowed. When the last mission is resolved, enough have gathered to constitute the quorum required by Artaxartoi law. The Jurga is formally called.

The business of the council is singular: whether to recognize Prince Eurimedos Toxamones Alicimidia as King of Artaxarta. The twelve Sadars present begin with dispositions shaped entirely by what the party accomplished in Act II. The lobbying period lasts two days. On the evening of the second day, the vote is taken. The cult has been watching, and will strike that night regardless of the outcome.

This chapter is primarily a political encounter — a set of individual conversations between the party (and their allies) and twelve leaders who have been making hard calculations about a grieving child in a mountain fortress, and who are about to make the most consequential decision of their lives. The DM should give it space.

### The Sadar Starting Dispositions

Each of the twelve Sadars begins the Jurga with a disposition toward the prince's recognition based on the category of Act II missions completed.

**Category Completions → Starting Disposition**

| Missions Completed (per category) | Sadars in that category |
|---|---|
| 0 | All 4 begin **Skeptical** |
| 1 | 2 Skeptical, 2 Neutral |
| 2 | All 4 begin **Neutral** |
| 3 | 2 Neutral, 2 Supportive |
| 4 | All 4 begin **Supportive** |

**Disposition → Vote Behavior**
- **Supportive:** Votes yes automatically unless actively turned against the prince during lobbying
- **Neutral:** Persuadable; the lobbying period determines their vote
- **Skeptical:** Votes no unless the party makes a compelling case during the lobbying period (DC 15 Persuasion)

**The threshold:** 7 of 12 Sadars must vote yes for the Jurga to recognize the prince. The party's goal during the lobbying period is to reach or exceed this threshold.

*Note: The cult will attack on the night following the vote regardless of the outcome. If the vote fails, the Jurga is reconvened after the cult's defeat as an epilogue beat, and that council recognizes the prince without debate. The vote matters for Sadar Council faction standing, the number of Sadars who survive the night attack, and the emotional weight of the chapter — but it does not change the story's path through Act III.*

### Named Sadars

The DM should treat the twelve Sadars as individuals rather than mechanical vote-counters. The following four are named and developed; the remaining eight can be played as variations on the concerns their category represents.

**Sadar Breskan** *(Civic — Neutral if rescued in M8, Skeptical if not)*
The Mila clan leader. Proud, precise, and resentful of a court he has watched treat the uplands as a resource for generations. He is not hostile to the prince personally; he is skeptical of the institution. His question, asked directly: "What changes if this boy is crowned? Does the crown's relationship with the Sadars change? Or does it return to what it was before the fires?"

**Sadar Doran** *(Military — his disposition depends on Military completion)*
Late sixties. Lost two sons in the Jinsoona attacks. He has been carrying his grief as fuel rather than as weight, and he is fiercely committed to the idea that the cult must be destroyed — but he is not sure an eight-year-old king is the vehicle for that destruction. His question: "Who commands the army? The boy, or the old lion?" He means Heskalo. He does not trust that Heskalo's grief is managed.

**Sadar Yala Ves** *(Spiritual — her disposition depends on Spiritual completion)*
A woman in her fifties with a reputation for theological scholarship unusual among the Sadars. She has heard the Brotherhood's preliminary account of what the cult actually serves. She is not frightened — she is furious. Her question: "How do we close this? Not the cult. The thing behind the cult. What is the plan for the entity?" She wants specifics. General statements about vigilance will not satisfy her.

**Sadar Keth** *(Civic — begins Skeptical in most scenarios)*
The youngest Sadar present, barely forty, representing a clan in the northern uplands that lost a quarter of its population to cult activity before the Jinsoona. He is not ideologically opposed to the prince. He is exhausted and represents exhausted people. His question: "What does this cost my clan? In riders, in supplies, in years. What is the honest number?"

### Scene 1: The Opening Session

The Jurga convenes in the Citadel's great hall — a room not designed for ceremony but pressed into it. Twelve Sadars, the prince, the General, and the party.

> *The great hall of Mazrok was built for military quartering, not for ceremony, and the Jurga being held in it makes this apparent. The Sadars occupy the long table in the order of their arrival — there is no formal precedence established, because no one planned this Jurga. They are twelve people who have traveled through a kingdom that no longer looks like itself to sit in a room and decide whether a child is a king. The child in question is seated at the table's end, very upright, with his hands folded in front of him and his face composed in a way that has cost him something.*
>
> *Knight Commander Velas opens the session, as the most senior figure outside the command structure. "The quorum is present," she says. "The matter before this council is singular. We will hear from those who wish to speak before the vote is called on the evening of the second day."*

*The opening session is primarily the Sadars speaking — their concerns, their questions, their conditions. The party listens. The DM should surface 3–4 of the named Sadar concerns in this session, letting the party hear what they will need to address in the lobbying period. General Heskalo speaks briefly, formally, and without his characteristic humor. The prince does not speak in the opening session; when it is clear everyone expects him to, he says only: "I'll speak when there is something worth saying."*

*DC 12 Insight (observing the Sadars):* The Skeptical Sadars are not hostile — they are calculating. They want to commit to something they can believe in. The thing blocking them is not dislike of the prince; it is the lack of evidence that this enterprise is survivable.

### Scene 2: The Lobbying Period

The two days between the opening session and the vote are the chapter's heart. The party may approach individual Sadars, deploy allies (Arslan, Marcus Ivor, Delana, Kaske), and make specific arguments for specific concerns.

*Each Sadar conversation is a skill check with context. The DM should run these as genuine conversations, not just check requests — the check determines whether the party's argument moves a Sadar, but the argument itself matters.*

**Addressing a Neutral Sadar:** DC 13 Persuasion. The party needs a specific argument relevant to that Sadar's category concern. Generic appeals to loyalty or tradition do not qualify.

**Addressing a Skeptical Sadar:** DC 15 Persuasion, with the same requirement for specificity. A Skeptical Sadar moved to Neutral can then be addressed a second time (DC 13) to reach Supportive, but the party only has two days — each Sadar can be meaningfully addressed once.

**Allies in the lobbying period:**
- *Arslan Zedan (if recruited):* Can address Military Sadars at DC 10. Can address Civic Sadars at DC 14 (his reputation complicates the approach). Cannot effectively address Spiritual Sadars.
- *Marcus Ivor:* Can address Spiritual Sadars at DC 11, providing the Brotherhood's testimony about the No-God's true nature. Most effective with Yala Ves.
- *General Heskalo:* Can address Military Sadars at DC 11. His emotional state is visible — a Sadar who observes it closely (DC 13 Insight) may note that the old lion's grief is not managed, which can cut against the case being made.
- *The Prince (if the party arranges a private meeting):* DC 12 to arrange; if the meeting happens, the Sadar in question gets a direct read on Eurimedos. If his Corruption is ≤ 3, this automatically moves a Neutral Sadar to Supportive. If his Corruption is ≥ 6, it may do the opposite (DC 14 Insight check for the Sadar — on success, they notice something and become harder to read).

**Complication — Sadar Keth and the honest number:** Keth will not move from Skeptical without a specific commitment of resources — not promises, but a plan. The party can offer this only if their Act II results support it. If Military missions are at 3–4 and The Legions faction is Allied or better, Keth will accept the honest number and move to Neutral. If the honest number is bad, he accepts it but does not move.

### Scene 3: The Vote

The vote is called on the evening of the second day. Each Sadar announces their decision in order of arrival.

> *The hall is quiet in a way it has not been for two days. The Sadars speak in order — yes and no and conditions stated plainly. When the count is final, Velas announces it. The number is what the party has made of it. The prince receives it with the same careful composure he has maintained since the opening session, and only the people watching very closely will notice the moment it costs him something.*

*If the vote succeeds:* The Jurga formally recognizes Eurimedos as King of Artaxarta. The room does not erupt; it settles, the way a question settles when it has been answered. Heskalo stands, and the Sadars who voted yes stand with him. The prince does not cry. He says: "Thank you." He says it to each of the Sadars who voted yes, by name, in the order they spoke.

*If the vote fails:* The same composure from the prince, with the specific quality of someone who has prepared for this outcome. He says: "Then we have work to do before we ask again." Heskalo, visibly, does not manage his expression for approximately three seconds. Then he does.

*Either way, the chapter ends here. The night follows.*

### Chapter 8 Conclusion

Award **Level 10** at the close of the vote, before the party retires for the night. The Jurga is done. The cult has been watching the Citadel for days. Tonight, they move.

## Chapter 9: The Night They Came *(Levels 10–11)*

### Chapter Summary

The night after the Jurga vote, the cult's forces attack the encampment. The scale of the assault scales with the military resources the party assembled in Act II. The outcome of the defense scales accordingly — ranging from overwhelming catastrophe to a costly victory. In all outcomes, the same events occur: Prince Eurimedos is captured and taken to Alcidea, and three members of the prince's retinue are taken to a nearby cultist stronghold scheduled for execution by nightfall the following day.

The morning after the attack, Delana delivers the intelligence. The party faces the pivotal decision of the campaign.

### Scene 1: The Silence Before

A brief scene — the encampment on the night of the vote, before the assault. The party has whatever time they choose to spend in it.

> *The Citadel is quieter than usual for a night of consequence. Some of the Sadars are still awake, talking in small groups near the fires. General Heskalo is at the gate, looking out at the dark below, which is what he does when he cannot sleep. The prince retired early. Through the narrow window of his quarters, a lamp is still lit.*

*This is the last quiet moment before the campaign's final act. Give the party space to have conversations they have been putting off — with each other, with Eurimedos, with the NPCs who matter to them. Whatever is said here will be the last thing said before everything changes.*

*If the party checks the perimeter (DC 13 Perception):* Nothing visible — the cult's advance element has been very careful. DC 17: a single extinguished torch on the cliff stairs below the gate, recently used. Something was at the base of the stairs an hour ago.

### Scene 2: The Attack

The assault begins at approximately the second hour of the night. How it unfolds depends on the Military missions completed in Act II.

**Low Military Completion (0–1 Military missions)**

> *The first signal fire goes out. Then the second. By the time the alarm is raised, the outer perimeter has already been breached at three points simultaneously — the cult's forces know the defensive layout in detail, which means someone told them. The encampment goes from sleeping to chaos in under a minute.*

*The assault is overwhelming. The party can fight — and should — but the goal quickly becomes survival and protection of the prince, not holding a line that cannot be held. The DM should run this as a fighting withdrawal through a collapsing defense.*

*Mechanical outcome:* The party must make three DC 14 group checks (Athletics, Stealth, or Acrobatics depending on path) to reach the prince's quarters before the cult does. On any failure, the cult gets there first — the prince is already gone. Three Sadars are killed in the assault. The loyalist force is scattered.

**Moderate Military Completion (2–3 Military missions)**

> *The first signal fire goes out. The second holds. By the time the alarm spreads, there is still a defensive line — damaged, contested, but a line. The cult's assault has numbers and coordination, but it runs into resistance that is not disorganized.*

*The party fights through a sustained combat that lasts approximately 30 minutes. The Sadars are being evacuated into the Citadel's inner keep; two of the most skeptical Sadars are cut off and require a rescue that costs the party two rounds of the fight. One Sadar is killed. The loyalist force sustains heavy casualties but holds.*

*Encounter:* 3 waves of **Cultist Soldiers** (Bandit stat block, arriving in groups of 8) with 1 **Cult Officer** (Veteran stat block, 58 HP) commanding each wave. The waves arrive over 6 rounds with a 2-round gap between them.

**Full Military Completion (4 Military missions)**

> *The first signal fire goes out. The second, third, and fourth are already manned and burning brighter — Arslan's outriders saw them coming an hour before they arrived. The cult hits a defensive line that was ready for them.*

*The assault is repelled over approximately 45 minutes of sustained combat. Arslan's cavalry catches the flanking element. Vennicus's legionaries hold the main approach. No Sadars are killed. The cost is real — loyalist forces sustain losses — but manageable.*

*Encounter:* 4 waves of **Cultist Soldiers** with **Cult Officers** commanding, as above, plus 1 **Cult Commander** (use Knight stat block, 52 HP, Appendix A) leading the assault. The Cult Commander is a named figure who can be defeated or driven off; if taken alive, he knows the location of the stronghold where the retainers are being held.*

---

**In all three outcomes, the following occurs:**

The cult sent a specific team for a specific objective. While the main assault consumed the defense's attention, a four-person inner-circle unit went directly for the prince's quarters. Abala Kadar is found unconscious outside the prince's door — alive, which suggests the cult did not want him dead, just out of the way, which is its own message. The prince is gone. Marcus Geminius Sabinus, Fleur Dontillion, and Abala Kadar are found, shortly after, to have been taken as well — seized in the chaos by a secondary cult element. Their destination, Delana will confirm in the morning, is a cultist stronghold approximately half a day's ride south.

Kaske Heskalo and Marcus Ivor are present and functional in all outcomes. The General is present and functional, but there is a quality to his stillness in the aftermath that those who know him will recognize as something close to the edge.

### Scene 3: The Morning After

The attack's immediate aftermath. Casualties are accounted for. Delana has been working through the night.

> *She comes to you at first light, before the General. She has the look of someone who has not slept and has made her peace with it. She sets two pieces of intelligence on the table between you — not dramatic, just the two pieces, the way she always does.*
>
> *"The prince is in Alcidea. They took him directly to the palace — he is alive and unharmed, and Orestes Sarkhazi is with him personally." She pauses. "The others are at the Redstone Hold, six hours south. Their execution is scheduled for nightfall today."*
>
> *She folds her hands. She does not tell you what to do. She has learned, over the course of this campaign, not to.*

### Scene 4: The Pivotal Decision

The party must choose.

**Option A — Save the comrades first:**
The party rides south to the Redstone Hold. The rescue is feasible — the Hold is lightly defended, the execution is scheduled for nightfall, and the party has time if they move immediately.

*The Hold encounter:* 1 **Cult Fortress Commander** (Veteran stat block, 58 HP) and 16 **Cultist Soldiers** (Bandit stat block). The retainers are in the Hold's lower level, alive and intact. The rescue is straightforward if the party commits fully.

*The cost:* The prince spends the additional hours in Orestes Sarkhazi's custody. Orestes has been waiting for exactly this window. The philosophical conversations that have been conducted carefully across months become direct and concentrated. Apply **+2 Corruption** when the party reunites with the prince at Alcidea.

*Marcus Geminius Sabinus is alive.* His protective influence on the prince resumes — but the +2 has already been applied.

**Option B — Rescue the prince first:**
The party rides directly to Alcidea, bypassing the Hold.

*The cost:* The three retainers are executed at nightfall. When the party learns this — and they will learn it — each of the three deaths is a permanent absence. Marcus Geminius Sabinus, who spent every evening for months sitting with the prince over military histories, is gone. Fleur Dontillion, who never explained herself and now never will, is gone. Abala Kadar, who had no ambiguity about his purpose and gave everything to it, is gone.

*The benefit:* The prince's additional exposure is prevented. The +2 Corruption from Option A does not apply. Eurimedos arrives at the final confrontation with whatever Corruption level he has accumulated through Acts II and III, without this additional increment. Endings 1 and 2 remain available if the other conditions are met.

*If Marcus Geminius Sabinus is killed:* Remove his protective benefit — the prevention of isolation-based Corruption — from all future turns. This is the permanent consequence of Option B that the party will feel in the final confrontation if the prince's Corruption is in the borderline range.

*The General's reaction to whichever choice is made:* He does not countermand it. He does not offer an opinion. He has been in wars long enough to know that this is exactly the kind of decision that has no correct answer and that the people who make it carry the weight of it forever. He carries his own weights. He knows what they feel like.

### Chapter 9 Conclusion

Award **Level 11** when the pivotal decision is made and the party has committed to their path. The march to Alcidea — or the rescue mission, followed by the march — begins.

## Chapter 10: The Dark Gospel *(Levels 11–12)*

### Chapter Summary

The loyalist force marches on Alcidea. What they bring with them — numbers, intelligence, civilian allies, Brotherhood support — depends entirely on the twelve turns of Act II and the choices made in Chapter 9. The assault proceeds through four stages: breaching the city's outer defenses, moving through the occupied streets, securing the Royal Library and the vault beneath it, and entering the palace for the final confrontation with Orestes Sarkhazi.

The prince is somewhere in the palace. His condition when found will tell the party, and the DM, which ending is available. The confrontation with Orestes is the last scene this adventure offers — how it resolves depends on what was built across the entire campaign.

### Scene 1: The Road to Alcidea

The force moves south through the mountain passes. There is a day of travel before the capital.

> *The road south looks different now from how it looked in the first chapter — you are older in it, and it is a different kind of road. The Citadel is behind you. Alcidea is ahead. The valley opens as you descend and you can see the capital from three miles out — not burning this time, which is worse in a specific way, because it means the people in it have learned to live with what is living among them.*

*The march south is a moment for the party to speak with their allies and to prepare for what is coming. The DM should offer brief character scenes — Heskalo checking the column's disposition with the precise attentiveness of a man who cannot afford to be elsewhere in his mind; Arslan riding the flanks, calculating; Kaske and Marcus Ivor talking quietly about something that neither of them continues when the party approaches.*

*One piece of intelligence arrives on the road, through Delana's network:* The prince is in the king's study, on the palace's upper floor. He has not been harmed. Orestes visits him twice daily. He has been given books.

### Scene 2: The Breach

The assault on Alcidea's outer defenses. How this proceeds depends on the intelligence gathered in Mission 12 and the military forces assembled in Act II.

**With Mission 12 intelligence (3+ objectives completed):**

> *The gate we want is the eastern servants' entrance — the one they never reinforced because it's not on the main approach maps. Vennicus knows where to put his people. We go in on three fronts.*

*The assault begins with tactical advantage. The cult's defenders are drawn to the main gate by Vennicus's 400 as a feint; the party enters through the servants' gate (identified in the reconnaissance) with a smaller force. DC 12 group Stealth to cross the gap between the gate and the first alley before the cult realizes the main assault is a diversion.*

**Without Mission 12 intelligence (0–2 objectives):**

> *We hit the main gate. There is no other option we know about.*

*A direct assault on the main gate. The cult has the defensive advantage; Vennicus's legionaries take the first push. The party fights through the gate in the company of the first assault element — a grinding combat that costs time and losses.*

*Encounter (main gate assault only):* 1 **Cult Gate Commander** (Knight stat block, 52 HP) and 20 **Cultist Soldiers** (Bandit stat block) defending the gate, with 2 rounds of reinforcements available (8 additional soldiers from the inner court). The gate's physical mechanism requires a DC 15 Athletics check to force from outside or a DC 12 from inside.

**Arslan Zedan's cavalry (if Committed):** Screens the approaches, prevents cult reinforcement from the valley roads, and cuts off the escape routes from the palace. If Arslan is not present, the cult can receive reinforcement from the south (add 1 additional encounter wave in Scene 3).

### Scene 3: The Streets

Moving through the occupied city toward the palace district. This scene uses the same movement framework as Chapter 3, now with the inverse dynamic — the party is the invading force, not the extracting one.

> *Alcidea under assault looks different from Alcidea falling. The sounds are the sounds of a city fighting back — not just the loyalist soldiers, but the civilians who have been living inside the occupation for months, who have been waiting. The network's signal has gone out. Several blocks near the agora are actively hostile to the cult's defenders now. It is not an army. It is a city making a decision.*

**With Mission 11 civilian network (tier 3–4):**

The network's safe corridors are active. The party moves through the city at double speed — cult patrols are being slowed, diverted, and in some cases physically obstructed by civilians who have decided the occupation is over. Yara Kessian (if she survived) is coordinating from the Magisterium with a precise calm that would be remarkable in normal circumstances and is extraordinary in these.

*No Stealth checks required in network-controlled districts. In non-controlled districts: DC 12 group Stealth, failure means a 4-**Cultist Soldier** patrol encounter.*

**Without civilian network (tier 0–1):**

The streets are contested but not assisted. The party navigates toward the palace through a city that is confused rather than coordinated.

*DC 13 group Stealth per district. Failure: a patrol of 6 **Cultist Soldiers**. Two such failures in sequence means the cult has begun concentrating forces toward the party's position — the DM should escalate.*

*One street encounter occurs regardless of network status:* The party passes the Temple of Ilura, where the remaining clergy have opened the doors and are treating wounded from the assault. This is not a combat encounter; it is a brief, human moment in the middle of violence. The DM should give it 30 seconds of table time and move on.

### Scene 4: The Royal Library and the Vault

The Royal Library stands adjacent to the palace, connected by a covered colonnade. The vault beneath it is accessible through a locked door in the restricted archives — one guard post, rotating every 6 hours.

*This scene is optional but significant.* Destroying the vault is one of the conditions for Ending 1. The Brotherhood combat unit (if Spiritual missions reached 3–4) is specifically positioned to assist here — they have the knowledge and the authority to perform the ritual cleansing that ensures the vault's contents cannot be recovered or re-consecrated.

> *The library is quieter than the streets outside. The shelves are intact — Orestes has protected this building specifically, which tells you something. The restricted archives door is locked. Behind it, according to Kessian, is the passage to the vault.*

**The vault itself:**

> *The vault is exactly what it is: a room full of very old things that have been kept carefully for a very long time, and the care shows. Stone shelves. Sealed cases. A reading desk with a lamp that has been used recently. At the center of the room, the oldest materials — cracked leather, ancient script, the specific quality of preservation that indicates these have been touched by something that is not ordinary scholarship.*
>
> *The air in the vault has a quality. Not supernatural, exactly — nothing that triggers magical detection. Just the quality of a place where a particular kind of thinking has been done, consistently, for decades. It is not comfortable.*

*Anyone who enters the vault must make a DC 14 Wisdom saving throw or experience a moment of the No-God's philosophical clarity — a brief, convincing sensation that the vault's texts are simply correct and that everything outside them is noise. This is not a game-mechanical effect; it lasts one round and passes. Marcus Ivor, if present, has failed this save before and is unsurprised by it.*

*The vault guard (1 **Cult Veteran**, Veteran stat block) is outside the vault door, not inside. If Kessian's access schedule was known (Mission 11), the party knows the rotation and can time their approach to catch the post change (DC 11 Stealth to neutralize the guard quietly during the handoff).*

**Destroying the vault:**

The Brotherhood's method requires 5 minutes of ritual preparation followed by a physical collapse of the vault's structure (DC 15 Athletics to force the support columns; the vault is built to contain things, not to survive a determined structural attack). Without the Brotherhood, the party can improvise — burning the materials requires sustained fire (30 minutes and a fire source) or physical destruction of each case (30 minutes of work, DC 12 Athletics to break the sealed cases in sequence).

*If the vault is destroyed:* Ending 1 becomes available (if the other conditions are met). Orestes, when he learns the vault is gone, will know it before the party reaches him — the party should see his reaction when they enter the throne room, and his reaction is not what they expect. He is quiet in the way of someone who has been relieved of a weight they did not know they were carrying.

### Scene 5: The Palace

The final approach to the palace interior. The cult has concentrated its most capable defenders here — these are inner-circle figures, not outer-layer soldiers.

> *The palace is what it has always been — the bones of the Xoshan governor's residence, wrapped in five centuries of Artaxartoi additions, lit now by a combination of torchlight and the fires still burning in the city. The great hall where the King received petitions is visible through the open main doors. Orestes is in there. He has not tried to run. Whatever this is, he is here for it.*

*The approach through the palace interior involves 2–3 encounter rooms, each with a cult defensive element:*

*Encounter 1 — The Colonnade (midpoint between library and palace):* 1 **Cult Champion** (use Gladiator stat block, 112 HP, Appendix A) and 4 **Cult Veterans** (Veteran stat block). The Champion fights as a deliberate obstruction — buying time, not holding the position.*

*Encounter 2 — The Upper Hall (approaches to the king's study, where the prince is):* 6 **Cult Veterans** (Veteran stat block). This is the guard detail protecting the prince's location. They will not surrender; they fight with the specific intensity of people who know what they are protecting.*

*Finding Eurimedos:*

> *The king's study is at the end of the upper corridor — the room where Telamon Aristedes Alcimidias II spent his evenings reviewing administrative papers and correspondence and, apparently, reading the same military histories his son would later memorize. The door is unlocked. Inside, the prince is seated at the desk.*

*His condition reflects his Corruption level:*

- **Corruption 0–5:** He rises immediately. He looks like someone who has been holding himself very still for a long time and has just been given permission to stop. "I knew you'd come," he says, and the emphasis is on the *knew*, not the *come*.
- **Corruption 6–7:** He rises, but there is a pause before it — a beat in which he seems to be consulting something internal. "I've been having the most interesting conversations," he says. Then, quieter: "I'm glad you're here."
- **Corruption 8+:** He is already standing when the door opens, as though he heard you on the stairs. He looks at the party with the calm of someone who has arrived at a conclusion and is no longer troubled by the approach of things that might disturb it. "He said you would come," the prince says. "He said you would be in time." A pause. "He was right about that too."

*In all outcomes, Eurimedos comes with the party. He is the prince; he is not their enemy. But the DM should note his demeanor carefully for the final confrontation — what he says and how he says it is the living evidence of what the Corruption Tracker has been measuring.*

*Award **Level 12** when the party finds the prince and begins the approach to the throne room.*

### Scene 6: The Final Confrontation

Orestes Sarkhazi is in the throne room — the room where King Telamon received petitions, where Orestes himself appeared before the King dozens of times across thirty years of service. He has not fled. He has not barricaded himself. He has sent his guards away.

He is sitting at the map table.

> *The throne room looks different from how you remember it. The maps are still on the table — different maps now, operational ones, with positions marked and supply routes annotated. He has been running a war from this room. He looks up when you enter, and his expression is what it has always been: the full attention of a man who is genuinely interested in what is in front of him.*
>
> *"I expected this to happen at some point," he says. "I did not know it would be you specifically. That is the only surprise remaining to me." He does not stand. He does not reach for a weapon. "I would like to talk, if you'll allow it. Not to delay you. Because there are things worth saying, and I have been thinking about what they are for a long time."*

*Whether the party allows the conversation or attacks immediately determines what ending is available.*

---

**If the party attacks without dialogue:**

Orestes rises, and the scholar's body moves with something that has been living in it for thirty years and is not entirely him anymore.

*Encounter:* **Orestes Sarkhazi** (see Appendix B for full stat block). He fights with the particular quality of someone who has no strong preference about the outcome — he will defend himself with genuine capability, but the defense has the quality of professionalism rather than desperation. He does not beg. He does not monologue. He fights.

*If the vault has been destroyed:* Orestes has one fewer Legendary Action available per round, representing the severance of his connection to the cult's primary anchor. The DM should note this openly — he pauses once, mid-combat, as though registering an absence.

*Ending 1 is available through this path if: Eurimedos's Corruption is ≤ 5 at the start of this scene and the vault has been destroyed.*

---

**If the party allows the conversation:**

The dialogue with Orestes is the most important scene in the adventure, and the DM should give it the space it deserves. He will speak honestly — this is who he is; he has always been honest when directness served him, and now it serves him in the only way remaining. He speaks about what he found in the vault as a young man. He speaks about his mentor, whom he killed, and why. He speaks about the thirty years between the pact and this room, and what was gained and what was lost, and whether he considers it to have been worth it. He will say: it was not worth it, plainly, without self-pity, as a fact.

*If the party engaged him in dialogue on 2 or more prior occasions* (the Ending 2 condition): The conversation will reach a point where Orestes says something that is the closest he can come to a question. He will not ask it directly. The party will have to hear it and respond to it. If they do:

> *He is quiet for a moment. Then he sets down the thing he has been holding — a ring, old, the kind a scholar would wear — and places it on the map table. "I knew this was the price when I accepted the pact," he says. "I have known it for fifteen years." He looks at his hands. "I want to tell you something, and I want you to hear it before I do what I am about to do."*
>
> *He tells you what the No-God's philosophy actually is, from the inside, from the perspective of someone who understood it completely. It is not a justification. It is the most honest description of an argument for oblivion that you have ever heard — and at the end of it, he says: "It is wrong. I have believed it for thirty years and it is wrong, and I know it is wrong because of what I am about to choose to do."*
>
> *He breaks the pact.*
>
> *The room does not change. No light, no sound. It is simply that the quality of attention in his eyes shifts — something that was keeping it in place is gone — and then he is not in the room anymore. What is left is a man in his late sixties who has spent thirty years doing a thing he cannot undo, and who has approximately four seconds remaining before the cost becomes complete.*

*Play it quietly. This is Ending 2. Let the room be still.*

---

### The Endings

**Ending 1 — The Long Vigil**

*Conditions: Eurimedos Corruption ≤ 5 at the start of Scene 6; vault destroyed; Orestes defeated by any means.*

The incursion collapses when Orestes's connection to the No-God is severed by his death. It does not go dramatically — there is no visible manifestation, no wave of energy. The cult members throughout the city simply stop, one by one, and in most cases they do not understand why. The Brotherhood will describe this later as "the withdrawal of attention." The No-God is not destroyed. It is simply elsewhere.

Artaxarta survives, damaged. The legitimate authority of the crown is restored. General Heskalo, in the weeks that follow, begins the work of rebuilding — slowly, carefully, with the particular method of a man who intends to see it through to the end. Prince Eurimedos, unbroken, will be a king in fact when he is old enough.

The Brotherhood and whatever remains of the Kryptea under Delana's reconstruction are charged with a permanent vigil. There is a meeting between Marcus Ivor and Delana Geminia Sabinus that the party may witness, in which two of the sharpest minds available to the vigil agree on a single principle: the No-God will return through philosophy before it returns through force. They will be watching for it.

The party knows the silence is not peace. They are right.

*Read aloud to close:*

> *The city is quiet by morning. Not the quiet of a city under occupation — the quiet of a city that has been through something and is sitting with it. You are in the throne room, or what is left of it. The maps are still on the table. Outside, the sounds of Alcidea beginning to function again: voices, movement, someone somewhere fixing something.*
>
> *General Heskalo stands at the window for a long time before he speaks. "We have a great deal of work to do," he says, finally. He turns around. "I would like to begin today, if you are willing."*

---

**Ending 2 — The Last Convert**

*Conditions: 2+ meaningful dialogue scenes with Orestes across Acts II–III; the conversation in Scene 6 reaches the moment and the party gives him the space to take it.*

The incursion collapses in the same way — not dramatically, without visible manifestation, a withdrawal of attention. The difference is that in the days following, when the Brotherhood begins the analysis of what happened and why, Marcus Ivor will produce something that none of the texts they have access to can fully account for: an act of will against the No-God, from inside the pact, performed with full knowledge of the cost and without any expectation of survival.

The theological implications will occupy Marcus Ivor for the rest of his life. The practical implications are the same as Ending 1.

What the party does with the truth of who Orestes was — the young man in the library, the murder he believed was righteous, thirty years of choices he cannot undo — is their decision. No one will tell them what to do with it. That is the point.

*Read aloud to close:*

> *He is still in the room, technically, for a moment — what is left of him, which is the weight of his body on the floor beside the map table and the ring he set down, and everything he knew, which is gone. The room is very quiet. Outside, Alcidea is beginning to come back.*
>
> *You will have to decide what to tell people. Most of them will not believe the true version. You will have to decide whether that matters. There is, as always, a great deal of work to do, and someone has to begin it.*

---

**Ending 3 — The Seeds of Ruin**

*Triggered: Eurimedos Corruption ≥ 8 at the start of Scene 6, or Option A chosen and Corruption reached 6+ before this scene.*

The cult is broken. Orestes is defeated — through combat, through the conversion, through whatever path the party took. The incursion collapses. The loyalist force holds. Artaxarta survives.

Eurimedos, protected by the party across the entire campaign, eventually takes the throne. He is a capable king — organized, intelligent, genuinely committed to the welfare of his people. He remembers the campaign. He names the party's contributions in the public record. He is, by every visible measure, what his father hoped he would become.

*Play the victory as a full victory. Do not shade it. Let the table celebrate it.*

Then:

*The DM delivers the epilogue alone, after the celebration has finished, in the quietest available register:*

> *Twenty-three years later, the cult of Umgul reappears in the five cities — not in the uplands, not from some surviving cell in the mountains, but from within the administrative apparatus of the palace itself.*
>
> *The investigation will take three years and implicate seventeen officials. The evidence, when it is finally assembled, will point toward a single origin point: the king's private study, where he has kept — for two decades, behind a false panel in the bookcase — a collection of texts that no one else has ever seen or been invited to discuss.*
>
> *Eurimedos Toxamones Alicimidia does not deny it. He is sixty-two years old. He has been a good king. He has, he says, believed what the texts say for as long as he can remember. He does not say when that was. He says: "I never acted on it. I want you to understand that. I never acted on it." He is telling the truth.*
>
> *He found the No-God's tenets during the long nights in the Citadel, when he was eight years old and his father had just died and no one had time for him. He found them in a book he was not supposed to have, and they made sense of his pain in a way nothing else had, and he kept that quiet for fifty years because he was also a good king and he understood the distinction between believing a thing and doing a thing.*
>
> *The people who protected him never knew how close it had gotten.*
>
> *The people who protected him never knew.*

---

### Chapter 10 Conclusion

The adventure is complete. What the party does after the ending — the reconstruction, the vigil, the decisions about what to tell people and how to tell it — belongs to the campaign's epilogue and to the table's own conversations. The DM Guide's work is done.

**Pact of the No-God.** Orestes has resistance to necrotic and psychic damage and immunity to the frightened condition. When reduced to 0 hit points, he can use his reaction to cast one spell of 4th level or lower before falling unconscious. This trait is unavailable if the ritual vault beneath the Royal Library has been destroyed before this encounter.

**Void Clarity.** Orestes cannot be surprised, cannot have his mind read by any magical means, and has advantage on Wisdom (Insight) checks. Any creature that targets him with *detect thoughts* or a telepathic ability immediately takes 14 (4d6) psychic damage.

**Thirty Years.** Orestes is immune to the effects of any spell that forces him to reveal the truth or act against his will.

**Spellcasting.** Orestes is a 15th-level spellcaster. His spellcasting ability is Intelligence (spell save DC 19, +11 to hit with spell attacks). He has the following spells prepared:

- Cantrips (at will): *chill touch*, *mage hand*, *minor illusion*, *toll the dead*
- 1st level (4 slots): *detect magic*, *mage armor*\*, *ray of sickness*
- 2nd level (3 slots): *hold person*, *silence*, *suggestion*
- 3rd level (3 slots): *counterspell*, *dispel magic*, *fear*
- 4th level (3 slots): *banishment*, *blight*, *confusion*
- 5th level (3 slots): *dominate person*, *hold monster*, *telekinesis*
- 6th level (1 slot): *eyebite*, *true seeing*
- 7th level (1 slot): *finger of death*, *forcecage*
- 8th level (1 slot): *power word stun*

\**Orestes casts mage armor on himself before combat.*

#### Actions

**Multiattack.** Orestes makes two attacks: two spells, or one spell and one Void Touch.

**Void Touch.** *Melee Spell Attack:* +11 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. *Hit:* 18 (4d8) necrotic damage. The target must succeed on a DC 19 Wisdom saving throw or be frightened of Orestes until the end of its next turn. Orestes uses this attack reluctantly — it is the pact's influence, not his preference.

#### Legendary Actions

Orestes can take 3 legendary actions, choosing from the options below. Only one legendary action option can be used at a time, and only at the end of another creature's turn. Spent legendary actions are regained at the start of each turn.

*If the vault beneath the Royal Library has been destroyed:* Orestes has only 2 legendary actions per round. He knows this the moment it happens.*

**Cantrip.** Orestes casts a cantrip.

**Void Word (Costs 2 Actions).** One creature Orestes can see within 60 feet must succeed on a DC 19 Wisdom saving throw or become incapacitated until the end of its next turn, overwhelmed by a moment of complete nihilistic conviction.

**Severance (Costs 3 Actions).** Orestes ends one spell of 6th level or lower affecting a creature or object he can see, without expending a spell slot.

*The Redemption Path: If the Ending 2 conditions are met and the party gives Orestes the moment described in Chapter 10, he does not fight. His stat block is irrelevant. What matters is only what he chooses to say, and what it costs him.*

## Appendix C: Magic Items and Treasures

### Setting-Specific Items

#### Sigil of the Bright Watch
*Wondrous item, uncommon (requires attunement)*

A polished silver disc on a fine chain, showing Ilura in her warrior-mother aspect on one face; the Brotherhood's founding motto in old Artaxartoi script on the other. While wearing this sigil, you have advantage on saving throws against being charmed or frightened. In addition, once per day as a bonus action, you can cast *protection from evil and good* on yourself (no concentration required).

*Awarded by Knight Commander Velas at the conclusion of Chapter 2, as acknowledgment of the debt the Brotherhood owes.*

---

#### Kryptea Dead Drop Cipher
*Wondrous item, uncommon (no attunement required)*

A small sealed case of oiled wood, indistinguishable from an ordinary traveling document case. Any document placed inside and sealed with the key phrase reads as mundane administrative text to anyone who opens it without speaking the key phrase within 5 feet first. Only creatures who know the key phrase can read the true content. The key phrase is set by the first creature to use the case and can be changed by spending 1 hour resetting the mechanism.

*Provided by Delana Geminia Sabinus at the start of Act II. A second copy exists — in the vault beneath the Royal Library, which is how Orestes has been running correspondence for thirty years.*

---

#### Orestes's Ring
*Ring, rare (requires attunement)*

A plain brass ring with no external ornament. The interior band carries a single line of Infernal script: *"What persists is an error of perception."* While attuned to this ring, you have advantage on Intelligence (Arcana) and Intelligence (History) checks. However, at each dawn, you must succeed on a DC 13 Wisdom saving throw or gain 1 level of exhaustion as the No-God's philosophy surfaces briefly and with uncomfortable coherence in your thinking. This exhaustion cannot be removed until the ring is removed and the following dawn has passed.

*Found in the throne room after the final confrontation, on the map table where Orestes set it down. Its historical value as an artifact of the pact is significant regardless of whether any character chooses to attune to it. Most characters will choose not to.*
