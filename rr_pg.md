Rainbow Road — Player's Guide
Context
The Rainbow Road GM Guide and Stat Block Appendix are complete. The natural next deliverable is a Player's Guide — the document handed to players at the campaign's start. The two existing Player's Guides (Terminus Pax, Dark Gospel) establish the structure and conventions; this guide adapts those conventions for PF1e mythic and the campaign's epic nine-world scope.

Output file: Rainbow_Road_Players_Guide.md Estimated scale: 800–1,000 lines System: Pathfinder 1e with Mythic Adventures

Design Decisions
What Players Know vs. What They Discover
The Player's Guide covers what a Nexus resident knows or could learn. It does NOT reveal:

The corruption score mechanic (hidden from players throughout)
Which specific Warlocks can or cannot be turned
The four endings
Tornsoul's identity as Sarena Tal
The Underworld's composition beyond "prison of the Asuras"
It DOES establish:

Nexus in full detail (starting world, home base, returning point)
The eight outer worlds as "known from historical records and legend" — brief lore sketches (2–3 sentences each), enough to make character choices meaningful without spoiling discoveries
The Rainbow Road as a concept, now mostly dark
The Warlocks as a class of beings (corrupted former heroes) without individual names/details
The Song of Heaven as a legendary concept from before the apocalypse
Mythic power as something the celestial system grants, by design
Nine Worlds Coverage
Nexus: full treatment (~60 lines, districts, landmarks, institutions, daily life) Outer worlds: 2–3 sentences each in a "Historical Records" section — public knowledge on Nexus, the kind of information a scholar, traveler, or educated resident would have. No plot spoilers. Just enough to make the prospect of visiting feel real.

PF1e Adaptations
Campaign Traits (not Backgrounds) — 3–4 entries
Archetypes or Prestige Classes (not Subclasses) — 2–3 custom entries
Mythic Paths section (not present in 5e guides) — all 6 paths with Nine Worlds flavor
"Game Master" not "Dungeon Master" throughout
PF1e race block format for any custom racial content
Section Structure
I. Front Matter (~8 lines)
Title block
Epistemic note: This document reflects what a knowledgeable Nexus resident knows or could discover. The Rainbow Road keeps its own counsel.
II. The Realm of Nexus (~60 lines)
What Nexus Is: Hub of the Nine Worlds; a continent suspended in mists; built as the anchor point of the celestial system
The City of Nexus: Architecture (original celestial infrastructure + 500 years of human rebuilding), districts, daily life; the full moon barricades; demon raids as accepted routine; the magitek tradition; the Gate Towns as ghost towns on the perimeter
The Great Ancillary: Exists. Off-limits. Something old and important is inside. Citizens know this without knowing more.
The Sky Above: Heaven — visible as an artificial star from Nexus at night. The Host of Heaven faith, mostly lapsed.
III. The Nine Worlds (~90 lines)
What the Nine Worlds Are: Brief cosmological framing — pocket dimensions built by celestial architects, each a distinct world with its own race, culture, and landscape; connected by the Rainbow Road
The Rainbow Road: What it was (interplanar highway, open trade and travel), what it is now (mostly dark for 500 years, the gate towns empty)
The Outer Worlds (Historical Records): 8 entries, 2–4 sentences each, written as excerpts from a pre-war geographic survey or scholastic text. Describes each world's nature and primary race; does NOT describe the Warlock or current state.
Diaspola (water world, Halflings, warm and pastoral)
Tanis (forested earth world, Gnomes/Humans, ancient druidic tradition)
Auzra (layered sky world, Hawkmen, magitek and scholarship)
Arlune (moon-lit desert, diverse, sandship trade)
Vatra (volcanic forge world, Tieflings, magitek manufacture)
Illium (sunlit world, Half-Elves, devout and chivalric)
Nocturne (perpetual twilight, Fetchlings, renowned for art)
Candela (suspended spheres, Half-Orcs, the greatest university)
Heaven and the Underworld: What citizens know — Heaven is real and visible; the Underworld exists as a prison below the worlds; the Asuras originate there
IV. Society and Culture (~90 lines)
Five institutions a Nexus PC would know:

The Court of Nexus — Casomir Zukra, the Warlock-King. Five centuries old. Benevolent but exhausted. The court governs the city, runs the gate towns (now hollow), and maintains the Ancillary's sealed status. His reputation: just, distant, haunted.
The Host of Heaven — The celestial faith, once universal across the Nine Worlds. Mostly lapsed on Nexus (five centuries without contact with Heaven degrades institutional faith). Still observed by some, especially older families. The celestials are real; they simply haven't spoken in a very long time.
The Magitek Tradition — Not a guild but a practice: Nexus has developed and maintained magitek technology through the apocalypse and after. The Foundry district, the construct workers, the transportation network, the Ancillary's own mechanisms. An educated Nexus citizen understands magitek the way a modern person understands electricity — they use it without necessarily understanding it.
The Gate Towns — Eight settlements on Nexus's perimeter, each built around a Rainbow Road terminus. Empty for five centuries. The towns are maintained by Casomir's court as a statement of intent, not out of immediate practicality. Travelers occasionally visit for ruins or history. Gate town residents are technically citizens of Nexus.
The Oracle's Sanctum — A figure who lives in a fortified location east of the city, accessible only by crossing demon-held territory. The Oracle is real and has been consulted for centuries. What she says and what it means is another matter.
V. Character Creation (~480 lines)
Races (~100 lines)
All PF1e core, featured, and uncommon races are available
Brief note on demography: Nexus is the most diverse settlement in the Nine Worlds — every race that existed before the apocalypse has descendants here
Custom entry: Hawkmen (Strix) — The avian people of Auzra occasionally travel to Nexus as scouts, traders, or exiles. They are notable enough to warrant a full racial entry. Uses PF1e Strix as base with setting-specific lore and 1–2 alternate racial traits.
Custom entry: Fetchling (Kayal) — Nocturne's native people. Rare on Nexus but present; those who escaped Nocturne before the curse have established a small community. Uses PF1e Fetchling as base with setting-specific lore.

Mythic Paths (~60 lines)
What mythic power is: a grant from the celestial system, not a personal achievement. It seeks worthy vessels; it is not earned by training or study.
When it is granted: at the Great Ancillary (Chapter 3), then once per world thereafter
The six paths and how they manifest in a Nine Worlds champion:
Archmage — the Song resonates through spellwork; magic becomes more than technique
Champion — the Song resonates through the body; combat becomes transcendent
Guardian — the Song resonates through endurance; to stand is to hold the whole Road
Hierophant — the Song resonates through faith; what the Host of Heaven believed becomes real
Marshal — the Song resonates through connection; the Warlocks were turned by people who saw them, not by weapons
Trickster — the Song resonates through adaptability; every world is new, and the Road rewards those who learn quickly
The Vessel of the Song: if a PC dies before tier 9, the mythic power seeks a new vessel on the current world; the new PC enters at the same level and tier, carrying inherited memories. Death at tier 9+ triggers Immortal (return after 24 hours), except in the Underworld.
Campaign Traits (~50 lines)
Four traits connecting PCs to Nexus and to the campaign's themes:

Gate Town Born — Raised in one of the eight empty gate towns; you grew up in a place built for a purpose it no longer serves. Benefit: +1 trait bonus on Knowledge (planes) and it becomes a class skill. You can identify Rainbow Road gate structures by sight (no check required).
Ancillary Curious — You have studied what little public information exists about the Great Ancillary and its mechanisms. Something in those records has stayed with you. Benefit: +1 trait bonus on all Knowledge (arcana) checks related to celestial or mythic subjects; once per day you may add this bonus to any Perception check while in a place of power.
Demon Season Veteran — You have survived enough full-moon barricades to know how to move through demon-held territory. Benefit: +1 trait bonus on Stealth and Survival in urban or near-urban environments; you gain a +2 bonus on initiative checks when surprised by Asuras.
Court of Nexus — Service — You served in some capacity in Casomir's court or administration. You know the city's infrastructure and its players. Benefit: +1 trait bonus on Diplomacy and Sense Motive; once per campaign, you may call in a favor from a named court contact for a single significant service (information, access, or resources).
Character Hooks (~30 lines)
Six questions. Shared with the GM. Will become plot-relevant.

You have lived in Nexus for some or all of your life. What is the one thing about the city — one institution, one practice, one person — that you genuinely love?
You have had at least one encounter with a demon. Not a battle — an encounter. What happened, and what did it leave behind?
The Gate Towns are a peculiar fact of Nexian life — eight ghost towns built for roads that no longer exist, maintained for five centuries by a king who has outlived everyone who remembers why. What do you think they mean?
Casomir Zukra has ruled Nexus for five hundred years. What is your honest opinion of him?
The Host of Heaven is real — Heaven is visible in the night sky, the celestials are documented fact, and the faith has its texts and its history. What does a person do with that knowledge?
If a greater power told you that saving the world would cost you something you considered essential to who you are — your memory of someone, a relationship, a part of yourself — what would you do?
VI. What Your Character Knows (~55 lines)
Common Knowledge (8–10 bullet points) Things every Nexus resident knows:

The Rainbow Road exists; it has been dark for five centuries; the gate towns stand empty
Casomir Zukra is immortal (or near enough); he has ruled as long as anyone can remember
Demon raids happen on a predictable schedule (roughly weekly, with full moon surges). Barricades work. People die but not often.
The Great Ancillary is sealed, maintained by Casomir's court, and off-limits to civilians
Heaven is visible at night as an unusually bright and slightly moving star; the Host of Heaven faith is real
The Oracle exists, answers questions, and is accessible only by crossing demon-held territory; the court occasionally sends envoys
The Warlocks are the nine rulers of the outer worlds — corrupted former heroes who survived the apocalypse; they have ruled their worlds for five centuries in various states of madness and tyranny
The Song of Heaven is a legendary pre-war concept — a melodic sequence supposedly embedded in the Rainbow Road itself; few people on Nexus take it seriously as a practical concern
Rumors Table (d12)

Mix of true, partially true, and false
Topics: Casomir's health and unusual behavior, the gate towns showing signs of activity, the Oracle's recent warnings, demon raid patterns changing, the Host of Heaven seeing a resurgence, a traveler who claimed to have crossed the Road, the Warlocks' names and rumored deeds (distorted), the Ancillary's mechanisms and what's inside
