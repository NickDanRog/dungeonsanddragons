# WORLD REFERENCE

## Calendar
The imperial calendar mirrors the Gregorian structure and is used throughout this document for consistency. Local cultures have their own names for months and days, but the structural relationship is direct. Years are tracked as BC (Before the Conquest) and AC (After the Conquest); Year 0 is the year of Xandar's Conquest Declaration.

| Milestone | Year |
|---|---|
| Xandar's Conquest Declaration | Year 0 |
| Conquest completion | 24 AC |
| Current year (campaign start) | 55 AC |

## Technology Level
Mid-1600s equivalent.

- **Urban infrastructure:** Good sanitation and plumbing in most cities; less reliable in smaller settlements.
- **Medicine:** Germ theory and basic pharmacology understood. Anesthesia-based surgical procedures exist; expensive and uncommon outside major cities.
- **Gunpowder:** Exists, but the raw elements required are extremely rare. Typically confined to large fixed-mounted siege cannons. Basic rifles exist in limited numbers.
- **Metallurgy:** Advanced. High-quality steel is standard for weapons, armor, and tools.

### Grand Duchy of Severan

**Part:** I | **Levels:** 1–3 | **Act:** I
**Naming Convention:** Baltic-inspired — Latvian and Lithuanian adapted, not direct analogs

**Geography**
A large island off the northeastern coast of the Ambracian mainland, separated from it by the Strait of Ventė. The island divides naturally into geographic zones. The south is dominated by the Sitka mountain range, surrounded by badlands and rocky coastline — sparsely inhabited, no major settlements. The central region is damp, lightly forested grassland: highly productive for agriculture but subject to severe spring flooding; settlements and farmland throughout this zone are structured around flood management. The general coastline is rocky with few sheltered bays; fishing is concentrated at the handful of accessible harbor inlets, and coastal upland communities subsist primarily through herding (sheep are particularly common). The northwest peninsula is higher ground with clay-based soil — flood-resistant, well-suited for certain agriculture; the capital Vilniva is situated here along the sheltered shores of Principe Bay, with regular ship service to the Ambracian port of Leinka. The east coast is wooded upland — higher ground, more isolated, considered the island's backwater.

**Provinces**
None. Region I has no provincial subdivision; all settlements report directly to the regional level.

**History**
Severan and Ambracia fought intermittently across roughly five centuries, trading the roles of conqueror and conquered through successive generations. The last of these conflicts cooled around 100 BC, giving way to a period of mutual peace that produced a marriage alliance in 87 BC and eventual unification under a shared crown. Severan was incorporated into Ambracia through diplomacy rather than conquest — a distinction the island's people remember.

When the High Kingdom conquered Ambracia in 24 AC, Severan fell with it. Grand Duke Alfonse II vanished a few days after the official end of fighting, along with his twin brother Emil — a powerful wizard. The remaining ducal family was transferred to the Ambracian royal palace "for their safety" by High Kingdom officials. An imperial governor was installed to administer the island. The circumstances of their disappearance were never publicly explained.

In the three decades since, Severan has been a low-priority imperial tax territory — taxed heavily but not past the point of collapse, administered at arm's length. The island's distance from the center of imperial power has permitted widespread informal non-compliance: laws are ignored, mandates go unenforced, and tax avoidance is a community practice.

The philosophical root of the island's recurring revolutionary activity is the work of Linus Kateiva, an Academy alumnus executed by the High Kingdom in 43 AC. His writings — building on older anti-nobility movements — center on collective struggle, mutual support, and freedom from centralized authority. His ideals were proto-communistic in nature. He is widely regarded as a popular martyr. Multiple iterations of the Freedom Brigade have formed and been suppressed under his banner. The most recent was crushed one week before the campaign begins, at the Battle of the Pelagri Dam. Its leaderless survivors are retreating to Rumari as the campaign opens.

**Culture and Economy**
The people of Severan divide themselves conceptually into the common people (farmers, fishers, herders, craftspeople) and the intelligentsia (artists, philosophers, scholars, government workers). The nobility was effectively eliminated by the Conquest; the few survivors maintain very low profiles or have been absorbed into imperial service.

The common people tolerate the intelligentsia as a cultural necessity but regard them as soft-handed shirkers, and reserve their real contempt for the imperial administration and its local servants. Cooperation with government mandates is minimal; tax avoidance is treated as a community value; laws that conflict with local custom are ignored wherever the island's distance from the imperial center allows it.

Severan has a small industrial base. Most goods are produced locally — decent quality but expensive to replace. High imperial taxation has reduced imports steadily over three decades, and luxury goods are scarce and costly. The island's material culture reflects this: repair, reuse, and self-sufficiency are community norms.

The intellectual life of the island centers on two institutions. The Academy in Rumari is a technically private school that has largely evaded formal government control; it emphasizes philosophical and medical education and has historically been the ideological home of revolutionary sentiment — Kateiva was among its alumni. The current student body is factionally split between those who support imperial rule (having grown up under it) and those who oppose it, with tension that occasionally escalates toward open conflict. The Queen's School in Vilniva — formerly the Ducal School — is a more conventional institution, known primarily for its military academy; its civilian programs focus on the physical sciences and produce many who end up in imperial service.

**Thematic Role**
The revolution's birthplace. The campaign's mechanical systems are introduced here — mass combat, the economy, The Cause. The party transforms a grief-driven, leaderless popular movement into the nucleus of a continental revolution. The island's isolation is both advantage and test: Severan is a controlled environment for learning the systems, but resources are limited and the decisions made here will set the revolution's character for everything that follows.

A tiny settlement perched on the sea cliffs of the rock islet of the same name, off the southwestern coast of Severan. Home to an ancient shrine that predates the Conquest and has been a pilgrimage destination for generations of islanders. The community consists of a small order of monks and their attendants; the imperial administration largely ignores it. Father Tomais, a retired scholar and former colleague of Lord Emil Karima, lives here.

**Imperial Presence**
The garrison is commanded by General Telaris and administered by Governor Kem Selunis (see Key NPCs below). No Immortal is assigned; the island is not strategically significant enough to warrant one.

**Immortal Assigned:** None

**Force Composition**

| Location | Force | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Fort Bauriva | 20 regular units | Main garrison; General Telaris commands |
| Vilniva | 2 militia units | Under local commanders; capital defense |
| Jūrave | 1 militia unit | Under local commander |
| Sava | 1 militia unit | Under local commander |
| Valmyra | 1 militia unit | Under local commander |

The militia units outside Vilniva have questionable loyalty to the High Kingdom. They can be persuaded to stand down or defect through diplomacy or a demonstrated show of military force.

**Enemy Response Protocol**

Once the rebellion acts in force — marching as an army or capturing a settlement — General Telaris and Governor Selunis will respond in stages:

1. **Initial Response:** General Telaris dispatches 5 units to engage the rebel force. Primary objective: destroy the rebel army. Secondary objective: retake liberated or occupied settlements.
2. **After Defeat:** If the initial force is defeated, General Telaris retreats to Fort Bauriva. He takes half his remaining forces into the field to hunt the rebels, leaving a 7-unit garrison to hold the fort and protect Vilniva.
3. **Strategic Priority:** Destroying the rebel army takes precedence over holding settlements throughout.

**Key Factions Present**
- High Kingdom (imperial governor and garrison; no Immortal)
- Freedom Brigade (leaderless after Pelagri Dam defeat; survivors retreating to Rumari at campaign start)
- The Academy / Rumari intelligentsia (divided — historically dissident; factionally split between pro- and anti-imperial student bodies)
- Queen's School (Vilniva; imperial-aligned; produces soldiers and government specialists)
- Ducal Remnant (ducal family held in Ambracia; Ducal Guard's current loyalty and status is a Part I design question)
- Revolutionary Forces (does not exist at campaign start; forms during Part I)

**Starting Regional Loyalty Disposition**
The general population is anti-imperial but demoralized by the Battle of the Pelagri Dam. Base disposition is sympathetic to the revolution but not actively committed. The Part IX starting loyalty formula (base 3 + Cause modifier + transition conditions) applies; the population disposition suggests the cleared-region loyalty will likely settle at or below Neutral (3) unless the party takes specific positive actions during Part I. GM Guide transition conditions for this region will define the specific modifiers.

#### Sigluun

**Type:** Daemon (Lacridaemon) | **Pronouns:** it/its | **Alignment:** NE | **Faction:** Demonic Patrons | **CR:** 3 | **First Appears:** Part I

**Role**
Assigned by its superiors in the daemon hierarchy to monitor the Emerald Coffin and prevent the rescue of Duke Alfonse II Karima and Lord Emil Karima. Sigluun does not merely guard — it savors. The sustained grief emanating from the Coffin's prisoners is a source of sustenance, and Sigluun has grown attached to its post in ways its superiors did not anticipate.

**Personality**
Lacridaemons embody grief and abandonment. Sigluun is drawn to suffering the way other daemons are drawn to carnage. It does not hate its prisoners — it feeds on them emotionally, and the destruction of that source would represent a genuine loss.

**Motivations**
Acts on orders to keep the Karima brothers imprisoned and inaccessible. Beyond its mission, it is drawn to grief in all its forms and will extend its presence wherever it can harvest emotional anguish.

**Key Relationships**
- assigned by: Demonic Patrons hierarchy
- detains (through the Coffin): Alfonse II Karima, Emil Karima

**Secrets (DM Only)**
Sigluun is not the Coffin's only safeguard — the Coffin itself has magical properties; Sigluun supplements rather than replaces them. If reduced to 0 HP, Sigluun retreats to Abaddon and will not return for 1d4+2 days.

**Combat Role**
Ambush predator. Uses *pass without trace* and *invisibility* to approach undetected. Opens with *overwhelming grief* (Will DC 14) to disable the most vulnerable party members, then closes to melee using poisonous-tear-coated claws. Targets isolated characters. Teleports to escape if reduced below 10 HP or clearly outmatched; may summon a second lacridaemon if overwhelmed (1/day, 50% chance). Key abilities: weeping aura (−5 penalty on Survival checks within 100 ft.), DR 5/good or silver, SR 14, immune to acid/death effects/disease/poison, resist cold 10/electricity 10/fire 10.

**Arc**
Cannot be redeemed. Can be destroyed outright, banished, or driven off temporarily. The Emerald Coffin remains accessible once Sigluun is dealt with.

**Stat Block Reference:** Appendix B (Lacridaemon — standard PF1e stat block; no modifications required)

#### Antina Pole

**Type:** Bandit Leader / Folk Hero | **Pronouns:** she/her | **Alignment:** CG | **Faction:** Unaffiliated | **Class/Level:** Rogue 4 | **CR:** 3 | **First Appears:** Part I

**Role**
Bandit leader operating in the hills and forests of Severan, known to the common people as the "Wild Ass of the Mountain." Commands a veteran crew of irregulars. A folk hero throughout the region for a long career of spectacular pranks at the expense of imperial officials and soldiers — her calling card being the tendency to leave her victims without their trousers.

**Personality**
Irreverent, theatrical, and genuinely funny. She approaches every problem — including problems that arguably should not be approached this way — as an opportunity for spectacle. Beneath the theatrics she is deeply loyal to the people of Severan and possesses a visceral hatred of self-important authority.

**Motivations**
No grand ideology, but a genuine and joyful hatred of anyone powerful who takes themselves too seriously — especially if they happen to be crushing her people underfoot. **Key Relationships**
- commands: irregular crew (veteran bandits)
- folk hero to: common people of Severan

**Combat Role**
Uses misdirection, terrain, and manufactured chaos to devastating effect. Prefers to strike from ambush and vanish before anyone can regroup. In direct combat relies on Sneak Attack and dirty fighting. Her crew is well-practiced in her methods and synergizes effectively with them.

**Arc**
Will join the rebellion on her own terms — she wants to be a thorn in the High Kingdom's side, not a soldier following orders. Liberation or Covenant suits her naturally. Reformation is workable if the party frames it correctly. Dominion is the sticking point — she will be deeply suspicious of any cause that sounds like replacing one set of self-important rulers with another, and will need convincing the party is not becoming what they are fighting against. If persuaded, she serves as an excellent irregular commander and a significant morale asset among the common people.

### Queen Tegea

**Type:** NPC — Antagonist | **Pronouns:** she/her | **Alignment:** Lawful Evil
**Faction:** High Kingdom | **Class/Level:** TBD (warrior/magical practitioner)
**First Appears:** Part I (by reputation and presence felt); Part VII (in person, final confrontation)

**Role in Campaign**
Primary antagonist and final encounter. Tegea is the face of the High Kingdom across all seven Parts — her decisions shape imperial policy, her agents (the Five Immortals) represent her throughout Acts I and II, and the party works toward her for the entire campaign. She does not appear in person until Part VII, but her history, her choices, and the question of what she was before she became what she is now should be present throughout.

**Personality**
Tegea is defined by conviction and by what conviction cost her. She is not a tyrant who enjoys cruelty; she is a person who made a series of decisions under pressure, each of which felt necessary, and who has been living with those decisions for decades. She is capable of patience, strategic thinking, and tactical brilliance. She is not capable of admitting that she was wrong, because doing so would require her to account for everything that followed from the first wrong choice. She is harder than her father. She is also lonelier.

**Key Relationships**
- daughter of: King Xandar (dead; his legacy defines and haunts her)
- commands: Five Immortals
- bound to: Demonic Patrons (pact)
- opposed by: Revolutionary Forces (PCs)

**Secrets (DM Only)**
Tegea knows what she has become. She does not discuss it. There are moments, rarely, when something of Xandar's daughter is still visible — but she has buried it under enough necessity that it no longer surfaces except in private. She has considered, more than once, whether the High Kingdom she built is worth what it cost. She has never answered the question honestly.

**Arc**
Shaped by the party's Cause. In Liberation or Covenant playthroughs: the final obstacle. In Reformation: the final conversation — whether she is reachable is the Reformation track's central question. In Dominion: a mirror for what the party is becoming.

### The High Kingdom

**Type:** Imperial
**Regions Present:** All seven

**Public Face**
The legitimate government of the continent. A force for order and stability after five centuries of chaos. Many citizens — particularly in the Highlands and in Ambracia's more integrated communities — believe this sincerely. The High Kingdom maintains roads, administers law, suppresses the regional conflicts that plagued the continent before Xandar. It is not wrong that these things happened. It is wrong about why they are worth the price.

**Hidden Truth (DM Only)**
Sustained by demonic pacts. The senior leadership is functionally immortal and has been drifting, for decades, toward the interests of their demonic patrons rather than their subjects. The High Kingdom is not a corrupt institution that was once clean — it is an institution that was founded with genuine purpose and has been slowly hollowed out by the cost of maintaining itself.

**Relationship to Revolution**
Existential threat. The High Kingdom's entire legitimacy rests on the premise that it provides what no previous power could — stability, order, the end of the cycle. A successful revolution proves that premise wrong.

Queen Tegea (ruler), Five Immortals (regional agents)

### The Freedom Brigade

**Type:** Revolutionary (pre-existing)
**Regions Present:** Grand Duchy of Severan (origin); expands with the revolution

**Public Face**
An anti-imperial resistance movement with roots predating the Conquest itself. Earlier iterations fought the excesses of the regional nobility; the modern Freedom Brigade opposes the High Kingdom. Grounded in the philosophical writings of Linus Kateiva (executed 43 AC), the Brigade has risen and been suppressed repeatedly across generations — by violence as often as by the loss of internal cohesion. It has scored occasional minor victories but has never sustained organized resistance long enough to produce lasting change. The most recent iteration was crushed at the Battle of the Pelagri Dam one week before the campaign begins.

**Hidden Truth (DM Only)**
The Freedom Brigade's failure pattern is not purely military. The organization has repeatedly collapsed under internal disagreement — factional splits over goals, tactics, and who the movement actually serves. The ideological foundation Kateiva provided is real and resonant; the structural capacity to maintain coherent organization under pressure has never been built. The party will need to build what the Brigade never had.

**Relationship to High Kingdom**
Existential opposition. The Freedom Brigade exists to oppose imperial authority; the High Kingdom has suppressed it repeatedly by force whenever it became organized enough to threaten stability.

**Relationship to Revolution**
The Freedom Brigade is the revolution's raw material. Its surviving members — demoralized, leaderless, and retreating after Pelagri Dam — are the party's first recruits and the nucleus of the Revolutionary Forces. The organization transforms or dissolves; there is no third option.

**Key NPCs**

### [Demonic Patron(s)]

**Type:** Supernatural Antagonist
**Regions Present:** Manifest primarily in the Highlands (Part VII); influence felt throughout

**Nature and Origin**
TBD. The demonic patrons are the entities whose pacts sustain Queen Tegea and the Five Immortals. Their nature — what kind of entity they are, where they come from, whether they are a single entity or a hierarchy — is TBD with setting development.

**Agenda**
The demonic patrons' agenda is not simply to support the High Kingdom. They have a stake in the cycle of conflict continuing. Whether they are the structural cause of the five-hundred-year cycle, an opportunistic participant in it, or something older and stranger is a question the campaign will answer. Their goals and the High Kingdom's goals are not identical — and that gap is exploitable.

**Relationship to Pact**
They sustain the Immortals and Tegea in exchange for something. What that something is — service, souls, the perpetuation of a specific state of the world — is TBD.

**Physical Form**
TBD. Do they manifest physically? Under what conditions?

**Key Relationships**
- sustains: Queen Tegea
- sustains: Five Immortals
- benefits from: Cycle of conflict continuing
- opposed by: Revolutionary Forces (indirectly); directly in Reformation track
