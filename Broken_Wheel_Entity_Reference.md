### Grand Duchy of Severan

**Part:** I | **Levels:** 1–3 | **Act:** I

**Character**
A large island constituting roughly one third of the former Kingdom of Ambracia's territory. The Grand Duchy is geographically isolated from the mainland — an asset for the revolution's early phase, as imperial response time is slower and the population is more self-contained. The Great Kingdom regards Severan as a political and economic backwater, which is why its imperial garrison is limited. The local population retains strong Ambracian cultural identity.

*[Terrain, major settlements, and economic profile TBD with setting development.]*

**Imperial Presence**
Small but professional garrison force. No Immortal assigned. Imperial administrator TBD.

**Immortal Assigned:** None

**Key Factions Present**
- Great Kingdom (garrison and administrator)
- Revolutionary Forces (forming)
- *[Local factions TBD]*

**Regional Loyalty (starting)**
High — Severan is Ambracian; news of the executions has landed hard here. The uprising is already beginning when the campaign opens.

**Thematic Role**
The revolution's birthplace. The campaign's mechanical systems are introduced here (mass combat, economy, The Cause). The party transforms a grief-driven popular uprising into an organized revolutionary force. The island's isolation is both an advantage and a test: can the revolution sustain itself before it has to face the mainland?

### Kingdom of Ambracia

**Part:** II | **Levels:** 4–6 | **Act:** I

**Character**
The players' home kingdom — the mainland territories outside Severan. Ambracia is where the Heir's failed revolt occurred and where the executions took place. The population has strong revolutionary sympathy, though the Great Kingdom's military presence here is more substantial than in Severan. Emotionally, this is the revolution's most charged theater.

*[Terrain, major settlements, and economic profile TBD with setting development.]*

**Imperial Presence**
A small but well-organized imperial field army. Military administrator TBD.

**Immortal Assigned:** None

**Key Factions Present**
- Great Kingdom (field army and administration)
- Revolutionary Forces (expanding from Severan)
- *[Local factions TBD]*

**Regional Loyalty (starting)**
Very high — the executions happened here. The population is primed. The party arrives as liberators among their own people.

**Thematic Role**
The revolution's home ground. This is where the party confronts the martyrs and what was done in the name of the old royal family. The revolution's character begins to take public shape here — early choices about governance and treatment of the population establish precedents that will follow the party for the rest of the campaign.

### Kingdom of Delft

**Part:** III | **Levels:** 7–9 | **Act:** II

**Character**
A large kingdom to the east of Ambracia. Delft has its own history and identity distinct from Ambracia; the Great Kingdom's conquest here is older and more normalized for the local population. The party arrives without the moral clarity of fighting on home ground.

*[Terrain, major settlements, and economic profile TBD with setting development.]*

**Imperial Presence**
More substantial than Ambracia — a larger and better-organized field force, plus administrative apparatus. The First Immortal governs directly.

**Immortal Assigned:** First Immortal

**Key Factions Present**
- Great Kingdom (First Immortal + field army)
- Revolutionary Forces (arriving as outsiders)
- *[Local Delft factions TBD]*

**Regional Loyalty (starting)**
Neutral to skeptical — the population has no particular love for the Great Kingdom, but neither does it have reason to welcome an Ambracian-led revolution. The party must argue for its cause.

**Thematic Role**
The first test of whether the revolution can extend beyond its home constituency. Winning Delft requires persuasion as much as force — the party cannot simply fight its way through. The First Immortal is the party's first direct encounter with what Tegea's pact has made.

### The Shining South

**Part:** IV | **Levels:** 10–12 | **Act:** II

**Character**
A cold southern region consisting of powerful independent city-states and a large territory formerly controlled by the church, now reorganized under imperial authority. The city-states each have their own interests and governance; no single military victory resolves this theater. The Second Immortal heads the Corrupted Church, which has deep institutional reach throughout the region.

*[Specific city-states, terrain, and economic profile TBD with setting development.]*

**Imperial Presence**
The Corrupted Church (institutional reach across the region). Imperial forces TBD. The Second Immortal governs via religious authority rather than direct military command.

**Immortal Assigned:** Second Immortal

**Key Factions Present**
- Great Kingdom (via the Corrupted Church)
- Corrupted Church (Second Immortal)
- Original Church remnant (in hiding)
- City-state factions (multiple, divergent interests)
- Revolutionary Forces (arriving)

**Regional Loyalty (starting)**
Divided — city-states are self-interested; populations in the former church territory have complex relationships with the institution that once governed them.

**Thematic Role**
Political complexity dominates. The party must navigate multiple city-state interests while dismantling the church's imperial grip. The original church remnant offers an alternative institutional structure if the party finds and supports it. The Second Immortal is a different kind of threat from the First — governing through religious authority and social control rather than military force.

The coastal and maritime nature of the region introduces naval and logistics challenges.

### The Confederation

**Part:** VI | **Levels:** 16–18 | **Act:** III

**Character**
The largest of the seven regions. The Confederation was historically a collection of competing states that the Great Kingdom unified by conquest — suppressing conflicts rather than resolving them. The dozen states have divergent agendas, no common history of voluntary cooperation, and no loyalty to each other beyond the imperial order that held them together. The Fourth Immortal commands a large field army that actively works to disrupt, interdict, and undermine the revolution throughout this Part.

*[The dozen states and their specific agendas TBD with setting development.]*

**Imperial Presence**
The Fourth Immortal with a large field army. Imperial administrative apparatus across the dozen states. This is the strongest military opposition the party has faced.

**Immortal Assigned:** Fourth Immortal (active throughout, not waiting for a set-piece confrontation)

**Key Factions Present**
- Great Kingdom (Fourth Immortal + army)
- Confederation State Factions (dozen, divergent)
- Revolutionary Forces (arriving)

**Regional Loyalty (starting)**
Fractured — different states have different relationships with the revolution and with each other. No single approach works across the whole Confederation.

**Thematic Role**
Maximum political and military complexity. The party must govern here, not just fight — it must build enough consensus among factions that distrust each other to function as a coherent theater. The Fourth Immortal's active disruption means the party cannot secure a state and move on; they must manage the whole Confederation simultaneously.

### The Highlands

**Part:** VII | **Levels:** 19–20 | **Act:** III

**Character**
The Great Kingdom's heartland — once the continent's forgotten backwater, now transformed into its economic and cultural center by decades of wealth extracted from the other six regions. The Highlands is where Xandar unified his people and launched the original conquest. It is also where the Great Kingdom's administrative, military, and supernatural infrastructure is concentrated. Defeating the Highlands does not just mean defeating an army; it means confronting the full accumulated power of seventy-five years of empire.

*[Geography, major settlements, and economic profile TBD with setting development.]*

**Imperial Presence**
Maximum. The Fifth Immortal commands the Highlands' defense. Any surviving Immortals from previous Parts may also be present. Queen Tegea herself is here.

**Immortal Assigned:** Fifth Immortal (+ surviving Immortals from earlier Parts)

**Key Factions Present**
- Great Kingdom (full remaining strength)
- Five Immortals (survivors)
- Queen Tegea
- Demonic Patrons (manifest at this stage)
- Revolutionary Forces (arriving)
- *[Highlands population factions TBD]*

**Regional Loyalty (starting)**
Low — the Highlands population has benefited from the empire's extraction economy and has the most to lose from its collapse. Some will support the revolution; many will not.

**Thematic Role**
The revolution arrives at its destination. All four Causes converge here but resolve differently. This is where the wheel's fate is decided — broken, repaired, or turned again. The final confrontation with Tegea is shaped entirely by the party's Cause and the accumulated weight of every choice made across Parts I–VI.

# KEY NPCS

### Queen Tegea

**Type:** NPC — Antagonist | **Pronouns:** she/her | **Alignment:** Lawful Evil
**Faction:** Great Kingdom | **Class/Level:** TBD (warrior/magical practitioner)
**First Appears:** Part I (by reputation and presence felt); Part VII (in person, final confrontation)

**Role in Campaign**
Primary antagonist and final encounter. Tegea is the face of the Great Kingdom across all seven Parts — her decisions shape imperial policy, her agents (the Five Immortals) represent her throughout Acts I and II, and the party works toward her for the entire campaign. She does not appear in person until Part VII, but her history, her choices, and the question of what she was before she became what she is now should be present throughout.

**Personality**
Tegea is defined by conviction and by what conviction cost her. She is not a tyrant who enjoys cruelty; she is a person who made a series of decisions under pressure, each of which felt necessary, and who has been living with those decisions for decades. She is capable of patience, strategic thinking, and tactical brilliance. She is not capable of admitting that she was wrong, because doing so would require her to account for everything that followed from the first wrong choice. She is harder than her father. She is also lonelier.

**Key Relationships**
- daughter of: King Xandar (dead; his legacy defines and haunts her)
- commands: Five Immortals
- bound to: Demonic Patrons (pact)
- opposed by: Revolutionary Forces (PCs)

**Secrets (DM Only)**
Tegea knows what she has become. She does not discuss it. There are moments, rarely, when something of Xandar's daughter is still visible — but she has buried it under enough necessity that it no longer surfaces except in private. She has considered, more than once, whether the Great Kingdom she built is worth what it cost. She has never answered the question honestly.

**Arc**
Shaped by the party's Cause. In Liberation or Covenant playthroughs: the final obstacle. In Reformation: the final conversation — whether she is reachable is the Reformation track's central question. In Dominion: a mirror for what the party is becoming.

**Stat Block Reference:** Appendix B (to be written in GM Guide, Part VII)

### King Xandar

**Type:** NPC — Historical Figure | **Pronouns:** he/him | **Alignment:** Lawful Good (former)
**Faction:** Great Kingdom (founder; dead ~40 years before campaign) | **Class/Level:** TBD
**First Appears:** Referenced throughout; no in-person appearance

**Role in Campaign**
Xandar is the adventure's historical conscience — the founding figure whose original purpose was genuine and whose failure frames the central question. The party will learn about him progressively across the campaign: first as the empire's founder, then as Tegea's father, then as a man who was trying to do what the party is trying to do. By Part VII, the full weight of his arc should be legible: the revolution that became an empire, and the daughter who finished it.

**Personality**
By all accounts: principled, ambitious, and genuinely ethical. He was not naive about the difficulty of what he was attempting — he understood that unifying the continent would require violence — but he maintained lines he would not cross. His death mid-campaign meant those lines died with him and Tegea inherited a war that had already crossed some of them.

**Key Relationships**
- father of: Queen Tegea
- founded: Great Kingdom
- killed during: Conquest (before campaign begins)
- originally attempted: to break the wheel

**Secrets (DM Only)**
Xandar's true historical record — his founding purpose, his methods, the choices he made before he died — is one of the adventure's revelations. The official Great Kingdom history presents him as a visionary conqueror. The truth is more complicated and more sympathetic.

**Arc**
Static — he is dead. His arc is revealed, not played. Every revelation about Xandar recontextualizes the revolution, the Great Kingdom, and (by the end) the party's own choices.

**Stat Block Reference:** N/A

### The Great Kingdom

**Type:** Imperial
**Regions Present:** All seven

**Public Face**
The legitimate government of the continent. A force for order and stability after five centuries of chaos. Many citizens — particularly in the Highlands and in Ambracia's more integrated communities — believe this sincerely. The Great Kingdom maintains roads, administers law, suppresses the regional conflicts that plagued the continent before Xandar. It is not wrong that these things happened. It is wrong about why they are worth the price.

**Hidden Truth (DM Only)**
Sustained by demonic pacts. The senior leadership is functionally immortal and has been drifting, for decades, toward the interests of their demonic patrons rather than their subjects. The Great Kingdom is not a corrupt institution that was once clean — it is an institution that was founded with genuine purpose and has been slowly hollowed out by the cost of maintaining itself.

**Relationship to Revolution**
Existential threat. The Great Kingdom's entire legitimacy rests on the premise that it provides what no previous power could — stability, order, the end of the cycle. A successful revolution proves that premise wrong.

**Key NPCs**
Queen Tegea (ruler), Five Immortals (regional agents)

**Loyalty to Revolution:** N/A (antagonist)

### The Revolutionary Forces

**Type:** Revolutionary
**Regions Present:** Expands with campaign progression (begins in Severan)

**Public Face**

### The Corrupted Church

**Type:** Religious (Imperial)
**Regions Present:** The Shining South

**Public Face**
The established religious institution of the Shining South, reorganized and "reformed" under imperial direction. Presents itself as a cleaned-up, doctrinally coherent version of the original church — more efficient, less politically fractured, more spiritually focused. Many ordinary believers experience it as simply their church.

**Hidden Truth (DM Only)**
The "reform" was a purge. Elements that resisted Great Kingdom authority were removed, exiled, or killed. The Second Immortal heads the institution. The original church's surviving members — the ones who kept the old values — are in hiding somewhere in the Shining South. They represent an alternative institutional structure the party may find and support.

**Relationship to Great Kingdom**
Core instrument of imperial control in the Shining South. The church is how the Second Immortal governs — through belief rather than force.

**Relationship to Revolution**
Hostile by institutional design. Individual believers may be sympathetic; the institution's leadership is not.

**Key NPCs:** Second Immortal

**Loyalty to Revolution:** Hostile (institution); variable (individual believers)

### The Elven Remnant

**Type:** Regional (suppressed population)
**Regions Present:** The Hundred Isles

**Public Face**
Scattered survivors of the former elven kingdom, now a marginal population within the Hundred Isles without formal political standing. Visible enough that their suppression is a known fact; powerless enough that the Great Kingdom does not regard them as a threat.

**Hidden Truth (DM Only)**
The Elven Remnant is not as fragmented as it appears. There is organization beneath the surface — memory, preserved institutions, communities that have maintained continuity despite the suppression. How organized they are and what they want is TBD with setting development.

**Relationship to Great Kingdom**
The Great Kingdom destroyed their kingdom. Their hostility is total and permanent.

**Relationship to Revolution**
Deeply skeptical. The revolution is another outside power arriving in their home with promises. They have heard promises before. What the party does — whether it treats their liberation as a goal or as a secondary concern, whether it keeps its word — determines whether the Elven Remnant becomes an ally, a neutral party, or a third faction the party must manage.

**Key NPCs:** TBD

**Loyalty to Revolution:** Low (starting); earned through specific actions in Part V

### The Confederation's Factions

**Type:** Regional (multiple)
**Regions Present:** The Confederation

*[Twelve states, each with their own agenda. Individual faction entries TBD with setting development.]*

**General Character**
The Confederation's states have divergent interests and no history of voluntary cooperation. Imperial rule imposed order on top of unresolved rivalries. What they share: a common experience of conquest and a common distrust of outside power. What divides them: everything else.

**Relationship to Great Kingdom**
