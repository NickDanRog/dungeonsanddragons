# DARK GOSPEL
## Design Document

*D&D 5e Adventure Module — Design Blueprint*
*Compiled: March 2026*

---

## Adventure Overview

- **System:** D&D 5e (2014 ruleset)
- **Level Range:** 3–12
- **Structure:** 3 acts, variable chapters
- **Tone:** Dark fantasy investigation — institutional corruption, forbidden religion, and genuine supernatural horror balanced against martial intrigue and intelligence work. Characters face not just a physical threat but a philosophical one: an entity that holds that existence itself is a lie, and has the patience and reach to make that argument through the people closest to the crown.
- **Player Character Type:** Native citizens of Artaxarta with a strong connection to the royal family. From the first session, PCs are drawn into royal service as members of a special unit charged with protecting the young Prince Eurimedos and executing missions on behalf of the crown in his name.

---

## Central Premise

The cult of Umgul — long suppressed in Artaxarta as worshipers of a dark god associated with sorcery, corruption, and evil — is not what the kingdom believes it to be. The traditional understanding connects Umgul to Altair, the dark aspect of the Twelve Divines. This is wrong.

The cult is actually a vessel for the **No-God**: an ancient nihilistic entity that predates the Twelve Divines and whose sole imperative is the destruction of all existence. The No-God does not seek worship, power, or dominion — it seeks annihilation. It forms pacts with mortals, usually unwittingly, through the seductive logic of its tenets: that existence is a lie, that suffering is the result of clinging to false permanence, and that oblivion is the only truth. Those who serve the No-God find that the price of the pact escalates inexorably — and that there is no exit.

The primary instrument of the No-God's current incursion is **Orestes Sarkhazi**, Lord of the Magisterium, a scholar-nobleman elevated to the King's council whose entanglement with the No-God began in an act of apparent piety and has over three decades corrupted him completely. He seeks to use Artaxarta — its armies, its fortifications, its people — as a base from which to prosecute the No-God's agenda against the wider world.

**What players do not know at the start:**
- The cult of Umgul has no connection to Altair or to any recognized deity
- The entity behind the cult is the No-God, something far older and more dangerous than the church's official account
- The incursion is directed by a specific individual within the kingdom's power structure
- The young prince they are charged with protecting has been identified by the No-God as a potential next vessel — and his grief, his isolation, and his hunger for understanding make him susceptible

## The World

*Full reference: artaxarta.md. Summary below.*

Artaxarta is an ancient mountain kingdom and one of the most powerful successor states of the former Bandorian Empire. It occupies a network of high mountain valleys fed by twelve river systems flowing from the Yashim peaks — rugged terrain that has defeated every would-be conqueror except the Bandorians, and even that conquest took twenty years and extracted enormous cost. The culture that resulted is a fusion of native Artaxartoi traditions with Xoshan and Durasi colonial influence, producing a society that prizes merit, martial excellence, and loyalty above sentiment.

**Key geographic features:**
- The **Yashim Mountains** — eastern border; virtually impassable
- The **twelve river valleys** — the agricultural and economic heart of the kingdom; the fertility that makes this country worth controlling
- The **high upland pastures** — home to herding clans, the Zavra-Prag tribal militia, and the Sadars
- **Alcidea** — the capital; ancient, planned in the old Xoshan style, situated at the foot of the cliff that holds Mazrok
- The **Citadel of Mazrok** — built into the cliff face above Alcidea; has fallen once in history (by a mountaineering raid); otherwise impregnable; contains its own aquifer and years of stores
- The **Brotherhood of Light's mountain fortress** — in the southern highlands; location of the Act I siege

**Key institutions:**
- The **Crown** — absolute monarchy; King (deceased by end of Act I) → Prince Eurimedos
- The **Kataphractoi** — royal heavy cavalry; 900 soldiers; the kingdom's military elite; the prince's guard unit draws from their ranks
- The **Legonis Hykyrianos** — seven professional legions descended from Bandorian Imperial forces; disciplined, heraldic, effective
- The **Zavra-Prag** — tribal militia; the bulk of the army; quality varies wildly by clan; fiercely loyal when properly led
- The **Brotherhood of Light** — holy order of 250 adepts devoted to Ilura; operate outside the crown's chain of command; the kingdom's primary suppressors of the Umgul cult; the Act I siege targets them specifically for this reason
- The **Kryptea** — the kingdom's secret intelligence service; embedded throughout the court and the noble class; functionally destroyed in the fall of Alcidea
- The **Sadars** — tribal leaders who serve as cultural nobility and royal representatives in the uplands; hold the loyalty of the Zavra-Prag; must be won over individually

**Religion:**
The Artaxartoi practice a form of the Twelve Divines faith heavily shaped by native tradition. Ilura (the warrior-mother goddess, native Lhakmana) is the dominant deity and the object of near-universal veneration. The cult of Altair is officially forbidden and punishable by death; worshipers of Umgul are persecuted under the same laws, as citizens and authorities do not distinguish between the two. The Brotherhood of Light exists specifically to enforce this prohibition. This is the misidentification the No-God has been hiding behind.

**Neighboring States:**
- **Kentana** — to the south; a wealthy mountain kingdom known for gemstone trade; secluded and secretive, with many commercial ties to Artaxarta but little broader engagement with the wider world
- **Asta** — to the southwest; shares a mountainous and nearly impassable border with Artaxarta; few ties between the two; critically, the Astans worship Altair — whom the Artaxartoi equate with their ancient enemy Umgul — making religious tension a permanent feature of the relationship
- **Morna** — to the west; a militaristic dictatorship with whom relations have always been poor; the Mornai claim a large portion of western Artaxarta and have fought several border wars over the region since the fall of the Empire, losing each one; the Artaxartoi regard Morna as dangerous but something of a joke, and are quietly planning to invade and install a client state; the practice of large-scale slavery in Morna is considered a mark of evil by most Artaxartoi
- **Kyrexos** — to the northwest; a regional power roughly equal in size and strength to Artaxarta; a Delian state with powerful northern allies; relations have historically been unstable, with several small border wars since the fall of the Empire; the king believes Kyrexos is preparing an invasion; despite the tension, both countries share cultural roots through their Delian heritage and their founding by the general Alcias
- **Kesh** — to the north; a large and populous state with strong trading ties and a generally peaceful relationship with Artaxarta; the Keshites are an ethnically distinct people whose culture the Artaxartoi find deeply strange
- **The Kelish States** — to the southeast; not a single state but seventy-two independent tribes, once unified under the Empire and now engaged in endless internal conflict since its fall; the border is virtually impassable; no meaningful contact with Artaxarta, though their armies are known to be large and disorganized

## The Antagonists

### Orestes Sarkhazi
*Lord of the Magisterium. Priest of the No-God. Primary antagonist.*

**Who they are:** Born to a family of modest means, Orestes Sarkhazi trained for a career in the royal civil service — and failed his examinations. The humiliation redirected his considerable intellect toward the royal library, where he found work as a clerk and copyist. His mentor there, a senior archivist and secret worshiper of the No-God, recognized the young man's hunger for knowledge and eventually showed him a hidden vault containing ancient Umgul texts.

When Orestes discovered what his mentor truly was, he killed him — a sincere act of piety he believed to be righteous. He hid the body in the vault in shame, not for the killing but for the method. What followed were years of dreams in which his mentor appeared to him: not in accusation, but in invitation. The dreams were seductive. The vault was there. Eventually, he read the texts.

What he found transformed him. The knowledge he extracted — on philosophy, natural history, mathematics, metaphysics, and hidden mechanisms of the world — proved applicable far beyond any forbidden theology. He applied it broadly and rose to become one of the most respected scholars in the five cities, cited and sought out on subjects he had taught himself from texts no one else had touched. He believed for a long time that he had taken the useful knowledge from the vault and left the darkness behind.

He had not. When the texts yielded nothing more, the No-God came to him in a dream with an offer: more knowledge, in exchange for a pact. He accepted. The increased mastery that followed brought him to the King's attention. He was appointed Lord of the Magisterium — leader of the crown's circle of foremost scholars and scientists, reporting to the King on matters of learning and natural inquiry — and elevated to the royal council. He has held that position for three decades, during which time he has quietly cultivated the cult's presence in every institution he could reach. The network he has built is patient, thorough, and nearly invisible. He is now the most dangerous person in the kingdom, and no one knows it.

**What they want:** To complete the No-God's incursion: destabilize the kingdom, eliminate the Brotherhood of Light (the institution most likely to identify the No-God's true nature), eliminate the royal line, and use Artaxarta's military and geographic position as a staging ground for the entity's broader destruction campaign.

**Methods:** Infiltration and manipulation before direct confrontation. Orestes has spent three decades cultivating the cult within the kingdom's power structure — corrupting officials, compromising the Kryptea, and positioning trusted agents near the crown. He is patient, intelligent, and was — before the corruption deepened — genuinely considered one of the kingdom's most capable minds. Direct confrontation is a last resort.

**Arc:** The No-God's pact began as intellectual seduction and escalated past any point of return. By the adventure's start, Orestes knows there is no exit and has made his peace with what he has become — but he is not without memory. He can still feel the shape of the person he used to be, the man who killed his mentor because he thought it was righteous, and the possibility of redemption, at catastrophic personal cost, remains accessible if the PCs create the conditions for it.

### The No-God
*Background antagonist; presence intensifies through Acts II and III.*

An ancient nihilistic entity that predates the Twelve Divines. It has no desires in the conventional sense — it has a single imperative: the destruction of all existence. It operates through the pacts it forms with mortals, bending them toward its purpose through a philosophy that is genuinely seductive to people in sufficient pain.

**Tenets of the No-God:**
- The world, those around you, and existence itself are a lie
- Destroy so that you may end the suffering brought on by the falsehood of perceived reality
- For there to be true peace, all things must end
- Devote yourself to purging those who work to uphold these impermanent falsehoods
- Give in to the darkness of oblivion — it is the one universal truth

The No-God does not manifest physically in this adventure. It operates through its pact-holders and through the corruption it induces in those exposed to its influence. Its most dangerous weapon is the internal coherence of its philosophy, which makes it capable of reaching people the cult cannot.

**In Act III:** The No-God's presence becomes felt more directly as the antagonist's plan approaches completion — environmental corruption, supernatural effects, and the full weight of its attention on the prince and the PCs.

## Key NPCs

### Prince Eurimedos Toxamones Alicimidia
*8 years old. The crown's heir. The PCs' primary charge.*

Small for his age, with the intense focused attention of a child who has spent more time among books than in training yards. He is not a warrior and shows no early signs of becoming one — but he possesses a near-obsessive knowledge of military history and tactical theory that regularly startles the adults around him. His father believed he would make a great king someday; whether the events of this campaign prove that right depends partly on choices the PCs make on his behalf.

**Role in the adventure:** Eurimedos is the PCs' responsibility, their connection to legitimate royal authority, and — unknown to him or to them — the No-God's identified next vessel. His grief at his father's death, his intellectual isolation, and his hunger for understanding make him susceptible in ways the cult is actively working to exploit. Whether he is protected or corrupted is determined by PC decisions across the campaign.

**Secret:** The cult is not trying to kill the prince. They are trying to reach him.

### General Tanos Mitradanes Heskalo (The Laughing Lion)
*Late 50s. Commanding General of the Royal Guard.*

Massive frame, a great blonde beard shot through with gray, and a battlefield reputation for laughing in situations that make other men pale — hence the name. He served alongside the king for decades and fought campaigns across the full breadth of the kingdom. He survives the fall of Alcidea and spirits the prince toward Mazrok, but the death of his oldest friend has broken something in him that laughter no longer quite covers.

**Role in the adventure:** The Laughing Lion is the PCs' commanding officer and the senior military authority in the prince's circle. He is experienced, capable, and completely trustworthy — but increasingly erratic as grief takes hold. The PCs may find themselves needing to protect him from his own worst impulses as much as from external threats.

### Delana Geminia Sabinus
*Junior Kryptea officer. The only known member of the service to survive the fall of Alcidea.*

She has spent years posing as a lady's maid to Kaske Heskalo, her childhood friend, as cover for her actual work — intelligence gathering at the heart of court life. She is meticulous, patient, and intensely loyal to Kaske. She is also carrying the full weight of watching an institution she dedicated her career to be dismantled overnight by an enemy that had been inside it for years.

**Role in the adventure:** Delana is the PCs' intelligence resource — their window into what remains of the Kryptea's network, their best asset for understanding the cult's infiltration of the court, and the thread from which any rebuilt intelligence capacity must grow.

## The Prince's Retainers

The following NPCs escaped Alcidea with the prince. They are allies with their own capabilities, loyalties, and story threads.

### Kaske Heskalo
*Cavalry officer. Daughter of the General.*

One of the best riders in the kingdom — the only person on record to win a Kataphractoi tournament slot after being relegated to the auxiliary corps. Decisive, capable, and possessed of the particular confidence of someone who has had to earn every position twice over. Her relationship with her father is close but complicated by military hierarchy.

### Marcus Geminius Sabinus
*Former legionary officer. Delana's twin brother.*

Quiet where his sister is watchful, contemplative where she is practical. He is the person Eurimedos spends long hours with discussing military history and tactical theory — a bond that is genuine on both sides and has given Marcus a quiet influence over the prince that no one else quite matches. He is wholly devoted to the prince. He does not speak about why he left the legions.

### Fleur Dontillion
*Former mercenary. One-eyed. The only foreigner in the royal guard.*

From the Kingdom of Murundus, far to the west. Her skills with lance and sword are described as legendary even in a unit that sets a high bar. No one in the guard is entirely certain how she arrived there; no one argues with the results. She is not forthcoming about her past.

### Abala Kadar
*Son of a tribal chieftain. The prince's personal bodyguard.*

Trained for this single role from childhood. He has no ambiguity about his purpose and no interest in acquiring any. He will go to any length to protect the prince — which is his greatest strength and, in the right circumstances, a vulnerability that someone with enough leverage could exploit.

## Recruitable NPCs

### Marcus Ivor
*Paladin of the Order of Light. Former Knight of Vista.*

A foreigner from the Free City of Vista, far to the west. He was once one of Vista's famous Knights before taking his oath to Ilura and joining the Brotherhood of Light — a transition that implies a significant prior history he does not volunteer. Can be recruited in Act I if the PCs help the Brotherhood successfully repel the siege of their fortress. His faith is genuine; his capabilities are formidable; his knowledge of the Umgul cult's history is the PCs' best early window into what the No-God actually is.

### Arslan Zedan
*Young tribal chieftain. Cavalry tactician.*

A lancer considered without peer even in a kingdom that takes horsemanship seriously, and famous for innovative cavalry tactics that have surprised commanders who thought they knew what a warband could do. His reputation borders on villainous — the details vary by who is asked. He wants revenge on the cult for the deaths of his family. He brings a small warband if recruited. Can be brought into the unit early in Act II if the PCs assist him in assaulting a cultist camp.

## Adventure Structure

### Act I: The Black Flame *(Chapters 1–3, Levels 3–6)*
*In which the PCs discover that a cult long assumed to be a historical footnote is active, organized, and connected to something far older than the church's official account — and return home to find that the world they knew is gone.*

- **Chapter 1 — A Summons and a Silence:** The PCs are called into royal service and dispatched to investigate an unknown force besieging the Brotherhood of Light's southern fortress. The road provides early evidence that something unusual is moving through the uplands.
- **Chapter 2 — The Siege of the Order:** The PCs arrive to find the siege active. They assist the Brotherhood in breaking it. In the aftermath, their first evidence emerges that the cult is not what the kingdom believes — these are not Altair's servants. Marcus Ivor can be recruited here.
- **Chapter 3 — The City That Was:** The PCs return to Alcidea on the night of the Jinsoona to find it already falling. The cult struck simultaneously across the kingdom at the height of the festival — the streets are full of fire and chaos, the king is dead, and the capital cannot be held. The PCs extract through the burning city, encounter Prince Eurimedos and his party on the road to Mazrok, and fight off pursuing cultists. Act I ends when they reach the Citadel.

### Act II: The Remnant Kingdom *(Turns 1–12, Levels 6–9)*
*In which the PCs operate as the crown's instrument from the Citadel of Mazrok — executing missions, building alliances, and gathering intelligence against the clock — while Orestes Sarkhazi is slowly identified and the prince's vulnerability quietly deepens. Act II ends when all missions have been resolved. The Jurga follows immediately, opening Act III.*

#### The Mission Framework

Act II is structured not as traditional linear chapters but as a **mission pool** of 12 available operations divided into three categories:

- **Spiritual Missions** — Target cult rituals, cult leaders, ritual sites, and the No-God's growing supernatural influence
- **Civic Missions** — Rescue captive populations, disrupt the cult's control over ordinary people, and protect loyalist communities
- **Military Missions** — Target cultist military assets, reinforce loyalist fighting strength, and secure strategic resources

Act II runs for a maximum of **12 turns**. Each turn represents **20 days**. The PCs may attempt **one mission per turn**. At the end of turn 12, Act II concludes regardless of how many missions remain — the Jurga is called, and Act III begins.

#### Turn Structure

Each turn proceeds in the following order:

1. **Intelligence Reveal Roll.** At the start of each turn, the DM rolls d20 + the party's current Intelligence bonus against the DC of each unrevealed mission in any unlocked wave. Every mission whose DC is beaten is revealed to the party.
2. **Mission Selection.** The party chooses which available (revealed) mission to attempt.
3. **Mission Execution.** The selected mission is played out.
4. **Intelligence Gain.** On completing a mission, the party gains +1 to their Intelligence bonus (maximum +8).
5. **Investigation Roll (Optional).** After completing a mission, any PC may attempt a DC equal to the higher of 15 or the mission's original reveal DC. On a success, the DM identifies which currently-available mission faces the highest expiration risk and conveys the urgency in narrative terms.
6. **Expiration Roll.** At the end of each turn, the DM rolls d100 for every revealed but uncompleted mission. Any mission whose roll meets or falls under its current expiration percentage is lost — the conditions that made it possible no longer exist.

#### Intelligence and Wave Unlocking

The party's **Intelligence bonus** begins at +0 and increases by +1 for each mission completed, to a maximum of +8. It serves two purposes: it adds to the DM's revelation rolls, and it unlocks higher-tier mission waves.

| Wave | Missions | Reveal DC | Unlocks When Intel Reaches |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wave 1 | 3 starting missions (1 per category) | Pre-revealed | Available immediately |
| Wave 2 | Missions 4–6 | DC 14 | Intel +0 (from turn 1) |
| Wave 3 | Missions 7–9 | DC 17 | Intel +3 |
| Wave 4 | Missions 10–12 | DC 20 | Intel +6 |

Missions in a locked wave cannot be rolled for, revealed, or expire until that wave unlocks. Expiration clocks begin when a wave unlocks, not from turn 1.

#### Mission Expiration

Every mission operates on one of three expiration tracks. The expiration percentage begins when the mission's wave unlocks and increases each turn thereafter.

| Track | Starting % | Increase Per Turn | Effective Window | Typical Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fast | 25% | +25% per turn | ~3 turns | Time-critical Wave 1–2 missions (rescue before execution, etc.) |
| Standard | 15% | +15% per turn | ~6 turns | Default for Wave 2–3 missions |
| Slow | 10% | +10% per turn | ~10 turns | Default for Wave 4 missions |

#### Narrative Milestones Within Act II

The following events occur within the mission framework regardless of turn order; the DM should weave them into the act as appropriate:

- **Arslan Zedan recruitment** — Available early in Act II via a Military mission; unlocks him as a recruitable NPC and adds his warband to loyalist forces
- **Orestes Sarkhazi identified** — The antagonist's identity surfaces through accumulated Spiritual and Intelligence missions in the mid-act; this changes the nature of the investigation and the personal stakes
- **The prince's vulnerability** — Subtle signs of Eurimedos's susceptibility begin to appear across Act II; attentive PCs will recognize them; inattentive ones will not, with consequences that determine Act III's available endings

#### Act II Conclusion

Act II ends when all 12 missions have been either completed or lost. The Jurga is called. Unable to convene safely in the aftermath of the Jinsoona, the Sadars have been gathering at Mazrok in ones and twos across the months of Act II. When the last mission is resolved, enough have arrived for the council to formally begin — and the question of who rules Artaxarta, and on what terms, must be answered.

The cumulative results of Act II — which missions were completed, which were lost, and in which categories — directly determine which outcomes and resources are available in Act III. This relationship is detailed in the Act III section and the Multiple Endings Framework.

### Act III: The Dark Gospel *(Chapters 8–10, Levels 9–12)*
*In which the Jurga is called, the prince's legitimacy is tested, the cult strikes again at the moment of greatest vulnerability, and the PCs are forced to choose between their comrades and their charge — a choice whose weight determines the shape of what survives.*

#### Chapter 8 — The Jurga

The council convenes at Mazrok. Twelve Sadars are present — the minimum required for a valid quorum — and the business of the council is singular: whether to formally recognize Prince Eurimedos Toxamones Alicimidia as King of Artaxarta.

**Sadar disposition** is directly shaped by Act II mission outcomes. The twelve Sadars are divided equally across the three categories:

- **4 Sadars** are primarily concerned with the welfare of the common people (Civic) — their support scales with Civic missions completed
- **4 Sadars** are primarily concerned with religious legitimacy and the honor of Ilura (Spiritual) — their support scales with Spiritual missions completed
- **4 Sadars** are primarily concerned with military capability and the prince's ability to protect the uplands (Military) — their support scales with Military missions completed

Sadars begin the Jurga with a disposition (supportive, neutral, or skeptical) determined by how many missions in their category were completed in Act II. The PCs have two days to lobby individuals directly before the vote is called.

**The vote** is taken at the end of the second day. The cult has been watching the Jurga and will strike regardless of its outcome — the story proceeds identically whether the prince is accepted or rejected. If the vote fails, a second Jurga is called after the cult's defeat at the campaign's conclusion; this council accepts the prince and formally resolves the question of succession as an epilogue to the campaign.

#### Chapter 9 — The Night They Came

On the night after the vote, the cult's forces attack the encampment. The scale and outcome of the assault is determined by the military support the PCs have assembled:

- **Low military completion (0–1 Military missions):** The defense is overwhelmed quickly. Several Sadars are killed. Most of the gathered loyalist forces are routed or scattered.
- **Moderate military completion (2–3 Military missions):** The defense holds long enough for most of the Sadars to escape, but at significant cost. Casualties are heavy.
- **Full military completion (4 Military missions):** The cult's assault is repelled. The Sadars survive intact, the loyalist force holds, and the cost — though real — is manageable.

**In all cases, the following occurs regardless of military outcome:**
- Prince Eurimedos is captured by the cult and taken to Alcidea
- **Marcus Geminius Sabinus**, **Fleur Dontillion**, and **Abala Kadar** are captured and taken to a nearby cultist stronghold, where they are scheduled for execution within the day
- **Kaske Heskalo** and **Marcus Ivor** escape in all outcomes and remain with the PCs

#### The Pivotal Decision

The morning after the attack, Delana delivers the intelligence: the prince is in Alcidea; the captured retainers are at the cultist stronghold and will be executed by nightfall.

The PCs must choose:

**Option A — Save the comrades first.** The retainers are rescued, but the prince spends additional time in Orestes Sarkhazi's custody, exposed to the No-God's philosophy and the cult's deliberate efforts to reach him. The prince survives, but the time spent without protection deepens his vulnerability. This path raises his corruption level going into the final confrontation and moves the campaign toward Ending 3.

**Option B — Rescue the prince first.** The PCs go directly to Alcidea. The retainers are executed. The prince's corruption is minimized by the speed of his recovery — but the cost is permanent. This path lowers corruption and keeps Endings 1 and 2 available, at the price of the characters who were closest to him.

**The climax** — whichever path is taken — culminates in a multi-stage assault on Alcidea, the seat of the cult's power and Orestes Sarkhazi's stronghold. The assault proceeds through several distinct stages leading to the final confrontation with Orestes and the resolution of the No-God's incursion. The specific stages, scene structure, and conditional outcomes of the assault are to be designed in the DM Guide.

## Possible Endings

### Ending 1: The Long Vigil
*The No-God is delayed, not destroyed. The incursion collapses. The world buys time.*

The PCs destroy the cult's infrastructure, defeat or redeem the noble antagonist, and sever the No-God's primary channel into the material world. The entity is too old and vast to be banished permanently — but this particular incursion ends. Artaxarta survives, damaged. Prince Eurimedos, unbroken, will eventually take the throne. The Brotherhood of Light and whatever remains of the Kryptea are charged with maintaining a permanent vigil. The PCs know the silence is not peace.

*Conditions: The antagonist is defeated. The cult's primary ritual sites are destroyed. Eurimedos remains uncorrupted through Acts II and III.*

### Ending 2: The Last Convert
*The noble antagonist breaks the pact. The act costs them everything — and opens the door.*

Confronted with the full weight of what they have become and what the No-God's plan means for the people they once served — the antagonist defects. The act of will against the No-God provides the opening the PCs need to collapse the incursion. The antagonist does not survive it. Artaxarta is saved. What the PCs do with the truth of who the antagonist was, and what they chose at the end, is their own decision.

*Conditions: The PCs built a relationship with the antagonist across the campaign sufficient to allow dialogue — confrontations that left room for something other than destruction. The antagonist's humanity had space to reassert itself.*

### Ending 3: The Seeds of Ruin *(Dark / Tragic)*
*The PCs succeed. The No-God loses this round. And the prince carries something home.*

The cult is broken. The antagonist is defeated. The No-God's incursion collapses. Prince Eurimedos, protected by the PCs across the entire campaign, eventually takes the throne. Decades later, the cult reappears — not from outside, not from a remnant cell, but from within the palace. The prince, in his grief and his hunger for understanding, found the No-God's tenets during the long nights in the Citadel. He never told anyone. The people who protected him never knew how close it had gotten.

*Conditions: The PCs failed to recognize or address the signs of Eurimedos's developing vulnerability — missed his isolation, dismissed the signs, or were too focused on the external threat to see what was happening inside the walls.*

## Key Mechanics to Design

1. **Corruption Mechanic** — A system for tracking exposure to the No-God's influence. Applies to both PCs and key NPCs, especially Eurimedos. Specific PC behaviors protect the prince (engagement, connection, honest conversation about the deaths they've seen) or expose him (isolation, neglect, allowing him access to cult materials). The tracker is the mechanical expression of Ending 3's availability.

2. **Faction Relationship Tracker** — Track PC standing with: the Brotherhood of Light, the Sadar council (tribal confederation), surviving legionary officers (consolidating around the prince's authority), and Arslan Zedan's warband. Standing determines mission availability, military resources, and which allies are present at the final confrontation.

3. **Act II Mission System** — The full mechanical framework for Act II: 12 missions across 4 waves, turn structure, Intelligence bonus accumulation (flat +1 per mission, cap +8), wave unlock thresholds (DC 14 / DC 17 / DC 20 at intel +0 / +3 / +6), mission expiration tracks (fast/standard/slow), and the Investigation roll early warning. Detailed in the Act II section. Mission outcomes feed directly into Act III availability.

4. **Kryptea Intelligence Network** — Delana Geminia Sabinus is the PCs' primary intelligence asset and the narrative engine behind the Act II Intelligence bonus. Rebuilding the network is the in-world explanation for how missions are revealed; Delana's capacity grows as the party completes missions and recovers resources. How thoroughly the Kryptea was penetrated before its destruction is one of Act II's central investigative questions.

5. **Milestone Leveling** — Advancement tied to act completion and key plot beats. Characters begin at Level 3.

6. **Multiple Endings Framework** — Act II mission outcomes (categories completed, missions lost) feed into a decision point tracker that covers Acts II and III. Specific choices and omissions determine which endings are available. The relationship between Act II results and Act III outcomes is to be detailed when Act III is finalized.

## Institutions and Factions

### Internal to Artaxarta

**The Crown (Prince Eurimedos / General Heskalo)**
The nominal authority the PCs serve. In practice: a grieving child, an aging general, and a collection of survivors operating from a mountain fortress. Their legitimacy is genuine; their practical power is limited to what the PCs can build for them across Act II.

**The Brotherhood of Light**
Powerful, respected, and badly damaged by the Act I siege. Their knowledge of the Umgul cult's history is the PCs' best early window into the No-God's true nature — but their official account is wrong in important ways they don't yet know. Marcus Ivor is the PCs' bridge to the Brotherhood. Rebuilding the relationship between the crown and the Order is one of Act II's key diplomatic threads.

**The Kryptea**
The kingdom's intelligence service. Functionally destroyed in the fall of Alcidea — which was not accidental; the cult identified and eliminated it as a priority target. Delana Geminia Sabinus is the surviving thread. How thoroughly the Kryptea was penetrated before its destruction is one of Act II's most important investigative questions.

**The Zavra-Prag / The Sadars**
The tribal militia and their leaders. Critical to any military effort to reclaim Alcidea. The Sadars are independent and will require real reasons to commit resources to the prince's cause — the Jurga provides the formal mechanism, but the PCs will need to build the trust that makes it work. Arslan Zedan is a bridge, though his reputation complicates the diplomacy.

**The Legions**
The professional infantry. Scattered after the fall of Alcidea, their loyalty flows to whoever can credibly claim legitimate authority. Consolidating the surviving legionaries around the prince is a background task of Act II with significant payoff in Act III.

### Adversarial

**The No-God's Cult**
The primary adversary. Not a traditional religious movement — an infiltration operation that has spent years embedding itself within Artaxarta's power structure. Its outer layer resembles the suppressed Umgul cult (forbidden practices, dark iconography); its inner circle knows what they are actually serving and what the pact requires of them. Most of the outer layer do not.

## Calendar and Holidays

**The Jurga** — The annual month-long council of Sadars with the King, normally convened in the weeks following the Jinsoona. The Jinsoona disaster made the scheduled Jurga impossible; the Sadars scattered, and many are dead or unreachable. Across Act II, they trickle toward Mazrok as the security situation allows. The Jurga is formally called when Act II concludes and enough Sadars have gathered to make it viable. It opens Act III — the question of legitimacy, military command, and the kingdom's future is settled here, with outcomes shaped entirely by the relationships and standing the PCs built during Act II.

**The Jinsoona** — Ilura's High Festival; held at midsummer throughout the entire kingdom. In the five cities it has taken on a Delian character — civic processions, statues of the goddess paraded through the streets, communal meals at sundown — while in the uplands it remains a more ancient and ritual practice centered on fire and the old rites. The night ends with great bonfires burning across every settlement in the kingdom.

It is on the night of the Jinsoona that the cult strikes simultaneously throughout Artaxarta. The communal gatherings, the open streets, the fires already lit — all of it provides cover. Every city, every fortress, every seat of power is hit at once. Alcidea falls. The king is killed. The Brotherhood of Light's fortress, already under siege since before the festival, is overwhelmed. By morning, the kingdom the PCs knew no longer exists.

*The Jinsoona is the hinge of Act I. Chapters 1–2 take place in the days leading up to it; Chapter 3 opens in its aftermath.*
