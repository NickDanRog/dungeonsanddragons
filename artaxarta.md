# The Kingdom of Artaxarta

## Overview
	Bestriding the bulwark of the Yashim Mountains which form the former Empire’s border with the lands of the Kelish to the south and east, the massive Kingdom of Artaxarta ranks among the greatest of states of the former Empire. Its unique culture reaches back far into the mists of antiquity, making it one of the oldest and grandest states of the region. From its snowy mountain peaks to its fertile river valleys, the beauty of the lands of Artaxarta awe all who pass through it, and it has been the woe of many a conqueror throughout the ages. Artaxarta is bordered on the south by Kentana, a Kingdom higher up in the mountains. Asta and Morna border Artaxarta to the west. To the north and west it is bordered by Kyrexos, and Kesh borders it to the north and northeast. In the southeast, it shares a long border with the Kelishite  state, though the mountains in this region make this border virtually inaccessible. As the mountains taper off toward the east, they encounter a large barren region that abuts the eastern ocean. These lands are virtually uninhabited, and remain unclaimed by any state.
	Though very mountainous overall, Artaxarta is home to twelve major river systems which flow out of the higher peaks to the east forming an intricate network of hundreds of valleys. These valleys are famous for their fertility, and in the modern age they are very well developed encompassing a surprisingly large acreage of arable land. This development, along with a specialized method of terraced farming invented during the Imperial era makes Artaxarta a major agricultural exporter. The higher ends of most valleys are given over mostly to herding, the traditional occupation of the native people of the region, and many of the high pastures are home to all manner of grazing animals, especially the large warhorses for which the country is famous. 
	Towns and cities throughout the Kingdom tend to be rather mixed in character. The major valleys teeming with hundreds of small towns and villages which have grown up to support the agricultural economy of these regions. Settlements tend to be more spread out in the higher valleys and more mountainous areas. Artaxarta is home to five major cities, including the capital of Alcidea, which are situated mostly in the northern part of the country. These cities were originally begun as Xoshan military colonies, and their initial rigid planning still has an impact on their modern character. The countryside also features many large military citadels built into the mountainsides. These strongholds were used in far ancient times as places of refuge, and were greatly improved upon and expanded during the periods of Xoshan and Imperial rule here. 

## History
	Much of the early history of this ancient country is lost to us today. What evidence that does survive takes the form of pictographic tablet writings left by the early native peoples, and accounts written by Xoshans during their period of conquest and colonization here. Most scholars agree that the peoples found here during their first contacts with Heartlanders were native to the region. These peoples were divided into different tribal or clan based groups, each centering on a certain valley or part of a valley. The major tribes seem to have been led by their own Kings, and the smaller clans in the high mountain regions were usually led by a chieftain or other strongman. Little is known of the actual political makeup of these mini-kingdoms, though the leaders of each seem to have been little more than petty warlords of a sort, and the few historical records remaining to us seem to point to a frequent rotation of dynasty and leadership even in the most organized of these states. 
	The lifestyle of these ancient peoples is thought to be similar to that found during the Xoshan campaigns here. These tribes seemed to be mostly herders and hunter-gatherers, roving with their herds throughout the regions controlled by their King or warlord. Very few towns and very little agriculture were evident in Artaxarta during ancient times. What few settled communities there were seem to have been centered in the most fertile of the river valleys and placed near easily cultivated or naturally occurring crops. These tribes seem to have warred with each other constantly, making conflict an everyday fact of life for these ancient peoples. A general description of these people can be found in the writings of the Xoshan traveler and geographer Strabo. While some of his comments border on racist, they do reveal much about the nature of the Artaxartoi prior to their contact with most Heartlanders. He traveled through this country about two hundred years prior to the initial invasions of his people, and recorded the following:  
*Each of the valleys that make up this land has its own King, who rules only through his own strength and without assistance of any law save the traditions of his kin and folk. Each of these tribes hates the other with great intensity and they war constantly with each other going to war for any reason whatsoever and constantly finding any excuse to do so or contriving one for themselves when one is not readily apparent. It seems to be the aim of these wars to take as many captives as possible, whom they often humiliate and brutalize, as well as much livestock and property as can be carried away.*
*I find them [The Artaxartoi] in general to be a devious and treacherous people. They cannot be trusted except to break an oath, and when paid for work they always fail to perform it. They have very few cities, and most of them [non city-dwellers] seem to look down on those who choose to live in these towns which they constantly attack. They do not grow crops and seem to shun work of any kind. Rather, they move in large groups about the countryside with their livestock whom they cause their slaves to look after for them, or even their wives when slaves are not plentiful – which they are often not due to their miserable treatment of them. When they cannot support themselves by their herds they instead steal from their neighbors.* 
*These men seem to account horses as wealth which each man owning several, and in most cases treat them better than their wives. They are excellent bowmen and lancers, especially while horsed, and while I do not find them especially brave they are certainly competent soldiers. In their fighting style, they shoot and then ride quickly away if a foe continues to look threatening, only to ride back and shoot again, and then suddenly wheel and charge straight ahead. Their lands are also quite striking and the valleys here are very fertile and suitable for easy cultivation, pity that these barbarians cannot make good use of them.* 
*From Strabo’s Geographia* 
	The religious practices of these people are also important to note as they were not only very prevalent at the time, but would also have an important impact on this region later in its history. In fact, the vast majority of the many intricate pictographic writings that have been found are religious in nature. Many of these native traditions survive almost unchanged even to this day. The ancient Artaxartoi worshiped two deities. The first and primary of these was a mother-goddess who protected people from harm and ensured the fertility of the people and their herds. When the Delians came into this region, they associated this goddess with their deity Ilura, though to the Artaxartoi she had a far more warlike aspect and was always depicted mounted on a horse and carrying a bow. The other deity, often associated with Altair, seems to have embodied the forces of darkness and is often depicted as having to do with beasts or birds and the movement of stars. He also seems to have been associated with sorcerers and magicians, whom these incredibly superstitious people feared and hated, and followers of his cult seem to have been rooted out and killed in ancient times. 
	This pattern of life exhibited by the ancient Artaxartoi seems to have continued for many thousands of years with little or no interruption. With increasing contacts with the heartlands peoples however, it slowly began to change. The increased commercial contacts began to enhance the power of the northern tribes living closer to the civilized heartlands, the warfare between them evolved from a system of continual raiding into a process of internal conquest. As a break to the power of these northern tribes, the southern peoples of the region began to form their own confederacies to combat them, thus increasing the scale and destructiveness of these tribal wars. While during the time of Strabo’s writings there were perhaps as many as three hundred tribes or clan groups in Artaxarta, by the end of this particular era there were only about twenty remaining. Though larger, these groups had changed little in character, and still continued to subsist primarily off raiding and livestock, though some of the towns in the north of the country increased significantly in size as they began to be used as markets for trading with foreigners. 
	This long era of relative isolation from the affairs of the great region came to an end with the expansion of the Xoshan Empire. After this state conquered many of the surrounding cities and kingdoms in the lakes region, it turned its sights toward the fertile valleys of Artaxarta. In a campaign lasting several years, the Xoshans conquered the northern third of this country, though pacifying it would take much longer. This began a long era of military occupation which saw significant development by the Xoshans as they attempted to exploit its wealth. Near-constant partisan action and constant raids and small invasions from the remaining tribes to the south, along with the unfamiliar and sometimes treacherous terrain seriously hampered their efforts however, and the entire region remained a serious bone of contention for the Xoshans for many decades. Several concerted efforts to conquer the rest of the region were subsequently attempted all of which failed, costing many a Xoshan General his head. 
	The final conquest of these lands was completed under the famous and storied General Alcias. Harnessing a wave of popular support earned by his many victories he spearheaded a renewed effort toward the conquest of Artaxarta, and managed to amass a large army to that effect much to the dismay of his many detractors. He was opposed by a newly arisen Artaxartoi leader named Bazarnes who styled himself the ‘King of Artaxarta’. Trained by Xoshans himself, Bazarnes was a chivalrous and noteworthy commander with a firm grasp of cavalry tactics. The war fought between the two famous leaders lasted five years and included many major battles fought all over the country. After finally capturing him, Alcias decided to pardon Bazarnes, and even adopted him into his family by marrying his younger sister. Together, they set the foundations for what would become the modern Kingdom of Artaxarta. 
	Falling into disgrace after a long series of scandals, Alcias was removed from his post as military governor of the region and stripped of his command of his army, which he was then leading against the city of Gela to the north. Upon hearing this news, the garrison he left in Artaxarta, which was still loyal to him, rebelled and declared his young son Ariston their King. The people of this new Kingdom rallied behind their new leader, and under the command of Bazarnes they drove out those still loyal to Xosha and crushed those that rose up in rebellion against the new regime. Within a few years the situation began to stabilize within the Kingdom, and with its many enemies closing in around it, the faltering Xoshan Empire no longer posed a threat, leaving this newly born state safe to enter into a long period of peaceful development. Alicas, after many years and many other adventures was finally able to return to this country which he helped to found, but sadly he passed away shortly after his return.  
	Artaxarta rose to become one of the pre-eminent powers in the region at the close of the City-States era. It resisted invasion from a coalition consisting of Tal-Anaxa and Asta, fought a successful war with Gela, and even began to extend its influence throughout the region, exacting a tribute from many of the smaller states wary of its fearsome armies. This period of regional domination came to a close however as the expanding Bandorian Empire began to slowly gobble up its neighboring states. After conquering the other states nearby, the Durasi paused briefly while they gathered their forces for a strike into the Kingdom. Their campaigns here took over twenty years and involved the entirety of the Imperial Guards, along with a large contingent of call-up forces from the Empire’s member-states. So used to stand up battles on the plains and fields of their homelands, the Durasi armies had serious difficulty negotiating the terrain of this mountainous land, and the stubborn defiance and partisan resistance of its people caused them to take extremely harsh measures with the conquered populace. Their conquest of Artaxarta became so brutal that scholars believe up to seventy-five percent of the military age male population living here was killed or seriously wounded during the war. Though suffering from terrible casualties, the Durasi juggernaut could not be slowed, and eventually they were able to capture the citadel of Mazrok outside Alcidea after an incredible ten year siege and with it the Artaxartoi King Telamon II. 
	The Durasi Emperor at the Time, Urian III Lachesis knew well the love these people had for their monarch, and as a gesture of reconciliation, he allowed him to keep his throne as a vassal. He made him answerable however to a Durasi Duke, and seriously restricted his powers making him into a virtual figurehead. Artaxarta was known as a rebellious province all throughout the period of Imperial rule, and suffered the presence of a massive Imperial garrison until the end of the Empire. Though oppressed, the Kingdom prospered greatly during this era, seeing an incredible increase in cultivation and development which positively impacted its future. 
	When the Empire began to descend into its final civil war 50 years ago, the Alcidean King Bialos II saw an opportunity and plotted to seize control of the situation. He secretly gathered his army to confront the Imperial Garrison. Though he could have easily overwhelmed them with his forces, he shrewdly negotiated with them instead, asking them to swear allegiance to him in exchange for land and for his protection. Seeing no escape and little left to escape to, the Legion commander agreed. The descendants of his men continue to serve the Kingdom to this day. Bialos also co-opted the Durasi Duke, whom he married into his family instead of executing or driving off. With a newly strengthened army at and the loyalty of the people firmly behind their restored King, the people of Artaxarta seemed in a unique position to resist the oncoming tide of chaos that threatened to swallow up the region and the world around it.

## Government
	Artaxarta has had a long history of not only tribal loyalty, but loyalty to its King. Since the rise of the Alician Kings after the Xoshan invasions, they have been long loved of their people and praised as children of both worlds. The military success and prosperity these Kings have brought to their Kingdom has firmly cemented this loyalty, and unlike many of the other true monarchies present in the federation, these rulers have little to fear from insurrectionists or rebels. The King of Artaxarta rules as an absolute monarch, though his powers are mitigated somewhat by a system of law derived not only from ancient Xoshan ideals, but native traditions Artaxartoi al well. Strangely, though this system is part Xoshan, it contains no traces of that city’s famous democratic traditions. Most scholars believe that the bloody mob rule atmosphere prevalent in the breakup of the Xoshan Empire, concurrent with the development of the Artaxartoi state, convinced these people that this system of government was either dangerous, or at best unstable. 
	The King empowers a body of fifteen ministers to manage his government. By and large, these officers are selected from among the nobility, though the King will sometimes choose promising commoners to serve in this capacity. The ministers serve at the King’s pleasure, and he may appoint and remove them at will with no recourse. Each ministry is run by a bureaucratic organization of its own, though in the tradition of these people these groups tend to be much more lean, if not more efficient than those in other states. By in large, much of the royal government functions this way, favoring function over form and being much smaller in scope than many of its contemporaries. One of these ministries in fact, is the called the Office of the Special Inspector, who is charged with rooting out corruption among the other branches of the government. Though closely monitored for malfeasance, each ministry otherwise operates independently with little interference from the monarchy. 
	While the King’s law and rule is easily maintained in the inter-connected and highly developed valley regions of the central and northern parts of the Kingdom, it becomes somewhat problematic in the uplands regions. In these areas, the King relies on the ancient tribal leaders, called Sadars, to enforce his authority. The Sadars do not function like traditional tribal or feudal lords; rather they act as a sort of cultural nobility who take the dictates of the King to the people and vice versa. For the most part, these tribes are somewhat scattered throughout the country, though they do cluster into enclaves in certain regions. These leaders rove throughout these regions to confer with their subjects, dispensing justice and adjudicating conflicts. With the Artaxartoi tradition of loyalty and obedience to their tribes and to the King, this allows these leaders to exercise a great amount of moral authority among the populace, and to act as representatives of royal authority. Once a year, these leaders meet with the King for a one month council session called the Jurga, where they allow him to mediate disputes between the tribes, and to help him gauge the mood of the people and learn of any problems prevalent in different regions of the country. 
	Matters of law and order are a bit more mismatched in Artaxarta than is the norm in most of the federation. Each city elects its own magistrates, who serve for a year, and court cases are heard by them with and judged by a jury selected by lot from among a pool of volunteers. All civil cases may be appealed to tribal leaders, though capital cases can only be appealed to the yearly Jurga, which acts as a high court of sorts. In the more rural upland regions of the country, people tend to favor appealing firstly to their clan or tribal leaders for justice, though any party may choose to have a case taken to the closest local court instead. One interesting feature of note about the civil court system is the use of paid jurors. These citizens are paid the prevailing daily wage from the public purse, making jury service a frequent occupation for the elderly or otherwise unemployed. Though it has a complicated legal system, Artaxarta is not an overly litigious society. People here tend to favor mediation and common sense compromise over reliance on the court system. Each city or town maintains its own police force. Law in rural areas is enforced by the retainers of the local clan chiefs. 

## Culture 
	The Artaxartoi exist today as one of the few fusion cultures in the east. While many peoples who were formed from a synthesis of different groups tend to take on the aspects of whichever culture become the most dominant, the Artaxartoi have managed to retain many aspects of both cultures. The vast majority of the people in this country now live in cities or towns, or in the farming villages in valleys surrounding them. While their ancient tribal lifestyle has changed now to an urban one, many of the traditions of their ancestors remain unchanged to this day. This is not true in highland regions of the country however. People living in these areas maintain a much more pastoral existence, and are considered by many, the majority of their countrymen included, to be culturally backward. 
	As a people, the Artaxartoi tend to be educated, brave, self-reliant, and hardworking, all while exhibiting a love of life and family. While they work very hard, they also play very hard as well and are famous for their games and love of sport. A peculiar sport of particular note native to this country called birok is practiced almost universally. Taking place in an ovular racing forum called a stadia, which can commonly be seen almost anywhere in the heartlands, two teams of players move the corpse of a freshly killed goat around with spears in an attempt to bring it to the opposing team’s goal. The rules of this game are rather loose, and injuries or the occasional death are not uncommon. Considered barbaric by most, this game is deeply loved by the Artaxartoi, and they take great personal offense to any insult to it. They also differ greatly from many of their neighbors in regards to gender roles. Taking a cue from their Xoshan ancestors, the Artaxartoi consider men and women to be almost completely equal. Women are allowed to perform any duty than men are, with the exception of being King, and are allowed to join the army or become active in politics as long as they are able to prove their ability. 
	Merit is the hallmark of social position for these people. They take the greatest stock in how a person is able to prove him or herself when performing whichever position they have chosen for themselves in life. Those who perform well are rewarded with the greatest respect, while those who fail at their tasks are scorned by everyone. Those who manage to improve themselves still have a chance for redemption and reward, but for the Artaxartoi, wounds to one’s pride heal much more slowly than wounds to one’s body. While there does exist an aristocracy of sorts here, being the families of the monarch and the tribal leaders, these people are expected to work twice as hard to prove themselves worthy of their position. Instead of living a life of privilege as most noblemen, they instead live lives of hard work and sacrifice, and train from an early age to be able to properly perform their duties. 
	Tribal life also maintains a very important place in Artaxartoi society. There are twenty three recognized tribes remaining in Artaxarta today – every subject of the King is accounted as a member of one of these tribes, either through ancient pedigree or through inter-marriage since their family’s arrival from the heartlands. Almost identical culturally, each tribe does maintain its own customs to some extent and is intensely proud of its own particular heritage. Though these groups are very mixed throughout the country, the Artaxartoi have a deep connection to their tribes and families and look on their tribal leaders and elders with the greatest of respect. 
	The Artaxartoi are also an intensely religious people. In ancient times, the dualistic religion practiced here was concerned with the worship of two main deities. The first of these was called Lhakmana, who embodied the forces of good. She was depicted as a warrior woman riding a horse and carrying a bow. She was worshiped as a combination fertility, sun, and war goddess, who led righteous people to victory and ensured the successful production of strong children and herds. The other deity, called Umgul, was feared and hated by these people. He was considered an evil wizard who corrupted people, stole their kata (strength, or life-essence), and inspired them to perform evil deeds. He was often depicted as be in opposition to Lhakmana, and a large body of folklore concerning the exploits of he and his followers still exists today, often being used to frighten small children. The conquering Xoshans identified these deities with Ilura and Altair respectively, and while this native form of religion was greatly changed by the heartlander worship of the Twelve Divines many ancient traditions still exist in Artaxarta today. While people now give homage to many of the Divines, especially those like Mora and Uriel traditionally favored by the Xoshans, Ilura is still the main deity here and is worshiped almost universally. Her cult has quite a different character in Artaxarta however, including the use of many iconic depictions and animal sacrifices not seen in the heartlands. There is a very famous order of holy knights devoted to her in who live in a priory just outside of the capital, and the warriors who make up this order are some of the most respected and admired people in this society. The cult of Altair in not tolerated in Artaxarta. Worshipers of this deity are hunted down and put to death when found, though foreign worshipers are usually expelled. 
	Though almost completely homogenous culturally, Artaxarta does contain to sub-cultural groups in its society. The first and largest of these is made up of the herders and tribal clansmen who inhabit the up-country and highland regions of the country. These people are rather backward culturally, and tend to exhibit many of the mores and traditions common to the ancient peoples who lived here before the arrival of the heartlanders. While they have given up the raiding ways of the ancestors, these people tend to be clannish, uneducated, and almost ridiculously superstitious. They have little contact with outsiders or even their countrymen, though their ancient traditions of guest rights and the need for trade make them at least somewhat hospitable. The other of these groups is made up of descendants or the Durasi soldiers and officials trapped here during the breakup of the Empire. Realizing the value of these people, the king at the time decided to incorporate them into his army and government instead of killing or expelling them, and many of their ancestors still hold these posts today. Though little now differentiates them from their fellow Artaxartoi culturally, they still do maintain some of the traditions of their forbearers, including the use of Durasi-style names. These people are represented by a tribe of their own. 

## Military

### The Citadel of Mazrok - The Stone of Kings
	The ancient and storied Citadel of Mazrok is a massive fortress overlooking the city of Alcidea, the capital of Artaxarta. This citadel is famous for having only fallen once to an attacker, even then after an incredible ten-year siege, and has long been a refuge to the Kings of this country in times of trouble. The citadel is built directly into a cliff-side in the mountains overlooking immediately west of the city, and its battlements loom ominously at a height of 600 feet over Alcidea. The walls of the city are built directly up to the walls of the cliff, making the mountain itself an integral part of its defenses. It is only accessible via a massive and incredibly steep staircase cut into the rock, and its entrance is secured by a single massive stone gate over thirty feet thick. This gate can be controlled only from the inside, making it virtually impenetrable to any attacker. Its defenses are further enhanced by several choke points built into the stairway, which are surmounted by platforms for defending soldiers to stand upon to shoot missiles or throw rocks at and attacker advancing upward. This castle is also incredibly well supplied, containing its own aquifer and cisterns, and is usually stocked with enough dried foodstuffs for a defender to holdout here almost indefinitely. 
	This fortification was built in far ancient times by the leaders of the clan native to this valley, who used it as a sanctuary against their enemies. Its construction was said to have taken over fifty years, and is thought to have taken the lives of thousands of slaves in its building. Whenever this valley was invaded during this era, the clansmen would retreat with their families into the citadel and wait for them to leave. While this often allowed raiders to carry off much of their livestock and ravage the countryside, these people often mounted raids of their own to recover their stolen property, or steal that of others, as soon as their enemies became restless and moved on. These tactics gave these people a very unsavory and cowardly reputation, though they also allowed them to become one of the strongest powers in the central region of what would one day become this country. 
	During the Durasi invasion of Artaxarta, the Imperials moved first to conquer the northern parts of the country before moving toward the capital. The hit and run tactics and ambushes used by King Telamon slowed their progress significantly, allowing him ample time to stock his citadel. After being defeated by the Durasi in a massive battle near Phocis, about fifty miles north of Alcidea, he began a strategic withdrawal toward the capital. Instead of trying to defend the city, he evacuated much of the population and moved a small portion of his army to the citadel, sending the rest to the south to rally around his brother who was acting his general. After failing to capture Mazrok outright and not knowing the full extent of his stores the Imperial general decided for a policy of cantonment, expecting Telamon to emerge eventually or face starvation. The King had other plans however and continued to send orders to his army via an ingenious signals system using beacons mounted on top of the castle, and controlled his army even while being held down by the Durasi. 
	It took several more years for the Imperials to conquer the remainder of the country, and even when they turned the full might of their army against the citadel they were unable to crack its defenses. Fed up with this stubborn defense, the Durasi Emperor Urian III offered the first man to enter the castle an incredible five hundred gold crowns as a reward. An enterprising group of mountaineers from the Luteia region took him up on his offer and during a spectacular night time raid a few of them managed to silently scale the cliff free hand. After attaching grapnels and guide ropes to the cliff face, they led a sizeable raiding force over the top of the battlements and into the castle where they were able to force open the doors, thus letting the remainder of the army inside and forcing its defenders to finally surrender. Sadly, the man who led the raid was killed in the fighting, but the Emperor, true to his word, made certain the money he promised was passed on to his descendants. 
### Legonis Hykyrianos
	The Legonis Hykyrianos, or Eastern Legions, are the main infantry force in the army of Artaxarta. This force is descended from the Imperial Legions left behind here during the dissolution of the Bandorian Empire over three hundred years ago. While much of the style in both fighting and equipment used by these fighters is similar to that used by their predecessors, it has been changed in part by its long association with this country. 
	The warriors of the Legions are equipped in a manner very similar to their Imperial forbearers. They carry a long Druasi-style straight blade as their main weapon, which they supplement with a few light throwing javelins. Like other Artaxartan warriors, each fighter also carries a small tribal dagger as a sidearm, though as many of the soldiers who make up this unit come from the tribe descended from Imperial stock almost all of them choose to carry the short, broad pugio dagger. They are armored in a suit of scale mail interwoven with multi-colored steel scales, giving it a great level of flexibility as well as a lustrous appearance. They wear a traditional Artaxartan-style ‘thimble’ helmet which consists of a conical yet flat-topped leather helmet to which steel scales are riveted. These helmets have the advantage of being relatively lightweight, while still providing a good level of protection. They carry a large round shield, similar in character to the aspis shields carried by most Delian hoplites, though they are a bit smaller in size. These shields are painted with the insignia and colors of each particular Legion. 
	These legions are organized in the traditional ancient style. 200 men are grouped into a cohort, which is commanded by a centurion who is assisted by eight section leaders. Artaxartan centurions are easily recognized by the distinctive headgear which is made out of the entire pelt of a mountain lion, the head of which sits atop the head of the officer wearing it, giving them a very fierce appearance. Instead of a shield, each centurion carries a staff which is topped by their Legion’s insignia, and to which is attached a token for each of the honors his cohort and legion has won. Ten cohorts comprise a Legion, which is commanded by an officer called a Legate. A Legate is assisted by three banner bearers, being the most senior centurions in the legion. One of these soldiers carries the legion’s standard, while the other two carry large pennants bearing the coat of arms of the royal family and the Kingdom respectively. Artaxarta currently fields seven full Legions as of this writing. Similar to the old Imperial Legions, these Artaxartan legions are famous for their focus on heraldry. Each Legion has its own traditional number and official name, as well as colors and coat of arms. Each Legion also supports a large group of auxiliaries, including its own corps of drummers, pipers, and flautists. Individual soldiers of these Legions are encouraged to keep up their kit and personal grooming to the highest standards possible. These groups also spend a large amount of their time on drill and ceremony as well, making them a professional and much disciplined unit. These Artaxartoi currently field a total of four full Legions. 
	The tactics used by the Artaxartan Legions also differ from many other units of the same type. While most legionary-style units fight from a very tight formation, the Artanxartans tend to fight in a more loose formation due to the larger nature of their shields and the longer blades they use. Though this looser formation is standard, they are just as comfortable fighting in closer quarters. They are also practiced in a staggered formation, in which individual ten man sections of soldiers stand in line spaced about a man apart, with each line spaced as to fill the gap left in the line in front. This provides an excellent amount of flexibility, allowing a soldier in the line behind to move forward to support the line in front, or allowing easy rotation of each line to keep fresh troops always at the fore. In general, these are some of the most well-trained and disciplined warriors in the eastern lands. 
### Zavra Prag
	The Zavra-Prag, or tribal auxiliaries, is the traditional native army of the Artaxartoi, and still make up the vast bulk of the country’s armed forces today.  This group consists of a wide range of different types of soldiers, varying in wildly in composition and organization based on the clan or other group that supports it. Unlike the auxiliary forces of many nations, the vast majority of the warriors that make up the Zavra-Prag are well trained and hardy soldiers, celebrated for their prowess in combat consonant with their country’s tradition of military service. 
	The basic unit found in the Zavra-Prag is called a kulak, or company, each of which is drawn from a particular clan. Each tribal leader maintains a muster roll called a kulak-mardu, or roll of companies, which lists what each clan is supposed to maintain in terms of forces. Each clan leader maintains their own kulak-mardu as well. These lists are usually quite extensive, especially the ones at the clan level which list the number of men expected to come up for service when called, what equipment they are required to bring with them, and other such information. Foreigners who are long-term residents of Artaxarta, or people who are otherwise not members of a particular clan are assembled into kulaks of their own as well. These are often referred to as ‘civic’ kulaks. As previously stated, the size and composition of each kulak varies wildly based on the size of each clan and what equipment its members can afford. Small clans of highland shepherds usually produce small kulaks of around 30 men armed with spears, slings, wicker shields, and perhaps a bow or two. Clans based in larger cities will kulaks in the hundreds, some even field warriors mounted on armored horses and carrying equipment as heavy as that of the royal guards. As the Artaxartoi have a great love of horses and are some of the best horsemen in the federation this force has a preponderance of cavalry, which is considered a rarity in most national militias. 
	Service in the Zavra-Prag is not done for pay, but is rather considered each individual’s civic duty. In fact, so committed are the vast majority of the Artaxartoi to their nation that when the King or tribal leaders call up the levy far more soldiers than are listed on the kulak-mardu will generally show up for muster. Consequently, the Zavra-Prag can only be called to serve in times or war or national crisis. Though only a part-time militia on paper, many clan leaders sponsor monthly or even weekly drills, keeping their units in fighting trim. These leaders often serve as commanders of their clan’s kulaks as well, though they may appoint a stand-in if necessary. In larger deployments a tribal leader will act as the commander of the various kulaks of their tribe. 
### Kataphractoi
	The Kataphractoi Basileakos, or King’s Heavy Horse, are the royal guards of Artaxarta. They are the true elite of the Artaxartoi army and some of the most powerful and feared horsemen in the known world. The men and women who serve in this force train practically from birth to enter its rolls, and are skilled almost beyond compare. A charge of these soldiers can break almost any line, and few have historically been able to withstand them. 
	Undoubtedly, what gives the Kataphractoi their incredibly powerful charge is the large mounts they ride, along with their heavy armor. The armor itself is of particular note – each suit consists of a supple backing of specially designed and oiled leather, which is in turn backed by a special cloth and silk weave designed to keep the wearer cool even in the blistering hot Artaxartoi summer. To the leather backing is attached small light steel scales, sewn together using steel rings and placed very closely together. Each scale is faced with precious metals like silver, gold, or bronze, or is enameled green, crimson, or purple. Every suit is unique, and great care is taken of them, often being passed down through generations. The mounts these soldiers ride is covered with a similar suit of mail as well. While expensive, heavy, and tough to maintain, these suits make for incredibly effective armor. For their main weapons, the Kataphractoi carry a long, heavy horse lance, and a long, curved cavalry saber based on the Xoshan kopis. They are also equipped with an Artaxartoi style re-curve bow, which they are trained to use to great effect from horseback.  They are also equipped with an Artaxartoi thimble-style helmet. 
	With their many different types of weaponry, the Kataphractoi have one of the broadest tactical doctrines that can be found anywhere. They are effective against almost any other type of force, including even pikemen who have a difficult time resisting their armored charge or are easily shot with arrows from a distance. They have also mastered the skirmish circle wherein they ride in a mobile circle outside of the enemy’s range with those closer firing their bows while those further back reload, a difficult tactic at best, and even more amazing when one realizes the weight of armor and soldier their mounts carry. While they make use extensive use of a variety of tactics, where they truly excel is in the charge. Typically forming themselves into three broad ranks, the Kataphractoi slam headlong into the enemy with their lances leading the way. The shock of their impact is usually enough to break almost any line, and in cases where it does not the Kataphractoi will often attempt to reform and wheel about to charge again at the enemy’s rear. All of these incredible feats are made possible primarily by the horses that these soldiers ride. Selectively bred from ancient lines of stock that emphasize both speed and endurance these are some of the largest and most powerful horses that can be found in the world, and most train extensively with their riders almost from birth. They almost make use of stirrups, a recent innovation in military technology which helps keep a rider on their horse and provides for better control, and a special arm guard and a backing on the saddle which help them absorb the shock of the lance impact.  
	The soldiers of the Kataphractoi undergo a series of trials starting at the age of ten. The competition for these slots is fierce, as appointments to the guard are for life and come with a small land grant and a pension upon retirement. The recruits come primarily from the noble classes, though anyone is allowed to try out for a spot. People of other classes rarely have an opportunity to compete however, as the extensive training required to compete is very expensive. Passing the initial trials allows a candidate to enter the Royal Riding School, which teaches the advanced horsemanship and other military skills required to join the guard, and other subjects as diverse mathematics and poetry. Candidates join the school full time, only going back to their families for the two hottest months of the summer. They are tested in horseman ship, archery, and other skills at the end of every year, with the examinations growing more and more intense as time goes on, slowly reducing the number of candidates. At the age of 17 the candidates face their final test, which takes place over a three day tournament. Only those who place in the top 25 spots in the tourney are allowed a placement in the guard, though in some years more are allowed in to make up for battlefield losses or other attrition. Those placing in the top 50 percent join an auxiliary corps, which is sometimes called up during times of war, though members of this group must also meet a yearly training requirement. Members of the auxiliary may also compete in the tournament for a slot in the active guard for the first five years after their graduation.  
	The Kataphractoi are organized into ala (wings) of 45 soldiers, led by a captain chosen by the King himself. There are a total of twenty wings in the guard as a whole, for a total of 900 soldiers. At least 25 of these soldiers are required to retire every year to make room for new recruits. The entire organization is commanded by an officer called the Master of King’s Horse, or Guard General, who is also appointed by the King and is usually a member of the royal family. Unlike many units of royal guards, the Kataphractoi do not act as full-time bodyguards of the Artaxartoi King, who usually goes about unescorted. Guardsmen are expected to employ the services of a groom to manage their horse and kit, and to serve them as orderlies in the field. Each horsemen will usually have at least two backup mounts with the supply train as well, and their grooms are trained to bring them quickly in to replace a tired or wounded mount even on the battlefield. 
### Brotherhood of Light
	An order of fighting monks dedicated to the goddess Ilura, the Brotherhood of Light is one of the most respected fighting organizations in the federation, and is celebrated throughout the world for its ability and the chivalry of its members. While this group exists outside the regular chain of command and is not under orders from the King of Artaxarta directly, it has a long history of aiding this kingdom during times of distress, especially against the forces of darkness, thus necessitating its inclusion here. Many of the members of this order are Artaxartan themselves as well, and their headquarters is located in the mountains of southern Artaxarta, though they do maintain several important chapter houses throughout the federation along with one in the western kingdom of Bandore. The insignia of the Order is the golden cross of Ilura, an Antaxian letter “P” with a cross bar near the bottom, and its colors are white and gold. 
	Members of the Order are drawn from the temple origination, and are only accepted from among male orphans donated to the temple. A young man at the age of 16 may request to undergo the trials to join the order. Little is known about these rituals, aside from their high attrition rate. A few particular trails are known to the general public, including climbing Mt. Sikosa without tools or equipment (one of the largest mountains in the world, located in southern Artaxarta), surviving for forty days and nights in the wilderness, and a barefoot pilgrimage from the Order’s headquarters to the Temple of the Twelve Divines in Kal-Duras. Few of those who undergo these and other trails survive them, and those that do are forever changed. Even after the trials, an aspirant must undergo five more years of intensive military and monastic training, which even fewer are able to complete, though at this stage the survival rate is considerably higher. After all these trails a candidate must undergo another series of secret and deadly trails before becoming an acolyte of the Order. Acolytes are expected to learn a useful skill such as horse training, armor making, or something similar that they may support the order until their activation.  
	Full inclusion in the Order is strictly limited, only allowing for a total of 250 active members on its rolls at any given time. Those that pass to the stage of acolyte must wait for one of these slots to open up in order to become a full member. In addition to the 250 full members, or adepts of the Order, there are 5 masters, or Knight Commanders, who rule over the Order and are ranked by seniority. The most senior of the masters acts as the head of the Order, and answers directly to the High Priest of the Temple. These masters have the final say in deciding who passes into the Order and on promotions in rank, and all must consent for an aspirant to pass.  Whatever their station, all members live simple lives of extreme asceticism and monastic simplicity, and are bound by a strict code of conduct emphasizing chivalry and piety above all else which includes vows of poverty and chastity. Members who break this code are expelled, though for minor infractions they may choose instead to undergo a ritualized quest of penance. 
	An adept of the Order wears as his uniform a suit of plate and mail armor with the plates enameled white and trimmed with real gold. Over this he belts a pure white tabard emblazoned with the insignia of the Order. For a helmet he wears a full visored number done in the old Varnoran style also trimmed with gold. His mount is armored as well, being covered in a suit of light plate of the same colors. All of these Knights are trained to use a heavy horse lance, and will carry a backup weapon of their own choice, with most preferring a Varnoran-style longsword or a heavy horseman’s mace. These Knights do not make use of the bow or any other missile weapon as part of their oath requires that they fight any enemy face-to-face. When the entire Order is called up for service, it is usually divided into five companies of fifty men each, with each company being commanded by one of the Knight Commanders.
