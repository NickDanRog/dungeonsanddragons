# ADVENTURE OVERVIEW

- **System:** D&D 5th Edition (2014 ruleset)
- **World:** Encarnia *(name provisional; may change)*
- **Level Range:** Levels 1–20 across five acts
- **Structure:** Five acts, exact chapter count TBD
- **Tone:** Hope vs. despair; the power of belief; heroic self-sacrifice. The adventure asks whether people can change the systems they live inside — and what it costs them when they try.
- **Player Character Type:** Natives. Most PCs will have grown up in the Perfect Isles where the adventure begins; some will have been rescued from the Unity and brought here. All are exiles or children of exiles, people who have been defined by what they escaped.

---

# CENTRAL PREMISE

The beings that mortals have come to worship as gods are not deities at all. They are spirits — vast, potent, and real — but they are created and sustained by mortal belief, and they change as belief changes. They are mirrors of what people need, not independent divine powers.

The true primordial forces of this world are the **Old Gods**: ancient spirits of enormous power, intimately connected to the land, sea, and sky. They did not create mortals, but they are bound to the same world mortals inhabit. Where the belief-spirits shift with human attention, the Old Gods are fixed — and when they suffer, the world suffers with them.

In the west, a government that outlawed magic and divine worship centuries ago has, without intending to, severed the relationship between its people and the spirits that maintained the world's health. The land is dying. The western civilization does not understand why. Their solution — colonizing the eastern continent — will destroy both an Old God who stands in their way and the last stable relationship between mortals and the primordial world.

The Old Gods are aware of what is happening. Several of their number have been captured or co-opted by mortal institutions in the west. They are deliberating whether mortals can be permitted to continue existing at all.

**What players do not know at the start:**
- The "gods" they know of are belief-created spirits, not true independent deities. They are shaped by what people believe about them.
- The Old Gods are a separate and far older category of being — primordial spirits whose health is tied to the world's physical and metaphysical integrity.
- The land dying in the west is a direct consequence of severing mortal connection to the spirits. This is not a coincidence or a slow resource problem. It is a wound.
- The Old Gods are close to a decision that will end the world.

The word "Unity" has become embedded in western cultural life. Citizens greet each other as "brother" or "sister" regardless of race. The customary parting is "walk in Unity." These phrases feel genuine to most people who use them — the cultural warmth is real even when the institutions behind it are not.

### The Western Continent (The Unity)
A medium-sized continent with a temperate climate — cool and wet in the northeast, warm and dry in the southwest. Home to the standard D&D races (humans, elves, dwarves, halflings, gnomes, and others), all unified under a single government also called the **Unity**. Several centuries ago, the Unity outlawed the practice of magic and organized divine worship, establishing a society built on reason, technology, and centralized governance. The standard of living rose dramatically. Over time, however, the land has begun to fail: crops yield less each generation, mineral resources are harder to find, water runs thin in places it never did before. The Unity has not connected this to the suppression of magic and spirit — they have attributed it to natural resource exhaustion and sought external solutions.

The Unity's current project is the colonization of the eastern continent. This effort is concentrated in the western edge of the Sacred Lands, the region nearest to the western shores across the Middle Sea.

The public face of the Unity is rational, ordered, prosperous, and peaceful — it genuinely delivered better lives to its people for generations. The dark truth is that its suppression of belief was not incidental but deliberate: the founders feared the power that divine worship and magic gave to groups outside the state's control. What they did not understand was that this suppression was also a severance — the relationship between mortal belief and the spirit world is not merely cultural. It is structural.

**The Northern Coast exception:** The northern coastal region of the Unity is anomalously productive — crops thrive, resources flow, and the standard of living there significantly outpaces the rest of the continent. This is not luck. A cabal within the ministry tasked with suppressing magic and divine worship has captured **Huma, the Storm Whale** (an Old God, one of the last great primordial spirits of the west) using a device of their own construction. Huma's captive energy is being channeled to sustain the region. The cabal operates in complete secrecy. They intend to use the northern coast as proof of concept and expand the program — capturing whatever Old Gods remain in the west, and eventually those of the Sacred Lands. *(Full details in Old Gods section below.)*

### The Eastern Continent (The Sacred Lands)
A much larger landmass, wilder and less densely settled than the west. The Sacred Lands have greater climatic and geographical extremes: deep jungles, vast plains, high mountain ranges, desert coastlines, and boreal forests, depending on region. Its peoples include the same races as the west alongside numerous anthropomorphic-animal peoples — Tortles, Harengons, and others — along with fey creatures, lycanthropes, and wilder beings not often found in the west.

The peoples of the Sacred Lands are deeply fractious. Divided by race, creed, tradition, and geography, they are most often at peace with each other but unified action has rarely been their character. The one force currently drawing them together is the resistance to Unity colonization, led by **Carnuth, the Stag of the New Moon** — an Old God who has taken on a mortal-accessible set of forms and is actively fighting the colonial advance. *(Full details in Old Gods section below.)*

The great western temperate rainforest of the Sacred Lands — **the Wildwood** — is Carnuth's domain and the primary theater of resistance against colonization.

### The Perfect Isles (The Archipelago)
A group of islands in the Middle Sea between the two continents, neither east nor west. The Perfect Isles were created several hundred years ago by magical means and in secret, built as a sanctuary for those fleeing persecution by the Unity. The original exiles — a diverse group united only by their need for escape — established a haven that has now become a civilization of its own: 30+ factions, each preserving its own teachings, traditions, and identity. The factions are unified by shared history and shared danger but divided on nearly everything else.

The Archipelago's dominant anxiety is discovery. If the Unity learned of its existence, it would represent an existential threat. The dominant institutional posture is caution, secrecy, and preservation of the old ways — which has calcified into a fear of change that is itself becoming a threat to the Archipelago's survival.

**The Barrier:** The Perfect Isles are concealed from the outside world by layers of obfuscating magic built up over generations, combined with careful management of all departure and return routes by the Wayfinders. The Unity has heard rumors of the Isles' existence but has no official knowledge that they are real. Return to the Isles from outside cannot be accomplished by ordinary ships — travelers must call for a specially prepared vessel using a **sending stone** given to them by the Council. The Council does not give these lightly.

**Relationship to the Sacred Lands:** Limited but real. There is some trade and contact between the Perfect Isles and the Sacred Lands, making for a fundamentally different relationship than the Isles have with the Unity. The eastern peoples know the Archipelago exists; the Isles' people know something of the Sacred Lands. The Guardians of the Hidden Vale maintain the most active presence in the east of any Isles faction.

The adventure begins here.

### Dremma
The largest settlement in the Perfect Isles and the primary departure point for expeditions east or west. Located in a northern bay on **Kartos's Isle** (called "the Big Island" informally) — the largest of the islands, which also hosts the Council site at its center. All travel between the Isles and the outside world passes through Dremma. The journey from the Council site to Dremma takes two days overland. The **Waning Moon** is a notable inn here — the party's lodging both before departure and on their return.

### Sanctuary
A small village approximately a day's travel inland from Dremma, also on Kartos's Isle. The **Oracle's Pagoda** stands on a hilltop above the village — the Oracle's primary residence when she is on the Big Island, and the base of operations for the sect that serves her. Carved into the rock beneath the pagoda is a well-lit ritual space prepared for use by the Circle. The village itself is small and organized around the practical needs of the Oracle's sect.

### Heida
A remote village on the northwest of Kartos's Isle. The **Wayfinders' Lodge** — the faction's headquarters — is located here. The village is small and oriented entirely around the Lodge's operations; most residents have some connection to the Wayfinders or their support network. The **White Willow House**, a local teahouse catering to travelers and Lodge visitors, is the primary social gathering point for outsiders in the village. Heida is where the party undergoes their six months of training in Act II.

### Keshigo
A settlement on the western coast of the Sacred Lands — the first port of call for travelers arriving from the Middle Sea. Less a proper town than the idea of one: a sprawling, loosely organized gathering of people who have found it convenient to be in the same general area. Travelers pass through; goods change hands without much documentation; information circulates freely if you know who to ask. The Guardians of the Hidden Vale maintain a presence here through an Aasimar agent named **Pinion**, who manages their affairs in the region.

### Meeting
A trading settlement in the old growth forest interior, used by wild elf hunter-gatherer tribes and a few other peoples of the region as a place of exchange — both among themselves and with travelers from Keshigo. The party may encounter it during their overland journey to the Witch. It is not a permanent settlement in any formal sense but is reliably occupied during trading seasons. Caravans from Keshigo travel to Meeting and the party could hire on with one as an alternative to Senna's guidance.

### Caskara Bay
A large sheltered bay on the western coast of the Unity. Home to the important port city of Daliketh at its head and Mahakam Island at its mouth.

### Mahakam Island
A large island at the mouth of Caskara Bay. The Dawnflower's actual destination — the party is smuggled from here into Daliketh rather than entering the city directly. A secluded cove on the island connects to a Wayfinder-operated shipyard run by Kaman, which serves as his cover and staging ground. A hidden section of the attached warehouse functions as a safe house — communal living space where rescued practitioners are held before crossing to the Isles.

### Daliketh
The major port city of Caskara Bay and the party's first direct encounter with Unity urban life. An industrialized city with a prominent skyline of smokestacks, gas lighting, and the infrastructure of a late Victorian-era economy. Conspicuously clean streets despite heavy industry. Notable districts: the port district, the city center, and **Northgate** (an outlying precinct where the Garden House is located). The **Advocates' Tower** — headquarters of the Ministry of Justice's Advocates unit — is also located in the city. The **Garden House** is a boarding house and restaurant in Northgate run by Neeta Whitecliff; it serves as a Wayfinder safe house.

### Balnor
The capital of the Unity, located deep in the interior of the western continent. The party's next stop after Daliketh — reached by airship. Home to the greatest concentration of the Unity Committee belief-spirits (the Congress). The seat of the Unity Council. Further details TBD.

### Krasno Bay
A town to the north of Daliketh on the Unity's western coast. An alternative departure point for the party in the event of a four-failure outcome in Daliketh — they travel here aboard the Dawnflower and then proceed to Balnor overland rather than by airship. Details TBD.

### Karale
A sizeable agricultural and manufacturing city on the rail line between Balnor and Jindark; a day-4 overnight stop for the party. One of the Unity's most important agricultural regions, but currently in crisis: years of crop failures combined with material shortages have driven chronic food shortages and high unemployment. The social safety net is holding but the population has been stripped of purpose, and resentment toward the capital is building. The local government has seen frequent turnover. A preview of the Unity's internal contradictions at full resolution.

### Jindark
A small city in the northwestern province of **Jakor** — the party's final destination in the Unity, reached by train from Balnor. Built into hillsides descending from a high ridge to a coastal harbor, roughly 800 feet of elevation. Highly industrial (ores and minerals from the surrounding hills feed its forges) and unexpectedly picturesque. The harbor is enclosed by a massive artificial breakwater: a solid central mass with a shipping channel at either end, each overseen by a lighthouse; the breakwater top serves as a public promenade and market, one of the city's most distinctive civic features. A large installation is under construction near the ridgeline, publicly described as an airship terminal. This is where the Cabal holds **Huma, the Storm Whale** captive — imprisoned within the breakwater itself. Freeing Huma is the central objective of Act III. The city also serves as the cover origin on the party's false documents.

### The Parna Valley
A lightly settled valley in the interior of the Sacred Lands, home to a village of the same name, predominantly human and halfling. The Witch of the Silver Stream lives on a high hilltop overlooking the valley in a small cottage.

### The Witch's Cottage
A small dwelling on a hilltop above the Parna Valley. Eclectic in the extreme — home to a wide assortment of arcane paraphernalia and furniture with almost nothing matching — but kept in very good order. A place that feels lived-in and purposeful rather than chaotic. The garden and yard are working spaces.

**The Witch — Hellie**
The Witch of the Silver Stream is a human woman who goes by Hellie. She appears to be in her early forties, with slightly graying dark curly hair gathered in a tail thrown over her left shoulder. She will come out personally to greet the party — she has been informed of their arrival by "some friends of hers" — and will greet Senna very warmly if Senna is present. She is generally kind but businesslike. She sets the party to work helping around the yard and assisting with supper before she sits them down to answers their questions.

*Two twin girls, approximately 10–12 years of age, are always nearby. They call Hellie "auntie" and have opposing heterochromia. They are her apprentices.* After supper, Hellie will explain this if asked.

Supper is vegetable stew, fresh fish, and homemade bread, served at a nice table outside. Several neighbors join the party: **Silver**, presenting as a very elderly gnome; and **Beam**, a somewhat befuddled young man with a significant amount of straw attached to his clothing. After dinner Hellie will answer the party's questions to the best of her ability, and ask them many in return.

**Silver (DM only):** The spirit of the Silver Stream itself, taking a mortal form at Hellie's invitation. He is here to help her evaluate the party. Whether the players ever learn this is at the DM's discretion and their own conduct at supper.

**Beam (DM only):** The spirit of the valley's farmsteads. His characteristic form in the physical world is that of a scarecrow — hence the straw. He is evaluating the party for his own reasons alongside Silver's.

**Hellie's foreknowledge:** She was informed of the party's approach approximately one day before they arrive by some of the local nature spirits. She will not volunteer this detail but will explain it if asked — she says it matter-of-factly, as though it is simply how things work in the Parna Valley.

**Senna** — A half-elven Ranger who travels occasionally to the Witch's cottage as a courier, bringing Hellie letters from known correspondents across the region. She is a close friend of Hellie's and uses the shared journey to evaluate whether the party represents a threat to the Witch or to the Sacred Lands more broadly. She is not a required character — the party can reach the Witch via Pinion's directions, another guide from Keshigo, or by joining a caravan to Meeting. If present, she does not continue beyond Act I; the lands she frequents are not connected to the party's later destinations.

# THE ANTAGONISTS

## The Unity
The western government is not purely villainous — it genuinely improved lives and represents a real achievement of reason and organization. Its antagonist function comes from specific leaders and systems: those who lead the colonization effort with no concern for the peoples being displaced; those who enforce the prohibition on magic and divine worship with violence; those who have rationalized that the ends of civilizational survival justify the destruction of anything in their way.

**What they want:** To secure the resources of the eastern continent to sustain their civilization. To maintain the ideological order that outlaws magic and worship. To not acknowledge that their civilization's crisis is their own doing.

**Methods:** State power, colonial force, institutional suppression. They do not think of themselves as villains. The most dangerous Unity leaders are the ones who genuinely believe they are saving their people.

**Arc:** The Unity is not defeated in the conventional sense. The adventure's success in this thread is institutional change — cracks in the ideology, leaders who can be persuaded, a population that can learn the truth about what their government has cost them.

**DM Notes — The Unity's Hidden Character:**

*The Unity does not feel oppressive on the street. It feels pleasant, organized, and warm. The wrongness is the absence of things: genuine private interiority, the possibility of a different future, transcendence, children becoming themselves rather than citizens. The party may spend significant time in the Unity uncertain whether it is actually bad or simply different. The answer is that it is both — and the people who understand this best are the ones who cannot leave.*

- **The school system.** Children live at school during the work week. Most citizens present this as an unremarkable organizational fact — a practical arrangement. The DM should not call attention to it. Let it sit. A century of this practice has produced parents who relate to their children as weekend guests, children who feel more loyal to their school cohort than to their families, and a population that was shaped entirely by the state before it had any chance to be shaped by anything else. Most Unity citizens do not experience this as a loss. That is the point.

- **Lon may be stronger in the Unity than anyone expects.** The party met Lon at Hope's fountain and knows he grows as despair does. The Unity does not look like a place of despair — it looks like a civilization that achieved something. But Lon's domain is not suffering. It is the *certainty that things cannot improve*. Citizens who have been told the great work is finished, who have been given every material thing they need and no permission to want something else, may be feeding Lon without knowing it. If the party reaches Lon or his domain in Act II, this can be a devastating revelation: not that the Unity's citizens are miserable, but that they have quietly stopped hoping — and Lon is sustained by exactly that.

- **The spirit of Unity is the crack in the wall.** From the Spirits Reference: the spirit of Unity itself is "confused and unhappy. It was built on something real, and it can feel that the ideal and the reality have diverged." This spirit is the party's most important potential ally among the Unity Committee in Act III, precisely because it is the truest believer and the most disappointed one. The Unity's founding impulse — genuine community, people genuinely belonging to each other — was real. The institution has grown up around that impulse like a callus, protecting the form while slowly hollowing out the substance. The spirit of Unity has felt this happening for a very long time and has had no one to tell.

## The Old Gods
The primordial spirits of the world have legitimate grievances. Several of their number have been captured or co-opted by the west's institutions. The world's physical health is degrading. Mortals have spent centuries demonstrating that coexistence with them is either impossible or undesirable.

The Old Gods considering the destruction of the world are not wrong to be afraid. They are wrong about the solution — but the party will have to earn the right to make that argument.

**What they want:** To be free. To have the world stop being damaged. To not watch their kind be enslaved or erased.

**Methods:** The Old God leading the eastern resistance is waging a war against colonization through a mortal champion form. The council of Old Gods deliberating over the world's fate are less active antagonists than a looming presence — a deadline the party is racing toward.

**Arc:** The Old Gods must be brought from the edge of a catastrophic decision to a position of cautious coexistence. This is not an argument won with force. It is a case made through action.

## The Archipelago System
Not a person or organization but an institutional posture: the accumulated weight of decades of fear, tradition, and self-preservation among the Archipelago's fractious factions. The faction leaders who represent this antagonist force are not evil — they are terrified, and they have convinced themselves that change means death.

**What they want:** To survive, exactly as they are. To preserve the teachings and traditions that define them. To not be found.

**Methods:** Institutional inertia, factional infighting, obstruction of anything that might draw attention or require compromise.

**Arc:** The adventure's best ending requires the Archipelago to change. This means confronting leadership that has calculated safety as the absence of risk — and demonstrating that this calculation is wrong.

# ADVENTURE STRUCTURE

## The Meaning of "The Crossing"

The title operates on multiple levels simultaneously:

- **The spiritual crossing** — the transit from the physical world into the spirit world to consult with the divine. Something that hasn't been done in decades. The PCs will learn how to do it.
- **The continental crossing** — the transit from the Archipelago to the eastern and western continents. The Archipelago was built between the two; reaching either requires a crossing that has historically been dangerous.
- **The historical crossing** — the original exile event, when the ancestors of the Archipelago's people fled the Unity and made the sea crossing to build something new.
- **The personal crossing** — the ongoing tradition among individuals who follow their ancestors' path, crossing from oppression to freedom, from one life to another.

All four meanings should be present throughout the adventure. They resonate with and illuminate each other.

## Act Structure

### Act I: The Four Keys (Levels 1–4)
**Summary:** The adventure begins in the Perfect Isles. The Oracle has made a pronouncement to the Council — a new Circle has been formed — but the identities of the Circle members are a closely guarded secret. The PCs are drawn to the Council by other means (TBD), and the truth of their status is revealed before they depart. Several factions, learning that something significant is afoot, may attempt to covertly influence or obstruct the party. The Council charters a ship to carry the party to Keshigo on the western coast of the Sacred Lands. From Keshigo, they travel inland on foot to find the Witch of the Silver Stream and, through her, the Old God she serves. The act ends with the party boarding the ship back to the Archipelago, carrying the Crossing ritual, the name of the first spirit, and the Old Man's warning that the Old Gods are deliberating whether mortals have the right to continue existing.

**Act I Scene Beats:**

*On the Isles:*
- Each PC receives a different pretext drawing them to the Council (tailored to background); those who know each other may receive a shared invitation
- The Circle's ascension Council meeting: all recognized factions may send a representative, though several decline or don't attend; the Oracle (12 years old, well-trained) does most of the speaking; backed up by Vinna Maar (Wayfinder representative)
- The party is tasked with the mission; their status as the Circle is revealed
- The road to Dremma — 2 days overland across Kartos's Isle (see faction encounters below)
- 4-day sea voyage from Dremma to Keshigo through the Barrier

*The Sacred Lands — Keshigo to the Parna Valley:*
- Keshigo: seek information about the Witch; optional contact with Pinion (Hidden Vale agent); find Senna or arrange alternative guidance (caravan to Meeting, or Pinion's directions)
- ~1 week overland through old growth forest; random encounter table; possible stop at Meeting (tribal trading post)
- Arrival at Hellie's cottage; Senna greets Hellie warmly if present; Silver and Beam attend supper incognito

*The Parna Valley — Learning the Ritual:*
- Supper at Hellie's: she reveals she cannot teach the Crossing ritual (she calls it "old magic" that Witches never perform — they do not enter the spirit realm unless invited), but she can teach them a ritual for summoning spirits in their places of power, which is the method Witches of the Sacred Lands use. She offers to demonstrate it tomorrow at a nearby location. She also explains the other keys in general terms. Silver helpfully suggests that the spirit of the stream is very fond of good bourbon as a gift.
- The party is given a small, comfortable guest room
- Morning: woken by the apprentices making breakfast; Silver, Beam, and Senna (if present) have already left
- 3-hour hike to the highest hilltop in the region, arriving around midday; the Silver Stream — a small waterfall that appears silver in noon sunlight — comes into view
- Behind the waterfall, a small shrine. Hellie teaches the ritual. Silver's response depends on party conduct:
  - *If the party was respectful at supper:* Silver appears, formally introduces himself, and enthusiastically thanks Hellie for the bourbon
  - *If the party was disrespectful to him, Hellie, or anyone at table:* Hellie informs them the spirit is demanding an apology; after they abase themselves and complete the ritual, Silver appears and gives them a thorough dressing-down
- Return to the cottage; Hellie explains the Crossing ritual is "old magic" she cannot teach — it can only be learned elsewhere. She directs them to a being she calls **the Old Man of the Mountain**, who can be found at a shrine on a mountaintop on the other side of the valley, three days' journey away. *(The Old Man is secretly the Old God Hellie serves directly.)*
- Hellie will host the party as long as they wish to stay and will honestly answer any questions — but she will put them to work while they're there

*The Parna Valley — The Old Man of the Mountain:*
- The party passes through the village of Parna on the way to the mountain; trade available here, but the villagers use barter — money is of little use to them
- Planned encounter: a group of **Wildlings** (goblins) ambush the party — a difficult fight, but avoidable through careful diplomacy
- The party reaches the mountaintop shrine and performs Hellie's ritual
- The Old Man appears in the shrine in the form of a foul-mouthed old man. He has been watching the party throughout their time in his lands and has already made a judgment about them. Two tracks:

  **Track A — The party has acquitted themselves well** (not despoiled the land, been courteous to other spirits, not killed except in defense) and approaches him respectfully with an appropriate gift: He gives them the Crossing ritual and the name of the belief-spirit seeking them — **Hope**. He explains the key difference between Hellie's ritual and his: Hellie's summons a spirit in its place of power; his can be performed anywhere in the physical world, but requires the true name of the entity they wish to commune with.

  **Track B — The party has not acquitted themselves well:** Before he will share anything, he demands a service: they must defeat a powerful undead creature that has taken up residence on the other side of the mountain. On completion, he allows them to call on him using the ritual.

  **Regardless of track:** If he is positively disposed to the party at the end of their audience, he will ask them to deal with the undead creature as a favor — even if he has already given them the ritual. This is a request, not a condition.

  **What the Old Man tells them:** In either track, if the party's conduct warrants it, the Old Man speaks frankly about why their mission matters beyond the ritual itself. He tells them that some of his kind — the Old Gods — are in active deliberation about whether mortals can be permitted to continue. He names the two driving grievances: the Unity's colonization of the Sacred Lands and its ongoing violence against the peoples and spirits of the east; and the loss of contact with the Old Gods of the western continent, who have gone silent one by one over generations and are now almost certainly gone. He does not know what happened to them. He fears the worst. He asks the party to learn what they can about the fate of the western Old Gods and bring word back to him.

  He is explicit about the range of opinion among his kind. Some want all mortals destroyed; some want only the Unity and its people gone. He himself takes no position yet — he is watching, waiting for evidence that the argument for restraint can hold. The party's actions are part of that evidence. He does not say this to manipulate them; he says it because it is true.

*Return to the Isles:*
- **Optional stop at Hellie's cottage:** If the party passes back through on their way to Keshigo, they will find **Senna** waiting there — she returned to the cottage while they were at the shrine. Senna can escort them on the return journey if they want her company. Hellie hosts under the same terms as before: honest answers, food, and work.
- **Return to Keshigo:** The return journey through the old growth forest carries a chance of hostile encounters. The Sacred Lands are not safe for strangers moving through without a local guide or escort, and the party's earlier passage will not have gone unnoticed by everyone.
- **Calling for the ship:** The party should use the sending stone before or upon reaching Keshigo. The ship takes four days to arrive after the stone is used; the party may have downtime in Keshigo while they wait.
- **In Keshigo during the wait:** Travelers' gossip now carries new rumors — refugees have been coming down from the north, people displaced by violence in the colonial zone. If the party investigates, they will meet **Tinsel**, a gnomish woman forced to flee her community. She speaks to the situation directly: the Unity has established a significant colonial presence in the northern Sacred Lands and is being actively opposed by an entity the locals call Carnuth. The violence is escalating and the colonial force is growing.
- **Lady Vigo encounter** (if she was not killed in Act I's first encounter; if she was, she is replaced by another Avengers operative): She initiates through conversation. If the party will not hand over the ritual and cannot convince her they share her goal — the destruction of the Unity — she and her acolytes attack. Their objective is capture, not killing; they want to extract the ritual. This is a challenging encounter.

**Act I Conclusion:** The party boards the ship home to the Archipelago, carrying both rituals (the Summoning ritual from Hellie and the Communing/Crossing ritual from the Old Man), the name of Hope, and the Old Man's warning. Act I ends at sea.

**The Road to Dremma — Faction Encounters:**

*Night 1:* **Malachite** (tiefling Wayfinder) and **Sir Mellic** (Path of Virtue Paladin) approach the party. Malachite is friendly and does most of the talking; Sir Mellic interjects only with grunts and chuckles and won't engage unless directly addressed. Malachite covers the "rules": don't reveal your origins in the Sacred Lands unless absolutely necessary; never bring anyone back with you. He will answer questions but is assessing the party's reliability throughout. If he decides they are a security risk, he will report to his superiors before they depart.

*Day 2 morning:* Malachite and Sir Mellic peel off. Shortly after, the party is joined by **Belladonna**, an Elf of the Hidden Vale, posing as a simple traveler. She asks an endless stream of questions and evaluates the party based on their answers and how they treat her. Patient and respectful treatment results in her revealing herself and sharing the name and location of **Pinion**, the Vale's Keshigo agent — who can provide the party with information about the region and a direct introduction to Senna. Dismissive treatment means the party does not learn of Pinion.

*Day 2, toward evening:* **Lady Vigo**, an aristocratic human, approaches the party. She is a senior Avengers operative — not the rogue the Avengers will later claim. She does not know the specifics of the party's mission; if they reveal it she attacks. Her goal is to incapacitate and delay, not to kill. She has a magic ring that teleports her to her lair if the party gains the upper hand and will attempt to escape rather than die. If she is not defeated, she will send agents to burn the party's ship in Dremma. If captured, she will be released to Avengers custody before departure — the Avengers will claim she is acting independently, which is partially but not entirely true. She will appear again in a later act if she survives.

*Dremma, evening of arrival:* **Varley Rathmandantus** (gnome, Eightfold Path) approaches the party at their lodgings. Scholarly and snooty; he circles around the point before asking them to return details of the ritual to him, offering magic items he can provide immediately. He frames this as academic interest.

*Dremma, morning of departure:* **Beldwin Stonefinger** (dwarf, Arcane Practitioners Association) approaches before they board. Direct and sincere; he also wants the ritual, but frames it as preservation — he wants to ensure it is not lost to future generations. He has nothing to offer now but makes a genuine promise of reward on their return.

### Act II: The Crossing (Levels 4–8)
**Summary:** The party arrives back at the Perfect Isles, changed by what they have seen and carrying a warning that will not keep. Their status as the Circle is no longer a secret within the Council, and news of their return — and what they carry — will spread. Before they can move on the Unity, they must perform the Crossing to Hope for the first time, receiving the full weight of the Old Gods' deliberation in a way the Old Man could only hint at. They must then prepare: learning the language, customs, and cultural surface of a civilization that outlaws everything they are; enduring the long crossing of the Middle Sea; and finding a way into the Unity without triggering its suppression apparatus. The act ends when they set foot in the Unity.

**Act II Scene Beats:**

*Return to the Isles:*
- **Sir Mellic is aboard the ship.** He volunteers nothing beyond that he is to bring them to Malachite in Dremma. If approached with patience, he opens up somewhat — he has worked as a sea escort for the Wayfinders for many years and met scores of practitioners rescued from the Unity. He has little on-the-ground experience inside the Unity itself but knows its people and culture well from those encounters.
- **Dremma:** Malachite is waiting at the **Waning Moon**. He tells the party he has been asked to escort them to the Oracle, currently at her pagoda in **Sanctuary**, approximately a day inland. The party may take a full day in Dremma if needed; he is prepared to leave immediately if they prefer.
- **Optional contacts in Dremma before departure:**
  - **Varley Rathmandantus** (Eightfold Path): If the party kept their agreement, he thanks them profusely and offers to assist going forward.
  - **Beldwin Stonefinger** (Arcane Practitioners Association): More businesslike in his thanks; produces a small chest from under his desk. The chest is larger on the inside than the outside. It contains a magic item per party member — apparently well-tailored to each — plus small gems worth 100gp per party member.
- **Journey to Sanctuary:** Sir Mellic and Malachite escort the party. The journey is peaceful. Malachite has worked extensively inside the Unity and will share what he knows if asked, though he notes the Oracle will want a full account when they arrive.

*The Meeting at Sanctuary:*
- The Oracle's Pagoda sits on a hilltop above the village. Waiting for the party are the Oracle, Vinna Maar, Malachite, Belladonna (Hidden Vale), and two representatives they have not previously met:
  - **Tarak Maal** — a powerfully built human in his mid-sixties; representative of the Blue Star Adepts
  - **Morning Wren** — a small, shy halfling woman; representative of the Greensingers
- The group questions the party extensively about the journey: the Witch, the ritual, and the encounter with the Old Man of the Mountain. The news that an Old God told them the Old Gods are deliberating whether to destroy mortalkind shocks the room.
- After several hours, the Oracle — largely silent throughout — calls for a break. She tells the party that if they wish to attempt the ritual and contact Hope, she can provide them a space to do so; the discussion will continue without them. Contacting Hope is required before the plot moves forward.

*First Contact with Hope:*
- The Oracle asks whether the party has a gift for Hope. If not, she produces a folded piece of paper bearing a **haiku poem** — this is an acceptable gift.
- She leads them to the **ritual space carved into the rock beneath the pagoda** — well-lit and clearly prepared for this purpose. **Tarak Maal and Morning Wren** join them at the Oracle's invitation.
- The party performs the ritual using the words given by the Old Man. A portal opens.
- **The passage:** Dark corridors filled with lost items (as established). The ethereal light leads them to a **plain altar set before a locked door**. The door cannot be opened by mundane or magical means and cannot be forced. The party must place the gift on the altar — or, in the case of the poem, read it aloud. The door opens.
- **Hope's domain:** The abandoned city square at twilight. **Hope** sits on the edge of the fountain alongside **Lon**, who withdraws quietly when the party enters.
- **What Hope tells them:**
  - She thanks them for going through such trouble to reach her.
  - She wants them to help her return more fully into the world — she believes the people of the world need her. She is asking them to save the world.
  - If asked why they were chosen, she is vague: *"your souls felt right,"* or something close to it.
  - She asks them to look into the fountain. The water shows a vision of **Huma, the Storm Whale**, suffering and held captive by an arcane machine constructed by the Unity. 
  - She asks the party to rescue Huma. Doing so, she says, can help bring hope not only to people but also to the Old Gods — they have a right to be angry, but the party can show them that not all mortals mean them harm. He has been trapped this way for several years and cannot survive many more. 
  - She asks them to return to her before they leave for the Unity: there is someone else she wants to introduce them to. *(This will be Revna.)*
  - She is willing to answer questions, but her connection to the world is tenuous and limits what she can say.
  - When the conversation is over, she opens a second portal for them to step through.
- **The return:** Tarak Maal reports the party was gone for only moments before a new portal opened and they stepped back through. Morning Wren says she felt Hope's presence when the second portal opened. The others agree.

*The Decision:*
- After hearing the party's report, the Oracle calls a meeting for the following morning with the same attendees: Oracle, Morning Wren, Tarak Maal, Vinna Maar, and Malachite. The village of Sanctuary is open to them in the meantime — a quiet community surrounded by rice farms — and they are welcome to stay at the pagoda.
- At the morning meeting, the Oracle formally asks whether the party is willing to take on the quest. Once they accept, Malachite speaks: plans are already in motion to smuggle them into the Unity, but they won't be going unprepared. He and other Wayfinders will spend the next several months training the party on everything they need to know to infiltrate the Unity and survive.
- **Unity background check:** If one or more party members are Unity refugees who crossed in their teenage years or later, Malachite asks that person to serve as expedition leader once inside — helping the rest navigate the Unity's complex social order. If no one qualifies, he tells them he has someone in mind for that role and they will meet that person shortly. During training, if it becomes clear that no party member has a Unity background, Tiamane will approach the party privately and offer to serve as their guide for the duration of their time inside the Unity. She frames this as a duty to the Isles — she is the person best positioned to keep them alive, and she cannot in good conscience send them in without her. The offer is genuine and her skills are exactly what she claims.
- **Timeline:** The training will take approximately six months. It is summer now; the Middle Sea crossing should not be attempted in fall or winter. Six months provides thorough preparation while leaving time to cross safely.
- **Week off:** The party is granted a week of downtime — an opportunity for roleplay interludes to visit family or friends before an extended absence. The Oracle says they may reveal they are going on a mission to the Unity, but to keep the specifics private. She expects the secret to leak eventually but wants to delay that as long as possible.
- **Training location:** The Wayfinders' headquarters is in the remote village of **Heida**, on the northwest of Kartos's Isle.
- *DM note:* The following sections contain considerably more material than any single scene should present. The DM should summarize, highlighting key points and giving a general overview. These notes serve as reference for answering specific party questions during and after training.

*Training — History and Politics:*
- The shorter first portion of training, lasting a few weeks. Despite its brevity, the party may find it taxing — the material is dry and academic, and their instructors seem to relish a captive audience.
- **Instructors:** Two half-elves who bicker constantly:
  - **Kelvar Garth** (history) — a proctor of the Founders' Keep; lectures in a droning monotone but perks up visibly when asked a genuinely good question
  - **Professor Liandrin Taita** (politics) — somewhat scattered; carries an enormous stack of notebooks which she references constantly
- **Pre-Unity history:** Since time immemorial, the western continent was divided into dozens of competing tribes, states, and kingdoms. A cycle of wars lasting over a thousand years — fought increasingly in the names of competing divine pantheons and fueled by destructive magical power.
- **Sister Neema and the One True Way (approx. 600 years ago):** A human monk known only as Sister Neema received what she called The Revelation and founded a movement called **The One True Way**. Its two central tenets: that the future is already determined, and that wholly devoted practitioners could know it; and that mortal ingenuity — not divine or arcane power — was the only true path to progress and freedom. The movement gained followers across decades thanks to Neema's uncanny predictive accuracy and the widespread exhaustion from centuries of fighting. Whole regions of the western continent came under its sway.
- **After Neema's death:** Her acolytes used their base of support to launch a systematic campaign of conversion. Many were convinced of the One True Way's inevitable victory — its prophecies were never wrong. In secret, the sect's leaders had lost access to Neema's genuine divinatory gifts and were arranging for their "predictions" to come true by any means necessary. It was during this period that the future exiles began the long, secret work of building the Archipelago.
- **The Last War:** The conversion campaign became a military campaign — the **Last War** — fought over the course of a century. With the western continent secured, the True Way launched an anti-magic, anti-divine pogrom, purging its society of all supernatural elements, and declared "Universal peace and an end to history, the realization of the One True Way." The sect's subsequent attempts to reorganize society — rewriting history, enforcing communal living, concentrating all wealth in the sect's hands — caused significant internal strife and escalating crackdowns over time.
- **The Great Schism (approx. 300 years ago):** An elite unit of True Believers — the sub-sect created specifically to combat the supernatural — stumbled upon undeniable evidence that the sect's leadership had been faking its divinatory powers since Sister Neema's death. Their leader, a half-orc named **Maakan**, spent a decade carefully and deliberately spreading this information. The result was a civil war called the **Great Schism**, which lasted a generation.
- **The Unity's founding:** Maakan's faction, calling itself **the Unity**, sought to preserve the genuine teachings of Sister Neema while rejecting the fraud and oppression that had grown up around them. After securing power, they reorganized society, emphasizing the communal, progressive, and humanist elements of the Way and installing a system of direct democracy with firm checks and balances.
- **Since the founding:** The Unity has continued advancing science and technology while actively combating the supernatural.
- **Political structure:** Every person on the western continent — regardless of race or gender — is a Unity citizen. Citizens participate through local councils (maximum 1,000 citizens each), which elect representatives to regional councils, then provincial councils, and finally the **Unity Council** of representatives from the 10 provinces.
  - Terms of office are shortest at the local level (one year), expanding up the chain to a maximum of ten years for a Unity Council seat. Seats turn over on a rotating basis.
  - Most issues are handled by councils directly. Any member may call for a vote on a plebiscite; if accepted by majority, the issue is sent to the council below for broader input — potentially cascading to the local level. Complex but deeply beloved: citizens are strongly attached to the principle that every voice counts.

*Training — Society and Culture:*
- The longer portion of training; taught by **Tiamane Irongate**, a dwarf woman who is herself a Unity exile, having arrived on the Isles approximately five years ago. She was a scholar who studied pre-True Way history; her research eventually led her to faith and clerical abilities when she encountered accounts of deities long thought forgotten.
- She drills the party mercilessly on speech patterns, mannerisms, and customs — anything her sharp mind can identify as a potential tell. She considers preparing them thoroughly a personal duty.
- **Language and greeting:** The Unity's communal values are embedded in everyday speech.
  - Greetings: *"Good day, citizen!"* / *"Greetings, brother/sister!"*
  - Farewells: *"Unity endures!"* / *"Kind day to you, citizen!"*
  - Expressions of surprise or joy: *"Unity preserve us!"* / *"By the Unity!"*
- **Community behavior:** Citizens go out of their way to help those in obvious distress; neighbors visit unbidden just to talk; communal gathering in social hours is the norm. Communal eating, sleeping, and daily activities are standard practice. Citizens have little of the modesty or privacy instinct common in the Archipelago.
- **Mutual responsibility:** Everyone is expected to contribute. Useful roles are found for children, the disabled, and the elderly. The Unity is deeply invested in its citizens' welfare — if it can be prevented, no one goes hungry, unclothed, or unhoused. Public goods may not be of superior quality, but they are available to everyone. Neighbors take active pride in looking after each other.
- **Work culture:** Shirking is not tolerated. The day is divided into strict working, resting, and social hours, with mandatory breaks during working hours and three days off per week (a weekend and a mid-week break).
- **Nosiness:** Citizens are openly curious and rarely hesitant to ask personal questions. Everyone is expected to have opinions on political matters at every level. *Tiamane specifically warns the party on this point:* expressing an uninformed political opinion will draw attention. Her recommended approach: engage, but stay neutral. *"I haven't made up my mind yet"* is always an acceptable response. Tiamane adds — carefully, as though still working out how to say it — that she knew for years something was wrong with this part of Unity life but could not name it until she left. The communal warmth is genuine. Neighbors do help each other. People do care. But the same social infrastructure that produces that warmth produces total mutual accountability. Being different in any way that doesn't map to Unity norms is immediately visible and immediately uncomfortable. People learn, at a cellular level, to flatten their inner lives in public — not because guards are watching, but because everyone is.
- **Dress:** Utilitarian in almost all contexts; ostentatious displays of wealth are deeply frowned upon, as are lavish lifestyles or living alone. Excess money carries strong social pressure to direct toward public causes. Even the highest-ranking councilor is expected to follow the same social norms as everyone else.
- **Economics:** An interplay of public and private enterprise. Large public enterprises handle major projects and public works; smaller private concerns make specific goods and provide services. The line between the two is often blurred. Taxes are paid in labor, goods, or services at the citizen's choice; a citizen may work part of the year for a public enterprise, and their private employer is expected to continue their salary during that period, which counts toward the employer's own tax contribution.
- **Arts:** Citizens love art, the more public the better. Music, comedic theatre, and political satire are the most popular forms. Poking fun at politicians and at the Unity itself is considered good form — but everyone instinctively knows where the line is, even if no one can precisely define it. Tiamane says this detail troubled her for years before she could say why. A written law can be argued in court. The instinctive line cannot be challenged, appealed, or even fully identified — it can only be violated. She tells the party they may witness what happens when someone crosses it without understanding what that person did wrong.
- **The absent future:** Tiamane says this is the hardest thing to explain, and the thing she understood fully only after she was gone. The Unity's founding ideology declared that the great work is finished — "universal peace and an end to history." That declaration did not die with the One True Way. It survived into the Unity's culture, quietly. Citizens have everything their ancestors fought for. They are cared for, housed, fed, entertained. What Tiamane slowly came to understand, living on the Isles, is what this does to hope. Hope requires an open future — the genuine possibility that things could be different, better, changed. The Unity's culture forecloses this without forbidding it. No one tells citizens they cannot want something new. They simply stop.
- **Education:** A publicly funded school system. As a holdover from True Way practice, children typically live at school during the work week, returning home on weekends and the mid-week break. The school system has long served as a vehicle for political and social indoctrination.
- **Law enforcement:** Strict but generally fair. The Unity's constabulary is omnipresent and respected, and generally unarmed. Officers tend to be even nosier than the average citizen but are typically friendly and helpful to visitors. Those who seem suspicious are carefully observed; if suspicion of being an outsider or trafficking in the supernatural is strong enough, the matter is referred up the chain to specialists.
  - **Social deviants:** Citizens who consistently refuse to live by Unity norms — the unspoken code that everyone knows and no one can fully articulate — may be registered by their local council as **social deviants**. Registration triggers formal reeducation: a structured period of monitored communal living, regular social evaluation, and close supervision. Reeducation is presented as care and support, not punishment — an opportunity to find one's way back to the community. Most citizens, including those undergoing it, accept this framing. The party should not assume reeducation is a euphemism for something overtly brutal; it may not be. Whether the outcome is a genuinely changed person or simply a more effective performance of normalcy is unclear, possibly even to the specialists involved.
  - **Magic collars:** Citizens with an inborn ability to practice magic are required to continuously wear a specialized magic item: a collar that monitors their magical use. If the wearer casts a spell, the collar immediately alerts the relevant authorities and transmits the wearer's precise location at the time. The collar can be **audited** by trained specialists — a process that reveals when the wearer last cast a spell. There is no social stigma attached to wearing a collar. Those who wear them are regarded as model citizens: proof that the state's trust is well-placed and that cooperation with public safety is possible even among the magically gifted. The collars are presented as a reasonable public safety measure, and most collar-wearers agree. *The party should be aware that any magic use in the Unity is potentially traceable.*
- **Technology:** The Unity is substantially more technologically advanced than any other region of the world — roughly analogous to the late Victorian era. Notable features:
  - Firearms: cartridge rifles and single-shot pistols
  - Publicly available gas lighting and heating
  - Basic plumbing
  - Steam engines powering both steamships and a very recent invention: primitive locomotives
  - Dirigibles — the most popular and beloved form of long-distance travel
- **Views on the outside world:**
  - *The Sacred Lands ("The East"):* A land of opportunity — natural resources and a population in need of the Unity's enlightenment.
  - *The Archipelago:* Officially, a myth. Citizens who speak of it are dismissed. Unofficially, a few government ministries — those dealing with the supernatural and with rebellious citizens — believe it *might* exist, and view it as a useful pressure valve: it removes many potential problems without requiring state action.

*Interludes and Factional Politics:*
- During training, the party will be approached by several Archipelago factions — some previously encountered, some new. These encounters should be interspersed throughout the training scenes.
- **Morning Wren (Greensingers):** Shyly approaches and asks if the party would be willing to discuss the Summoning ritual Hellie taught them. If they agree, she is deeply grateful. If a party member belongs to the Greensingers, she privately shares her belief that the Summoning ritual and the Greensingers' practice of **spirit singing** are closely connected — an insightful party member may have already made this link. She believes the ritual can be adapted to speak more reliably with local nature spirits, which would be a significant boon for her sect. In either case, if the party shares what they know, she asks them to seek out any local spirits they encounter in the Unity and bring back whatever they can about the spirit world there.
- **Belladonna (Hidden Vale):** Approaches if the party had a positive encounter with her in Act I, or if a party member belongs to her sect. More forthcoming than Morning Wren — she explains she believes there is a connection between some of her sect's rituals and what they learned from Hellie, and with this in mind asks them to share details of the Summoning ritual. If they agree, she is delighted and offers them a chance to take part in her sect's initiation rituals upon their return.
- **Bix Harbaugh (Eightfold Path):** A halfling lawyer appears with a proposal — if the party fulfilled their terms with the Eightfold Path. He wants them to covertly deliver an arcane device into the Unity (he frowns deeply at the word "smuggle"); precise placement is flexible, but closer to the capital is better. In exchange: 2,000gp in gems and two specifically requested magic items delivered a few days before departure, plus one powerful magic item (or equivalent GP) per party member upon return. He has a contract ready for immediate signature. The contract includes a clause that the party should not directly reveal the device's origin, though they may acknowledge its existence if discovered or asked by another party. *Note: placing this device will create specific complications in Act III.*
- **Beldwin Stonefinger (Arcane Practitioners Association):** Travels to Heida personally to make an offer — if the party fulfilled their terms with the Association. He wants technical information on the Unity's public works (dams, levees, canals) and/or detailed specifications for airships or locomotives — the more specific the better. His goal is to blend technology with magic to improve quality of life throughout the Archipelago. He cannot offer anything upfront, but promises a custom magic item created for each party member at the Association's expense when they return.
- **Urdo Vhane (Avengers):** A sinister-looking human warlock approaches the party at the **White Willow House**, a local teahouse catering to travelers. He introduces himself as the inquisitor of the Avengers sect and says he wants to ask about their encounters with Lady Vigo (and, if applicable, the other Avengers-affiliated warlock from the Act I Sacred Lands encounter). If he believes the party is being truthful (+12 to his Insight check against any Deception attempt), he thanks them formally and sincerely, then offers a gift: a small rod that can be used to summon a moderately powerful fiend (CR 4 Incubus) to perform a single demanded service, after which it returns to the Hells. He also asks — separately — whether the party would bring back specific information about the Unity's military and defenses, which he has been unable to obtain through the Wayfinders. He frames this as patriotic duty: he considers war with the Unity inevitable, and any intelligence advantage saves lives. He notes that the party, as a neutral entity, is not bound by the same constraints as "those perpetual do-gooders."
- **Tamar (Wild Bunch):** A wandering duelist appears outside the gates of the Wayfinder's Lodge and asks to speak with the party. She has heard of the party's "legendary journey" to the east and wants to fight one of them. She offers a **javelin of lightning** she carries to anyone who can defeat her. The bout is to first blood; she will be a difficult but defeatable opponent (CR 3 Veteran stat block). If no one agrees, she warns she travels far and will share the news; a second refusal earns a curse and she walks away. She is gracious in both victory and defeat — honoring a loser who chose to stand against a superior opponent, and surrendering the javelin as promised to a victor.

*Meeting with Revna:*
- If the party forgets their promise to visit Hope before departure, she will visit one of them in a dream a few days prior to remind them.
- **The crossing passage (second visit to Hope):** Different from the first. Takes the form of a house decorated for a wedding reception — built in the style of the Archipelago but impossibly large, containing a seemingly infinite number of rooms. The party must find the gifts table (by following a rather innocuous white garland through the house) and leave an appropriate offering.
- **Hope's domain (second visit):** The same abandoned city square, but night birds can be heard chirping in the distance and the fountain is very slightly more active than before.
- Hope opens a portal within the spirit world and leads the party through it to Revna's domain. This is a transit between spirit domains rather than a return to the physical world.
- **Revna's domain:** A large structure at the center of a partially ruined city. From the outside it is impossible to determine its function — it reads as civic hall, cathedral, and palace simultaneously, or something that is all three, or none of them. The interior is a sprawling network of rooms, spaces, and corridors in a half-state of being rebuilt, redecorated, and renovated: walls partially repainted or covered in graffiti, mismatched furniture in various states of disrepair, half-ruin, and restoration. The whole space feels like something in the process of becoming something, without yet knowing what.
- **Revna's chamber:** A central domed room in the same spirit as the rest — plain stone, growing plants, water dripping from the ceiling in a few places — but with a more naturalistic quality than the outer rooms. Revna waits on a throne cobbled together from mismatched pieces. She takes the form of a thin, dark-haired girl. One hand is covered by a clawed black gauntlet; at her hip she wears a bright, shining sword.
- **The dynamic between Revna and Hope:** When the party arrives, Hope sits at the base of Revna's throne. Revna rests her gauntleted hand firmly on Hope's shoulder and uses her free hand to play absently with Hope's hair. Hope does not speak unless directly addressed; she wears a wistful expression with eyes lowered, occasionally looking up at Revna.
- **What Revna wants:** She tells the party it is time for wrongs to be righted, and she wants them to be her instrument. She asks them to free her uncle, who is being held captive by what she calls **the Congress** — the spirits of the Unity. She is extremely evasive about who her uncle is, telling the party to trust her: true rebellion can only begin once he is set loose. She does note that Placida is unaware that she seeks her uncle's freedom, and is not to be told. 
- **Placida:** Revna explains that one member of the Congress — **Placida** — has agreed to aid their cause. If the party performs the Communing ritual (the second ritual, taught by the Old Man of the Mountain) at a location near where the Congress's power is greatest (in or near the Unity capital), Placida will lead them to a place where Revna's uncle can be freed.
- **The gifts:** One of Revna's servants — an angelic but angry-looking halfling — appears carrying a tray bearing two items: a key to free her uncle from his prison, and a scroll to serve as a gift for Placida.
- **Further questions:** Revna will answer what questions she can but will not reveal more about her uncle or her longer-term goals. The party must earn her trust before being admitted further into her counsel.
- **If the party is respectful and doesn't press past her stated limits:** She tells them they are welcome to commune with her whenever they wish. She opens a portal to return them to the physical world at the interview's conclusion.

*Ritual Note — The Two Rituals:*
The party has learned two distinct rituals by this point. The **Summoning ritual** (taught by Hellie) contacts a spirit at a place of power and draws it into physical manifestation — no name or gift required, but both help. The **Communing ritual** (taught by the Old Man of the Mountain, also called the Crossing ritual) requires the four keys and always involves a full Crossing into the spirit world. Reaching Placida requires the Communing ritual performed near the seat of the Congress's power — the party will cross bodily into her domain, and from there she will guide them to where Revna's uncle is held.

*The Sea Crossing:*
- **Timing:** The party attempts the Middle Sea crossing in mid-spring. Two weeks of downtime separate the end of training and departure. The crossing itself takes approximately two months.
- **Warebock:** The party is introduced to **Warebock**, a short, veteran human Tidecaller with extensive experience crossing from the Archipelago to the western continent, approximately one month before departure. He is short on crew but willing to take the mission if the party agrees to supplement his workers aboard ship.
- **The Dawnflower:** Warebock's vessel — a mid-sized sailing ship with three decks and a sterncastle. Much of the ship's routine function is managed by clockwork machines designed and maintained by his First Officer, **Cuculahan**. Cuculahan spends a few days before departure training the party on how the ship and its mechanisms operate. He is a nervous man; his machines are not.
- **The crew:** Twelve sailors plus five notable figures:
  - **Chimal** — female Aasimar; member of the Avengers; handles ship security
  - **Orthon** — male elf Wizard; Arcane Practitioners Association; specializes in weather magic; uses a magic item to detect large sea creatures and steer around them — one of the primary hazards of the Middle Sea
  - **Dalen** — male halfling Ranger; Seawatch; works in partnership with **Funi** (a giant albatross) and **Kuni** (a giant dolphin) to maintain constant watch around the ship
- **Party duties:** The party is assigned rotating shipboard tasks — assisting the cook, hauling cargo, swabbing the deck. Two party members are assigned to crow's nest watch duty as part of a continuous rotation.
- **Random encounter table:** Used to generate weather events and minor encounters throughout the crossing. TBD — draft when writing DM Guide.
- **Major encounter 1 — Kelpie's Island:**
  - Early in the crossing, Orthon detects a pair of leviathans and the Dawnflower makes a major course correction to avoid them. The detour takes the ship into uncharted territory, which makes Warebock and his crew visibly nervous.
  - On the second day of the detour, the ship sails into a mysterious fog early in the morning. Orthon reports it is magical in nature and he cannot dispel it. Visibility deteriorates steadily. The party eventually detects land by sound — surf, birds — before the ship runs aground on a sandbar. The fog lifts quickly, revealing a small island nearby.
  - Cuculahan assesses the damage: minor; a few days' work to repair. Warebock suggests — not orders, he considers this outside his purview — that the party explore the island while repairs are made.
  - **The island:** Systematic exploration reveals the island is not mundane. Plants and creatures exhibit unusual behaviors, strange colorations, and signs of higher-than-normal intelligence. The island takes a full day to explore. The northern portion is considerably more rocky.
  - **The grotto:** Exploration of a small rocky beach on the north side reveals massive piles of bones and filth outside a grotto-like cave. Exploring the cave requires the party to swim through a short submerged passage to reach its full depth.
  - **The lair:** At the center of the deepest cavern, the party finds a small shrine that has been profaned and converted into a lair. They must fight through three waves of hideously deformed Sahuagin before facing their leader — a CR 5 monstrosity. Details TBD for DM Guide.
  - **The shrine:** After combat, the party may attempt to repair the shrine. Only after it is restored can the island's spirit be contacted via the Summoning ritual.
  - **Kelpie:** A lesser nature spirit of the island. Takes the form of a halfling-like mermaid and speaks in the third person. She explains that the Sahuagin replaced "the other two legs of the sea" — visitors who used to come to her island. Investigation suggests these were likely merfolk of some kind, though Kelpie has no further details; she only knows she hasn't seen them in "quite some time."
  - The Sahuagin's slaughter of the island's inhabitants gave rise to a belief-spirit, which Kelpie names **Alvolea**. With the Sahuagin now largely destroyed, Alvolea is rapidly declining in power. Kelpie says she has him firmly in hand.
  - Kelpie cannot offer the party much of direct use, but will answer any questions asked of her honestly. She also grants them open access — they may call on her whenever they wish, though her power is limited and there is little she can do in most situations.
  - As a gift in thanks, she offers the party a minor artifact: **the Voice of the Sea**, a large conch shell. She asks only that they return it to her at some point in the future. *(Full appendix entry to be written for DM Guide. Statistics below.)*

    **The Voice of the Sea** *(Minor Artifact — Conch Shell)*
    *Loaned by Kelpie, nature spirit of the unnamed Middle Sea island. Must be returned to her at some point in the future.*

    When blown on or next to the sea once per week, the shell summons aid from friendly sea creatures. Roll d100 to determine what answers the call:
    - **01–50:** A pod of 2d6 **dolphins**
    - **51–80:** A pod of 1d4 **orcas**
    - **81–100:** 1d2 **humpback whales**

    Summoned creatures are of normal intelligence but will follow the wielder's instructions to the best of their ability, so long as those instructions do not require them to commit violence or place them in direct danger. They remain until the task is complete or they can no longer assist.

    While attuned to the Voice of the Sea, the wielder also gains:
    - *Water breathing* once per day (no spell slot required)
    - *Water walk* once per day (no spell slot required)
    - Swim speed equal to their standard movement speed
    - +5 bonus to all Strength (Athletics) checks involving swimming

- **Major encounter 2 — The Errant Venture:**
  - As the party nears the Unity, the Dawnflower approaches the waters near a major peninsula before turning into **Caskara Bay** — their final destination. A large fishing trawler, the **Errant Venture**, is encountered here, having drifted off course after a storm.
  - A party member on lookout duty when the ship enters visual range must make a high-DC Perception check to spot the Venture before it spots the Dawnflower.
  - **If spotted in time:** Orthon casts an upcasted fog cloud to conceal the Dawnflower. The Venture settles in to do deep-sea fishing and does not leave immediately. Warebock sends the party and Dalen to keep watch. Orthon maintains the concealment while maneuvering around the Venture for the day — this fully taxes his abilities, requiring a full day of rest afterward. The party then continues on their way without incident.
  - **If not spotted in time:** The Venture's crew notice the Dawnflower and move to investigate. Sailing ships are considered old-fashioned in the Unity; a sailing vessel rigged with clockwork automation is a genuine curiosity. The Dawnflower cannot outrun the Venture's steam engine. A direct encounter is inevitable.
  - Warebock tells the party that he and his crew are crossing experts but have no training for this — they'll need the party to do the talking. If Tiamane is present, she volunteers to take the lead; players should make her checks in that case. Three increasingly difficult Deception checks are required as the Venture comes alongside:
    1. *What is the deal with your ship?* A sailing ship with clockwork automation is unusual — why not use steam power like everyone else?
    2. *Where are you from and where are you heading?* The captain knows the regional shipping world well; any answer must be both careful and convincing.
    3. *Can we come look at your ship?* The crew is extremely curious about the Dawnflower's mechanisms. Unity citizens are communal and friendly — someone with an unusual vessel would typically love to show it off. Any refusal must be worded very carefully to avoid arousing suspicion.
  - **If all three checks succeed:** The Venture departs with a disappointed but satisfied crew.
  - **If any check fails:** The captain becomes seriously suspicious — he considers whether they might be rebels or possibly a scouting vessel from the east (rumored but never confirmed by anyone in the Unity). The party has one final opportunity: one PC makes a Deception, Intimidation, or Persuasion check against his +8 Insight. Success sends the Venture on its way. Failure means he sends rifle-armed crewmembers across to capture the Dawnflower.
  - **After combat:** The party must decide what to do with the Errant Venture and its surviving crew. The crew will not resist capture, particularly if more than three crewmembers or the captain were killed. **Chimal** will argue persuasively that the Venture must be sunk with all hands — the risk of discovery is too great otherwise. **Tiamane** (if present) will push back and take up the counterargument if the PCs do not engage.
  - The party may propose their own solutions. **Orthon** is not present — he was hidden below to rest and reduce suspicion — but if sought out he will suggest a solution he has been saving: a set of scrolls (deep slumber, modify memory) he stowed for exactly this kind of emergency. He has just enough to put the crew to sleep for several days and modify their memories to forget the encounter. Chimal will note this does not explain the missing crewmembers to anyone who comes looking; Warebock will be persuaded to try the non-lethal solution regardless.
  - *This is the party's first direct contact with ordinary Unity citizens and should be treated as significant. What they choose to do here — and how — is a preview of how they will operate inside the Unity.*

*Mahakam Island:*
- **Wayfinder standing note:** Before the party leaves the Dawnflower, Warebock explains that their status with the Wayfinders is that of an unknown quantity. They will not be directly trusted until they have proven themselves. If the party handled the Errant Venture encounter carefully, this counts significantly in their favor and Warebock will say so in his report. If the encounter did not occur or was handled badly, he will have little — or nothing good — to pass on.
- **Approach:** Caskara Bay is a large sheltered bay; the important port city of **Daliketh** sits at its head. **Mahakam Island**, a large island at the bay's mouth, is the Dawnflower's destination. The party will be smuggled from here into the city. The Dawnflower cannot approach openly and must make landfall at night. Orthon shrouds the ship in a fog cloud on approach, triggering it only after a signal light is flashed from a secluded cove on the island's shore.
- **The shipyard:** The cove connects to a Wayfinder-operated shipyard used as cover for smuggling people to and from the mainland. The party is quartered in a hidden section of an attached warehouse that has been converted into communal living space — a staging area where rescued practitioners are held before crossing to the Isles. Present alongside the party: a crew of Wayfinders and a half-elf druid named **Kore**, a recent rescue awaiting passage home. Warebock and his crew will rest here a few days before departing — they are waiting on one additional rescue before the Dawnflower returns.
- **Kaman:** The party is introduced to **Kaman**, a human Wayfinder who has run this leg of the smuggling operation for decades, using the shipyard as his cover. He speaks briefly with Warebock (and with Tiamane, if present), then returns very early the next morning with a set of documents for each party member. Their cover: a technical crew from **Jindark** (their eventual destination) returning north after completing consulting work on an experimental vessel in his shipyard. Their contact in Daliketh will connect them with transportation for the next leg and explain how to reach them. Kaman will take the party to the city himself by tug — a journey of several hours, arriving around midday.
- **Search (conditional):** If the party had a direct encounter with the Errant Venture but successfully turned the crew away, the shipyard will be subject to a constabulary search before the party departs. The warehouse goes into lockdown. Kaman handles the constables deftly — no one is found. After they leave, he gives Warebock a thorough tongue-lashing and reschedules the party's departure to the following morning.

*Daliketh:*
- **Arrival:** The party arrives just before midday on a mid-week break day. Citizens are out in force — community events, errands, families fishing at the pier, children swimming at the beach. The scene is warmer and more ordinary than the party expected, but the crowding and noise will be overwhelming for those raised on the Isles.
- **The city approach:** This is the party's first direct experience of Unity life. The DM should take care with the contrast: the industrial skyline, smokestacks, omnipresent gas lighting, the smell of coal and industry — and despite all of it, streets that are conspicuously clean. The air will be a shock for characters not from here.
- **The objective:** Kaman sends them off on their own for the first time. Their destination is the **Garden House**, a boarding house and restaurant in the **Northgate** precinct, run by **Neeta Whitecliff**, a halfling Wayfinder contact. They will wait there for a travel document courier. The walk takes approximately 90 minutes: through the port district, across the city center, and into Northgate. Three moderate-DC Intelligence checks are required to follow Kaman's directions correctly.

*The Contact:*
- Neeta welcomes them warmly. She is a matriarchal halfling woman who shows them to a private suite on the second floor and reminds them not to draw attention to themselves. They are on their own until the following day.
- **The neighboring Wayfinder team:** The suite next door contains another Wayfinder crew on a separate recovery mission. Neeta does not identify them to the party. A moderately high DC Perception check can pick them out as outsiders; an Insight check during shared mealtimes can reveal the same. These two checks should be offered to different PCs. If the party identifies and carefully approaches the team, their leader — **Malachai Ironshear**, a male dwarf — presents himself to them in their rooms and explains his mission. His team is the rescue that Warebock and the Dawnflower are waiting for before returning to the Isles.
- **Pulara:** At lunchtime the following day, a tiefling woman named **Pulara** approaches the party's table and begins asking casual questions in the manner of a curious local — this is an interview. She is determining whether they can be trusted. If they pass, she presents herself in their rooms shortly after lunch. If they fail, she waits another day to observe them further. If they draw attention to themselves, she continues to wait until she is satisfied. She can also be reached through Neeta, who will pass messages — but Pulara is the final arbiter of when it is safe to approach. Prior positive contact with Malachai, Warebock, or Kaman counts in the party's favor.
- **Pulara's examination:** When she does contact them, Pulara asks a series of probing questions about their cover identities. The stronger their answers, the more satisfied she is. Poor answers earn a hard dressing-down. She returns a day later with tickets for an **airship** from Daliketh to the capital **Balnor**, deep in the interior. Alongside the tickets she gives them a **sealed envelope** — she instructs them not to open it until they are aboard the airship and well away from Daliketh, and to destroy it immediately after reading. The envelope contains details of how to reach their contact in Balnor and an overview of the situation they will find there. The airship departs just after dawn, two days hence. She tells them to lay low until then.

*Interlude — The Failed Mission:*
- Late the following evening, there is a disturbance in the boarding house — slamming doors, raised voices. The suite next door. If the party investigates, they find two heavily injured people being helped inside.
- If the party already met Malachai, he waves them in. If not, he tells them to mind their own business — but the party gets one final chance to identify his crew as Archipelago by a high-DC Perception check. Failure means the incident resolves without the party's involvement.
- **Malachai's situation:** His team was ambushed by a unit of **Advocates** — a special unit of the Ministry of Justice tasked with hunting rebels and practitioners of the supernatural. Standard Wayfinder protocol keeps rescues unaware of the Archipelago until the moment of departure; rescues are told they are being helped by rebel elements. In this case, two of his team members and the rescue they were extracting have been captured. He has no time to contact Kaman for authorization and no reinforcements to call on. He asks the party to join him and his one uninjured operative — **Renata**, a human woman — in a covert extraction from the **Advocates' Tower**.

*The Advocates' Tower — Five Stages:*

The DM should track failures across all five stages. The total failure count determines the aftermath.

**Stage 1 — Getting to the Tower**
The authorities are on elevated alert at the Advocates' request, and the party will encounter a group of constables on the way. A high-DC Persuasion, Intimidation, or Deception check bypasses the encounter. On failure, the constables attempt to arrest the party. Combat: an equal number of constables to party members (including Malachai and Renata), using the **CR 2 Scout** stat block. Malachai gives a stern warning: no killing. The party must defeat the constables within 3 rounds or reinforcements arrive (1d4 additional constables per 3 rounds). Constables who engage in combat blow their whistles, drawing public attention. Alternatively, the party may attempt to flee — resolve via three skill checks of escalating difficulty.
- *Success:* Pass the constables without triggering combat.
- *Failure:* Combat engaged. Counts double if any constables are killed.

**Stage 2 — Infiltrating the Tower**
Renata knows a covert entry point: a service entrance to the tower's basement that she has had under surveillance for years. The party must make a high-DC Stealth check to cross the grounds undetected. Failure triggers a constable encounter as in Stage 1.
- *Success:* Cross the yard without being noticed.
- *Failure:* Forced to fight constables.

**Stage 3 — The Service Entrance**
The entrance is locked and trapped. A Perception check notices the alarm; two Sleight of Hand checks are required — one to pick the lock, one to disable the alarm (which rings a bell in the garrison if triggered).
- *Success:* Lock picked and alarm disarmed.
- *Failure:* Alarm triggered. No immediate consequence if the party didn't notice it — but they may be surprised when the garrison investigates.

**Stage 4 — The Cells**
No guard is posted in the cell block. Both of Malachai's captured operatives and the rescue are held here. The party needs: a Stealth check to avoid waking other prisoners; a Persuasion or Intimidation check to keep the rescuees quiet while someone works on the lock; and a Sleight of Hand check to force the cell lock. If the party knows the alarm was triggered (Stage 3 failure), they can post a watch for the garrison descending the stairs. Guards use the **CR 1 Spy** stat block. A guard dealt with in the first round does not sound the alarm, giving time to escape; otherwise, they retreat upstairs and return with reinforcements one round later. If the lock is not picked within three rounds, a guard on rounds appears at the top of the stairs. The other prisoners may be released as a distraction — they will attempt to follow the party out and, if they spot the party, will call out as the PCs flee.
- *Success:* Prisoners extracted without alerting the garrison.
- *Failure:* Garrison alerted and engaged.

**Stage 5 — Flight Across the City**
Malachai will not lead pursuit back to the Garden House (protecting Neeta and his wounded operatives) or to Mahakam Island (too exposed). He uses his sending stone to alert Kaman and request a boat at the docks. The party must pass three encounters crossing the city: a Stealth check past a constable patrol, a Persuasion check past a group of curious citizens, and a Deception check through a checkpoint at the docks. Failure on any check triggers a constable encounter as in Stage 1. If the extra prisoners tagged along, all checks are made with disadvantage. Malachai will ask the party to deal with them — shedding them requires a high-DC Persuasion, Deception, or Intimidation check.
- *Success:* All three encounters passed without triggering a response.
- *Failure:* One or more checks failed.

Takes place at Kaman's shipyard on Mahakam Island. - **Malachai** takes personal responsibility for any failure, regardless of the circumstances. The party's reputation among the Wayfinders will be negatively affected for the time being by whatever heat they brought.

### Act III: Into the West (Levels 8–12)
**Summary:** The party is inside the Unity — a society that has outlawed everything they represent. They must travel across the interior of the western continent, learning its geography and social fabric while remaining undiscovered, to reach the northern coastal region where the Cabal holds Huma captive. Along the way, they may make contact with the belief-spirits of the Unity — the ideals its citizens hold with something approaching religious fervor — and with the reformers within the Unity's own institutions who can be reached. The act ends with the freeing of Huma and the party departing the Unity, knowing what is at stake in a way they didn't before.

*The Journey to Balnor:*
- **0–1 failures (airship, no complications):** Tickets stamped with little ceremony; a porter leads the party aboard. Three-day journey. Communal quarters and meals, plus a lounge, a restaurant, and a magnificent observation deck. If they follow the rules and keep their heads down, the journey passes without incident.
- **2 failures (airship, mild scrutiny):** Party undergoes mild scrutiny at boarding — their documents are excellent and their tickets are real. A few simple questions; no real danger.
- **3 failures (airship, interview):** The party is pulled aside at the dock for an extensive interview by an **airship corps inspector**. This requires two moderate-DC Deception checks. Failure means the inspector attempts to detain them for further questioning — the party may attempt to flee or fight their way out. If they fail to board, they go to ground until contacted by **Pulara**, who will arrange to smuggle them out of Daliketh aboard a courier ship to **Krasno Bay** for the overland crossing (see below).
- **4 failures (overland from Krasno Bay):** The airship is not an option. The party takes the Dawnflower north to Krasno Bay and proceeds overland (see below).
- **Pulara — if encountered again:** If the party runs into Pulara before departure, she thanks them for the rescue mission and/or admonishes them for nearly getting caught. If they were intercepted at the dock and she is smuggling them out, she arranges the courier ship to Krasno Bay and provides new contact details for Balnor; otherwise she issues replacement airship tickets and they proceed as on the three-failure track.
- **The sealed envelope:** On the airship, the party should open the envelope from Pulara. It contains the contact information for **Giacomo Hearthbane**, their Balnor handler, and an overview of the situation in the city. Pulara's instructions were to read it once and destroy it immediately.

*Interlude — The Overland Route (Krasno Bay to Balnor):*
The party must first determine how to make the journey. **Kastel** or **Pulara** can lay out their options. The best option is to link up with a bi-monthly trade caravan and hire on as laborers or guards. They can also travel independently, though travelers on foot can draw suspicion at Unity towns and villages along the way. Either option takes approximately one week. There are three major encounters:

**1. The Hanging.** Several rebels are being hanged in a town square early one morning as the party is preparing to leave. The townspeople have been talking about the captures the night before. The execution is carried out by the regular army (several soldiers wear bandages) and overseen by an Advocates officer. A local man can be seen begging the soldiers to spare his son before the hanging proceeds.

**2. The Rebels.** Midway through the journey, the party encounters rebel outriders.
- *If traveling with a caravan:* The rebels shadow the group for a few hours before the caravan master sends a security detail to chase them off quickly — the caravan is far too large a target to swallow.
- *If traveling alone:* The party is surrounded. They may fight, surrender, or negotiate (high-DC Persuasion). Twelve outriders; use the CR 1/2 **Thug** stat block, substituting a single-shot pistol for the crossbow and a rapier for the mace. If the party surrenders or convinces the outriders to negotiate, they are brought to the rebel camp and meet the leader: **Esko Voss**, a human male. Voss interrogates them closely. They may attempt to convince him they are rebels (Deception) or tell him the truth about their mission — in either case, success earns release; failure means he holds them pending orders from above. After two days of captivity (if they have not escaped), a regular army unit destroys the camp and rounds up survivors. The lieutenant examines the party's documents and lets them go.

**3. The Roadblock.** About a day from Balnor, the party encounters a military roadblock.
- *If traveling with a caravan:* The party passes through without trouble, though they notice a suspiciously large number of civilians being held in a nearby open-air cell.
- *If traveling alone:* Significant scrutiny. The party may attempt to bypass the roadblock (high-DC Perception to spot it before being spotted themselves; moderate-DC Stealth to slip past the sentries) or pass through it. The sergeant manning the post will ask pointed questions — particularly why anyone traveling toward Balnor is departing from Krasno Bay. A convincing cover story and a successful Persuasion or Deception check vs. his +12 Insight is required to pass. Failure: the army attempts to arrest them. Fight or submit. Eight soldiers (CR 1 **Spy** stat block); the sergeant (CR 3 **Veteran**).

*Arrival at Balnor:*
- **By land:** The party must seek out Giacomo independently. Pulara's note identifies him as a senior manager at the **Unity Bank**. The DM should play this encounter carefully — Giacomo is cautious and must be approached in the right way.
- **By airship:** Giacomo meets the party at the dock himself, a private carriage (on loan from the bank) ready to take them to his home.
- **Giacomo Hearthbane** is a halfling banker — a native of the Isles who has lived in the Unity for over forty years. He is married, has nine children, and is a recognized pillar of his community. He is in a state of semi-retirement; the Wayfinders have not operated actively out of Balnor for years following an intelligence failure that nearly exposed the network (he had no part in it, and will make this clear). He hosts the party because Pulara asked him to and because he trusts her judgment implicitly.
- Giacomo explains the situation: his wife **Bedelia** does not know about his clandestine work, but she does know he periodically hosts old friends — she is aware of the party's arrival if they came by airship. He asks the party to participate in normal neighborhood social life to avoid raising her or his neighbors' suspicions.
- The party will need to stay three days until the next northbound train is available. His children are away at school for the first two days and return the day before departure.
- He will purchase their train tickets himself that afternoon.
- He is direct: he is providing cover as a favor to Pulara, not out of active commitment to the cause. If the party exposes him, he will disavow them and turn them over to the authorities. He says this without malice, as a matter of fact. He means it.

*Life in Balnor:*
The capital is everything the Unity is, writ large. Great monuments and public artworks celebrate the people of the Unity and their struggles against the supernatural. The cobbled streets are ramrod straight and uniformly clean; the city is as full of tranquil public parks as it is rowdy public squares where citizens enjoy each other's company with open civic pleasure. People are well but simply dressed. Misery, unemployment, and the downtrodden cannot be openly seen. The army is a much more visible presence here than anywhere else the party has been — military airships fly high overhead, and army units in parade uniforms march the streets to the beat of raucous citizen volunteer brass bands that trail in their wake.

During their stay the party will face multiple social encounters. These will illuminate the fabric of daily life in the capital but also allow players to see more clearly the cracks running beneath the surface. Balnor's encounters should feel distinctly different from Daliketh's — the military, administrative, and cultural weight of the capital is present everywhere, and what is merely strange in a port city becomes harder and more specifically wrong here to anyone raised on the Isles.

*Interlude — The Device:*
If the party accepted the device from the Eightfold Path and places it anywhere in or around Balnor, the consequences could be severe. The only exception: if they approached by land and placed it somewhere outside the city's suburbs before arriving.

The device is a complex magical intelligence-gathering mechanism representing a significant investment by the Path. It deploys one thousand invisible magical probes across the surrounding area. The probes locate sites of social importance — anywhere people gather — and record everything they find: conversations, documents, individuals, objects. After one hour, the probes return and the gathered information is compressed into a ball of energy that travels to a receiver on the Isles. The device then self-destructs. The Path's wizards, confident in their own superiority, have no idea the Unity can track and identify the nature of foreign magical workings. If the device was placed in the city, the self-destruction will coincide almost exactly with the moment Advocates investigators arrive at its location — and one of their officers will be killed.

- **Fallout by placement:** The device emits a large, visible ball of light when its work is complete; this light travels noticeably to the southeast before fading.
  - *In or near Giacomo's home:* The Advocates trace it directly to the address. Giacomo and Bedelia are arrested. The party's descriptions are immediately disseminated and the city goes to lockdown searching for them.
  - *Elsewhere in the city:* Within hours, the Advocates publish a statement describing an unknown type of magical attack which they believe is connected to the rebellion. News travels fast. Any outsiders who seem out of place will immediately draw suspicion from citizens and the constabulary alike.
  - *Outside the suburbs (overland route only):* The ball of light is visible from the city walls but the source cannot be easily pinpointed. The Advocates investigate but the party is not directly implicated.

- **Fallout by timing:**
  - *Day 1 or 2 placement:* Giacomo summons the party to his office within hours, or takes them for a walk if it is after business hours. (The "going for a walk" approach also applies on day 3, when his children are present.) He produces a newspaper bearing a headline about the "attack" and asks bluntly whether the party was involved. A bluff requires a Deception check opposed by his +15 Insight; if he believes they are lying, he throws them out and tells them not to return. If they are honest, he expresses exasperation at "those meddling idiot wizards," thanks them for it, and tells them to lay low. He arranges for them to move to a boarding house near the main train station and admonishes them to explain this to Bedelia with extreme care if he happens to be at work at the time — a high-DC Deception or Persuasion check will suffice.
  - *Day 1 placement only:* This gives the Advocates enough time to track down potential outsiders in the city. The party is approached at their boarding house by **Inspector Racewind Thrumblecastle**, a no-nonsense gnome, who has a number of pointed questions for them. Failure to satisfy him (Deception opposed by his +10 Insight) causes him to call upon a waiting unit of guardsmen to arrest them — resistance results in a fight. Four guardsmen (CR 3 **Veteran** stat block); Thrumblecastle is a Level 5 Rogue.
  - *Day 3 placement:* The fallout occurs after the party has already departed, sparing them the immediate consequences.

- **General lockdown:** Any confrontation with the Advocates in Balnor results in a general lockdown. Giacomo will be imprisoned or unwilling to help further; the party's descriptions will be widely circulated. They may encounter wanted posters of themselves depending on timing. This forces the party to find alternative transport out of the city.

*Interlude — The Unity Congress:*
The Unity Congress is the name by which the belief-spirits of the Unity are collectively known — though the spirits themselves did not choose the name and are largely unaware of one another as a formal body. They are the spiritual embodiments of the ideals the Unity's citizens hold with something approaching religious fervor. They are extremely powerful spirits, but unlike most belief-spirits they are driven more by their believers than by any independent will of their own. Their believers do not worship them, do not know they exist, and would find the concept alarming. For the most part, the Congress spirits are similarly confused about their own nature.

The exception is **Placida**, the spirit of the Common Good, who exists among the peoples of the Archipelago as well as the Unity. This dual presence gives her a far stronger and more coherent sense of self than any of her Congress counterparts. She appears as a smiling, matronly woman with glowing blonde hair. She has tentatively allied herself with Revna, believing that the spirit of rebellion needs a greater presence among the people of the Unity before true public good can be achieved. She is, however, unaware of Revna's plan to free her captive uncle, or of the party's role in that scheme.

*The Congress Interlude — The Crossing:*
When the party performs the Communing ritual for Placida, the portal opens as usual. This crossing takes the form of a community feast. The party steps through into a busy city square, where people are filing into a grand civic hall. The contrast with earlier crossings is immediate: the colors are much brighter, the scene is full of people who seem fully real and can be engaged in conversation.

The feasthall is packed; the party must wait in line before entering. Fellow guests share stories of past feasts and express excitement about this one. Inside, the party may join the buffet line — the fare is simple but the atmosphere is joyous, the space large and open without ostentation, and genuine warmth moves through it. Ad hoc entertainments break out throughout the hall.

**Resolution:** The party must place their gift on a silver tray marked "donations." They cannot find the tray on their own — they must ask one of the attendees, any of whom can point it out. They may use the scroll provided by Revna (so long as it remains unopened) as their gift, or substitute one of their own. When an appropriate gift is placed, it disappears. Nothing else happens until the party finds seats.

Once seated, a kind older woman in a simple white robe appears in an empty chair at their table and asks if they are enjoying the party — this is **Placida**. She is somewhat surprised to see them.
- If they did not offer Revna's scroll, they must explain themselves.
- If they did, the scroll's contents serve as an introduction.

She explains their timing is good: "the principles" are about to convene for a discussion. She asks them to find seats among the spectators when they arrive and not draw attention to themselves.

**If the party mentions Revna's uncle** (note: Revna specifically asked them not to): Placida is immediately taken aback and asks what they know. She cannot be deceived — but the party may choose how much to reveal. If pressed, Placida will tell them plainly: *"He is War. To set him loose will inevitably lead to conflict."*

If the party does not tip their hand, Placida leads them through a door at the back of the hall. They pass through into another space.

*The Theatre:*
The space Placida leads them into is not her domain — it is **Praefecta's**. A vast open-air theatre set in a bowl-shaped depression among a beautiful pine forest. The scale evokes grandeur without ostentation. The sky above is a perfect summer afternoon — too perfect to be real. At the center of the stage, a large polished stone table sits atop a marble dais surrounded by eleven chairs. Congress spirits are taking their seats as the party arrives; spectators fill the stands above.

Behind the table on the dais is a great wall of cells. The majority of the captive spirits within are insubstantial or growing that way — they stare blankly at the Congress and the audience. One spirit on the lowest level remains fully formed: a great bear of a man wearing fine chainmail, close-cropped dark beard, long hair, face permanently flushed — not exertion, but complexion. He paces his cell and stares directly at the party the moment they are seated.

Ten Congress spirits are present (Praefecta is absent). Mnema takes notes.

*The Discussion:*
The Congress does not make decisions — it discusses the issues of the day, and the responses of each spirit are formed from the opinions of their believers. Placida, Novus, and Lexan have more autonomy than the others; Ratia and Sentia are close behind — these are spirits that exist with some substance outside the Unity, in the Archipelago and the Sacred Lands. All of them are careful not to upset the balance. The audience responds to each speaker with applause, boos, or other commentary calibrated to that spirit's relative power.

Three items are discussed:

*Maritas:*
Once the Custodes leave, the figure in the cell calls out: *"It's safe to come out now, little rabbits."*

He introduces himself as **Maritas, the spirit of War** — ancient in a way that exceeds any other belief-spirit the party has encountered. The party now faces one of the most consequential decisions of the campaign.

**His case for freedom:**
- War has been present throughout the history of mortal life. Without him, the world has fallen into stasis.
- The Old Gods who wish mortals destroyed cannot be withstood without him.
- The rebellion within the Unity is toothless. The party may be formidable, but they are vastly outnumbered.
- The Unity has ended history, erased true freedom, and is destroying the physical world to sustain itself — the party just heard this from the spirits' own mouths.
- His freedom gives a fighting chance to all who oppose the Unity: the party, the Archipelago, the Sacred Lands, and those within the Unity who refuse to be smothered by conformity.

*Jindark:*
Jindark is a small city on the northern coast, built into the hillsides that descend from the high ridge the party passed over, all the way down to the port district at sea level — roughly 800 feet of elevation. Most of the slopes are gentle; some are stark in a way that makes outsiders uneasy. Most buildings are constructed directly into the hillsides, their lower levels held up by pillars or stilts. The harbor is enclosed by a massive artificial breakwater: a solid central mass with a shipping channel at either end, each overseen by a lighthouse. The top of the breakwater serves as a public promenade — one of Jindark's most distinctive civic features, wide enough for a market on one side and a walking path on the other, with views of the harbor on one face and the open sea on the other. The city is both highly industrial and unexpectedly picturesque.

No checkpoints await the party at the station. Nor do any contacts — they must arrange their own accommodations and begin their investigation without guidance.

Jindark has an intensely industrial character. Ores and minerals flow down from the nearby mountains and hills, feeding the city's many forges and manufacturing concerns. As the party descends from the ridge, they will notice a large airship terminal under construction near the ridgeline, and the train depot is strikingly busy for a city of this size. The city's character resembles Daliketh more than Balnor — the Unity here is at its most productive and confident. The oppressive undercurrent felt in Balnor and the simmering unrest of Karale are both absent. Social encounters in Jindark should reflect this: the Unity working as advertised, citizens who believe in it and have reason to. *(Jindark encounter table to be written — this table should be longer than Daliketh or Balnor, as the party will likely spend more time here than anywhere else.)*

*Huma's Captivity — The Breakwater:*
The artificial breakwater enclosing Jindark harbor is the cage. The Cabal designed it from the outset to contain Huma, who was lured into the harbor when it was still an open bay. The two lighthouses at either end of the shipping channels are not navigation aids — they are containment projectors. The two channels are the one gap in the lattice the Cabal left, carefully calibrated to allow ships through without breaching it.

The harbor is Huma's prison. Citizens walk the promenade, fish from it, sell goods from it. Nobody knows.

**Power extraction:** Pressure differential turbines built into the breakwater's submerged foundations harvest the energy of Huma's constant motion, converting it into electrical current — a phenomenon unknown to anyone outside the Cabal, and the secret behind the Jakor region's anomalous productivity. The front company maintains the turbines and the projector mechanisms as routine harbor infrastructure; their workers have no knowledge of what the system actually does and no access to the chamber below.

**Physical access:** A submerged maintenance tunnel beneath the breakwater's central mass leads to a pressurized chamber at the harbor floor. Access is reserved exclusively for the highest-ranking members of the Cabal. The Summoning ritual performed in this chamber reaches Huma — he is physically present and can hear and respond — but he is deeply confused about the geometry of his prison. He knows he is surrounded on all sides. He does not know by what.

**Freeing him — sliding scale:** The breakwater's design creates a legible spectrum of consequences:
- *Disable the projectors first:* The containment lattice collapses; Huma recovers his senses before anything structural is disturbed. He can then be spoken with clearly about what comes next. This is the only approach that gives the party meaningful agency over what follows.
- *Open the breakwater in controlled sections:* With the projectors already down, Huma can be guided out through a deliberate breach. The harbor sustains significant damage; the port district floods; the city's shipping capacity is severely disrupted. Manageable, eventually.
- *Full simultaneous destruction:* Projectors and breakwater demolished at once. A panicking, disoriented Old God erupts directly into the harbor with no warning. Total destruction of the port district; a tidal event affecting the entire northern coast; Jindark's economy destroyed. The party should understand before they act that the breakwater is not only Huma's cage — it is also the city's harbor, and everyone in Jindark's lower districts lives and works in the shadow of what they are about to do.

*Note: The large installation under construction near the ridgeline — which the party will have noticed on arrival and which is publicly described as an airship terminal — is a Cabal facility related to the monitoring and maintenance of the containment system. Its true purpose is TBD.*

**The Investigation:**
The party has no contacts in Jindark and no map to follow. They must identify leads, cultivate sources, and piece together a picture of what the breakwater actually is. Four factions or individuals can help them, each offering a different angle on the same truth. The party may find one, two, or all four. They are not expected to follow a prescribed sequence.

*The Criminal Underground*

Jindark is the only city in the Unity with a meaningful criminal underworld. The Unity's enforced order has suppressed organized crime everywhere else, but Jindark's explosive, chaotic growth over the past five years created gaps the law hasn't had time to close. The people running the organization are career criminals — not displaced workers, not idealists. They use the city's labor casualties as functionaries and foot soldiers. The party will never meet the principals.

The network has flagged the breakwater's restricted sections as professionally intolerable — territory within their harbor that they don't control and can't read. They have also, independently, made a connection that no one else in Jindark has: power conduits run from the breakwater's submerged foundations into the city's electrical grid. They don't know what this means. They know it is significant.

What they will offer, in exchange for a job done: harbor access through their dock worker contacts, Cabal operative rotation patterns for the southern section, and what they know about the conduits. A job means something that serves the network's interests — lifting something from a Cabal shipment, creating a distraction for an operation, dealing with someone who has been talking to the wrong people. They are transactional. They do not care why the party wants access.

*First contact:* The party will notice they are being followed — the tails are not skilled enough to avoid detection by an attentive group. Tracing the tails back leads to a private social club near the docks. If the party manages to get inside — through bluff, social engineering, or a convincing enough cover — one of the network's face people will approach them. The party has been flagged as outsiders. The question is what they're here for.

*Long-term:* The party carries an obligation. If the breakwater is damaged or destroyed when Huma is freed, harbor operations collapse — which hurts the network's livelihood. They may feel double-crossed, depending on how the party handled the relationship.

*The Rebellion*

The rebellion has established a small but capable network in Jindark, exploiting the same chaotic growth conditions that enabled the criminal underground. The handler is most likely a former trade union organizer — collective action having been illegal under the Unity for decades — now running a quiet intelligence operation under a legitimate cover. The network's size and confidence are conditional: if the party freed Maritas earlier in Act III, this network is actively strengthening, receiving resources and contacts it did not have before. If Maritas remains imprisoned, they are smaller, more cautious, and offer less.

What they have is partial but valuable: months of surveillance on Cabal operatives, partial movement maps, and the knowledge that operatives enter the southern lighthouse section and don't exit through the same door. They cannot complete the picture on their own. The party can.

What they want: a piece of hard evidence from inside the Cabal's operation, help protecting a threatened contact, or assistance recruiting someone they have identified as sympathetic. The stronger they are, the more they ask for.

*First contact:* A symbol or phrase the party may recognize from their time with the rebellion in the west — left at their lodgings, marked somewhere the party passes regularly, or murmured briefly on a busy street. If the party has had no prior contact with the western rebellion, the approach is slower: an anonymous summons to a location, with enough identifying detail to indicate the sender knows who they are.

*Long-term:* The party's entanglement with the rebellion deepens. Jindark becomes a more significant rebel node — especially if Maritas was freed. Future Unity encounters carry additional risk if this association surfaces.

*Cross-path note (Criminal Underground):* The criminal network is aware the rebellion operates in Jindark and views them as a liability — people asking political questions attract official attention, which is bad for business. If the party is visibly associated with both, the criminals will become more guarded. Kept separate, the two sources are complementary: the criminals have physical access knowledge; the rebellion has Cabal personnel surveillance. Together they can triangulate operative movements and give the party both the when and the where.

*The Geologist*

A Unity academic — a natural philosopher specializing in geology and fluid dynamics — has been posted to Jindark to investigate anomalous seabed behavior around the breakwater. Thermal readings that shouldn't exist. Current disruption patterns that don't follow the tides. Seismic signatures the engineering commission cannot explain. She has filed multiple reports. They have been quietly buried. She does not know why, and the confusion has begun to harden into suspicion.

She is a Unity loyalist. She trusts the system. She is not looking for trouble. She is looking for an explanation, and the system is refusing to give her one.

What she has: thermal maps, seismic readings, and current disruption data that — interpreted correctly — outline the shape and position of something enormous within the breakwater's core. She reads it as a geological anomaly. The party can see it differently. She also knows exactly where the restricted zone boundary is and precisely when it is guarded; she has been turned away from it multiple times and has noted every detail of those encounters.

What she wants: access to the restricted zone to take readings she cannot take from the promenade. She cannot get authorization through official channels. She is not asking the party to break the law. She is asking them to help her do her job.

*First contact:* She is visible — she works on the promenade with portable instruments, part of the scenery. The party may observe her being turned away from the restricted section by a harbor official; a small, routine confrontation that reveals both her purpose and that the restrictions exist. She is easy to approach, and an initial conversation costs nothing.

*Long-term:* When Huma is freed, she must grapple with a reality that overturns her understanding of the world. A Unity true believer who may emerge as something else. Her documented data — thermal maps, seismic readings, the suppressed reports — could become significant evidence in the aftermath if properly circulated.

*Cross-path note (Harbormaster's Office):* The geologist and the harbormaster are natural allies who have not yet found each other. Both are Unity loyalists trying to do their jobs and being obstructed by forces they can't identify. If the party connects them — carefully — they may begin sharing information without the party needing to broker every exchange. The geologist's scientific data and the harbormaster's institutional records together paint a nearly complete picture of what the Cabal has built. This is the most powerful combination available to a party that wants to operate entirely in the open.

*The Harbormaster's Office*

The civil service apparatus nominally responsible for the Jindark harbor — berthing assignments, cargo manifests, navigational records, maintenance oversight — has been operating with a gap in its authority for the past several years. A section of the southern breakwater was placed under central authority jurisdiction. The harbormaster was not consulted. Officials she has never seen presented credentials she cannot verify. A complaint was filed. It went nowhere. She is a career administrator — a decade in this post — and she is professionally furious.

She does not know the Cabal by name. She does not know that what has been built beneath the breakwater is a prison for a living being. She knows that part of her harbor has been taken from her without explanation, and that the Unity's processes for handling this have failed her.

What her office holds: procurement documents from the breakwater's construction that don't match any standard engineering specification on record; maintenance logs with redacted sections; the names of the officials who presented the credentials, which the party may be able to trace; and — most valuable — a complete pre-construction survey of the breakwater's foundations, including the submerged access tunnel, from before the Cabal's involvement. She has no idea what those access points are for.

What she wants: for someone to take this seriously and do something about it. She is not asking the party for help, exactly. She is venting to people who are asking the right questions and who appear capable of acting on the answers.

*First contact:* The party has a legitimate reason to visit the harbormaster's office — inquiring about harbor operations, freight routes, or the breakwater's construction history. The harbormaster is visibly short-tempered on the subject of the southern section. Her deputy is more forthcoming in private.

*Long-term:* When Huma is freed and the breakwater is breached, her harbor is in chaos — but she has the records that explain why it happened. She may choose to use them. She could become an unexpected institutional voice for accountability in the aftermath.

*Cross-path note (Rebellion):* The harbormaster is a Unity loyalist. If she discovers the party is also working with the rebellion, she will either withdraw from the relationship or — more dangerously — feel obligated to report what she knows. The two paths are best kept separate. Used carefully, they are the most comprehensive combination available: the rebellion has Cabal operative patterns, the harbormaster has institutional records. Kept in separate compartments, they give the party both the people and the paper trail without either source knowing the other exists.

**The Strike on Huma's Prison:**

*Contact contributions*

Each contact the party has cultivated provides an operational advantage when the strike begins. These advantages are DM-facing; the party should not be told they are accruing a score. What they will feel is the difference between a well-prepared operation and one conducted in the dark.

| Contact | Approach | Strike | Without them |
|---|---|---|---|
| Criminal Underground | Dock worker cover; maintenance access to restricted zone; patrol patterns for Cabal operatives | Coordinated distraction drawing Cabal attention during the window | No legitimate access to the restricted zone |
| Rebellion | Optimal timing window — when Cabal staffing in the southern section is at its lowest | Perimeter watch; slows Unity emergency response | Party must choose their own timing blind |
| Geologist | Identifies alternate entry: the outer seabed maintenance hatch (visible in her structural survey data) | Can confirm projector locations from observable external features | Alternate entry unknown; party limited to the guarded interior route |
| Harbormaster | Pre-construction tunnel maps; location of the dry-access shaft in the interior mechanical spaces | Can delay official harbor response with bureaucratic obstruction | No map; party navigates the interior blind |

The formula is additive and DM-managed: zero contacts means the operation is conducted at the worst possible conditions — full Cabal presence, no map, no timing intelligence, all prepared hazards active. Each contact removes one major disadvantage. With three or four contacts, some prepared hazards are preempted, the Cabal's response is delayed, and the party enters with a working understanding of the layout.

*The breakwater interior — three zones*

**Zone 1: The Surface.** The promenade and lighthouse exteriors. The containment projectors are at the base of each lighthouse, disguised as maintenance equipment — not at the top. Both must be disabled to collapse the containment lattice. Disabling one without the other weakens the lattice and triggers an immediate Cabal alert.

The core puzzle of the surface zone: the party must disable both projectors simultaneously. Options are to split the group (risk: two separated fights at the same moment), or to find and use the master control panel in the deep chamber, which shuts both remotely. The remote option is cleaner but requires reaching the deep zone first.

**Zone 2: The Interior.** Below the promenade, the breakwater is an engineered structure: service corridors, turbine maintenance spaces, Cabal guard posts, and the Cabal's operational monitoring station. This is the dungeon crawl section.

The active turbines fill the central mass. They are enormous, loud, and hazardous to anyone who enters the maintenance passages directly. The noise makes stealth harder throughout the interior but provides some cover for movement. Guard posts are staffed by Cabal operatives dressed as harbor maintenance workers; their training is genuine. Deeper in, the operatives are senior.

The Cabal monitoring station tracks the containment system in real time. It contains operational notes — both a plot item (explains how the system works in enough detail to disable it correctly) and a story item (confirms in the Cabal's own words what Huma is and what has been done to him). Recovering these notes before reaching the deep zone gives the party meaningful agency over what happens next. Without them, the party must work out the system under pressure in the chamber itself.

**Zone 3: The Deep.** The submerged tunnel beneath the central mass and the pressurized chamber at harbor floor level.

Two entry points:
- *The dry-access shaft* descends from the interior mechanical spaces. It is guarded. No swimming required. This is the route the harbormaster's surveys reveal.
- *The outer seabed hatch* is on the breakwater's harbor face. The geologist's data identified it as a structural anomaly. Reaching it requires navigating approximately 30 meters underwater, working an external hatch mechanism, and entering the tunnel from the sea side — bypassing interior guards entirely. This approach requires either magic capable of providing underwater breathing or a mechanical underwater breathing apparatus. The criminal network or the harbormaster can supply the equipment; alternatively, the party can purchase it independently, though it is expensive, technically specialized, and its purchase may draw attention from the authorities.

The pressurized chamber is kept dry by an active airlock-style entry. The mechanism must be understood and operated correctly to enter without incident. The Cabal can also initiate a partial flood deliberately as a combat measure if the party is inside. The master control panel that governs both lighthouse projectors is here.

*The final combat*

The Cabal prepared this room as a kill zone. They expected that anyone who found their way this deep had the capability to reach the chamber and have anticipated it.

Prepared hazards:
- *Electrical discharge:* The chamber has exposed conduit runs from the turbine system. The Cabal can direct current through sections of the floor and the structural contacts. If the party recovered the operational notes in Zone 2, they can identify and neutralize the discharge points before combat — or use the system against the Cabal themselves.
- *Partial flooding:* The Cabal can initiate a controlled flood, denying sections of the chamber and forcing the party into compressed kill zones. If the party understands the pressure system, they can counter or redirect the flooding.
- *Pressure doors:* The tunnel connections back to the interior can be sealed, splitting the party or cutting off retreat. This creates a clock element if party members are separated.
- *Turbine spillover:* Combat can reach the turbine maintenance passages. Entering these spaces is a significant environmental hazard — not an instant kill, but a dangerous one.

**Who the party fights:** Senior Cabal operatives — 3 to 4 individuals, not the regional leader, who is at the Citadel. At minimum one arcane operative, one martial, one tactical. At least one named individual who can speak. In defeat or capture, this person confirms the Citadel's existence and reveals that the Cabal's intent was never to kill the party in this room — they are more useful as interrogatees, and the Citadel is where that would happen. This is the bridge to the optional section that follows.

**Outcomes:**

*Activating contacts*

When the party is ready to move, each contact is activated differently. Contacts cannot be held in reserve indefinitely — if the party delays too long after establishing a relationship, circumstances may shift.

- **Criminal Underground:** The party returns to the social club and states they are ready. The face person will arrange the specifics.
- **Rebellion:** The party passes a prearranged signal through the contact channel established at first meeting.
- **Geologist:** The party asks her to provide her survey data formally, or to accompany them. She will ask what they intend to do with it. She will not lie to herself about the answer if the party is honest.
- **Harbormaster:** The party presents her with enough of what they know that she opens her records willingly. At that point she is committed, whether or not she fully intends to be.

*Contact with Huma*

**Before the projectors are disabled**, the party can attempt the Summoning ritual in the pressurized chamber. Huma is present and can respond — he is physically there — but he is deeply confused. He knows he is surrounded. He knows something is holding him. He does not understand the geometry of his prison. He does not know how long he has been here. Communication is fragmented and disorienting for both parties. This contact is primarily atmospheric: confirmation that the party has found the right place, and a first glimpse of what years of captivity have done to a being of his age and nature.

**After the projectors are disabled**, Huma's confusion clears. He becomes coherent. He is still in the harbor — freedom for him means the breach, not merely the lattice dropping — but he can think clearly for the first time in years. He speaks through the structure itself: vibration in the stone, resonance in the water, a pressure in the air of the chamber that forms into something like language.

Key moments in this conversation:
- He was lured. He knows this now. *"I was called. I should have known what kind of call it was."*
- He has been aware of the Old Gods' deliberation, distantly, through the prison. He asks what has happened in the world — his knowledge is years out of date.
- He asks what the party wants him to do. He will not act until he understands the situation. If the party has been careful and communicates clearly, he will cooperate with a managed breach. If the situation is chaotic and the party cannot explain themselves coherently, he is frightened — and a frightened Old God is dangerous regardless of intent.
- His regard for the party is earned here, not given. This is the beginning of a relationship that will matter in Act V.

**Attack on the Citadel (Optional):**

The ridgeline installation the party noticed on arrival — publicly described as an airship terminal under construction — is the Citadel: the Cabal's regional operations base for the Jindark operation. The defeated operatives in the pressurized chamber will confirm its existence and its purpose. The Cabal's intent to bring the party there, rather than kill them, tells the party that the Citadel is a place where things happen — including interrogation.

The party is not required to attack the Citadel. Freeing Huma ends the imprisonment and the Cabal's energy extraction operation regardless. But attacking the Citadel offers three things the prison strike does not: a permanent break on Cabal operations in the region, vital intelligence about their broader network and future plans, and a sense of closure — the organization responsible for Huma's captivity has been dealt a full accounting, not merely interrupted.

*The Citadel exterior*

Construction cover is nearly complete but not fully convincing at close range. Legitimate workers are present on the surface. The operational areas are accessible only through disguised entry points within the construction zone. By the time the party arrives — whether on the same day as the harbor event or on Day 2, the last viable window before the lockdown reaches the ridgeline — Cabal security is on elevated alert.

*The Citadel interior — three areas*

**Area 1: The Surface Construction.** Navigating past or through legitimate workers and Cabal security. Finding the entry points to the operational sections is itself a puzzle — the construction cover is designed to conceal them. Workers are not threats but can raise alarms.

**Area 2: Operational Level.** Communications equipment, monitoring stations — now disrupted and chaotic following events at the harbor — personnel quarters, and potentially detention cells if any party members were captured during the prison strike. Standard dungeon crawl with operatives on full alert. The intelligence recoverable here: operational communications with Balnor, the Cabal's list of future capture targets (additional Old Gods), technical specifications for the containment system that could identify similar installations elsewhere, and the identity and position of Circe if not yet known.

The archive in Area 3 contains, among other materials: notes in Circe's own hand concerning Carnuth's role in the eastern rebellion, and the fact that the Cabal's field operatives are developing a plan to "deal with it permanently." This is the earliest warning the party will have of the Act IV threat. The archive also contains materials the rebellion can use to expose the Cabal's existence and methods to the broader Unity public. The Unity government will respond by officially labeling the materials as forgeries, offering plausible alternative explanations for each document, and classifying public discussion of them as conspiracy. The Cabal's exposure will nonetheless cause significant reputational damage to the Unity, and the Advocates will launch an internal investigation to identify and eliminate Cabal members within their own organization — triggering a shadow war between the two bodies that will run through Act IV.

**Area 3: Archive and Command.** The deepest section. The intelligence repository. The regional leader's office. The final confrontation.

*The final confrontation*

**Circe Lovelace** and 2 to 3 lieutenants. Circe is a short, serious, dark-skinned human woman with close-cropped hair. She is an original founder of the Cabal and a member of its inner circle, classed as an Oath of Devotion Paladin — her devotion is to the Unity itself, elevated to the status of a sacred cause. She has done monstrous things. She knows it. She considers them necessary, and she has made peace with that. She deeply cares for her subordinates; killing any of them in her presence will send her into a cold rage.

This fight is structurally different from the prison fight. The pressure chamber was a kill zone built for intruders. The Citadel command level is the Cabal's home ground — optimized for their operations, not for defending against assault. Circe has a priority and a plan.

The priority is the archive. This creates a **dual-objective combat**: Circe and her lieutenants fight to buy time while systematically destroying archive materials — burning documents, wrecking equipment, eliminating records. This begins at the start of the encounter and continues every round. The party cannot simultaneously fight, protect archive materials, and pursue Circe. They must choose.

Circe, if caught or cornered, will attempt to negotiate. She is a genuine true believer who thinks she can make the party understand why the Cabal exists and why its work must continue. She is not performing conviction — she has it.

**Regardless of how the encounter ends, Circe will enact her escape protocol.** This is a standing order she gave herself years ago, not a contingency. She will not die in this room if she can avoid it, and she has prepared extensively for the possibility of this exact scenario. She escapes. She will be encountered again in Act IV.

*Destruction of the facility*

The Citadel's electrical infrastructure connects to — and once monitored — the breakwater containment system. With that system already disabled or destroyed, the Citadel's connections to it are live, unstable, and potentially catastrophic if deliberately overloaded.

The party can initiate a full destruction by damaging three key nodes in sequence: the main power coupling (in the operational level), the monitoring system hub, and the communications array. The sequence can be triggered immediately (the party must move fast to escape the resulting collapse) or set for a delay (the party has a window, but the delay also triggers the lockdown faster once it fires).

Full destruction eliminates the Cabal's Jindark operation completely and denies them whatever archive the party could not save. It also produces a visible explosion on the ridgeline that is seen from the entire city, compressing the escape window significantly.

**Coda:**

*Huma's freeing as public spectacle*

There is no version of Huma's release that goes unnoticed. The city sees it. Everyone on the promenade sees it. The harbor district sees it.

**Controlled outcome:** The lighthouses go dark simultaneously. The promenade shudders. From the harbor, something moves — a shape that doesn't fit the space it occupies, that fills the harbor and then passes through the channel and is gone into the open sea. Citizens of Jindark have just watched something impossible in broad daylight. The port district has significant flooding. The city is stunned and does not know what it watched.

**Significant breach:** As above, but the breakwater's structure is opened. The flooding is severe — several blocks of the port district are underwater. Huma's passage is longer and more violent. The spectacle lasts. More people are in danger during it, and more people witness what emerges.

**Full destruction:** The breakwater collapses. The harbor becomes open sea. Huma erupts into it in panic and confusion. The port district is destroyed. A tidal surge moves along the northern coast. The sky above where the harbor was is wrong for hours afterward. This is a catastrophe. Its effects will be felt in the world beyond Jindark, and for longer than Act III.

*Post-freeing city timeline*

**Day 1:** Shock. The city cannot function. Harbor operations are suspended in every outcome. The Unity's emergency response apparatus begins to mobilize, but nothing in its experience has prepared it for this. This is the party's largest window for action, movement, or escape.

**Day 2:** Unity response begins in earnest. Military and Advocate units are deployed to the harbor district. Initial lockdown of the affected area. If the Citadel has not yet been attacked, this is the last viable window — the ridgeline will be under observation by the end of the day.

**Day 3–4:** The investigation spreads outward. The lockdown extends. Whoever the Cabal had in the city's administration is now in full crisis mode and calling in every resource. The party is a named priority.

**If the Citadel was also destroyed:** The explosion on the ridge is visible from the entire city. Day 1 is not a window — it is a race. The Advocates begin mobilizing within hours of the harbor event, and the Citadel explosion removes any ambiguity about what they are looking for.

*Escape avenues*

**By sea — Criminal Underground.** If the harbor is navigable — controlled or significant breach outcomes — the criminals have boats and they know the harbor. This is the fastest exit available. Conditions: the party maintained the relationship, and the harbor exists to escape through. Not available in the full destruction outcome.

**By land — Rebellion.** The rebellion has a safe house chain leading out of the city, usable regardless of harbor state. This route takes 2–3 days of moving through the lockdown and requires lying low while the investigation is active. Slower than sea escape; works in all outcome scenarios. Stronger and more reliable if Maritas was freed earlier in Act III.

**Official departure — Harbormaster.** In the controlled outcome, the harbor is still structurally present and the first day's chaos is total. The harbormaster retains nominal authority over harbor departures. She could authorize a vessel's exit — if the party reaches her before she understands the full scope of what they did, and if their relationship with her is enough for her to use her position on their behalf. This is the most difficult escape avenue to access. It requires either a persuasive argument or a relationship that goes beyond the transactional.

**Overland without rebel support.** Possible; slow; exposed. The mountain terrain surrounding Jindark offers cover but not speed. For parties who have no remaining contacts or chose not to cultivate any.

**By air — Citadel infrastructure.** If the party did not destroy the Citadel, the ridgeline facility has incomplete but potentially functional airship infrastructure. This requires someone capable of operating the equipment, movement through a now-monitored area, and accepting that the Cabal will know exactly what left from where. Available only if the Citadel was spared.

### Act III–IV Interlude: The Return

The interlude covers the party's escape from the Unity and return to the Archipelago. Before running it, the DM should establish the party's situation across four variables: how Huma was freed, whether the Citadel was destroyed, what the state of their Jindark contacts is, and what — if anything — their prior actions in Balnor and Daliketh did to the Wayfinder network. These facts govern the difficulty of the Original Plan route and the availability of each escape avenue in the Coda.

---

*The Original Plan (Conditional Difficulty)*

The standing plan was to retrace the Wayfinder network's route in reverse: escape Jindark by rail, transit through Balnor, take an airship to Daliketh, connect with Kaman's operation, and wait for the next outbound ship. This remains possible, but the difficulty at each stage reflects the state the party leaves behind.

**Jindark to Balnor.**
If Huma was freed without catastrophic damage and the Citadel was not destroyed, a narrow window remains on the day following the harbor event. The city is in shock, and lockdown procedures are only beginning to take effect. The rebellion and criminal network contacts can get the party onto a northbound train before the window closes. Once it closes, rail service from Jindark is suspended for approximately a week while the authorities investigate. When service resumes, checkpoint scrutiny approaches the impenetrable; only exceptional disguise work or a very convincing cover story will carry the party through.

If the Citadel was destroyed, the window effectively does not exist. The explosion on the ridge is visible from the entire city and the Advocates' response begins within hours. Escape via rail without contact support is nearly impossible in the immediate aftermath. The week's suspension applies here as well, and scrutiny on resumption is at least as severe — the Citadel's destruction has added a second incident for the authorities to attribute to the same party.

**Balnor.**
The situation in Balnor depends on two variables: the party's exposure status from the device interlude, and whether news of the Jindark events has arrived before them.

If the device did not expose the party and did not lead to Giacomo's arrest, he is still available. He will take them in under the same terms — they are houseguests, not operatives, and he will disavow them without hesitation if pressed. He can arrange airship tickets. If the Citadel was destroyed, however, news of the disaster will reach Balnor at roughly the same time the party does. The capital will be in uproar. Scrutiny at the aerodrome will be significantly elevated, and the party will face intense examination at boarding.

If prior events compromised the party in Balnor or led to Giacomo's arrest, they have no contact and cannot move freely in the city.

**Daliketh.**
If the rescue mission at the end of Act II dismantled the Wayfinder network in Daliketh, Kaman's operation no longer exists. However, Pulara is still in the city, overseeing the evacuation of the last remaining network personnel. She can be contacted through whatever channel the party established with her before departure. She will be cautious, pressed for time, and unwilling to take unnecessary risks — but she will get them out if they reach her.

If the Daliketh network is intact, Kaman's operation runs on its usual schedule. The party waits for the next outbound ship.

*Asking the Spirits (Easy)*

The spirits have not, as of Act III, demonstrated the ability to transport people between locations in the physical world. They can do this — they simply have not been asked, and the party has no reason to know it is possible. This option is available to any party that thinks of it. The spirits can only move people between places within their sphere of influence, and not all will help.

**Hope.**
Hope will answer the party's call without hesitation. She will break the standard mechanics of the crossing — meeting the party in transit rather than waiting in her domain. The form of the transit reflects the outcome of Huma's freeing. If he was freed cleanly with minimal harm, the crossing takes the form of a beautiful sunlit afternoon on the Jindark breakwater promenade — the promenade the party walked without knowing what lay beneath it, now peaceful and full of ordinary life. If the freeing was more damaging, the scene is quieter, the light thinner, the promenade less populated. In either case, Hope offers to send the party home directly and does so with very little ceremony.

**Placida.**
Placida will answer only if the party did not free Maritas. She too will skip formality, meeting the party in transit in the form of a public civic celebration — glad or subdued depending on how Huma was freed, reflecting her read on the state of things. If the party encountered Maritas but did not free him, she will address this directly: she thanks them, absolves them of any residual complicity in Revna's scheme, and tells them they made the right choice. She does not comment on Huma's release — she views it as an unambiguous public good and a driver of necessary change, which is an insight into her character. She will not offer to transport the party home unprompted. If asked, she will do it.

**Revna.**
Revna will answer regardless of circumstances. She will not extend the same courtesy as Hope or Placida — she insists on the full crossing process.

The crossing passes through five spaces, each presenting a question. The questions have no correct answers; what matters is that the party answers honestly and from the heart. A party member who deflects, equivocates, or answers dishonestly will be called out by name, and the question will be directed to someone else.

- *The Classroom:* A teacher at the front of a room full of students looks past them at the party. *"What is the Unity's biggest problem?"*
- *The Restaurant:* Friends around a long table. The old man at the head sets down his fork. *"Are people in the Unity free?"*
- *The Funeral:* Mourners gathered around a casket. A relative of the deceased, red-eyed, turns to the party. *"Do the people of the Unity believe it's worth fighting for?"*
- *The Rally:* A massive political gathering, thousands strong. The speaker on the platform interrupts their address and points directly at the party. *"In the end, what will make the people of the Unity rise up?"*

The fifth space is different. An altar on a mountaintop beneath a surreal, storm-heavy red sky. A monk in a black robe meditates at the altar, face obscured. Below the altar, a stone bowl stained by generations of use. The monk speaks without looking up: *"What are you willing to sacrifice for the Revolution?"* This is not a question — it is an invitation. The acceptable offering is blood: one of the party members must cut their palm and bleed into the bowl. A gift judged insufficient will be returned. Blood offered willingly opens a door in the cliff face to the party's right, which leads to Revna's domain.

Revna's domain appears largely as before, but more active — evidence of plans being made, work underway. If Maritas was freed, it has a more organized and militant character.

When the party reaches Revna's throne, her reception depends on what they did at the Congress:

If Maritas was freed: she greets them with genuine warmth. She tells them that great things are ahead, that they have made a true alliance with her, and that their acts will matter more than they yet understand. She offers to return them to the Archipelago without being asked.

If Maritas was not freed: she upbraids them severely — not with shouting, but with the colder anger that comes naturally to her. She tells them exactly what their failure cost and what it cost her. She returns them, without ceremony, to wherever they conducted the ritual.

**Maritas.**
Maritas will answer the party's call if he was freed. He enforces the full crossing.

The transit takes the form of a crowded, bloodsoaked arena. The party materializes behind the portcullis to the arena floor. The master of gladiators — a heavily scarred human woman — looks them over and asks which of them volunteers as the challenger. The portcullis drops behind the volunteer, separating them from the rest of the party. The volunteer faces a challenger of equal level who is their functional opposite: an arcane caster faces a martial fighter; a Paladin faces a Warlock; a Rogue faces a Cleric. The bout is fought to unconsciousness or surrender. The outcome does not matter — only that it is fought with genuine effort. A volunteer who gives less than their best will find the crowd silent and the master unmoved.

Whether the volunteer wins or loses, the master calls it — *"Well fought!"* or *"A glorious victory!"* — and the crowd responds. The volunteer is restored to full hit points. The master leads them through a doorway.

Maritas's domain: a ridge overlooking a wide open battlefield on a clear sunny day. Thousands of bodies. The winning side's soldiers and workers are visible below — finishing off the dying, aiding the wounded, stripping the dead. Maritas sits on a rock nearby, picking his fingernails with a small knife, entirely unbothered.

He will look up when the party arrives and ask what they want.

If they ask him to send them home: he refuses, flatly and without heat. *"I am not your wet nurse. Deal with your own problems, my little rabbits. And don't bother coming again,"* he adds, *"unless you've found something worthy of my attention."* A dismissive gesture. The party is returned to wherever they conducted the ritual. They go home without his help.

Maritas is not punishing them. He simply does not provide this service. He is the spirit of War.

*Stealing an Airship (Moderate to Hard)*

If the party attacks the Citadel, they exit through one of its upper floors. Below them, an access stairwell leads to the ground. Above them, tethered to the ridgeline and unattended in the immediate chaos, is a Unity airship. If the Citadel's destruction sequence is active, every decision has a hard deadline.

**Taking control.**
A Unity airship's controls are not intuitive to anyone without training. Any party member may attempt a high-DC Intelligence check; the party may make a total of three attempts before the situation forces itself to a resolution.

On success: the airship gets airborne in time. If the Citadel's destruction is underway, they clear the blast radius.

On failure: the party survives. The airship's lifting mechanism provides enough passive buoyancy to prevent a fatal fall, but the explosion throws the vessel to the ground. Each party member takes 2d6 force damage from the blast and 3d8 bludgeoning from impact. They are alive, outside the Citadel, with whatever escape window remained now significantly narrowed.

**Flying it.**
Keeping the airship aloft over any meaningful distance requires a trained crew. Two contacts can provide one:

- *The Rebellion:* The contact will arrange a crew of volunteers without hesitation — these are people who have been waiting for an opportunity like this. The cost is minimal: when the party reaches Daliketh, they should see that the volunteers receive a bonus for their trouble. The hidden cost is that the rebellion wants to keep the airship. It represents a significant resource for their operations, and they will make this clear before the crew boards.

- *The Criminal Network:* They will hire a competent, no-questions-asked crew of specialists. The price is 1,000 gp, or the airship itself, delivered to a specified contact in Daliketh. They do not negotiate on either option.

If the party attempts to hire a crew independently, without either contact, they will find it possible but very difficult. In the chaotic aftermath of the harbor event, few competent people are both available and willing to take the risk. The DM should make this path costly in time, gp, and scrutiny.

**The fuel problem.**
A Unity airship carries finite reserves of coal and lift gas. Flying to Daliketh without a resupply stop will exhaust those reserves before arrival. A trained crew will tell the party this clearly. Three resupply options exist:

- *Karale:* The party knows this city from the train journey. It has a functional aerodrome, and news of the Jindark events will not have arrived there before the party does. Scrutiny is standard. The problem is distance: Karale is at the edge of the airship's comfortable range. The route is feasible but leaves no margin.

- *Balnor:* Fully equipped, no supply constraints. News from Jindark will have arrived. Scrutiny at the aerodrome will be significant. The party will face questions about cargo, route, and credentials. Manageable for a well-prepared group, but a meaningful obstacle on top of everything else they are managing.

- *Bashere:* A large manufacturing city on Lake Janelia in the province of Janelia, in the central hills region — considerably further south than the hill country the party passed through on the train. It involves a detour that tests the airship's range but offers a resupply point that is far less likely to be on elevated alert. A crew familiar with Unity airspace will know the route and can advise the party on its feasibility.

**Military patrols.**
A Unity military airship patrol may intercept the party in transit. This is not certain, but the DM should treat it as likely given current conditions. Military vessels communicate by semaphore and will ask the party to identify themselves, state their cargo, and declare their destination. Failure to respond convincingly — or to respond at all — will result in a boarding action.

The military crew is trained; the vessel is armed with multiple cannon; the party's vessel is not armed. This is not a fight designed to be won. The party's best tools are compliance, preparation, and a coherent story.

**The Middle Sea.**
Flying an airship across the Middle Sea is not viable under standard conditions. The distance is too great, the fuel reserves insufficient, and the weather over open water unpredictable in ways that are lethal to a civilian vessel. A party that wishes to attempt it will need a magical solution of their own devising. The DM should not supply one — but should not prevent a creative party from finding one either.

# THE ORACLE

The **Oracle** is not a person. She is the spirit of Belief — one of the belief-spirits, created and sustained by mortal faith, but unusual among them in that she has existed since before the Unity's suppression and has never been diminished by it, because belief itself cannot be outlawed. She is consistently incarnated by passing among mortal hosts, always female. The host lives a normal mortal life — works, loves, ages — except when she is seized by the spirit and makes a pronouncement. She cannot choose when this happens. The last pronouncement of each host is always the same: where to find the next vessel.

The Oracle is the only neutral figure in the Perfect Isles. One of the Archipelago's many sects was built up around her care and access; this sect is universally respected and politically untouchable. People travel far to consult her. Few are admitted. Even fewer receive a formal pronouncement.

**The pronouncement that opens the adventure:** The Oracle summoned the council directly and pronounced that a new Circle has been formed — that certain individuals should be brought to her in secret. The contents of this pronouncement are closely guarded. That she spoke to the council at all is widely known and already destabilizing.

**What the Oracle knows (DM only):** She is aware of her own nature as a belief-spirit. She knows the Old Gods' deliberation is underway. The circle she named was not chosen by her but by something deeper — she does not know whether it came from the Old Gods, from Belief itself, or from some other force. She will tell the party this if they ask directly. She does not lie.

# THE CROSSING — MECHANICS

## The Four Keys

There are two distinct rituals the party must learn in Act I. The Oracle knows both are necessary; recovering them is the core objective of Act I.

**The Summoning ritual** is taught by the Witch of the Silver Stream. It allows its user to contact a spirit of any type, but must be performed at a location that is important to the spirit in question. If the spirit is willing to respond, it sends a physical manifestation into the physical world. The true name of the spirit and a gift are not required components, but both increase the likelihood of a response. This is the ritual the party first uses — with Hellie's guidance — to summon Silver at the Silver Stream, and later uses to summon the Old Man of the Mountain at his mountaintop shrine.

**The Communing ritual** (also called the Crossing ritual) is taught by the Old Man of the Mountain. It allows the contacted spirit to open a portal into the spirit world and transport the Circle there bodily. This ritual requires all four of the following conditions to be met — the Four Keys. A full Crossing into the spirit world is always a component of this ritual when used.

### The Circle
A specific group of individuals called upon to serve as representatives of the mortal world in the spirit realm. The circle must always be of its original number — if a member dies, the existing circle must choose a replacement before the next Crossing can be attempted. Only members of the Circle can pass through a Crossing's portal. *(The size of the Circle — whether it is the full party or some subset — is TBD.)*

### The Ritual
The Communing ritual itself — the specific form of words, materials, and preparations taught by the Old Man of the Mountain. The party cannot perform a Crossing without it. Its exact material requirements are TBD and should reflect the setting's themes.

### The Name
The Circle completing the ritual must know the true name of the spirit they wish to visit. Without it, the ritual produces no passage. Seeking these names will be one of the investigation threads of Act I and II.

### The Gift
The Circle must present an acceptable gift to the spirit being visited in order to gain passage beyond the crossing point. What counts as acceptable varies by spirit. A poorly chosen gift may not simply fail — it may offend.

## How a Crossing Works

When the ritual is completed correctly with all four keys in place, a physical portal opens to the spirit world. The portal can only be used by members of the Circle.

Each Crossing is unique to its circumstances — to the spirit being visited, to the Circle's current state, and to the world's condition at the time of the Crossing. The passage between worlds takes the form of a metaphysical journey: the Circle passes through an intermediate space before arriving at the spirit's domain.

**Example — First Crossing (to Hope):**
- *The portal* opens into a long series of dark passageways filled with lost items in various states of disrepair. No non-magical light source allows the party to see clearly. An ethereal light moves ahead of them; they must follow it.
- *The spirit's domain* is an abandoned city square at twilight, with geometry that is slightly wrong in ways that are hard to name. At the center of the square is a fountain, nearly dry — maintained by just a trickle of water. A young girl sits beside it.

**Evolution of spirit domains:** The condition of a spirit's domain reflects the state of belief in the physical world. As Hope's message resonates more among mortals, her domain changes: the fountain fills, the city begins to populate with people, and the twilight progresses toward dawn. This is a measurable effect players can witness across multiple visits.

## Spirit World Properties

The spirit world has substance and form, but those forms are subject to change and are often constructed in ways that make mortal visitors uncomfortable — not wrong exactly, but off, as if the rules of physical reality are suggestions here rather than laws. The specific character of the spirit world depends on which spirit's domain you are in. Traveling between domains (if this is ever required) is an open question for design.

# THE OLD GODS

## What the Old Gods Are

The Old Gods are not belief-spirits. They predate mortal civilization. They are primordial forces of the world — bound to it, shaped by it, and shaping it in return. Where the belief-spirits are formed by what mortals need, the Old Gods simply are. They do not depend on worship, though they can be influenced by mortal action. Their health is directly tied to the physical world's health: if an Old God is diminished, the aspect of the world they are connected to is diminished. If an Old God dies, something in the world dies with them.

Most mortals do not know the Old Gods exist as distinct from the belief-spirits they worship. This ignorance is part of why the crisis is so advanced.

## Carnuth, the Stag of the New Moon

The Old God of the western temperate rainforest of the Sacred Lands — the **Wildwood** — and the driving force behind the eastern resistance to Unity colonization.

Carnuth does not follow mortal thought patterns. He follows natural ones. His concern is the Wildwood: threats to it are threats to him, and he responds to threats with the force appropriate to them. He does not take pleasure in destruction, but he does not shy from it. He has no interest in the wider political situation unless it directly affects his domain.

**His three forms:**
- **Daylight form:** A giant stag, sleeping peacefully in a forest meadow. Quiescent; harmless unless threatened.
- **New moon form (power at peak):** A gargantuan multi-legged stag of great magical and destructive power. This is when he is most dangerous and most active in the war against the colonists.
- **Full moon form (power at ebb):** A giant stag-headed battle sorcerer — physically reduced, magically formidable, capable of mortal-legible communication.

**Approach:** If approached respectfully and with an appropriate gift, Carnuth is kind and generous. He is a potential ally. He is also the Old God most likely to be destroyed by Act III's antagonists, and preventing this is one of the act's primary objectives.

**His role in the Old Gods' deliberation:** Carnuth has not abandoned his desire to defend the Wildwood, but his war against the colonists has given him more sustained contact with mortals than most Old Gods — and this has made him, in some ways, the most conflicted member of the council. He has seen what mortals can become. He is not certain the answer is extermination. This makes him a potential internal advocate.

## Huma, the Storm Whale

An Old God of the western continent — one of the last great primordial spirits of those lands, and one of the few whose diminishment has not yet reached a point of no return. The natural spirits of the west have largely been reduced to insubstantial shells as the Unity's development consumed and suppressed the land; Huma, connected to sea and storm, survived longer than most because the ocean cannot be developed. But in a weakened state, he was vulnerable.

**Capture:** A cabal within the Unity's ministry tasked with suppressing magic and divine practices — the body that enforces the prohibition — discovered what Huma was and constructed a device capable of capturing him. They did so without the knowledge or sanction of the broader Unity government. Huma has been held captive since, his energy channeled to power the northern coastal region, which has become the most productive and prosperous part of the Unity as a result.

**The cabal's intentions:** They view the capture of Huma not as a crime but as an achievement — proof that the Unity's principles can be extended to the spirit world. They intend to replicate it: finding and capturing the remaining Old Gods of the west, and eventually extending into the Sacred Lands. The cabal leader genuinely believes this is how civilization survives.

**What captivity has done to Huma:** His condition after freeing depends on how it was accomplished — specifically on the loss of life involved.

*Minimal damage and loss of life (controlled outcome):* Huma returns to himself within days. He is a spirit of the sea and its weather, and the natural systems he governs reassert themselves quickly — the weather along the northern coast will right itself within a few weeks. He is shaken by the experience of captivity and grateful to the party in a way that is characteristic of a very old being: not effusive, but real. He will allow the party to contact him via the Communing ritual whenever they wish. In the best outcome, he offers each party member a single boon, chosen from the following:

- **Breath of the Sea** — *Air Bubble* 3/day
- **Call the Storm** — *Lightning Bolt* (cast at 5th level) 2/day
- **Divine Wind** — *Warding Wind* 3/day

Each boon is a permanent innate ability — the character may use it a set number of times per day without spell slots or material components. Full statistics for each in the DM Guide appendix.

*Catastrophic freeing (significant loss of life):* Huma is wounded in a way that is not physical. The deaths caused in part by the manner of his release — however unintentionally — mark him. He will go into isolation. He will not speak to the world for some time. He is reachable if the party seeks him out for a final conversation, and he will speak honestly with them about what happened and what it means. He will not offer the Communing connection or a boon. His isolation will have consequences for the natural world in Act IV that the DM may develop.

# INSTITUTIONS AND FACTIONS

## The Unity (Western Government)
**Public face:** A rational, just, and prosperous civilization. The greatest achievement of organized mortal society. Outlawed magic and divine worship as destabilizing forces; built technology and reason in their place. Current project: the colonization of the eastern continent, framed as necessary survival.

**Hidden truth (DM only):** The suppression of magic and worship was a deliberate power consolidation by the founders, not purely philosophical. The consequence — the severing of the spirit connection — was unintended but is now undeniable to anyone who looks clearly. The leadership knows the land is failing; they do not know why. The colonization effort is a bet that they can outrun the problem.

**Relationship to central premise:** Direct cause. The Unity's actions created the crisis. Its reform is a necessary component of the best ending.

## The Archipelago Factions (30+)
**Public face:** A diverse collection of communities united by shared exile, each preserving its own teachings, traditions, and identity. Every faction has its own distinctive dress, practices, and place in the life of the Isles. Most are small.

**Hidden truth (DM only):** The preservation instinct has become an end in itself. The factions have lost sight of *why* they preserve what they preserve. Fear of the Unity has been institutionalized into a fear of all change.

**Relationship to central premise:** The Archipelago holds knowledge relevant to the Crossing — the original exiles were people who practiced magic and worship and knew the spirit world. That knowledge has been fragmented across factions. Reunifying it is part of Act I.

### Known Factions

**Clerical**

**The Godcallers** — A monastic sect that maintains detailed records of the deities of pre-Unity times and can often be found chanting their names as they go about everyday activities. They dress in tattered black robes to which they sew white ribbons bearing the names of forgotten gods and snippets of scripture. They are the Isles' primary institutional memory of what was lost when the Unity suppressed worship.

**The Keepers of the Veil** — A small order of female nuns who serve the temporal needs of the Oracle and keep detailed records of her utterances and teachings. They dress in white with head coverings and veils and follow a vow of silence in public. They are universally respected and politically untouchable. They know more about the Oracle's pronouncements — past and present — than anyone else on the Isles. The Keepers also maintain a covert scrying network across the Archipelago, with eyes on developments in both the Unity and the Sacred Lands. Their intelligence work is not widely known; their vow of public silence makes them easy to underestimate. When they choose to share what they have seen, their interpretations can be cryptic — but they are rarely wrong.

**The Twilight Order** — A small sect that communes with the spirits of the Archipelago's dead, maintaining detailed genealogical records and offering — to those who know how to ask — consultation with departed ancestors. Their practices make most people uneasy, and they are careful not to advertise their services widely. But their records of lineage and their capacity to reach the recently deceased make them quietly indispensable to families with questions the living cannot answer.

**Druidic**

**The Blue Star Adepts** — A faction that combines druidic practices with some arcane study, making them among the most technically literate factions on the Isles. They are familiar with technology but use it sparingly to avoid comparisons to the Unity. They serve the community as healers and are particularly knowledgeable in astronomy.

**The Greensingers** — A small, insular faction deeply attuned to the minor nature spirits of the islands. Their summoning of these beings is incidental to their practice — not connected to the spirit-crossing rituals — and reflects a genuine, informal relationship with the Isles' spiritual ecology. They know more about the Isles' native spirits than anyone.

**The Wild Hunt** — A small and insular circle of druids devoted to living as close to nature as possible. The Wild Hunt counts many members with lycanthropic blood among its ranks and embraces the druidic tradition of shapechanging above all other practices. On the first new moon of each year, they conduct a ritual hunt across the Big Island — and anyone found outdoors during the hunt is captured and held for ritual ransom until their release can be negotiated. The practice is regarded as eccentric at best. Most people on the Isles simply stay indoors that night.

**Monastic**

**Order of the Even Hand** — A small monastery that eschews spiritual teachings entirely. The Even Hand follows a specific regimen of extreme physical activity and meditation, seeking enlightenment through the inward perfection of self. They have no doctrine about the spirit world; they do not discuss it. They are respected for their discipline and their willingness to act when action is needed.

**Order of the Five Mountains** — A highly spiritual monastic order whose practices seek enlightenment through understanding the physical world and the spirits that live within it. More engaged with the spirit world than almost any non-clerical faction, though from a philosophical rather than ritual direction.

**Paladin**

**The Path of Virtue** — A highly structured order preaching strict adherence to seven cardinal virtues. An offshoot of the broader Pathist philosophy. They are among the most morally earnest factions on the Isles and the most likely to have strong opinions about the ethical dimensions of any course of action.

**Sorcerer**

**The Truebloods** — A faction of sorcerers from multiple bloodlines, united by devotion to the preservation and practice of magic itself above all other concerns. They are the most politically difficult faction on the Isles — they care about magic, not about community — but their knowledge of innate magical traditions is unmatched.

**The Discordians** — Where the Truebloods welcome any sorcerous bloodline, the Discordians limit their membership exclusively to those with Wild Magic in their heritage. They are as unwelcome for their habitual mischief and pranks as they are celebrated for their spectacular and deeply unpredictable magical performances. The relationship between the Discordians and the Truebloods is one of mutual exasperation.

**Warlock**

**Order of the Seven Stars** — An esoteric group that makes pacts with beings they believe represent a higher spiritual reality. These beings are in fact uncaring extraplanar entities. The Order is sincere, well-intentioned, and largely unaware of this. Whether to tell them is a moral question the party may face.

**The Avengers** — A semi-monastic order that makes pacts with lower planar entities to prepare for what they see as an inevitable war with the Unity. They believe reality is cyclical and that a final confrontation with their ancient enemy is coming. They are among the most militant factions on the Isles and among those most resistant to any peaceful solution.

**Guardians of the Hidden Vale** — Practitioners who undergo a ritual sanctification at seven sites in a hidden valley of the Sacred Lands, forming bonds with the fey spirits of the region. They are the Archipelago's best source of knowledge about the Sacred Lands and maintain the most active ongoing presence there of any Isles faction.

**Wizard**

**The Eightfold Path** — The largest faction of wizardly practitioners on the Isles. A semi-monastic group that teaches the eight schools of magic in the form of esoteric knowledge. Somewhat insular and rarely engaging with non-members, but their breadth of arcane knowledge is the deepest of any faction.

**The Arcane Practitioners Association** — More a guild than a faction. A collection of independent wizards gathered for mutual support. Highly involved in Isles community life, seeking to improve the reputation of arcane practitioners through public works. The most approachable of the wizardly factions.

**Philosophical**

**The Pathists** — More an encompassing philosophy than a specific faction. The Pathists are the largest single group among the people of the Isles, and their teachings — centered on the spiritual journey specific to each person — are broad enough that members of many other factions also identify as Pathists. The Path of Virtue is an offshoot. They are not a political bloc but their values permeate Isles culture.

**The Wayfinders** — Spiritually devoted to the protection and preservation of the Perfect Isles, the Wayfinders are highly respected by all factions. They take it upon themselves — at great personal risk — to seek out and rescue magical practitioners within the Unity, bringing them to the Isles. They frequently recruit members of other factions to support specific missions. They are the faction most likely to have Unity-side contacts and operational experience.

**Martial**

**The Lodge of Perfection** — A formal academy of martial practice, the Lodge holds that the use of weapons can be elevated to an art form equivalent to any other. They offer tutelage to anyone willing to commit to their rigorous training regimen, regardless of faction affiliation. The Lodge is one of the few groups on the Isles that openly advocates for military preparation, holding that armed conflict with the Unity is a matter of when, not if.

**The Wild Bunch** — Loosely organized to the point of barely qualifying as a faction, the Wild Bunch is a roving fellowship of bravos, duelists, and martial adventurers united by a shared appetite for challenge and spectacle. They travel the Isles seeking feats of strength and daring and settle disputes — between themselves and anyone willing — through formalized duels designed to be impressive rather than lethal. They have no doctrine, no fixed base, and no leadership in any meaningful sense.

**Civic and Cultural**

**The Troubadours** — Made up of singers, musicians, visual artists, and theatre companies, the Troubadours are welcomed wherever they go and constitute one of the Archipelago's few true sources of shared cultural life. Many practice as solo entertainers; others have formed traveling companies that move between the islands together. They are one of the only Isles groups to regularly make journeys to the eastern mainland, where their performances are received with considerable enthusiasm.

**The Pentad** — An organization of researchers and scholars, the Pentad is structured around five disciplines: Chronicles (history), Literature, Poetry, Preservation (library science), and Natural History. Their operations are centered at the **Nexus** — a substantial library in the village of **Candela** on **Santama**, one of the southern islands. They are the Archipelago's primary institutional memory for subjects outside the spirit world and produce most of its written scholarship.

**The Shadowed Disciples** — Secretive to the point where many on the Isles doubt they exist at all, the Disciples are said to practice hidden magical rites in the deep caverns beneath the Big Island. Those who claim to have encountered them describe an organization that offers clandestine services — conducted under cover of night — for those who can find them and meet their price. Whether they are a genuine faction or a useful fiction maintained by parties unknown is a matter of ongoing debate among those who take them seriously.

**The Greenwardens** — The Greenwardens have taken it upon themselves to protect the wild places of the Archipelago from encroachment by the Isles' growing settlements, and to ensure that civilization does not overwhelm the natural landscape that conceals and sustains the community. They are combative by inclination and have been known to make significant trouble for those they judge to be upsetting the balance of nature. Most factions find them irritating; nearly all acknowledge that without them, the islands' natural character would have been lost generations ago.

**The Seawatch** — The Seawatch patrols the stretch of Middle Sea along the western edge of the Barrier between the Archipelago and the Unity. Their methods rely on animals of sea and sky as advance scouts and on calling upon the power of the Barrier itself to turn back vessels or individuals approaching from the west. They are the Isles' first line of defense against discovery and are treated very seriously by the Council.

**The Messengers** — Equipped with fast boats and faster feet, the Messengers carry news and small parcels between the settlements of the Archipelago. Their neutrality is inviolable, their discretion absolute, and their service is what keeps the fractious community of the Isles connected across its many islands. They function as an unofficial postal service and their couriers are welcomed and protected everywhere without exception.

**The Concordat** — Diplomatic mediators whose sole purpose is maintaining the fragile peace between the Archipelago's many factions. They serve as neutral arbiters, treaty-keepers, and negotiators when disputes between factions threaten to escalate. Universally respected in principle; universally resented in practice when they intervene. Without them, the factional tensions of the Isles would almost certainly have broken into open conflict generations ago.

**The Tidecallers** — Sailors, navigators, and sea-workers who maintain the Archipelago's fleet and know the Middle Sea's currents, reefs, and weather patterns better than anyone. Without them, reliable travel between islands — and to the continents — would be far more dangerous. They train their own and accept apprentices from any faction willing to commit to the sea life.

**The Honored Forge** — The craftspeople of the Honored Forge produce items of exceptional quality across every discipline — metalwork, woodcraft, leatherwork, textiles, and more. Many of their finest pieces are imbued with magical properties, and a commission from the Forge is considered a mark of distinction on the Isles. Among the wealthiest factions; they use that wealth to support organizations and projects they consider worthy.

**The Returned** — Former Unity citizens who escaped to the Archipelago and organized around their shared experience. They hold the most current firsthand knowledge of Unity society, culture, and institutions of any group on the Isles — and are viewed with suspicion by many other factions, who question their loyalties. They are the most passionate advocates for eventually going back to liberate those still trapped within the Unity.

**The Provisioners** — A mercantile faction that manages the Archipelago's food supply, coordinating fishing fleets, farming operations, and trade between the islands. Unglamorous and essential. They have significant economic leverage and considerably less idealism than most factions. They care about feeding people, not spiritual philosophy, and they are refreshingly direct about it.

**The Founders' Keep** — Devoted to the history and memory of the original exile, the Founders' Keep maintains the Archipelago's oldest records, founding documents, and the ancestral lineages of the families who made the first crossing. They are headquartered in an old keep and lighthouse complex built by the original exiles — one of the oldest standing structures in the Isles and among the most symbolically significant. Deeply conservative and resistant to change; they view themselves as custodians of what the Archipelago fundamentally is and must remain.

**The Healers' Circle** — A combined mundane and magical healing organization that serves the entire Archipelago regardless of faction affiliation. Their complete neutrality and humanitarian mission make them the one faction that can move freely even between hostile groups. They accept no payment for emergency care; their operations are funded by voluntary contributions from the factions they serve.

## Unity Institutions

### The Unity Council
The supreme governing body of the Unity, made up of elected representatives from its ten provinces. Meets in the capital, Balnor. Operates by consensus and democratic procedure; any member may call for a broader plebiscite. The Unity Council is not a monolith — it contains factions, reformers, hardliners, and everything between. Its public proceedings are a matter of record and are closely followed by citizens across the continent.

*Details TBD — relevant in Acts III and V.*

### The Advocates
A special enforcement unit operating under the Ministry of Justice. Where the ordinary constabulary handles public order and petty crime, the Advocates are tasked with the Unity's more sensitive security concerns: hunting rebels, identifying and suppressing practitioners of magic and the supernatural, and rooting out organized dissent. They have considerably broader authority than the constabulary, can compel cooperation from local officers, and operate with a significant degree of independence from civilian oversight.

The Advocates are not widely feared by ordinary citizens — most people never encounter them and think of them as a necessary safeguard against genuine threats. Their reputation among the Unity's underground (rebels, rescued practitioners, Wayfinder contacts) is another matter entirely. They are thorough, well-resourced, and patient.

**The Advocates' Tower** in Daliketh is one of their regional headquarters — a facility with cells below, a garrison, and a service entrance that Wayfinder operative Renata has had under surveillance for years.

*The Advocates are the primary enforcement threat the party faces in Act III. Further details — leadership, organizational structure, specific capabilities — TBD when Act III is outlined.*

### The Cabal
A covert organization operating entirely outside the knowledge of the Unity Council and the broader government. The Cabal originated within the Ministry of Justice — the same ministry that houses the Advocates — but it is not a ministry function. It is a shadow within a ministry: a small, self-selected group of senior officials who concluded that the Unity's survival requires actions the Unity's laws and ethics do not permit, and that someone must therefore take those actions without the Unity's knowledge or sanction.

Their philosophy is explicitly pragmatic over moral. They believe in the supremacy of the Unity above all else, and they understand that what they do is, by the Unity's own standards, evil. They do not consider this a problem. In their view, the Unity's idealism is a luxury afforded by the unpleasant work they do in secret. The Unity can remain good precisely because they are willing to be something other than good on its behalf. They do not seek recognition. Discovery would destroy the thing they protect.

**What they have done:** Their proof of concept — the capture of **Huma, the Storm Whale**, an Old God, using a device of their own construction — is the most significant covert achievement in Unity history. Huma's captive energy now powers the northern coastal region of the Unity, producing the most productive and prosperous land on the continent. The broader Unity government has no explanation for the northern coast's anomalous productivity. The Cabal intends to keep it that way.

**What they intend:** They plan to replicate Huma's capture — locating and securing the remaining Old Gods of the west, and eventually extending into the Sacred Lands. In their framing, this is not conquest of the spirit world. It is resource management. The Unity is dying; this is how it survives.

**The shift to Act IV:** Having demonstrated they can capture an Old God, the Cabal turns its attention in Act IV to Carnuth, the Old God of the Wildwood. Carnuth cannot be captured — his power in his own domain is too great. The Cabal has therefore concluded that elimination is the only viable option. This is, for them, a practical calculation. It is also an experiment: they want to know if an Old God can be killed.

**The danger they represent:** The Cabal's leaders are not corrupt in the ordinary sense. They are true believers — in the Unity, in its mission, in the necessity of what they do. The party cannot simply expose them and expect the Unity's institutions to handle the rest. The Cabal has anticipated exposure and prepared for it. The argument against them is not primarily legal; it is philosophical. They must be shown that what they are doing is destroying the very thing they believe they are protecting.

**Leadership:** The Cabal's regional leader in Jindark is **Circe Lovelace** — one of the organization's original founders and a member of its inner circle. She is a short, serious, dark-skinned human woman with close-cropped hair; she presents as an Oath of Devotion Paladin whose oath is the Unity itself. She does not believe she has done anything that was not necessary. She cares deeply for her subordinates. She has a standing escape protocol and will not be killed in the Citadel encounter; she reappears in Act IV. The Cabal's broader leadership structure and membership beyond Lovelace are TBD.

## The Eastern Peoples
**Public face:** Fractious, diverse, frequently in conflict, occasionally at war. The one thing unifying them is resistance to colonization.

**Hidden truth (DM only):** Their fragmentation is partly the result of Old God influence — some Old Gods have shaped communities around themselves, and the conflict between communities is sometimes a reflection of tensions between Old Gods.

**Relationship to central premise:** The east is where the Old Gods are most present and where the party will spend the most time. The eastern peoples are affected by both colonization and the Old Gods' deliberation.

## The Old Gods
**Public face (what mortals believe):** Unknown to most — the peoples of the Sacred Lands worship the Old Gods and understand their nature; the Archipelago's faction members have varying levels of awareness; the Unity does not know they exist at all.

**Hidden truth (DM only):** Primordial spirits connected to the world's physical fabric. Not organized into any formal council. They communicate freely with each other, most often with those whose territories overlap or abut. They are as often at odds with each other as not, and their relationships change frequently.

Each Old God is represented in the physical world by a chosen **Witch** — a mortal with whom the Old God has formed a direct bond. Witch covens exist within each Old God's region. A **greater coven** convenes rarely, with one representative from each regional coven, and its proceedings filter back to each Old God. This structure is a guideline rather than a rule — the Old Gods do not enforce it.

The Old Gods' collective position on mortals is not a formal vote. It is an ongoing conversation, and the conversation is moving toward destruction. Some Old Gods are more sympathetic; some are more hostile. The party's actions across all five acts — particularly freeing Huma and preventing the destruction of Carnuth — are the evidence they will make their case with.

**Relationship to central premise:** The central antagonist force and the ultimate persuasion target. The adventure ends in a direct conversation with the Old Gods.

## The Belief-Spirits (Mortal "Gods")
**Public face:** The gods that mortals worship. Powerful, responsive, real.

**Hidden truth (DM only):** Created by and sustained by mortal belief. They change as belief changes. They have no independent existence apart from the network of belief that creates them. Some are aware of this; most are not. They have genuine power but no primordial connection to the world — they are passengers, not foundations.

Many belief-spirits were once worshiped as specific deities, which gave them more defined form and clearer personalities. As organized faith declined — especially in the Unity — many have become less distinct, somewhat confused about their own nature, and less fully themselves than they were. They remain genuinely powerful because belief does not require formal worship to generate spiritual energy.

The Unity, which outlawed worship, has nonetheless empowered a cluster of belief-spirits through its citizens' deeply held investment in the state's ideals (Law, Public Order, Public Good, Knowledge, Oppression, and Unity itself). These spirits are not worshiped and barely understand what they are. The Archipelago similarly generates its own cluster of belief-spirits. The Sacred Lands, which worship the Old Gods directly and make regular offerings to nature spirits, have the healthiest spiritual ecology in the world.

Spirits can split when a belief expands: a spirit of War might generate spirits of Strategy, Soldierly Virtue, or specific military traditions, with the parent spirit retaining a paramount relationship to the offshoots.

**Relationship to central premise:** Potential allies in Act II. The party may be able to use the belief-spirits' influence to begin the process of mortal reform, since they understand both mortal society and the spirit world. See *The Crossing Spirits Reference* for individual spirit entries.
