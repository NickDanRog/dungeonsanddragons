# History of Talumh
In the beginning, there was the One, and through its imaginings countless realties came to pass. In what is the blink of an eye to us, universes were born containing worlds, civilizations, and peoples too many to number. All of these passed before the One, and through them It knew all there was to know. As quickly as they came, these realities faded away, leaving all of existence as void. Contented, the one slumbered for eons beyond counting. 
As it dreamed, the One looked inward, and the dreams begat countless iterations of itself. Through time that was no time, these spirits were endowed with their own life force of a sort, reflections of the numberless visions experienced by their creator. Over millennia, they contended, fought, built, loved, and dreamed with each other. Through their strivings, a great civilization took shape, brining form to the Void for the first time without the will of the One. 
Over millennia more, the spirits were brought together into alliances, kingdoms, and finally, two great empires, one shining as the sun, and the other glimmering as the moon. Locked in contention with each other, these realms nonetheless relied on each other to survive, as only together could they retain the form they had given their world.  For an endless age the undying spirits learned, warred, and achieved, creating a civilization greater than all others before or since. 
Not all were content however. Two philosophical factions, one within each empire, arose which sought to overturn the order of things. One faction advocated that the spirits work together to create a new reality, outside the purview of the One.  The other sought to return to harness the collective power of the spirits to destroy the One once and for all, and claim reality as their own.  Heedless of the danger their actions posed, these factions sought to settle their differences in a great war, into which the empires were unwillingly drawn. 
This war, called the War of the Spirits, was unlike any other before it in scope, wreaking great devastation throughout the land and slowly dissolving the civilization that the spirits had worked countless ages to create. In their struggle to dominate each other, they had also offset the precarious balance of the cosmos that allowed their realm to exist, and the dreaming of the One slowly began to return all to void. As a last ditch effort to stave off oblivion, the champions on either side of the conflict worked together to capture those who had started the conflict in the first place, banishing them deep into the void. 
While the war had stopped and the balance had been regained, the world as the spirits knew it had been irreversibly changed. They knew it was only a matter of time before it too would final fade back into the dream of the One. Desperate to save the world they so loved, the spirits poured their life force into the structure of the world, sealing it for all time from the Void. As the spirits joined with a world given new and permanent form, new creatures were born of their essences, populating the world for the first time with life all its own. 
Two of their number they left as guardians for this new born land. These two great spirits set watch over the new peoples and creatures which had been born to the world, tending to them and to the land as a gardener tends to a garden. Using their vast powers they drew the last remaining city of their civilization into a secluded valley in the high mountains at the center of last continent, a place which they named Trí-na-nóg.

## The Age of the Tuthá 
Into the world of Talumh, which the great spirits had chosen for the name of their new realm, were born a multitude of creatures. Many of these creatures had been endowed with the essence of the spirits which had created them, granting them sentience and free will. Many individuals of these new races worked together in harmony to build a new civilization. Many more, driven by their wild natures and accountable to no one, took what they wanted and preyed on those weaker than themselves. Best on all sides by violence, those who sought peace made a great pilgrimage to Trí-na-nóg to beg the protection of the great spirits who they called the Lord and the Lady. 
The Lord and the Lady made a great covenant with these pilgrims, who they entitled the Tuthá. In exchange for following their teachings, the great spirits endowed the Tuthá with magical powers from their own essences and according to the nature of each race.  They granted an even greater boon to the two most prominent leaders among the Tuthá – the gift of immortality. These two leaders would serve as emissaries of the Lord and the Lady among the Tuthá, and were each instructed to form their own courts and recruit followers from among the people. 
Empowered by the great spirits, the Tuthá were able to push back their violent and barbaric kin, many of whom gave up their savage ways to join in a new civilization. Those who refused to yield were slowly transformed into hideous monsters though a combination of their own bestial natures and the magic of the Tuthá. 
The courts formed by the leaders among the Tuthá: the Radiant Court led by Queen Glaréad, and the Glimmering Court, led by King Dwynnan. While monsters and ferocious beast continued to roam the wild lands between them, the many tribes of the Tuthá settled into a long and comfortable time of peace among themselves. Like the spirit empires who had come before them, the Courts saw each other as rivals, and competitions amongst them for land, prestige, resources, and influence were often fierce. Unlike their predecessors however, they also saw each other as kin, and while wars among them were not infrequent, their leaders would step in to negotiate peace when matters became dire. 

## The Coming of Men 
While the great spirits struggled to save reality from oblivion and the Tuthá strove for mastery of the land, those who had been banished during the War of the Spirits did not sit idle. The faction that had called for the creation of a new reality, styling themselves the Exiles, cast about for new tools to realize their dreams of a new existence. Witnessing the covenant the Lord and the Lady had made with the Tuthá, they thought to make one of their own by seducing other creatures with power to use them as pawns to seize control of the world. Their efforts in this direction were to no avail however – while they could peer into the world their brethren had created, they could not community with or affect it. 
After a few centuries of finger pointing and blame shifting as they contended amongst themselves, one among the Exiles happened upon another plan. In his dreams, this spirit found he was able to travel into the imaginings of the One. Teaching this technique to the others, they searched the infinity of the Dream in the Void for suitable allies to draw into their scheme. They knew by the very nature of the Dream they would only be able to attempt this once, and thus their search was very careful and debate about the best candidate fierce among them. Eventually they settled on one creature they though best as a pawn for their schemes: Men. 
Shorter lived then the races of Talumh, Men were driven, adaptable, and over all, ambitious. In the depths of the Dream, the Exiles happened upon a large group of Men trapped on a dying world. Promising their salvation in return for servitude, they made a pact with these Men, drawing them through the Void and into Talumh, where they alighted on the western shores after passing through the mist. Initial contacts between Men and the Tuthá were not hostile at first as they were more curious about the newcomers than anything else. The numbers of Men soon began to swell however, and this peaceful co-existence and trading gave way to tension and eventually armed conflict after a few generations. 

## The Great War
Five hundred years after their arrival in Talumh, Men had colonized and the nearly half of the western expanse of the continent. Inter-tribal wars among them saw the rise of six distinct Kingdoms. As these Kingdoms consolidated their power, they firmly supplanted the Tuthá as the dominate people of the region. The Tuthá did not take kindly to these advances, and many of them, especially among the Glimmering Court, called for open warfare to eliminate the human menace. 
Behind the scenes of this developing cold war, another power was moving in secret to bring its own vision into the world. The group of spirits who had contributed to the onset of the War of the Spirits, the faction in opposition to the Exiles, had also been plotting from their place of banishment deep in the Void. Unlike the Exiles however, these spirits, now styling themselves the Shadow Lords, had taken to preying on each other and thus became thoroughly corrupted. After a few centuries, only a dozen of these entities remained. Sensing their own destruction, these beings called a truce amongst themselves. While they were in no longer in any way united, they nonetheless worked on their own toward their shared goal.  Their vision of wresting control of the cosmos from the One had changed over the centuries of their imprisonment however – now they sought to unravel the universe and make it anew in their own image. 
One of the most powerful of these spirits, calling itself Penumbra, had come to realize the Shadow Lords would never be able to reach any of their goals if they did not seek to break out from their imprisonment. Under his leadership, the Lords were able to escape their place of banishment through the Dream, though like the Exiles they were not able to enter or directly affect the world created by their former kin.  They were however to utilize the Dream to influence and effect the only other outsiders who had penetrated into Talumh: Men. 
Though a campaign of subtle manipulation, the Shadow Lords began to influence the course of human history as soon as they landed on the shores of Talumh. During the age of the six kingdoms, Penumbra appeared to a number of Men who he had closely attuned to himself, gifting them a new type of powerful magic he and his followers had discovered in the Dream. This magic, unlike that of the Tuthá which was drawn from their own natures, drew instead on the power of the Void, manipulating through words and rituals, and items of power to energize its effects. Armed with this magic, Penumbra’s human followers conquered the six kingdoms, forming a vast empire which sought to control all of Talumh. In a few short years the forces of this empire struck out, devastating the Tuthá and pushing them further into east with each passing year. 
The Lord and the Lady had thus far taken a neutral stance toward Men, hoping to allow their followers to deal with the threat these newcomers posed. Terrified by the magical power Men had gleaned from the Shadow, the decided to personally intervene in the conflict. They drew to them the all of the Tuthá, and in the hidden mountain valley near Trí-na-nóg they fought the massed armies of the Empire of Man in a titanic battle lasting five days. While the Tuthá were greatly outnumbered, the might of the Lord and the Lady was not to be denied, nor was the Tuthá’s magical power. 

## A New Age
After the defeat of their magical champions, the human armies began to crumble and flee. Many tens of thousands were captured, but many more still escaped, only to perish by the score in the mountains. Fueled by vengeance, the Tuthá swept into the realms of Men, capturing all they could find and slaying any who resisted. With the majority of humanity in cantonment, debate raged among the Tuthá as to what to do with them. Those of the Glimmering Court felt it their right to enslave mankind, where as those of the Radiant Court though it best to build ships for them and send them back to whence they came. 
The debate was settled in the end by the Lord and the Lady. They decided that as the majority of Men were not responsible for the crimes of their leaders, they should have the opportunity to live free. Never again would they be allowed to threated the Balance in such a way however. To this end, they scattered the remaining Men into twelve distinct communities throughout Talumh, each far removed from the other. 
Two hundred years has passed since the end of the Great War, and the agreement imposed on Man, called the Settlement, has maintained peace throughout these centuries. They and the Tuthá have again learned to live together, if at least a semblance of harmony. A new age has dawned on Talumh for all the races, and while the people of this land are no longer at war, the Exiles and the Shadow Lords have begun to plot again, and factions among Men and Tuthá are again stirring up mutual antagonism. Will this land see another great war, or will this newfound peace last? Only the decisions of those made in this time will shape what is to come.
