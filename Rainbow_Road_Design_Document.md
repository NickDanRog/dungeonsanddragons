# RAINBOW ROAD
## Design Document

*Pathfinder 1st Edition Adventure Module — Design Blueprint*
*Compiled: March 2026*

---

## Adventure Overview

- **System:** Pathfinder 1st Edition with Mythic Adventures
- **Level Range:** 1–20
- **Mythic Tier Range:** 1–10
- **Structure:** 3 phases — Nexus prologue (levels 1–4, mythic tier 1), eight-world sequence (levels 5–19, mythic tiers 2–9), endgame (level 20, mythic tier 10)
- **Tone:** Mythic heroic — high fantasy with tragic undertones. The PCs are genuine chosen heroes on a grand quest through a broken multiverse of pocket worlds, each ruled by a corrupted champion who was once among the greatest mortals ever to live. The weight of the campaign comes from the Warlocks' stories, the cost of redemption, and the stakes of a conflict that spans planes. Redemption is hard-won but possible, and the PCs' choices about who can be saved determine the shape of the worlds that survive.
- **Player Character Type:** Residents of Nexus, the hub world at the center of the Nine Worlds. From the campaign's first sessions, the PCs are drawn into the service of Casomir Zukra, the Warlock-King of Nexus, whose desperate need for heroes sets them on a path that will take them across every world in the system and into the Underworld itself.

## Central Premise

The Nine Worlds — a constellation of pocket dimensions suspended in the Ethereal Plane and linked by the Rainbow Road — were created for a purpose that no one now remembers. The celestial beings of Heaven built them as a crucible: a system designed to find worthy mortal champions, imbue them with mythic power, and send them out to fight evil across the wider planes. For thousands of years the system worked. Chosen heroes walked the Rainbow Road, faced a series of ordeals and tests on each world, and visited its place of power upon completion — carrying the strength they gained into battles that shaped the multiverse.

Five hundred years ago, the system broke. Ten of the greatest heroes the Nine Worlds had ever produced — the first to travel the Rainbow Road as a group — completed the circuit and carried the Song of Heaven to its culmination. Having witnessed the Asuras' influence firsthand on every world they crossed, the ten conceived a plan to open a direct path to the Underworld and install a permanent warden at its gates. Sarena Tal, the youngest of the ten, volunteered. Her death was the key — the casting of the ritual that opened the way required a willing sacrifice, and Tal gave herself to it deliberately, her soul passing through to become the Underworld's eternal warden. The ritual succeeded, but its unleashed energies triggered a catastrophe. Demons poured physically into the Nine Worlds for the first and only time. The war that followed destroyed over ninety-five percent of the demon population, with the vast majority of casualties occurring in a suicidal assault on Heaven itself, but the damage to the worlds was immense. The Rainbow Road collapsed. The portals to the wider planes went dark. The Nine Worlds were sealed off from everything. The tragedy is compounded by the fact that a gate to the Underworld already existed within the Great Ancillary on Nexus, accessible through the Song of Heaven with the gods' direct permission. The ten did not think to ask, and they deliberately concealed their plan from the gods. The apocalypse was unnecessary.

The nine surviving heroes endured the war, but at a price none of them understood at the time. The demonic corruption they had sought to contain had touched each of them, and over the centuries that followed, it twisted their greatest virtues into defining flaws. They became the Warlocks — nine ruling the outer worlds in various states of madness, tyranny, and despair. Sarena Tal, bound to the Underworld by her own sacrifice, carried out her function as warden for centuries before the corruption consumed her utterly. She is now known as Tornsoul, and she has spent five centuries building a demon army with one purpose: the destruction of the Nine Worlds and Heaven alike.

The PCs do not know any of this at the start. They know that the worlds are broken, the Road is closed, and Nexus is under perpetual threat from demon raids that the Warlock-King Casomir Zukra has spent years fighting to contain. What they will discover — through the quests Casomir sets them, through the Oracle's cryptic confirmation, and through the journey that follows — is that the system still works. The places of power still grant mythic strength to those deemed worthy. The Song of Heaven, a melodic key whose pieces are held at each place of power, still opens the Road one world at a time. And the heroes the celestials designed the system to produce are, at long last, the PCs themselves.

**What players do not know at the start:**
- The Nine Worlds were built to produce mythic champions — their entire structure is a crucible, not a natural cosmology
- The Warlocks were once the greatest heroes the system ever produced, corrupted by the same power the PCs will now seek
- Tornsoul is actively exploiting the progressive reopening of the Rainbow Road to move demon forces through prepared ritual sites on Nexus — the demon raids are not random, they are a screen for a gathering invasion
- The Song of Heaven exists, and its completion is the key to the campaign's endgame

## The World

*Full reference: nine_worlds.md. Summary below.*

The Nine Worlds are pocket dimensions of varying size and character, each approximately the landmass of Australia, suspended in the Ethereal Plane and connected by the Rainbow Road — a network of interplanar pathways that once allowed travel between them in minutes for those with mythic power. The Road is shut down at the campaign's start and is progressively restored as the PCs access places of power on each world.

At the center is **Nexus**, the hub world: a small continent surrounded by the mists of the Ethereal Plane, home to the great city of Nexus, eight abandoned gate towns (each built around a Rainbow Road terminus), and the Great Ancillary — the ancient temple at the system's center where the celestials grant mythic power to worthy champions.

Above the worlds orbits **Heaven**, an artificial sun of clockwork and magitek, home to three races of celestials — Archons, Angels, and Agathions — who collectively created the Nine Worlds. They are not true gods, but heavenly beings whose collective worship grants divine power equivalent to that of a deity. This is the basis of the Host of Heaven faith, the dominant religion across the Nine Worlds, though it has largely fallen away in the five centuries since the apocalypse.

Below is the **Underworld**, the prison of the Asuras: demonic entities formed from the corrupted souls of mortals across the Nine Worlds. The Underworld was built by the celestials as a containment measure, but the Asuras' lingering influence has plagued the worlds throughout their history. The current crisis began when the ten heroes' ritual to open a direct path to the Underworld triggered the apocalypse, allowing demons to physically enter the worlds for the first and only time. A gate to the Underworld already existed within the Great Ancillary — accessible through the Song of Heaven with the gods' permission — but the ten heroes did not use it. A complete roster of Asura types (9 PF1e SRD types + custom types designed for this campaign) is maintained in `rainbow-road_org-notes.md` under THE UNDERWORLD section. All demon encounters throughout the campaign should draw from this roster.

The eight outer worlds — Diaspola, Tanis, Auzra, Arlune, Vatra, Illium, Nocturne, and Candela — each possess a distinct environment, a primary racial population, a ruling Warlock, and a mythic place of power. Each world's crisis is unique, shaped by the specific way in which its Warlock's corruption has manifested, and the resolution of each crisis depends on whether the PCs can find the means to turn the Warlock back to their former self or must destroy them instead.

## The Antagonists

### Tornsoul (Sarena Tal)
*Human Summoner. The Warden of the Underworld. The campaign's ultimate antagonist.*

Sarena Tal was the youngest of the ten heroes and the one who bore the heaviest burden. When the ten conceived their plan to open a direct path to the Underworld and install a permanent warden, she volunteered — and died in the casting of the ritual that opened the gate. This was anticipated and willing. Each of the other nine heroes granted her a portion of their mythic power, creating a reservoir they hoped would sustain her spirit and protect her from corruption indefinitely. Before descending, she merged her form with her Eidolon, transforming into a being of immense power.

It was not enough. The demons could not break through the mythic reservoir directly, but they found another way — spiritually scratching away portions of the Eidolon's soul over the course of a century to create an opening. Seeing the corruption take hold, Sarena began torturing demons endlessly, consuming their soul essence to maintain her mythic abilities while sinking deeper into madness with each passing century. She is now a being of pure vengeance and ruin, possessed of untold mythic power drawn from her own abilities, the expanded strength of her Eidolon, and five hundred years of consumed demonic essence.

**What she wants:** To return to the Nine Worlds at the head of a demon army and destroy everything — the worlds, Heaven, and the system that produced her. She does not seek dominion. She seeks annihilation.

**Methods:** The Asuras cannot access the gate to the Underworld beneath the Great Ancillary — that portal opens only through the Song of Heaven and with the gods' direct permission — nor do they approach the gate towns. Instead, Tornsoul has effectively hijacked the Rainbow Road through a network of ritual sites prepared by the Beast Blavin, Casomir's corrupted alter ego and her instrument on Nexus. The Beast draws on Casomir's power to gate demons through these sites in numbers limited only by the Beast's capacity. Tornsoul has been amassing forces in secret for years, and as the Rainbow Road reopens world by world, she exploits each restored pathway to move larger groups of demons through the prepared ritual sites outside the city of Nexus. Her access to the system has grown with each world reopened. The demon raids that have plagued Nexus for decades are not the main threat — they are a screen for the gathering army.

**Arc:** Tornsoul is not redeemable. Five centuries of corruption and self-inflicted suffering have burned away every trace of who Sarena Tal once was. The PCs may attempt to reason with her, but success is next to impossible. The only true resolution is destruction — and death in the Underworld is permanent, for Tornsoul and for the PCs alike.

**Power scaling:** Each of the original nine heroes granted Sarena a portion of their mythic power. When a Warlock is turned, they reclaim that portion, weakening Tornsoul. When a Warlock is killed, their portion remains with Tornsoul. The number of Warlocks turned versus killed directly determines the difficulty of the final encounter — a Tornsoul who retains all nine portions is a near-insurmountable challenge, while one stripped of most of that borrowed power is formidable but beatable.

### The Corruption
*Systemic force. The campaign's thematic antagonist.*

The corruption is not a single entity or spell. It is the residual influence of the Asuras — a spiritual contamination that attached itself to each of the ten heroes during the demon war and worked on them slowly over the centuries that followed. Its defining characteristic is precision: it does not impose alien desires, but rather twists each person's strongest quality into their greatest weakness.

Tana White-Lightning's love of knowledge became paranoid hoarding. Garl the Even-Handed's compassion became an obsession with his own suffering. Kerlin Whitemane's inventive brilliance became destructive jealousy. Sona Brightspring's desire to protect her people became paralyzing fear. Tamaron the Just's piety became fanatical delusion. Baselia the Undaunted's courage became despairing obsession. Garm the Honest's trustworthiness became isolating secrecy. Ourania Lachos's scholarly dedication became insane confinement. Casomir Zukra's protective strength became an uncontrollable monster.

The corruption matters to the campaign because the PCs are walking the same path. They will gain mythic power at the same places of power, through the same system, that produced the Warlocks. The question of whether they can succeed where their predecessors failed — whether the system can again produce champions who carry the power without being consumed by it — is the campaign's thematic spine. The corruption is not a mechanical threat to the PCs; it is the lens through which every Warlock encounter gains its weight.

## Key NPCs

### Casomir Zukra / Beast Blavin
*Human Magitek Knight. King of Nexus. The first Warlock the PCs encounter.*

The leader of the original ten heroes, proclaimed King of Nexus after rallying the city's defense against the demon remnants in the war's aftermath — an office he did not seek but has tried to honor for five centuries. Casomir is the only Warlock who understood what was happening to him early enough to act. He used alchemy to proactively contain the corruption, and the attempt partially succeeded — but it created a catastrophic side effect. His corruption manifests as Beast Blavin, a gigantic mutated creature that emerges whenever the transformation pressure builds past a threshold. The Beast knows everything Casomir knows, always finds a way out of confinement, and always kills.

Casomir cannot be turned. He has no lever. He is fully aware that his condition is beyond remedy, and he has made his peace with what must happen. He serves as the campaign's starting point: the PCs' employer, their introduction to the Nine Worlds' crisis, and ultimately their first test, when he asks them to end his life so that the path to the Great Ancillary can open and their journey can begin in earnest.

**Role in the campaign:** Quest-giver for Phase 1. His two quests establish the PCs as capable and confirm them as the chosen champions. His death is the narrative hinge that transitions the campaign from its prologue into the mythic journey proper. His seneschal has standing instructions to open the passage to the Great Ancillary after Casomir falls.

### The Eight Warlocks

Each of the eight outer worlds is ruled by a Warlock — a former hero whose corruption has shaped the world's current crisis. Every Warlock except Casomir and Tornsoul possesses a lever: a person, object, or combination of both that can break through the corruption and restore them to their former self. Turning a Warlock is always more difficult than killing one, but it produces better outcomes for the world in question, provides a potential ally for the endgame, and reclaims the portion of mythic power that Warlock granted to Sarena Tal before her descent — weakening Tornsoul for the final encounter.

### Other Key NPCs

**The Nexian Oracle** — An ancient being who dwells on Nexus and serves as the arbiter of worthiness to walk the Rainbow Road. In former times, candidates seeking mythic power visited the Oracle for confirmation before proceeding to the Great Ancillary. At the campaign's start, Tornsoul has sent demons to block access to the Oracle — not because they can control her, but because they fear what she might confirm. When the PCs reach her, her cryptic message to Casomir sets the endgame of Phase 1 in motion.

**Celestial Representatives** — Emissaries of the three celestial races who created the Nine Worlds. The PCs meet them twice: first at the Great Ancillary after Casomir's death, where they confirm the PCs' mission and grant mythic tier 1 along with the first piece of the Song of Heaven; and again at the Ancillary after the Battle of Nexus, where they grant the final mythic tier and open the portal to the Underworld.

**Khaga** (Diaspola) — A Nixie witch and the true power behind the Snow Queen. She leads the corrupted fey who manipulated Sona Brightspring into freezing Diaspola, and she is the primary antagonist the PCs must contend with on that world. Khaga can be bested by those who understand fey etiquette.

**Tekticula the Unraveler** (Illium) — A demon lord of hate and ruin whose dying soul passed into Tamaron during the final battle of the demon war. Permanently bound to Tamaron's soul, Tekticula cannot leave his body — destroying Tamaron's body kills both, though the demon is briefly released and may attempt possession. The only clean resolution is to fight and destroy Tekticula within Tamaron's mindspace, which requires the Sign of the Host and the Crown of Ascension.

**Haplo the Prophet** (Vatra) — Blackfire's sole surviving apprentice. Effectively runs Vatra on Blackfire's behalf. He created a false religion to legitimize the regime and is kept alive by a magitek life support apparatus that has twisted his body and mind. A central tenet of his religion forbids any mention of the past.

**Garm's Great-Great-Grandson** (Arlune) — Captain of an anti-pirate sandship. The PCs can encounter him through an optional side quest available soon after arriving on Arlune. He carries the Lucky Coin — Garm's lever — and will give it to the PCs if they earn his trust. The coin provides a solid introduction to the Trademaster and bypasses many of Garm's initial reservations.

**Casomir's Seneschal** (Nexus) — Holds standing instructions to open the passage to the Great Ancillary after Casomir's death. The PCs' bridge between Phase 1 and Phase 2 of the campaign.

## Adventure Structure

### Phase 1: Nexus *(Levels 1–4, Mythic Tier 0 → 1)*
*In which the PCs are drawn into the service of a dying king, discover the truth behind the demon raids, and are set on a path that will take them across every world in the system.*

The campaign opens on Nexus. The PCs are residents of the city — its largest and most diverse population center, sustained by magitek foundries and converted farmland districts, defended by Casomir Zukra's forces against the demon raids that have plagued the hub world for decades. The PCs begin at level 1 and advance through three milestone events before leaving Nexus at level 4 with their first mythic tier.

#### Quest 1 — The Foundry Crisis *(Level 1 → 2)*

A magitek foundry critical to the city's survival is malfunctioning. Casomir's forces are stretched thin dealing with the latest round of demon raids, and he needs capable people to investigate what his soldiers cannot spare the numbers to handle. The PCs are hired — whether through direct recruitment, recommendation, or circumstance — and discover that demons have infiltrated the foundry, sabotaging the machinery that keeps the city armed and fed.

This quest introduces the campaign's core elements in microcosm: the magitek infrastructure that sustains civilization across the Nine Worlds, the demon threat that has been grinding it down for centuries, and Casomir Zukra as a ruler who is stretched past his limits. The PCs' success brings them to Casomir's direct attention. Level 2 is awarded on completion.

#### Quest 2 — The Oracle's Prison *(Level 2 → 3)*

Impressed by the PCs' performance, Casomir entrusts them with a more dangerous assignment. He believes a demonic leader is holding the Nexian Oracle hostage — an ancient being who once served as the arbiter of worthiness to walk the Rainbow Road. He needs to know if the Oracle still lives, and he has a question only she can answer.

The reality is more complex than Casomir understands. The demons were sent by Tornsoul to block access to the Oracle, but they are not holding her hostage — they are terrified of her and are simply preventing anyone from reaching her. The PCs must defeat the demonic leader and its forces to clear the way.

When the PCs speak with the Oracle, she gives them a cryptic message to carry back to Casomir. What Casomir hears in this message is the answer to the question he has carried for centuries: whether he and the Beast can ever be separated. The Oracle's answer is no. This is the trigger that sets Casomir's final plan in motion. Level 3 is awarded after meeting the Oracle.

#### Casomir's Death and the Great Ancillary *(Level 3 → 4, Mythic Tier 1)*

Armed with the Oracle's confirmation, Casomir puts into motion the plan he has prepared for this eventuality. He asks the PCs to kill him — or, if they cannot bring themselves to do it, he engineers a situation in which the Beast emerges and they have no choice. However it happens, Casomir falls, and the Beast dies with him.

After Casomir's death, the seneschal carries out standing instructions: the passage to the Great Ancillary is opened for the first time in five hundred years. The PCs enter the ancient temple at the very center of Nexus, step into the rune circle beneath the great magitek orrery, and are taken outside space and time.

There they meet representatives of the three celestial races. The celestials confirm what the Oracle hinted at: the PCs are the champions the Nine Worlds were designed to produce. They grant the PCs their first mythic tier and the first piece of the Song of Heaven — a melodic fragment that, when carried to the gate town of the first world, will reopen that segment of the Rainbow Road. Level 4 is awarded after visiting the Ancillary. The journey begins.

### Phase 2: The Eight Worlds *(Levels 5–19, Mythic Tiers 2–9)*
*In which the PCs travel the Rainbow Road from world to world, confronting or redeeming each Warlock, gathering the pieces of the Song of Heaven, and progressively restoring the interplanar network that once connected the Nine Worlds — while a demon army gathers behind them on Nexus.*

#### The Song of Heaven

Each mythic place of power across the eight outer worlds holds a piece of a melody. When the PCs access a place of power, they receive that world's melodic fragment — a sonic key that opens a specific segment of the Rainbow Road to the next world in the sequence. The progressive addition of these fragments forms a structured composition called the Song of Heaven. The complete Song, assembled from all eight fragments plus the initial piece granted at the Ancillary, is the key that opens the final gate to Heaven in Phase 3.

The Song of Heaven serves three functions in the campaign. Mechanically, it structures the world order — each melodic key opens only the next world in the fixed sequence, preventing the PCs from skipping ahead. Narratively, it is the physical manifestation of the PCs' growing connection to the system the celestials built, a testament to their journey that accumulates beauty as it accumulates power. Thematically, it is the counterpoint to the corruption — where the Warlocks' power twisted them, the Song represents the alignment of purpose and worthiness that the system was meant to produce.

#### Per-World Structure

Each world follows a common structural pattern, though the specific content varies widely:

The PCs arrive via the Rainbow Road segment opened by the previous world's melodic key. They enter a world in crisis — each shaped by the unique way its Warlock's corruption has manifested — and must orient themselves, build relationships with the local population, and understand the situation before they can act.

The investigation phase is where the turning mechanic comes into play. Each Warlock possesses a lever — a person, object, or combination that can break through the corruption. Finding the lever is never straightforward: it requires exploration, negotiation, side quests, and in some cases foreknowledge from earlier worlds. The PCs can always choose to confront the Warlock directly without the lever, but doing so means a fight to the death rather than redemption, with consequences that ripple forward through the corruption score, the ally-tracking mechanic, and the state of the world left behind.

The world arc culminates in a confrontation with the Warlock — either a turning scene if the PCs have secured the lever, or a boss encounter if they have not. Afterward, the PCs access the world's mythic place of power, gain a mythic tier, and receive the next piece of the Song of Heaven. The consequences of their choices play out on the world they leave behind: a turned Warlock begins rebuilding, while a killed Warlock leaves a power vacuum whose effects persist into the endgame.

#### Background Escalation

Throughout Phase 2, the situation on Nexus deteriorates. Tornsoul exploits each new segment of the Rainbow Road as it opens, using the restored pathways to move larger groups of demons through prepared ritual sites outside the city. The demon raids on Nexus grow in frequency and scale. Casomir's death removed the Beast — and with it, Tornsoul's primary gating instrument — but the Rainbow Road itself now provides alternative routes.

The PCs become aware of this escalation gradually. Reports from Nexus reach them on the worlds they visit. Returning to Nexus between worlds reveals a city under increasing strain. By the time Candela is complete and the full Rainbow Road is restored, the demon army on Nexus has reached critical mass. The Battle of Nexus is imminent.

### Phase 3: The Endgame *(Level 19 → 20, Mythic Tier 9 → 10)*
*In which the PCs rally their allies, fight the Battle of Nexus, complete the Song of Heaven, receive their final mythic power, and descend into the Underworld to end Tornsoul's reign.*

#### Rally the Allies

After completing the eight worlds, the PCs return to Nexus with the full Rainbow Road restored. Before the demon army launches its assault, the PCs have the opportunity to travel back to each liberated world and rally allies for the coming battle.

The strength of each world's contribution depends on the outcome of the PCs' earlier choices. A world whose Warlock was turned contributes its full allied force — the Warlock themselves, their followers, and whatever military strength the world can muster. A world whose Warlock was killed contributes reduced forces or none at all, depending on the state of the power vacuum left behind. The ally-tracking mechanic quantifies these contributions and determines the total strength available for the Battle of Nexus.

#### The Battle of Nexus *(Level 20)*

The demon army — amassed throughout the campaign in secret — launches its assault on the city of Nexus. This is the penultimate confrontation of the campaign: a mass battle in which the PCs and their allies face the full weight of Tornsoul's gathered forces.

The scale and outcome of the battle are shaped by the ally strength the PCs have assembled. With strong allies, the battle is hard-fought but winnable, and the PCs can focus on the most dangerous threats while their forces hold the line. With few allies, the battle becomes a desperate holding action in which every loss is felt and the city's survival is genuinely in question.

Level 20 is awarded on completion of the Battle of Nexus. The demon army is broken, but the source remains — and the PCs know where it is.

#### The Final Meeting *(Mythic Tier 10)*

After the Battle of Nexus, the PCs return to the Great Ancillary one last time. They carry the complete Song of Heaven — nine melodic fragments assembled across the eight outer worlds and the initial piece granted at the Ancillary itself. Playing the Song opens the gate to Heaven.

The celestial representatives appear for the second and final time. They acknowledge the PCs' journey, the choices they made, and the allies they gathered or failed to save. They grant the PCs their tenth and final mythic tier — the full measure of power the system was designed to produce — and offer to open a portal to the Underworld when the PCs are ready.

#### The Descent

The PCs enter the Underworld at level 20, mythic tier 10. They are, at this moment, the most powerful mortal beings the Nine Worlds have ever produced — which is exactly what the system was built to create.

The Underworld is a featureless grey plain where powerful demon lords have carved strongholds from the barren landscape. The PCs must fight through progressively more powerful groups of demons to reach Tornsoul's stronghold, where the final encounter of the campaign awaits.

Death in the Underworld is permanent. No resurrection magic functions in this place. This is true for Tornsoul and for the PCs alike. The difficulty of the final encounter is determined by the corruption score — a Tornsoul stripped of her borrowed mythic portions by turned Warlocks is a formidable but beatable opponent, while a Tornsoul who retains all nine portions is a near-insurmountable challenge that may well claim PCs' lives even in victory.

When Tornsoul falls, her death saps significant strength from the remaining demons and leaves them leaderless. The Underworld endures — its nature is too deeply woven into the fabric of the Nine Worlds to be unmade — but its denizens will be unable to affect the realms again for centuries. The Song of Heaven carries the PCs home.

## Possible Endings

Four endings, determined by four axes: the **corruption score** (how many Warlocks were turned versus killed), **ally strength** (the forces assembled for the Battle of Nexus), **PC survival** (who lives through the Underworld), and **world restoration** (the state of the worlds left behind). These axes are not independent — a high corruption score tends to produce strong allies and restored worlds, while a low score tends toward weakness on all fronts — but they can diverge in ways that produce distinct outcomes.

### Ending 1: The Worlds Reborn
*The system works. The champions it was built to produce have arrived at last.*

All or most Warlocks turned. The corruption score is low. Tornsoul has been stripped of most of the mythic power the original heroes granted her, and the final encounter, while dangerous, is within the PCs' capacity. The Battle of Nexus was a decisive victory, fought alongside turned Warlocks and the full strength of the liberated worlds. The PCs survive the Underworld.

In the aftermath, the Nine Worlds begin to heal. Turned Warlocks resume leadership of their realms, working to undo the damage of five centuries of corruption. The Rainbow Road is fully restored and functioning as the celestials intended. Heaven begins to repair itself. The system the celestials built has, after its greatest failure, produced its greatest success — champions who walked the full path, gained the full power, and were not consumed by it. The Song of Heaven endures as a testament to what was accomplished.

*Conditions: 6 or more Warlocks turned. Battle of Nexus won decisively. At least one PC survives the Underworld.*

### Ending 2: The Long Dawn
*The threat is ended. The cost was real. The recovery will take generations.*

A mixed result — some Warlocks turned, some killed. Tornsoul was weakened but not crippled, and the final encounter extracted a genuine price. The Battle of Nexus was won, but not without significant losses. Some worlds are rebuilding under their restored leaders; others are leaderless, their Warlocks dead and their populations left to find their own way forward.

The Rainbow Road functions, but the Nine Worlds are diminished. The connections between worlds that once sustained a thriving interplanar civilization will take decades to rebuild, and some worlds may never fully recover from the power vacuums left by slain Warlocks. The PCs return home as heroes, but the victory is bittersweet — they know what the worlds could have been, and they know which choices made the difference.

*Conditions: 3–5 Warlocks turned. Battle of Nexus won. Tornsoul defeated.*

### Ending 3: The Price of Victory
*Victory is a fact, not a feeling.*

Few Warlocks turned. Tornsoul retained most of her borrowed mythic power, and the final encounter was a brutal affair. The Battle of Nexus was a near thing — fought with few allies, every loss felt, the city's survival in genuine doubt. Some PCs may have died permanently in the Underworld. The worlds the PCs passed through are largely shattered: power vacuums, devastated populations, broken infrastructure with no one left to repair it.

The demon threat is ended. Tornsoul is destroyed. The Underworld's denizens are weakened and leaderless. But the Nine Worlds that survive bear little resemblance to what they were before the Warlocks, and less still to what they were before the apocalypse. The Rainbow Road is open, connecting ruins to ruins. The PCs who survive carry the full weight of every choice they made — and every choice they did not.

*Conditions: 0–2 Warlocks turned. Battle of Nexus won at heavy cost. Tornsoul defeated, possibly with PC deaths.*

### Ending 4: The Silence
*The Song of Heaven falls silent.*

The worst outcome. The Battle of Nexus was barely survived — or was not survived at all, with the PCs fighting through a city already falling to reach the Ancillary and the Underworld beyond. Tornsoul is at maximum power: all nine borrowed portions intact, five centuries of consumed demonic essence, and the full strength of her merged Eidolon. A total party kill is a real possibility.

If the PCs win, it costs everything. Most or all of them die permanently in the Underworld. The Nine Worlds are devastated — the Battle of Nexus left the hub world in ruins, the outer worlds are leaderless and broken, and the allies who fought and died did so for a victory that none of them will live to see. The demon threat is ended, but the bare fact of survival is all that remains.

If the PCs fall, the Nine Worlds fall with them. Tornsoul's army overruns what is left, Heaven is destroyed, and the system the celestials built is extinguished. The Song of Heaven is silenced. Nothing endures.

*Conditions: 0–1 Warlocks turned. Battle of Nexus barely survived or lost. Tornsoul at full power. TPK possible.*

## Key Mechanics to Design

The following mechanics are central to the campaign's structure. Each requires detailed design before the DM Guide can be written. They are summarized here with their purpose, core function, and relationship to other systems.

### 1. The Song of Heaven / Rainbow Road Progression

The campaign's structural backbone. Each mythic place of power grants the PCs a melodic fragment — a sonic key that opens the Rainbow Road to the next world in a fixed sequence. The complete Song, assembled from all nine fragments (eight outer worlds plus the initial piece from the Ancillary), opens the gate to Heaven for the final mythic tier grant.

**Design requirements:** How the melodic fragments are presented to the PCs at each place of power. Whether the Song has a performative component (do the PCs need to play or sing it?). How the gate towns respond when a new segment opens. Whether fragments can be lost or corrupted.

**Rainbow Road Travel Model (Confirmed):** Hub-and-spoke. The Rainbow Road connects each world to Nexus only — there is no direct world-to-world travel during the campaign. After completing a world chapter, the party returns to Nexus via the Rainbow Road, then travels to the next gate town to perform the Song and open the next segment. The party cannot return to Nexus mid-chapter; once on a world, they must resolve it before the Road carries them back. Previously opened segments remain active but the party cannot travel to previously visited worlds during Phase 2 — the Road only opens forward. In Phase 3, the fully restored Road allows free travel between all nine worlds for the Rally the Allies sequence and beyond. This model provides periodic Nexus interludes (resupply, narrative updates on the escalating demon threat, check-ins with Maranthe) that build investment in the hub world before the Battle of Nexus. Arlune's role as an inter-world trade hub is historically accurate and becomes relevant again once the Road is fully restored in Phase 3 and the epilogue.

### 2. Mythic Progression

Tier 1 is granted at the Great Ancillary. Tiers 2–9 are gained at each world's mythic place of power, one per world. Tier 10 is granted by the celestials after the Battle of Nexus.

**Design requirements:** Which mythic paths are available and appropriate for the campaign's tone. Whether specific places of power grant specific mythic abilities or whether players choose freely. How mythic power interacts with the Song of Heaven thematically and mechanically.

### 3. Corruption Mechanic

A hidden score tracked by the DM. Each Warlock turned reduces the score; each Warlock killed increases it. The score determines Tornsoul's power level in the final encounter and maps to the four possible endings.

**Design requirements:** The specific score thresholds that map to each ending. Whether factors beyond turning/killing affect the score (e.g., collateral damage on a world, treatment of NPCs, choices within each world arc). How the score translates mechanically to Tornsoul's stat block — reduced hit points, fewer abilities, lower mythic tier, or some combination.

### 4. Warlock Turning Framework

Each turnable Warlock has a unique lever. Turning is always harder than killing but produces better outcomes across all four axes (corruption, allies, world restoration, and Tornsoul's power level). Cross-world dependencies add complexity — the Illium-to-Nocturne chain and Candela's foreknowledge requirement mean that choices on earlier worlds constrain options on later ones.

**Design requirements:** Per-Warlock turning scenes (narrative, not combat). How the PCs learn about levers — what information is available on each world, what requires cross-world knowledge, and what can be discovered through investigation alone. The mechanical consequences of turning versus killing for each specific world.

### 5. Ally-Tracking Mechanic

Each world can contribute allied forces to the Battle of Nexus. A turned Warlock produces full allied strength; a killed Warlock produces reduced or no contribution. The total determines the scale and difficulty of the Battle.

**Design requirements:** Per-world ally contributions (what each world sends, in what strength). A simple tracking system the DM can maintain across the campaign. How ally strength translates to mechanical outcomes in the Battle of Nexus — fewer enemies, additional NPC combatants, narrative advantages, or a combination.

### 6. Battle of Nexus System

A mass battle that must feel consequential without becoming a wargame. The PCs are the central actors, but the allied forces around them determine the battle's shape.

**Design requirements:** Whether to use PF1e mass combat rules, an abstracted narrative system, or a hybrid. How the PCs' individual actions interact with the larger battle. Scaling based on ally strength — the battle should feel different at full strength versus minimal allies. Duration and pacing within a session.

### 7. Milestone Leveling

An explicit schedule: levels 1–4 on Nexus, two levels per world for worlds 1–7, one level on Candela, level 20 after the Battle of Nexus. Twenty levels across ten phases.

**Design requirements:** The specific milestones within each world that trigger each level-up. Whether the two per-world levels are awarded at fixed points (e.g., mid-world and end-of-world) or flexibly based on content. How to handle the single-level Candela arc — is it shorter by design, or does it compensate with mythic power and narrative density?

### 8. World Restoration Tracking

The state of each world after the Warlock confrontation feeds into the ending determination. A turned Warlock begins rebuilding; a killed Warlock leaves a power vacuum.

**Design requirements:** Per-world restoration states (turned, killed, and any intermediate outcomes). How these states manifest narratively during the Rally the Allies phase and in the epilogue. Whether world restoration has mechanical effects beyond the ally-tracking system.

## Cross-World Dependencies

The following dependencies are critical to the campaign's design. They create meaningful consequences for the PCs' choices and reward thorough investigation across worlds.

| Dependency | Detail | Player-Facing? |

| **Illium → Nocturne** | Tamaron must be turned on Illium for Baselia to be turnable on Nocturne. If Tamaron is killed, Baselia is unturnable — the PCs must fight her. This is the campaign's most significant cross-world consequence. | No — discovered through play or NPC dialogue |
| **Candela foreknowledge** | Locating Ourania's personal sphere on Candela requires information that only previously turned Warlocks can provide.
