# DARK GOSPEL
## Player's Guide to the Kingdom of Artaxarta

*A setting guide for players in the Dark Gospel campaign*

---

> **A note to players:** This guide contains everything your character would know as a native of the Kingdom of Artaxarta. It does not contain secrets. Some things described here are incomplete or partly wrong — that is intentional, because your character's knowledge is also incomplete and partly wrong. You will learn more as you play.

---

# WELCOME TO THE REALM

## What Makes Artaxarta

Artaxarta has stood for over a thousand years. It was here before the heartlanders came, before the Xoshans built their roads and planting colonies in the northern valleys, before the Bandorian Empire folded this kingdom into the great machine of its civilization and then left it to find its own way when the machine broke down. The Artaxartoi survived all of it — the wars, the occupations, the long slow pressure that turned most of the ancient peoples of the east into reflections of their conquerors. Artaxarta did not.

The country you were born into is a fusion of native and heartlander tradition, and it has chosen its inheritances deliberately. From the ancient tribes it kept the goddess, the horse, the high pastures, and the understanding that loyalty — to family, to tribe, to king — is not sentiment but survival. From the Xoshans it kept the roads, the terraced farms, the city planning, and the professional military. From the Bandorian Empire it kept the legions, the civil service, and the habit of thinking of itself as a civilization rather than a collection of clans. It discarded the things it did not want. When the Empire dissolved, Artaxarta was one of the few successor states that could look at the wreckage and feel, genuinely, that it had been managing its own affairs all along.

This gives Artaxartoi people a particular quality — a pride that does not announce itself, a confidence that does not require external validation. Your kingdom has faced down every attempt at conquest in living memory. The culture prizes this. You have grown up knowing that the mountains do not yield, and neither do the people who live in them.

## The Current Moment

For the past three months, the **Brotherhood of Light** — the kingdom's most celebrated holy order, devoted to the goddess Ilura and charged with suppressing the cult of Umgul — has been under siege at its mountain fortress in the southern highlands. The cult that is laying siege to it is larger, better organized, and more persistent than any cult activity in recent memory. It has not broken off. It has not been driven away. The Brotherhood has not asked for help, and the crown has not formally announced that it is providing any.

The official account, distributed through the fifteen ministries, is that an organized resurgence of Umgul worship in the southern highlands is being addressed. Officials speak of "isolated extremists" and "a manageable disturbance." Recruitment notices for the legions have gone up in the five cities. The Kataphractoi have been on elevated readiness for eight weeks. Orestes Sarkhazi, Lord of the Magisterium, gave a lecture at the Scholars' Hall three weeks ago on the historical patterns of cult resurgence and why they always fail. The audience was large. The applause was genuine.

Most people find these explanations adequate. A few people find them slightly thin and have not said so out loud. The distinction between those two groups is one you are about to understand.

## Life in Artaxarta

Artaxarta is home to roughly four million people distributed unevenly across its network of mountain valleys, high pastures, and five major cities. The population is one of the most homogenous in the eastern kingdoms — almost entirely descended from the fusion of native Artaxartoi tribal stock and the Xoshan and Durasi settlers who came during the colonial and Imperial periods — but it has sorted itself into distinct cultural registers depending on where it lives.

**In the cities and valley towns:** Urban Artaxartoi life is commercial, cosmopolitan, and organized around the rhythms of a sophisticated economy. The great markets of Alcidea, Phocis, and the other northern cities handle agricultural exports, horse trading, and the regional commerce that flows through the mountain passes. Citizens in these areas are generally educated, legally literate, and accustomed to institutional life — the ministries, the legions, the guilds, the temple organizations. They play birok in the stadiums, argue about politics in the taverns, and take genuine pride in the complexity and antiquity of the civilization they live inside.

**In the valley farming communities:** The agricultural heart of the kingdom is organized around the twelve river systems, with hundreds of small towns and villages supporting the terraced farming economy. Life here moves more slowly than in the cities and is organized around the growing season, the local temple, and the clan affiliations that still structure social obligation even among people who have lived in settled communities for generations. People here are hardworking and hospitable to travelers; they are also deeply attuned to the rhythms of their valley and will notice when something is wrong long before the cities do.

**In the highland uplands:** The high pastures above the treeline are home to the herding clans — the communities that most closely preserve the ancient ways of the native Artaxartoi. Life here is pastoral, traditional, and organized around the authority of the **Sadars**, the tribal leaders who serve as cultural nobility and the king's representatives in the uplands. Highland communities are clannish, superstitious by valley standards, and deeply suspicious of outsiders; they are also bound by ironclad traditions of hospitality and guest rights that make them unexpectedly welcoming once those thresholds are crossed. The old names for the goddess are still in use here. The old rites are still performed.

## The Jinsoona

The **Jinsoona** is the great midsummer festival of Artaxarta — the kingdom's most beloved celebration, marking the mythic victory of the goddess Ilura over the forces of Umgul in ancient legend. Cities and towns across the kingdom celebrate for three days and nights: markets, games, torchlight processions, horse races, dramatic performances of the old myths, and communal feasting that crosses every class boundary. For those three days, the distinction between noble and commoner is suspended, and the entire kingdom shares a table.

This year's Jinsoona was the night the king died.

# THE WORLD AROUND YOU

## Geography

Artaxarta occupies a network of high mountain valleys fed by twelve river systems flowing from the **Yashim Mountains** — an eastern range so massive and so impassable that the frontier states beyond it might as well be another world. The terrain descends from these eastern peaks through a series of highland valleys and upland pastures into the more accessible northern river valleys that hold the five major cities, then rises again to the west and south where the mountains form the kingdom's borders with its neighbors.

**The Northern River Valleys** are the agricultural and commercial heart of the kingdom. The soil here is exceptionally fertile — a consequence of thousands of years of sediment from the mountain rivers — and the valley floors are dense with farms, orchards, and the terraced hillside fields that were one of the Bandorian Empire's lasting contributions to this landscape. The five major cities are all located in this region, planned in the old Xoshan style with regular streets and public forums, though centuries of growth have layered far more organic development on top of that original grid. The royal court, the ministries, the legions' main garrisons, and most of the kingdom's institutional life are concentrated here.

**The Central Highlands** occupy the mid-elevation territory between the northern valleys and the high southern mountains. Mixed farming and herding communities predominate here, with a culture that sits somewhere between the cosmopolitan northern cities and the traditional pastoral life of the uplands. The central highlands have historically been a bridge region — where valley culture and upland culture negotiate their differences — and the people here tend to be unusually skilled at that kind of negotiation. Several of the most respected Sadars come from this region.

**The Southern Mountains** are Brotherhood territory. The Brotherhood of Light's headquarters — a mountain fortress called the **Priory of the White Gates** — is located in the southern highlands, as are several smaller monasteries and chapter houses. The terrain here is the most rugged in the accessible kingdom: steep ridgelines, narrow passes, and valleys that hold snow well into summer. Communities are sparse and isolated. The Brotherhood's presence has, over generations, given this region a reputation for piety and austere living that its inhabitants wear with quiet satisfaction.

**The High Pastures** stretch across the elevated interior of the kingdom, above the tree line but below the permanent snowpack. This is Sadar and Zavra-Prag territory — the homeland of the herding clans, organized around the seasonal movement of livestock through the high meadows. Twenty-three tribes are recognized by the crown, and most of them maintain their primary cultural presence here even if individual families have moved to the cities over the generations. The high pastures are beautiful, exposed, and governed by the old ways. The people here know the names that precede the names on the official maps.

**The Eastern Yashim Border** is virtually uninhabited by Artaxartoi standards. The terrain approaching the Yashim peaks becomes progressively more hostile, and only a handful of border clans maintain permanent settlements in these valleys. Beyond the mountains are the **Kelish States** — seventy-two independent tribes that have been in continuous internal conflict since the dissolution of the Bandorian Empire. No meaningful contact with Artaxarta; their armies are said to be large and deeply disorganized.

**The Western Valleys** are wetter, more heavily forested than the eastern regions, and share a long border with **Asta** to the southwest — a neighbor with whom relations are permanently complicated by religion. The **Astans** worship Altair, whom the Artaxartoi identify with Umgul. Cultural tension along this border is a permanent feature; diplomatic relations exist but are cold.

## Alcidea

The capital has stood for over eight hundred years, and it shows — not in decay, but in the layered depth of a city that has been continuously important for a very long time. The original Xoshan colonial grid is still visible in the Old City's regular street pattern, but every generation since has built on top of it, around it, and occasionally through it, producing a capital that rewards those who know how to read it and disorients those who don't.

**The Palace District** occupies the elevated center of the Old City. The royal compound is not a single building but a collection of structures accumulated across centuries — audience halls, private apartments, administrative offices, stables, gardens, and the barracks of the Kataphractoi guard — all enclosed within a wall that dates to the Xoshan period. Access to the Palace District requires either an invitation or a demonstrated connection to one of the fifteen ministries. Citizens can approach the main gate; actually entering requires the right names.

**The Temple Quarter** lies immediately south of the Palace District, organized around the **Great Temple of Ilura** — the dominant religious site in the capital. The temple complex includes the main sanctuary with its famous mounted statue of the goddess, smaller shrines to each of the Twelve Divines, the residences of the senior clergy, and the archives of the temple library. The temple quarter is also where the **Processional Way** begins — the broad avenue used for festivals and official ceremonies, lined with stone relief carvings of scenes from the goddess's mythology.

**The Magisterium Hall** is a converted Bandorian-era building in the administrative district east of the Palace — heavy stone, severe lines, still bearing the faded insignia of the Imperial scholarly corps on its lintel. The Magisterium employs several hundred scholars, researchers, and administrative staff, and its library is the largest in the eastern kingdoms. Lord Sarkhazi keeps offices on the second floor.

**The Great Market** extends along both banks of the **Araxes River** as it passes through the city — a continuous commercial district of permanent shops, temporary stalls, warehouses, and the trading halls of the major guilds. On any given morning it is loud, crowded, and completely indifferent to whatever political events are unfolding in the Palace District. The Great Market has operated continuously through every crisis in the capital's history. Most citizens find this reassuring.

**The Legionary Barracks** occupy the northwestern quarter of the city — a self-contained military compound housing the garrison legion and the administrative corps of the Eastern Legions. The **Stadia of King Ariston** adjoins the barracks on its eastern side, giving the soldiers a convenient venue for the birok games that are played there every week during the season.

**The Gate of the Twelve** is the main southern entrance to the Old City, named for the twelve relief figures of the Divines carved into its facade during the Bandorian period. The Divines are depicted in their heartlander forms, which the Artaxartoi have always found slightly incorrect — Ilura in particular looks nothing like the goddess in Artaxartoi religious tradition, being depicted as serene rather than mounted and armed — but the gate is beloved anyway and has been since anyone can remember.

**Mazrok** looms above the city from the west — the ancient citadel built directly into the cliff face, its walls visible from almost anywhere in Alcidea. It has fallen once in history, by a mountaineering raid in the Bandorian period. Before and since, it has been impregnable. The sight of it, especially at dawn when the cliff face catches the first light and the citadel walls glow briefly gold, is one of those things Alcideans take for granted and visitors find overwhelming.

## The Crown

Artaxarta is an absolute monarchy, and its people are broadly comfortable with this — a product of history and of the quality of the monarchs who have held the throne since the kingdom's founding. The Alician line has produced effective rulers for over eight centuries, and the cultural memory of what happened to kingdoms that experimented with more democratic arrangements (specifically: the chaotic, bloody dissolution of the Xoshan state, which the Artaxartoi observed from a very short distance) has never fully faded.

The king's authority is administered through **fifteen ministries**, each led by a minister selected by the crown and serving at the crown's pleasure. The ministries manage everything from the legions to the tax system to the courts to the official regulation of magical practice. They are deliberately lean organizations — the Artaxartoi preference for function over form is visible in every ministry's staffing — and they are subject to oversight by the **Office of the Special Inspector**, one of the fifteen, which is specifically charged with investigating corruption among the other fourteen.

King **Telamon Aristedes Alcimidias II** was the kingdom's ruler until this year. His death on the night of the Jinsoona left the throne to his son and heir, **Prince Eurimedos Toxamones Alicimidia**, who is eight years old. The regency arrangements are being formalized. In the immediate crisis, military and administrative authority rests with the royal council, including **General Tanos Mitradanes Heskalo** — the Commanding General of the Royal Guard, known to the public as the Laughing Lion for the ease with which he presents even difficult news — and the other senior ministers.

The **Kataphractoi** — the King's Heavy Horse, the royal cavalry guard — are the most visible symbol of royal authority in the capital. Nine hundred soldiers in their distinctive silver-and-gold scale armor, drawing from the noble families and trained almost from birth, they are the military elite of Artaxarta and the envy of every cavalry commander in the eastern kingdoms. In the current crisis, a select unit of Kataphractoi has been assigned to the prince's personal guard, supplemented by other sworn members of the royal household.

## The Crisis

**What you observe:** The Brotherhood of Light's fortress in the southern mountains has been under siege for approximately three months. This is unprecedented — the Brotherhood has defended against cult activity for generations, but never at this scale, and never for this long. The crown is mobilizing; recruitment has accelerated; the ministries are in something resembling a controlled heightened state. Reliable information from the southern highlands is scarce, and the official accounts have a quality of studied reassurance that your instincts may be reading differently than your reason.

**What the official account says:** A resurgence of organized Umgul worship in the southern highlands — larger than previous instances, but categorically the same kind of threat. The Brotherhood is holding. The legions are being positioned. The situation is being managed. Citizens are encouraged to report suspicious activity and to continue their normal lives.

**What your character does not know:** The full geographic extent of the cult's presence in the kingdom. The identity of the person directing the campaign. The true nature of the entity the cult actually serves. Why the cult made the Brotherhood its first target rather than the capital. Whether the official account is wrong because those giving it lack information, or for another reason.

## Neighboring States

**Kentana** lies to the south — a wealthy mountain kingdom known for its gemstone trade and its reputation for discretion. Artaxarta's oldest commercial partner and among its least politically complicated neighbors. Kentana is secluded, secretive in matters of government, and has never shown the slightest interest in the internal affairs of anyone else.

**Asta** lies to the southwest. The Artaxartoi regard Asta with the specific, contained hostility of neighbors who have been religious enemies for longer than anyone can precisely calculate. Astans worship Altair. In Artaxarta, Altair and Umgul are understood as aspects of the same dark divinity — the enemy of the goddess, the enemy of existence. Diplomatic relations exist; the border is quiet; the cultural gulf is as wide as a mountain range.

**Morna** borders Artaxarta to the west — a militaristic dictatorship with an ongoing territorial claim to a large portion of western Artaxarta and a history of losing border wars over it. Most Artaxartoi regard Morna as dangerous and faintly ridiculous, which is an attitude that requires updating periodically when the Mornai do something more dangerous than ridiculous. The practice of large-scale slavery in Morna is considered a mark of moral bankruptcy by the Artaxartoi, and this view is not diplomatic code — it is genuine.

**Kyrexos** to the northwest is Artaxarta's most significant rival — a Delian state of roughly equal size and strength, with powerful northern allies and an unstable history of border conflict with the kingdom. The current king believed, before his death, that Kyrexos was preparing an invasion. Relations have been in a watchful deterioration for the past decade. Despite the tension, both states share cultural roots through their Delian heritage and are not without a grudging mutual respect.

**Kesh** to the north is large, populous, and culturally foreign in ways that Artaxartoi find genuinely difficult to parse. The two states have strong trading ties and a generally peaceful relationship; social relations between Artaxartoi and Keshites are pleasant and slightly baffled on both sides.

# SOCIETY AND CULTURE

## The Brotherhood of Light

The **Brotherhood of Light** is the most admired institution in Artaxarta that you will never be able to join. It is an order of 250 sworn adepts — warrior-monks devoted to Ilura, the goddess of the kingdom — and it is open only to male orphans donated to the temple as children, who must survive a series of trials that make the legionary training program look welcoming. The trials include free-climbing **Mount Sikosa** — one of the largest peaks in the known world — without equipment; surviving forty days and nights in the wilderness without shelter or supplies; and a barefoot pilgrimage from the order's mountain headquarters to the **Temple of the Twelve Divines** in Kal-Duras. Those who complete these trials begin five more years of intensive military and monastic training. Those who complete that begin another series of secret trials. Fewer than one in ten aspirants reach adepthood.

The result of this extraordinary filtration is an organization of 250 people who are, by any reasonable measure, among the most capable warriors in the world — and who are bound by a code of conduct that makes them nearly as famous for their moral weight as for their martial prowess. The word of a member of the Brotherhood is considered unimpeachable. Their chivalry is not a performance; it is the result of a selection process that eliminates those for whom it would be.

In practical terms, the Brotherhood serves as the kingdom's primary institution for the suppression of Umgul worship. They have done this work for generations. The current siege of their fortress is, therefore, not merely a military problem — it is a challenge to the institution that has made Artaxarta safe from the cult in every living memory.

The Brotherhood's insignia is the golden lance cross — an Artaxartoi letter shaped like a "P" with a crossbar, in gold on white. Their armor is white-enameled plate trimmed with real gold. They are recognizable from a considerable distance and intended to be.

## The Temple of the Twelve Divines

Every citizen of Artaxarta lives within the cultural orbit of the **Temple of the Twelve Divines**, but the Artaxartoi relationship with the Twelve Divines has never been a simple transplant of heartlander theology. When the Xoshans and later the Bandorians brought their religion to this region, they encountered a faith already deeply rooted — and the result was a synthesis that kept the names of the heartlander gods while filling them with older meanings.

**Ilura** is the dominant deity of Artaxarta and the object of near-universal veneration. In the heartlander tradition, she is a goddess of civilization, wisdom, and craft. In Artaxarta, she is a warrior mounted on a warhorse, carrying a bow, who leads righteous people to victory and ensures the fertility of both fields and families. The mounted statue in the Great Temple of Alcidea shows a goddess that heartlander visitors sometimes fail to recognize; local worshipers find this satisfying. **Mora** and **Uriel** also receive significant devotion, as they did among the Xoshans whose descendants make up a portion of the population. The other nine of the Twelve receive their due observances, with individual families and guilds maintaining particular attachments to various patrons.

**Clerics** occupy a distinct position in Artaxartoi society: they are not made through faith but chosen by the gods. When a cleric appears — manifesting genuine divine power, healing the sick, calling on the protection of the Divines in ways that work — it is understood as an act of divine will, not as the result of years of priestly study. The temple trains its priests in theology, ritual, and administration; most of those priests never develop the abilities associated with clerical power. Those who do are regarded with an awe that ordinary priesthood does not command. To meet a true cleric in Artaxarta is to understand, viscerally, that the gods are paying attention.

**The Jinsoona** is the most sacred holiday in the calendar — the midsummer festival marking Ilura's mythic victory over Umgul. Three days of celebration, beginning at sunset on the longest day. The festival includes the **Great Procession** through the Processional Way of Alcidea, communal feasting in every district of every city, horse races dedicated to the goddess, and dramatic performances of the ancient texts. The entire kingdom observes it, regardless of which specific Divine a family primarily worships.

## The Old Ways

Before the heartlanders brought the Twelve Divines, the native Artaxartoi worshiped two: the mother-goddess **Lhakmana**, who protected people from harm and ensured fertility, and the dark divinity later identified with Umgul, who was associated with sorcery, corruption, and evil. When the heartlanders arrived and the synthesis that produced modern Artaxartoi religion occurred, Lhakmana was identified with Ilura — an identification most Artaxartoi consider accurate, since the two divinities are functionally the same figure. But the older traditions associated with Lhakmana did not disappear. They persist, especially in the highland communities, as the **old ways**: a set of ritual practices that predate heartlander contact and that the temple has never seriously attempted to suppress, because they are understood — correctly — as an older expression of the same religion.

The old ways include seasonal offerings at natural sites: high meadow cairns, mountain springs, ancient stone markers. They include animal sacrifice practices not seen in the heartlander tradition. They include the use of the old names — Lhakmana rather than Ilura, the ancient epithets and invocations that survive in the highland clan traditions. And they include the practitioners called **Druids**: figures who stand at the boundary between the human world and the natural one, who know the old rites, who speak to the land in terms the land recognizes.

Druids are respected throughout Artaxarta, including in the cities, precisely because what they practice is understood as ancient Artaxartoi tradition. A druid who arrives in a village to conduct the spring planting rites is not viewed as a suspicious outsider; they are viewed as a continuation of something the village has always done. The old ways are not separate from the faith of the Twelve Divines — they are, in Artaxartoi understanding, its roots.

## The Legions

The **Legonis Hykyrianos** — the Eastern Legions — are the professional infantry backbone of Artaxarta's military, descended from the Bandorian Imperial legions left behind when the Empire dissolved. They are organized in the traditional style: cohorts of two hundred soldiers, each commanded by a **centurion** identifiable by the distinctive headgear made from a mountain lion pelt. Ten cohorts make a legion, commanded by a **legate**. Seven full legions are currently active.

The Legonis carry the traditions of their Imperial predecessors with unusual fidelity — the heraldry, the drilling, the emphasis on discipline and ceremony — but adapted for Artaxartoi terrain and culture. Their long straight blades and throwing javelins are supplemented by the small tribal dagger that every Artaxartoi soldier carries as a sidearm, and their tactics are built for the mountain passes and valley floors of the kingdom rather than the plains where the Imperial legions cut their teeth.

The **Zavra-Prag** — the tribal auxiliaries — is the bulk of the kingdom's actual military force: clan-organized militia units called **kulaks**, each drawn from a particular tribe and maintained at a readiness level that varies dramatically depending on the clan. A city-based clan may field hundreds of soldiers with armor as heavy as the royal guard's; a small highland clan may send thirty men with spears and slings. What they share is loyalty — to their tribe, to their Sadar, and to the king — and when that loyalty is properly invoked, they come in numbers that exceed any formal muster roll.

The **Kataphractoi** are the elite: nine hundred men and women in silver-and-gold scale armor on the largest horses in the known world. Their training begins at ten years old, their equipment is sometimes a family inheritance spanning generations, and their charge has broken lines that didn't think they could be broken. They are, in a technical military sense, the single most powerful cavalry force in the eastern kingdoms. They are also, in a cultural sense, the most visible symbol of what Artaxarta is.

## The Magisterium

The **Magisterium** is the scholarly ministry of the crown — one of the fifteen ministries, charged with advising the king on matters of learning, natural philosophy, and the regulation of arcane practice. It employs several hundred scholars, researchers, archivists, and administrative staff, and its library in Alcidea is the largest collection in the eastern kingdoms outside of the great temple archives.

The Magisterium's particular relevance to magical practitioners comes from its relationship with the legacy of the **Imperial Mage's Guild**. During the Bandorian period, the Guild was the sole licensed body for magical practice in the Empire — a state-chartered institution that registered practitioners, supervised research, controlled the distribution of magical knowledge, and was empowered to investigate and prosecute unsanctioned use. The Guild no longer exists as an institution; when the Empire dissolved, its administrative infrastructure collapsed with it. But its cultural legacy persists, especially in Artaxarta, where the Ministry of the Magisterium has inherited much of the Guild's regulatory role and its institutional memory.

In practical terms: a wizard trained under Guild-descended methods — meaning a wizard who learned through structured institutional study, whose practice is documented and supervised, and who holds a certification from the Magisterium or its recognized affiliates — is viewed in Artaxarta as a licensed professional. They are regarded with wariness, as all magic users are, but a wariness that is contained by the institutional framework around them. This is as close to culturally acceptable as arcane practice gets in this kingdom.

**Orestes Sarkhazi** has served as Lord of the Magisterium for thirty years. He is, by any objective measure, one of the most respected figures in the five cities — a scholar of genuine breadth whose publications on natural history, metaphysics, and ancient languages are cited throughout the eastern kingdoms. He is also, by several accounts, unexpectedly warm for a man of his position and his reputation; people who have met him in professional contexts often describe him as someone who makes you feel that your questions are interesting. He gave the reassurance lecture at the Scholars' Hall three weeks ago and the applause was not perfunctory. People genuinely find him comforting.

# FACTIONS / OUTSIDE GROUPS

## The Crown and Royal Guard

**What they are:** The legitimate governing authority of Artaxarta, currently operating in the shadow of a dynastic crisis. The young prince is eight years old. The royal council is managing the country in his name, with **General Heskalo** as the primary military authority and the fifteen ministers maintaining their institutional functions. The Kataphractoi unit assigned to the prince's personal guard is the most visible expression of royal protective power in the current moment.

**How they relate to the crisis:** The crown is the primary force attempting to address the Brotherhood siege and suppress the cult resurgence. Its capacity to do so is constrained by the assassination of the king, the youth of the heir, and the disruption of normal institutional function. The royal council is functional; it is not undamaged.

**Membership:** Characters who serve the crown — in the royal guard, the legions, or the ministries — understand themselves as part of a chain of obligation that runs from their specific post up through the hierarchy to the throne itself. The oath matters. The fact that the person on the throne is eight years old does not change what the oath means.

## The Brotherhood of Light

**What they are:** The kingdom's foremost holy order — 250 adepts sworn to Ilura, whose principal mission has been the suppression of Umgul worship for generations. They operate independently of the crown's chain of command, answering ultimately to the High Priest of the Temple. They have rarely needed the crown's help and have rarely asked for it.

**How they relate to the crisis:** They are the crisis. The Brotherhood fortress is where the cult chose to strike first, and the fact that it has held for three months says something about the quality of the people inside it. The Brotherhood has been the kingdom's shield against Umgul worship for living memory. Characters who have trained with them, who have family there, or who have a professional relationship with the order will be feeling the weight of the current silence from that fortress.

**Membership:** No one joins the Brotherhood as an adult adventurer. But characters may have a meaningful connection to the order — a sibling who is an adept, a former teacher, a period of training at one of the outer chapter houses before the player's path diverged. The Brotherhood's code of conduct — chivalry, piety, absolute honesty — is something that rubs off on people who spend time near the order, even those who never took the final vows.

## The Kryptea

**What they are:** The kingdom's intelligence service. Everyone knows it exists. No one discusses it, especially not with strangers. The Kryptea has historically maintained operatives embedded throughout the court, the noble class, the ministries, and the institutional life of the five cities. Its reach was once considered comprehensive.

**How they relate to the crisis:** The Kryptea's network has not been communicating since approximately three weeks after the Jinsoona. Whether this means the network was neutralized, has gone to ground, or is operating under a silence protocol that no one in your circle has the keys to is not publicly known. What is known — to those who were close enough to the network to notice — is that the dead drops have gone dark, the contacts have stopped responding, and the institutional resource that would normally be supporting a crisis of this magnitude appears to be unavailable.

**Membership:** Characters with a background in Kryptea work understand themselves as operating in a world where the normal support structure has been removed. Assets they cultivated may still be reachable. Protocols they learned may still function in their immediate area. The institution itself is not available to call on. Whether they continue to operate by the service's old methods, or by the methods of people who no longer have a service, is a question the adventure will put to them.

## The Sadars

**What they are:** The tribal leaders of Artaxarta's twenty-three recognized tribes — figures of cultural authority who serve as the king's representatives in the uplands and as the voice of the tribal communities to the crown. They are not feudal lords; they hold no land grants and command no permanent armies. They hold moral authority — which, in Artaxarta's tribal culture, translates into the willingness of the Zavra-Prag to muster when called.

**How they relate to the crisis:** The annual **Jurga** — the month-long council session where the Sadars meet with the king — was disrupted by the Jinsoona events. It has not been formally reconvened. Some Sadars have been in contact with the royal council through unofficial channels; others have been in contact with each other in ways that fall outside the normal Jurga structure. The uplands communities are watching the crisis from a distance and making their own assessments. Whether the crown can count on the Zavra-Prag depends on whether it can bring the Sadars to an agreement — which depends on reaching them, earning their trust, and giving them a reason to believe that the crown's cause is also theirs.

**Membership:** Characters from upland backgrounds have relationships with the Sadar network that valley-born characters do not. For those characters, the Sadars are not an abstract institutional resource but specific individuals — people with names, histories, grievances, and loyalties that character knowledge can navigate in ways that formal diplomacy cannot.

## The Cult of Umgul

**What they are:** The followers of Umgul — a dark deity associated with sorcery, corruption, and the destruction of existence — who are banned throughout the kingdom and punishable by death. The cult has been a persistent presence in Artaxarta since ancient times, suppressed to near-extinction repeatedly by the Brotherhood of Light and the combined forces of crown and temple, and repeatedly resurgent in the generations that follow each suppression. Citizens do not generally understand why the cult keeps returning; the answer that the authorities provide — that evil is persistent and vigilance cannot be relaxed — is accepted because it is adequate.

The cult's tenets, as understood by the general public, teach that existence is suffering, that the world and those around us are illusions, and that the only truth is oblivion. Its followers are understood to actively seek destruction — their own and others'. The official theological account connects Umgul to Altair, one of the darker aspects of the Twelve Divines, which creates a permanent ambient association between the cult and the Asta-worshipping border states.

**How they relate to the crisis:** The cult is the crisis. The siege of the Brotherhood fortress is the most organized, most persistent, and most geographically significant cult action in living memory. Its forces are not behaving like a militia or a mob; they are behaving like something with different priorities than a normal army. What those priorities are is not yet understood by most people, including most of the people trying to stop them.

**Membership:** No player character would willingly be a member of the cult. But characters may have encountered cult activity, known someone who was found to be a member, or have a personal reason to understand what it is and why it works on the people it reaches.

### Fighter

Artaxarta produces fighters the way its valleys produce grain — constantly, in quantity, and with the quality that comes from a culture that has understood war as a condition of existence since before it had a written language. The **Legonis Hykyrianos** produces the kingdom's professional infantry: disciplined, heraldic, devastating in formation. The **Zavra-Prag** produces the tribal militia: variable in equipment, uniform in loyalty, and capable of numbers that no professional force can match. The **Kataphractoi** produce the finest heavy cavalry in the eastern kingdoms: trained from childhood, armored against anything, and bred for the kind of charge that ends battles.

A fighter in Artaxarta has no shortage of institutional homes. Every clan maintains its kulak. Every city has its legion garrison. The royal guard is always recruiting from the Kataphractoi rolls, and the Kataphractoi are always accepting candidates who survive their initial trials. The fighter's particular competence — controlled, professional violence — is not only respected in this culture, it is understood as a form of citizenship.

What distinguishes fighters here from their counterparts in more settled societies is the culture of excellence that permeates Artaxartoi military life. Merit is the hallmark of social position; a soldier who performs well is rewarded with genuine respect; a soldier who fails is scorned regardless of birth. This creates an environment in which fighters are constantly measured against each other, and in which the standards are not theoretical. There are living people in this kingdom who have charged into forces three times their size and won. You are expected to be able to do the same.

#### Lancer

*The Kataphractoi do not fight battles. They end them.*

The **Lancer** is the fighter of the cavalry tradition — the Kataphractoi combat doctrine translated into a playable subclass for characters who have trained in mounted warfare. Not all Lancers are Kataphractoi; some come from the cavalry-heavy kulaks of the western clan militias, and some have trained under retired Kataphractoi officers in provincial garrisons. But the subclass represents a fighting style that is, in Artaxarta, associated specifically with the most elite military tradition in the kingdom.

**Mounted Warrior (Level 3).** You gain proficiency with lances. While mounted, you can wield a lance in one hand without disadvantage, and you do not suffer disadvantage on lance attacks against targets within 5 feet of your mount. Additionally, any effect that would force you to dismount requires you to fail a Strength saving throw (DC 12) before it takes effect.

**Shock Charge (Level 3).** When you move at least 20 feet in a straight line toward a target while mounted and then hit it with a melee weapon attack on the same turn, the attack deals an extra 1d8 damage of the weapon's type. The target must then succeed on a Strength saving throw (DC 8 + your proficiency bonus + your Strength modifier) or be knocked prone.

**Cavalry Instinct (Level 7).** While mounted, you have advantage on melee weapon attack rolls against creatures that are prone. Additionally, your mount's movement does not provoke opportunity attacks from creatures you have attacked this turn.

**Iron in the Saddle (Level 10).** Your mount gains extra hit points equal to three times your proficiency bonus. While you are mounted, if your mount would be reduced to 0 hit points, you can use your reaction to redirect up to 20 points of that damage to yourself; your mount drops to 1 hit point instead. You cannot use this feature if the redirected damage would reduce you to 0 hit points.

**Devastating Lance (Level 15).** Your Shock Charge extra damage increases to 2d8. When you knock a creature prone with Shock Charge, each enemy creature within 10 feet of it that can see you must succeed on a Wisdom saving throw (DC 8 + your proficiency bonus + your Charisma modifier) or become **frightened** of you until the end of your next turn.

**Unstoppable (Level 18).** Once per turn, when you use Shock Charge, you may continue moving through the target's space. Any creature whose space you enter during this movement (other than the target) must succeed on a Dexterity saving throw (DC 8 + your proficiency bonus + your Strength modifier) or take your Shock Charge extra damage and be knocked prone. You may move through a creature's space this way only once per turn.

### Paladin

The **Brotherhood of Light** is the kingdom's most visible expression of the paladin tradition, but it is not the only one. Paladins exist throughout Artaxarta in the service of the Twelve Divines — sworn warriors who carry divine sanction into the field and whose power comes from the same source as a cleric's: the will of the god they serve, not the years of study they have accumulated. This distinction matters enormously in Artaxarta's cultural calculus. A paladin is not a magic user in the suspicious sense. A paladin is someone the gods have endorsed for violence, and the Artaxartoi respect that endorsement.

The Brotherhood of Light has a formal structure: 250 adepts, five Knight Commanders, the elaborate trial system, the oaths of poverty and chastity. Independent paladins who have sworn to one of the Twelve Divines outside the Brotherhood's structure are less formally supported but command the same basic cultural respect — their power is understood to come from above, not from arcane study or innate nature, and that makes all the difference.

Paladins in this setting carry weight. When you walk into a room with the visible marks of divine sanction — the bearing, the manner, the power that comes when you call on your oath — people understand what they are looking at. That is an asset. It is also a responsibility: paladins are expected, in Artaxarta, to be what they appear to be. The culture does not have much patience for the gap between the symbol and the reality.

#### Oath of Purity

*The goddess does not ask her warriors to be gentle. She asks them to be unbreakable.*

The **Oath of Purity** is the oath sworn by paladins of the Brotherhood of Light tradition — the formal vow taken at the completion of the Brotherhood's trial process. Characters who take this oath need not be full members of the Brotherhood; independent paladins devoted to Ilura may take the same oath through the temple's formal ceremony. The oath's demands are absolute: honesty, piety, the active suppression of the cult's influence wherever it is found, and the protection of the innocent at whatever cost is required.

**Oath Spells.** You gain the following spells at the listed paladin levels. These spells count as paladin spells for you and do not count against the number of paladin spells you can prepare.

| Paladin Level | Spells |
|---|---|
| 3rd | *protection from evil and good, detect evil and good* |
| 5th | *zone of truth, warding bond* |
| 9th | *daylight, beacon of hope* |
| 13th | *death ward, freedom of movement* |
| 17th | *dispel evil and good, holy aura* |

**Channel Divinity (Level 3).** When you take this oath at 3rd level, you gain the following two Channel Divinity options.

**Consecrated Ground.** As an action, you present your holy symbol. The ground within 30 feet of you becomes consecrated for 1 minute. Fiends, undead, and aberrations that enter or start their turn in this area take 2d8 radiant damage and must succeed on a Wisdom saving throw (DC equal to your spell save DC) or be **frightened** until the end of their next turn. This area functions as holy ground for the purpose of any spell or feature that references it.

**Strike of Purity.** When you hit a creature with a melee weapon attack, you can use your Channel Divinity as a bonus action. The attack deals an additional 2d8 radiant damage, and the target loses all damage resistances and condition immunities it possesses until the start of your next turn.

**Aura of the Unbroken (Level 7).** You and friendly creatures within 10 feet of you cannot be **charmed** or **possessed** while you are conscious. Friendly creatures within 10 feet that are concentrating on a spell have advantage on Constitution saving throws to maintain concentration. At 18th level, this aura increases to 30 feet.

**Purified Soul (Level 15).** You are immune to the **charmed** and **frightened** conditions. Additionally, when you are reduced to 0 hit points, you can use your reaction to expend a spell slot; if you do, you drop to 1 hit point and regain hit points equal to five times the expended spell slot's level.

**Avatar of Ilura (Level 20).** As an action, you take on the martial aspect of Ilura for 1 minute. During this time:
- You and all friendly creatures within 30 feet of you regain hit points equal to your Charisma modifier at the start of each of your turns.
- Hostile fiends, undead, and aberrations within 30 feet must make a Wisdom saving throw (DC equal to your spell save DC) at the start of each of their turns or be **frightened** of you until the end of that turn.
- Your melee weapon attacks deal radiant damage in addition to their normal damage type.

Once you use this feature, you can't use it again until you finish a long rest.

### Ranger

Rangers in Artaxarta are most commonly found in two places: the upland scouting corps attached to the Zavra-Prag tribal militias, and the network of independent trackers and mountain guides who operate throughout the highland passes. Both types share a deep practical knowledge of terrain — the mountain valleys, the passes, the seasonal patterns of the high country — and a hunter's relationship with the world above the treeline. In a kingdom where the terrain is as much a defensive asset as the army, a ranger who knows which passes flood in spring and which ridgelines provide a line of sight to the Brotherhood road is not merely useful. They are irreplaceable.

Rangers from upland clan backgrounds tend to work within a social structure — answerable to their Sadar, integrated into the clan's scouting network, familiar with the informal agreements between tribes about territory and movement. Rangers who operate independently have often left the clan structure behind, or were never firmly embedded in it, and have built their operational knowledge through personal experience rather than inherited tradition. Both types are recognized and respected in their respective contexts.

### Rogue

Artaxarta has need of people who work in the spaces between institutions, and it has produced them. The **Kryptea** is the most formal expression of this — the intelligence service that has, for generations, maintained operatives embedded throughout the court, the noble class, and the institutional life of the five cities. But the Kryptea is not the only context in which a skilled operator might have developed their abilities. The Great Market of Alcidea has its guild of factors and information brokers, loosely affiliated and loosely regulated, who know that the most valuable commodity in any commercial center is the thing someone else doesn't know yet. The network of safe houses and unofficial communication channels maintained by various factions produces people who are, by necessity, skilled at movement without notice.

A rogue in Artaxarta is not automatically a criminal — though some are. They are, more precisely, someone whose effectiveness depends on operating outside the visibility that formal institutional affiliation provides. In a culture that prizes directness and merit, there is something culturally ambiguous about expertise in indirection. Rogues are respected for what they can do and regarded with a specific wariness that their skills do nothing to dispel.

#### Kryptea Operative

*The Kryptea has no faces. The ones who survive keep it that way.*

The **Kryptea Operative** is the rogue of the intelligence tradition — a character who has trained, formally or informally, in the methods of the kingdom's intelligence service. Formal operatives were recruited, trained, and embedded by the Kryptea itself; informal operatives absorbed the tradecraft through proximity, prior affiliation, or the process of surviving in environments where those skills were necessary. The Kryptea's network has been functionally destroyed since the Jinsoona. Operatives who remain active are doing so on their own resources, with whatever contacts and institutional knowledge they retained.

**Network Contact (Level 3).** In any settlement, you can spend 1 hour discreetly gathering information and make an Intelligence (Investigation) check. On a success, you learn useful intelligence about a target, location, or situation. The DM determines the DC: DC 10 for common knowledge, DC 15 for guarded information, DC 20 for closely held secrets. You have advantage on this check in settlements where you have previously operated.

**Tradecraft (Level 3).** You gain expertise in either Deception or Stealth (your choice) — your proficiency bonus is doubled for ability checks using that skill. Additionally, you can establish, use, and detect concealed message locations that are undetectable to others without magical assistance. Only you and designated recipients can recognize the signs of a dead drop you have established.

**Shadow Asset (Level 9).** You have cultivated a single embedded contact — a specific individual in a position of useful access (a city guardsman, a noble's servant, a minor ministry official). Work with your DM to determine this person's specific identity and position. Once between long rests, you can send a message to your asset requesting information or discreet assistance. Whether the asset complies depends on the risk to them and the current state of your relationship with them.

**Cover Identity (Level 13).** You maintain a constructed identity supported by documentation, references, and a practised backstory. While operating under this identity, Wisdom (Insight) checks against you have **disadvantage**. When a spell or feature attempts to magically discern your true identity (such as *detect thoughts* or *zone of truth*), the caster must succeed on an Intelligence check against your Deception skill check result or receive no useful information about your true identity. If your cover is destroyed, you can reconstruct a new one over 7 days of downtime activity.

**Ghost Protocol (Level 17).** When you take damage that would reduce you to 0 hit points, you can use your reaction to instead drop to 1 hit point, immediately become **invisible**, and move up to your speed without provoking opportunity attacks. The invisibility lasts until the start of your next turn or until you attack, cast a spell, or use a feature. Once you use this feature, you can't use it again until you finish a long rest.

### Wizard

The **Imperial Mage's Guild** no longer exists as an institution — it dissolved when the Bandorian Empire did, three hundred years ago — but its cultural legacy is the reason wizards in Artaxarta are not immediately killed or expelled. During the Imperial period, the Guild was the sole licensed body for magical practice: it registered practitioners, supervised research, controlled the distribution of arcane knowledge, and was empowered to investigate and prosecute unsanctioned use. The arrangement was not generous to wizards, but it made them legible — professionals in a recognized field, subject to oversight, their power catalogued and their oath of service documented.

The Magisterium inherited much of this role. A wizard who has trained under Guild-descended methods — structured institutional study, documented practice, a certification recognized by the Magisterium — is regarded in Artaxarta as a licensed professional. Not beloved, not fully trusted, but accepted within a framework that the culture understands. The wariness is real; so is the accommodation. A Guild-certified wizard who presents their credentials to a city official will not be arrested. A wizard who cannot produce such credentials and cannot explain their abilities through an institutional framework will have a very different conversation.

Wizards who choose to work within this system find that it provides genuine professional infrastructure: access to the Magisterium library, recognition by the ministries, and the social legitimacy that comes from being understood to have been checked. Those who operate outside it find that the cultural suspicion that surrounds all arcane practice applies to them with nothing to mitigate it.

#### Guild Mage

*The Imperial Mage's Guild produced many things. Mediocre wizards were not among them.*

The **Guild Mage** is the wizard who has trained under the formal methodology of the Guild tradition — the structured approach to magical study that emphasizes precision, efficiency, identification, and the countermeasures that the Guild developed as the institutional authority responsible for regulating everyone else. Guild Mages are not merely wizards who happen to have Magisterium certification; they have internalized a specific approach to the craft, with particular strengths in identifying, neutralizing, and out-sustaining other practitioners.

**Guild Certification (Level 2).** You gain proficiency in the History skill. If you are already proficient in History, your proficiency bonus is doubled for Intelligence (History) checks related to arcane traditions, scholarly institutions, or magical history. Additionally, you can cast *identify* as a ritual without expending material components. You must still spend the normal ritual casting time.

**Methodical Casting (Level 2).** Your Guild training emphasizes sustained, structured concentration. You have advantage on Constitution saving throws to maintain concentration on spells. Additionally, when you are forced to make a concentration check, you can use your reaction to add your Intelligence modifier to the roll (minimum +1). You can use this reaction a number of times equal to your Intelligence modifier, and you regain all uses after a long rest.

**Regulatory Knowledge (Level 6).** You have advantage on Intelligence (Arcana) checks to identify spells, magical effects, and the school and source of magic you observe. Additionally, when you cast *counterspell* or *dispel magic*, you can add your Intelligence modifier to the check required to counter or dispel a spell cast using a higher-level spell slot than you used.

**Sanctioned Studies (Level 10).** Choose two spells from any class's spell list, each of 5th level or lower. These spells count as wizard spells for you, and you add them to your spellbook. You can cast each of these spells once without expending a spell slot; you regain the ability to cast each spell this way after finishing a long rest.

**Guild Mastery (Level 14).** When you cast a spell of 1st level or higher, you can choose one of the following enhancements: the spell's range doubles; the spell's save DC increases by 2; or the spell forces each target to make its saving throw with **disadvantage**. You can use this feature a number of times equal to your Intelligence modifier (minimum once), and you regain all uses after a long rest.

### Cleric

A cleric in Artaxarta is not someone who went to seminary and emerged with a theological degree. A cleric is someone the gods chose. This distinction is understood broadly by the Artaxartoi population and shapes the entire cultural position of the class: where a wizard is a professional in a licensed field, a cleric is an instrument of divine will, and the implications of that are not subtle.

When a cleric manifests genuine divine power — healing the sick, calling down protection, turning the forces of darkness — it is understood as an event. The gods are paying attention. Something has warranted their direct involvement. This produces an awe that ordinary priesthood cannot command and that even the most accomplished wizard cannot replicate. A cleric of Ilura who walks into a room full of skeptics and then heals someone who should not be healable has, in that moment, won every argument she will ever need to have in this country.

The temple structure provides a social context for clerics — the clergy of the Twelve Divines includes everyone from the most junior temple acolyte to the High Priest, and a cleric who operates within that structure has institutional support and a recognized place in the hierarchy. Independent clerics — those chosen by the gods outside any formal temple affiliation — are accepted but less socially located; the question "which temple are you with?" is both practical and, in the current moment, not entirely neutral.

---

### Druid

Druids occupy the specific and unusual cultural position of practitioners who are regarded with genuine warmth in a setting that views most magical practice with suspicion. This is not because druids are perceived as less magical; it is because what they do is understood as ancient tradition — the continuation of rites that predate heartlander contact, tied specifically to the goddess in her oldest aspect. A druid arriving in a highland village to conduct the spring planting rites is not an outsider; they are a continuation of something the village has always done.

The figure of the druid in Artaxartoi culture is specifically connected to **Lhakmana** — the ancient name for the goddess before the heartlanders called her Ilura. Druids who operate in this tradition are understood to work the boundary between the human world and the natural one, speaking to the land in the terms the land recognizes. This is not metaphorical; the Artaxartoi take it literally. The high pastures respond to the right words spoken in the right season. The mountain springs produce clean water when the old prayers are offered. Whether this is the goddess's work, the land's own nature, or something else entirely is a theological question that most Artaxartoi do not feel the need to resolve.

Valley-dwellers are somewhat less familiar with the druidic tradition than highland communities, but even in Alcidea there is a residual cultural understanding that druids represent something real and old and not to be dismissed. The wariness that attaches to other magical practitioners simply does not attach to druids here.

### Barbarian

The barbarian's particular gift — the capacity to access a state of fighting fury that transcends normal physical limits — is understood in Artaxartoi culture through the lens of the highland clan tradition. The upland warriors of the Zavra-Prag have their own word for it: **kata**, the life-essence or strength that can be called upon in extremity. The concept predates heartlander contact; ancient Artaxartoi texts describe warriors who, in the moment of greatest need, became something beyond what a human being normally is. This is not magic in the arcane sense. It is not associated with sorcery or with Umgul. It is understood as the deepest expression of the body's own capacity — what a person becomes when they have nothing left to hold back.

Barbarians are found throughout Artaxarta — in the highland clans most visibly, but also in the legions, where a soldier who can enter this state is a valuable asset in a breach or a desperate defense, and in the outlying communities that still maintain the old clan traditions of the uplands. They are respected for what they can do and, like all things that exceed normal human capacity, regarded with a mixture of admiration and careful distance.

---

### Bard

Artaxartoi oral tradition stretches back thousands of years — the tribal bards who preserved the history, mythology, and law of the native peoples through the long eras before heartlander contact, and the subsequent generations who absorbed the Xoshan and Durasi literary traditions while maintaining the thread of native verse-making. The result is a rich, layered tradition that includes everything from the epic poems commemorating the old tribal wars to the sophisticated court poetry of the Alcidean literary culture to the work songs and herding ballads of the highland pastures.

Bards in Artaxarta function as storytellers, historians, negotiators, and — in their more practical incarnations — intelligence gatherers who move through social contexts with an ease that more obviously dangerous individuals cannot manage. The bardic tradition gives its practitioners a form of access that other classes have to work for; a bard who knows the old songs and can produce them at the right moment in a highland clan hall has done something that a Kataphractoi officer with a letter of commission cannot replicate.

The cultural suspicion that attaches to arcane magic does not attach to bardic ability in quite the same way. Bards are understood as practitioners of inspiration and skill — the magic in their work is of a different character than the studied power of a wizard. That said, a bard who is visibly manipulating people with magical means in a context where that manipulation is unwelcome will not find that the cultural category protects them. The distinction matters at the level of first impression; it does not provide unlimited cover.

---

### Monk

The Brotherhood of Light is the most famous monastic tradition in Artaxarta, but it is not the only one. The temple complex in Alcidea maintains several smaller orders — contemplative communities devoted to specific aspects of the Twelve Divines, practicing austere living and physical discipline that does not reach the heights of the Brotherhood's combat training but produces practitioners of genuine capability. The Brotherhood's outer chapter houses are also associated with lay communities of practitioners who trained in Brotherhood methods without taking the final vows.

Monks in Artaxarta are understood through the lens of disciplined practice — the body as a tool refined over years toward a specific excellence. This fits comfortably into the Artaxartoi cultural framework that prizes merit and expertise; a monk who has achieved their abilities through sustained discipline is comprehensible in a way that innate power is not. The specific abilities associated with the class — the capacity to channel internal energy, to strike with precision beyond ordinary human speed — are associated with long practice and the cultivation of *kata*, the concept of life-essence that appears throughout Artaxartoi culture.

### Sorcerer

Sorcerers face a social reality in Artaxarta that is not theoretical. During the Bandorian Imperial period, the Temple of the Twelve Divines conducted active purges of sorcerous practitioners throughout the eastern kingdoms — a campaign that lasted for generations and that drove sorcerers to the edge of extinction in this region. The reasoning was theological: innate arcane power, power that manifests without study or divine sanction, was associated with Umgul and with the darkness his cult embodied. The ancient Artaxartoi had the same belief, expressed in older terms: those born with this kind of power were touched by the dark deity, and followers of his cult were rooted out and killed in ancient times.

The Empire's collapse ended the formal purges. It did not end the cultural inheritance they left. A person in Artaxarta who manifests sorcerous power — power that is clearly not learned, not granted by a recognized divine patron, and not explainable through any legitimate institutional framework — is in danger. Not from a formal legal apparatus (the Imperial law that mandated the purges no longer exists), but from the cultural reflex that the purges trained into a population over generations. Most people who discover a sorcerer do not pause for theological nuance. They reach for a weapon, or they run to someone who will.

Sorcerers who survive in Artaxarta do so by not being sorcerers, publicly. They pass as wizards if their abilities permit it, present Guild-adjacent credentials if they can obtain them, and maintain the fiction of studied practice with whatever persistence it takes. They live inside the careful management of what other people believe about them. This is not hypothetical caution — it is the practical condition of their existence in this setting.

---

### Warlock

Pact magic has no legitimate face in Artaxarta. There is no institutional framework that accommodates it, no historical tradition that normalizes it, and no cultural category that makes a warlock's explanation of their abilities feel like anything other than an admission of something deeply troubling. The nature of the pact — a bargain with an entity of significant power, exchanging service for magical ability — resonates in a culture already sensitized to the danger of exactly that kind of arrangement. The cult of Umgul is understood, at some level, as people who made a bargain with the wrong kind of power. A warlock describing their patron relationship to an ordinary Artaxartoi citizen is going to land in a conceptual space adjacent to that understanding, and no amount of explaining will fully move it.

This does not mean a warlock character cannot exist in this setting. It means the character carries a significant social management challenge that is part of their life in this world. The specific nature of a warlock's patron matters: a warlock who serves a celestial entity and can credibly frame their abilities as divine sanction may be able to pass as a cleric to those who are not looking closely. A warlock whose patron is something stranger will find the options for explanation narrower. The character knows what they are and what that means in this place.

## Custom Backgrounds

### Royal Guard

You served the crown directly — as a member of the Kataphractoi, an officer in one of the seven legions, or an attached member of the royal household. Before the cult's campaign began, your life was defined by the specifics of your post: the hierarchy, the training, the oath sworn in the king's name, and the professional relationships with the people who served alongside you. Some of those people are now dead. Some have gone missing. A few of them, you cannot account for in ways that trouble you. The kingdom you swore to protect is not the kingdom you are fighting in now, and you are still working out what your oath means in the world that's being made.

**Skill Proficiencies:** Athletics, Intimidation

**Tool Proficiencies:** One type of gaming set

**Languages:** One of your choice

**Equipment:** A set of traveler's clothes, a military insignia or medal from your service, a shortsword, a letter of commission bearing the royal seal, and a belt pouch containing 10 gp.

**Feature: Chain of Command.** You are recognized as a figure of royal authority by military and administrative institutions. You can requisition food, lodging, and basic equipment from military outposts and government facilities for yourself and up to four companions. Individuals affiliated with the Legions or the Royal Guard will generally cooperate with your reasonable requests unless they have been given specific contrary orders by a superior officer.

*Suggested personality:* You know what it means to follow orders and to give them. What you don't know yet is what to do when the institution that gave you both has been struck at its foundation — and when the people who should be giving you orders are eight years old.

### Kryptea Operative

The Kryptea does not officially exist. You do not officially exist as a member of it. For some years, you did the kingdom's necessary work in its necessary darkness — cultivated contacts, identified threats, moved information across lines that official channels could not cross. You became very good at it. Then Alcidea fell and the network you served was not dismantled so much as it was cut off at the roots. You are still operational. You are simply operational alone now, with contacts going dark one by one and no one left to report to.

**Skill Proficiencies:** Deception, Insight

**Tool Proficiencies:** Disguise kit or forgery kit (your choice)

**Languages:** One of your choice

**Equipment:** A set of common clothes, a disguise kit or forgery kit matching your tool proficiency, a small knife, a set of forged documents establishing a false identity, and a belt pouch containing 15 gp.

**Feature: Dead Drop Network.** You have established concealed communication points in settlements throughout the kingdom. In any settlement of significant size, you can spend 1 hour and make a DC 13 Intelligence (Investigation) check to locate an existing dead drop or establish a new one; on a success, you can retrieve any information that has been left for you (the DM determines what, if anything, is there) or leave messages for others in the network. Additionally, you have one contact in a specific city or town — determined with your DM — who is not yet compromised and who can be reached through normal means.

*Suggested personality:* You have built your life on the understanding that information is the only currency that doesn't devalue. The question now is whether the people who used to buy it are still in business, or whether you are the last one left who knows how the market works.

### Child of the Uplands

You were raised in one of the highland clans — the pastoral communities that cling to the high pastures above the river valleys, keeping ways that the valley-dwellers have largely forgotten. You know the old songs and the old names. You know which peaks are sacred and which passes flood in spring. You know how to read a Sadar's mood from the way he holds his riding crop, and you know that the goddess they pray to in Alcidea's marble temple is the same one your grandmother called by a different name and worshiped at a cairn of stones in a high meadow. The cities are vast and disorienting to you, but the mountains are home.

**Skill Proficiencies:** Animal Handling, Survival

**Tool Proficiencies:** Herbalism kit

**Languages:** One of your choice

**Equipment:** A set of traveler's clothes, an herbalism kit, a tribal token (a carved bone pendant, a clan-marked ring, or a similar small item of personal significance), a hunting knife, and a belt pouch containing 8 gp.

**Feature: Sadar's Name.** You have a relationship with one of the named Sadars — either through your own clan or through a mentor who vouched for you. Work with your DM to determine which Sadar and the nature of your connection. When dealing with highland communities and tribal leaders, you can invoke this connection to receive hospitality, an honest hearing, and a degree of trust that outsiders rarely earn. The Sadar in question expects loyalty and discretion from you in return. Reaching them requires travel to the uplands, but their door will not be closed to you.

*Suggested personality:* You understand what it means to belong to a people who kept their ways intact through everything that has been done to this country. The question the adventure will put to you is whether keeping those ways intact is worth what it is going to cost.

## Character Hooks

Answer the following questions when building your character. Your answers are shared with the DM and will be plot-relevant. There are no wrong answers — only answers that are more or less interesting to play.

1. **The night the king died:** Where were you when the Jinsoona attack began, and what did you see or hear in those first hours? Is there something you noticed that you haven't told anyone — not because you hid it deliberately, but because you weren't sure what it meant?

2. **The Brotherhood:** Name someone connected to the Brotherhood of Light — an adept, a lay servant, a chapter house contact — who you have not been able to reach since the siege began. How long have you known them, and what does their silence mean to you?

3. **The first sign:** What is the first thing you noticed that didn't add up — not the siege, but something smaller, something closer to your own life? A colleague whose behavior changed, a routine that was quietly altered, a question that got an answer you didn't quite believe? What did you tell yourself to explain it at the time?

4. **The prince:** Prince Eurimedos has been in your orbit, in some capacity, since before the crisis. What is your honest impression of him? What worries you about the way he is handling what has happened to his family?

5. **The institution:** Is there a figure in the kingdom's power structure — a minister, an official, a respected scholar, a senior officer — whose reassurances have started to feel slightly thin to you? You have not acted on this feeling. Why not?

6. **The line:** What would make you walk away from this — from the mission, the crown, the people beside you? You haven't crossed it yet. Where is it, and what would it take for someone to push you over it?

# WHAT YOUR CHARACTER KNOWS

## Common Knowledge

The following things are known to every native character without explanation. They are the assumed baseline that needs no in-character discovery.

- **The cult of Umgul is a capital offense.** Membership, facilitation, and active worship are all punishable by death. The Brotherhood of Light is the primary institutional force responsible for suppressing the cult, and has been for generations. The prohibition is not controversial.

- **The Brotherhood's fortress is under siege.** The **Priory of the White Gates** in the southern mountains has been surrounded by cult forces for approximately three months. This is unprecedented. The Brotherhood does not ask for help. The crown has not formally announced that it is providing any. The official account calls it a manageable disturbance.

- **King Telamon is dead.** He was killed on the night of the Jinsoona festival. The official account says he died in the opening hours of a coordinated cult attack. Prince Eurimedos, his son, is eight years old. The regency is being organized.

- **Orestes Sarkhazi is the Lord of the Magisterium.** He has held the post for thirty years. He is one of the most respected scholars in the five cities — a man of genuine breadth and well-documented achievement. He gave a public lecture on cult resurgence patterns three weeks ago. It was well-received.

- **The Kryptea exists.** Everyone knows this. No one discusses it, especially not with strangers, especially not now.

- **How you came by your power matters.** Magic is uncommon in Artaxarta, and the source of magical ability is a social fact. A wizard with Magisterium certification is a licensed professional. A cleric or paladin carries divine sanction. A druid practices ancient tradition. A sorcerer is something that no one announces they are, in this kingdom, unless they are prepared for what follows.

- **The Sadars hold the Zavra-Prag.** The crown cannot mobilize the tribal militia without Sadar cooperation. The annual Jurga was disrupted by the crisis. The Sadars have not been formally convened since.

- **Artaxarta has never been successfully conquered.** This is not sentiment. It is the bedrock fact of the national identity. The kingdom has survived the Xoshans, the Bandorian Empire, and every border conflict since. Whatever is happening now is not the thing that will break it. This is what every Artaxartoi believes, and most of them believe it correctly.
